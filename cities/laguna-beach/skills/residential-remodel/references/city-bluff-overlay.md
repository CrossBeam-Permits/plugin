# Laguna Beach — Bluff Overlay (§25.50.004(B)(4) + §25.50.020)

This file documents Laguna's bluff-top setback rule and oceanfront-lot site-coverage shrinkage. There is **no standalone "Bluff Overlay" chapter** in the LBMC. The load-bearing rule is at §25.50.004(B)(4) (Building Setbacks on or Adjacent to the Pacific Ocean and Beaches), Chapter 25.50 (General Yard and Open Space Provisions). Companion provisions live in §25.50.020 (Oceanfront lots).

---

## §25.50.004(B)(4) — 25-Foot Bluff-Top Setback

From the City source document (not included in this plugin) and `ch25-50` source on ecode360, verbatim:

> *"In addition to (1), (2) and (3) above, no new building, additions to existing buildings, or structures or improvements shall encroach beyond the applicable building stringline or shall be closer than twenty-five feet to the top of an oceanfront bluff; the more restrictive shall apply. Greater setback may be required by the city engineer or building official in order to protect the public health, safety or welfare. Pools and spas shall be no closer than twenty-five feet to the top of bluff. Public accessways shall be exempt from this provision."*

### Rule

- **Hard 25-ft minimum** to the top of an oceanfront bluff.
- **Building stringline more restrictive applies** — if the stringline (defined below) places the building further inland than 25 ft, the stringline controls.
- **Greater setback** may be required by the city engineer or building official for public health/safety/welfare.
- **Pools and spas** — also no closer than 25 ft to top of bluff. (Same standard as buildings; no separate pool standard.)
- **Only exception** — public accessways. Public accessways are exempt from the 25-ft bluff setback.

### Oceanfront Bluff — Geometric Definition (§25.50.004(B)(4)(a))

> *"An 'oceanfront bluff' is an oceanfront landform having a slope of forty-five degrees or greater from horizontal whose top is ten or more feet above mean sea level."*

- **Slope ≥45°** from horizontal AND
- **Top ≥10 ft above mean sea level**

Sub-rules (§25.50.004(B)(4)(a)(i)–(ii)):
- Irregular or multiple-slope conditions: setback is taken from the **most inland** 45°-or-greater slope.
- Less-than-45° landforms: case-by-case determination by the Director of Community Development.

### Building Stringline (§25.50.004(B)(4)(b))

> *"The building stringline averages the setback of oceanfront buildings on both adjacent sides of coastal lots and is defined as follows: The stringline setback shall be depicted as a line across a parcel that connects the oceanward ends of the nearest adjacent walls of the main buildings on adjacent lots. Posts or columns that extend to grade from upper story decks, balconies, stairways and other types of similar features shall not be used to define the building stringline criteria."*

- Connects the oceanward ends of the nearest adjacent walls of main buildings on adjacent lots.
- **Posts/columns from upper-story decks, balconies, stairways shall NOT be used** to define the stringline.

§25.50.004(B)(4)(b)(i): If there is no applicable stringline on adjacent lots, the setback shall be at least 25 ft from the top of the oceanfront bluff (i.e., the 25-ft floor controls).

§25.50.004(B)(4)(b)(ii): The DRB **may modify** the building setback only when the stringline is **significantly more restrictive** than the 25-ft setback AND unique conditions relating to landform, lot orientation or excessive building setbacks on adjacent property prevent or severely restrict residential development. (This is a TIGHTENING-side modification, not a relaxation-side modification — the DRB cannot bring a stringline-controlled lot back to 25 ft.)

### Deck Stringline (§25.50.004(B)(4)(c))

A deck stringline may be used for decks, defined as a line across a parcel connecting the oceanward ends of the decks on main buildings on adjacent lots.

### Building Projection Setback (§25.50.004(B)(4)(d))

Verbatim (§25.50.004(B)(4)(d)(i)):

> *"Balconies, patios or decks in excess of thirty inches above the finished grade, including patio deck covers, and other similar architectural features may project a maximum of five feet beyond the applicable building setback or to the applicable deck stringline, whichever is least restrictive. **In no case shall such projections be closer than ten feet to the top of an oceanfront bluff.**"*

§25.50.004(B)(4)(d)(ii):

> *"Decks, patios and other similar improvements that are thirty inches or less above finished grade shall not encroach closer than ten feet to the top of an oceanfront bluff."*

So projections (>30" or ≤30") are limited to a 10-ft bluff floor.

### No Permitted Projections Within Required Oceanfront Setback (§25.50.004(B)(5))

> *"Permitted projections into required yards as specified in Section 25.50.008 are not allowed within the required oceanfront setback areas."*

i.e., the §25.50.008 projection allowances (eaves, chimneys, etc.) do not apply within the oceanfront setback.

### Ordinance History — Pre-Existing Rule, NOT Ord. 1688

Section history at end of §25.50.004:
> *"(Ord. 811 § 1, 1974; Ord. 832 §§ 21, 22, 23, 1975; Ord. 1187 § 3(5), 1989; Ord. 1223 § 1, 1991; Ord. 1354 § 2, 1999; Ord. 1416 § 14, 2002)"*

- The 25-ft bluff setback was added by **Ord. 811 (1974)** and refined through 2002.
- **Ord. 1688 (7/11/2023) is NOT in this section's history.** Ord. 1688 amended §§25.07.008 and 25.07.012 (CDP procedures), per the section history at the end of §25.07.008 in the City source document (not included in this plugin). Earlier Laguna research drafts misattributed the bluff overlay to Ord. 1688 — that is wrong. Cite **§25.50.004(B)(4)** (statutory base) and the section's own ordinance history (Ord. 811 1974 → Ord. 1416 2002) for attribution.

## §25.50.020 — Oceanfront Lots (Site-Coverage Shrinkage)

From the City source document (not included in this plugin), verbatim:

> *"(B) Building site coverage shall be calculated using the following formula:*
> *Building Site Coverage (%) = 35 + (8,000 − A) × 9 / 4,000*
> *Where A = lot area as calculated using the building setback lines described in Section 25.50.004(B)(1), (2) and (3) **or the oceanfront bluff whichever is more restrictive**. Where A is less than four thousand square feet, the site coverage shall not exceed forty-four percent of A. Where A is eight thousand square feet and over, site coverage shall not exceed thirty five percent of A."*

### Why This Matters

The site-coverage formula shrinks **A** (the lot area used for the calculation) from the legal lot area to the smaller of:
- The legal lot area minus the elevation-line setback (12 ft / 13 ft / 14 ft above MSL depending on segment per §25.50.004(B)(1)–(3)), OR
- The legal lot area minus the area seaward of the oceanfront bluff edge.

Whichever is more restrictive controls. Practical effect — a project that survives the 25-ft bluff setback may still fail site-coverage because the developable area shrinks. This is the **second-largest financial trigger** after the 25-ft setback itself.

### Section History

> *"(Ord. 811 § 1, 1974; Ord. 1416 § 18, 2002)"*

Same ordinance lineage as §25.50.004 — pre-existing rule.

## Anti-Game Buried-Bluff Rule

From the Bluff Edge Determinations Quick Guide (March 2024), `source-corpus:laguna-beach/cdd/https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/10222/638533536501570000`, verbatim:

> *"Oceanfront bluff edge or coastal bluff edge is defined as the upper termination of a bluff, cliff, or seacliff. In cases where the top edge of the bluff is rounded away from the face of the bluff, the bluff edge shall be defined as that point nearest the bluff face beyond which a downward gradient is maintained continuously to the base of the bluff. In a case where there is a step like feature at the top of the bluff, the landward edge of the topmost riser shall be considered the bluff edge. Bluff edges typically retreat over time as a result of erosional processes, landslides, development of gullies, or by grading (cut). **In areas where fill has been placed near or over the bluff edge, the original bluff edge, even if buried beneath fill, shall be taken to be the bluff edge.**"*

This rule blocks the gaming pattern where fill is graded over the bluff to push the apparent "bluff edge" seaward. Operationally:
- Always require the bluff-edge survey to verify against historical site conditions, not just current topography.
- Cross-check against historical aerials, prior CDP records, and CCC enforcement files for the parcel.
- A bluff-edge survey based on current topography only is insufficient for any project that involves prior grading or fill near the bluff edge.

## Required Reports for Major Remodels on Bluff/Oceanfront Property

From the Bluff Edge Determinations Quick Guide ("Minimum Content Requirements" section), required for "New development (including accessory structures), major remodels, and additions on oceanfront or oceanfront bluff property":

### Geologic Hazards Identification

> *"Identification of geologic hazards affecting the project site and necessary mitigation measures."*
> *"Statements that the project site is suitable for the project and that the development will be safe from geologic hazards for its economic life; project will not create or contribute significantly to erosion, geologic instability, or destruction of the site or surrounding area or in any way require the reliance on existing or construction of protective devices that would substantially alter natural landforms along bluffs and cliffs."*

### Slope Stability Analysis

> *"Slope stability analysis and estimates of the long-term average bluff retreat/erosion rate over the expected life of the development. The predicted bluff retreat shall be evaluated considering not only historical bluff retreat data, but also acceleration of bluff retreat made possible by continued and accelerated sea level rise, future increase in storm or El Niño events, and any known site-specific conditions. To assure stability, the development must maintain a minimum factor of safety against landsliding of **1.5 (static) or 1.2 (pseudostatic, k=0.15** or determined through analysis by the geotechnical engineer) for the economic life of the structure. Factor of safety should be identified for oceanfront and bluff top lots on a plan or cross section."*

| F.S. metric | Required value |
|-------------|----------------|
| Static | ≥1.5 |
| Pseudostatic | ≥1.2 (k=0.15, or determined through analysis by geotechnical engineer) |

### Sea Level Rise

> *"Range of sea level rise scenarios, based on the best available science, which is currently the **2018 Sea-Level Rise Guidance**, including the **medium-high risk aversion scenario of approximately 6.6'** in the erosion analysis."*

| SLR scenario | Required value |
|--------------|----------------|
| Reference document | 2018 California SLR Guidance (best available science as of Mar 2024) |
| Medium-high risk aversion scenario | ≈6.6 ft rise |

### Wave Uprush and Impact

> *"Wave uprush and impact report that considers a seasonally eroded beach combined with 75-year erosion, high tide conditions, combined with 75-year projections for sea level rise, storm waves from a 100-year event or a storm that compares to the 1982/83 El Niño event."*

| Wave uprush component | Required input |
|-----------------------|----------------|
| Beach state | Seasonally eroded |
| Erosion duration | 75-year |
| Tide condition | High tide |
| SLR projection | 75-year |
| Storm event | 100-year storm OR 1982/83 El Niño event |

### Reference

The Quick Guide cites:
> *"References from the California Coastal Commission*
> *• Geotechnical Review for 2647 Victoria Drive"*

i.e., the operative interpretation of these report requirements aligns with CCC's geotechnical review precedent (e.g., the 2647 Victoria Drive review).
