# Laguna Beach — Major Remodel Methodology (Operative-but-Interpretive)

This file documents the operative methodology Laguna Beach city staff applies in plan review for major-remodel determination. It is the city overlay's most consequential test — a "major remodel" requires Design Review under §25.05.040(B)(1)(a), trips the §1103.5.4 sprinkler retrofit, the §25.05.040(C)(4) Fire Safety Standards Review (Fire Department or Building Division, as applicable), the Ch. 25.07 CDP requirement (in coastal zone), and the §1101.1 + §1103 CEBC additions framework (when scope adds SF). New §25.56.011 also classifies the result as a new building for the separate current-standards consequences described below.

---

## Authority Chain (cite in this order)

The methodology is **layered**, not codified in a single section. Cite all three sources, in this order:

### 1. LCP Land Use Element (statutory base)

The City's certified Local Coastal Program Land Use Element defines "major remodel" verbatim (per the June 2026 Major Remodel Quick Guide, paragraph 2):

> *"The City's Land Use Element defines 'major remodel' as the '[a]lteration of or an addition to an existing building or structure that increases the square footage of the existing building or structure by 50% or more; or demolition, removal, replacement, and/or reconstruction of 50% or more of the existing structure …'"*

This is the **statutory base**. The LCP is a CCC-certified document, so this language has the force of law within the coastal zone. Outside the coastal zone, it is the city's adopted interpretation.

### 2. June 2026 Major Remodel Quick Guide (operational application)

The handout is what city staff actually applies in plan review. The operative dual methodology — coastal zone appealable areas vs coastal zone non-appealable areas — lives in the handout, not in the codified Municipal Code.

**Edition and source.** The current edition is "CITY OF LAGUNA BEACH QUICK GUIDE — Updated June 2026", titled "MAJOR REMODEL INTERPRETATION". The City publishes the same text in two places:

- the standalone handout on the Planning Applications & Handouts page [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/23171/639179723814800000, "Updated June 2026", as of 2026-09-29];
- Exhibit F of the SB 9 application, "Major Remodel Handout", which prints the same June 2026 text [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/26958/639232726993730000, Exhibit F, as of 2026-09-29].

The June 2026 edition replaces the August 2025 edition. Do not apply the August 2025 text. Every quote in this file is from the June 2026 edition.

The handout opens with the consequence of a major remodel (paragraph 1, verbatim):

> *"Whenever modifications to an existing building or structure constitute a major remodel, the building or structure shall be classified as new development subject to current zoning regulations and all requirements applicable to the construction of a new building or structure, including undergrounding requirements, required dedication of on- and off-site improvements, and payment of new development fees."*

Paragraphs 2 and 3 continue, verbatim:

> *"As there is no certified language in the Municipal Code that further clarifies the methodology for determining whether a project rises to the level of a major remodel, the California Coastal Commission ('CCC') and City staff have had differing interpretations of the term. In some cases, that has led to the CCC finding substantial issue with City-approved projects on appeal. While a Municipal Code amendment is in process to achieve a long-term resolution, City staff now interprets 'major remodel' in a manner similar to that of the CCC staff for properties located in the appealable area of the coastal zone."*

And the operative hedge (handout, paragraph 4 — **mirror this in any output that quotes the methodology**):

> *"Provided below are the calculation methods that City staff believes the CCC staff uses for their assessment of projects in determining whether a project is a major remodel. These calculation methods apply to any currently pending and future development applications. Please note that the language below is City staff's best attempt in interpreting CCC staff's analysis based on their previous appeal reports. As such, these calculation methods have not been approved by the CCC staff; and therefore, the City cannot guarantee that the CCC staff will apply the identical interpretation. A separate methodology applies to projects that are not located within a coastal-appealable area."*

**This skill applies the dual methodology as operative city-staff interpretation, not as statutory rule.** Do not soften this in output — frame it as "city staff's interpretation of CCC staff's analysis," not "code says X."

### 3. LBMC §25.10.008(O) — Single-Sentence Statutory Backstop

The only Municipal Code definition of "major remodel" is the trailing sentence of the R-1 zone Landscaped Open Space Requirement (§25.10.008(O), Ord. 1475 § 1, 2007):

> *"A major remodel is a structural renovation and/or addition, which equals or exceeds fifty percent of the original gross floor area of the structure on the lot."*

This is the **statutory backstop**. It says nothing about:
- Dual methodology (appealable vs non-appealable)
- Per-floor and per-structure counting
- Cumulative baseline (Jan 13, 1993)
- ADU carveout
- Foundation or roof (only "structural renovation and/or addition")

Those operational details live in the LCP and the handout. Cite §25.10.008(O) as the floor; cite the LCP and handout for the operative methodology.

> **Phase 0 correction:** earlier Laguna research drafts placed the Major Remodel definition in §25.08 (Definitions and Standards). §25.08 is an alphabetical glossary and does NOT contain "major remodel" anywhere. The on-code definition is at §25.10.008(O), as documented above.

## Unit of Measurement — Each Structure, Each Floor

The June 2026 edition changes what one calculation covers. Apply both rules before any percentage.

**Each structure separately.** Verbatim from the handout (appears after each methodology section):

> *"For sites with more than one structure (whether primary dwelling unit or detached accessory buildings or structures, or similar) the above calculation methods shall be applied to each such building or structure independently."*

- List every building or structure on the site: the primary dwelling, a detached garage, and each detached accessory building or structure.
- Run the full calculation for each one on its own. Use only that structure's own foundation, walls, roof, and floor area.
- Do not add the garage or a detached structure to the primary dwelling's totals. Do not use one structure's intact walls to dilute another structure's demolition.
- Report a result (YES / NO / FLAG) for each structure. An ADU stays exempt (see ADU Carveout).

**Each floor separately (walls).** Both methodologies now count walls "on each floor". Compute one percentage for each floor. Report each floor's numerator, denominator, and percentage.

**How floors combine — the handout does not say.** The June 2026 handout computes walls "on each floor". It does not state how the floor results combine into one result for the structure. Compute two numbers for each structure:

- the **floor percentages**: (wall LF removed or reinforced on that floor) ÷ (existing wall LF on that floor) × 100, one for each floor;
- the **whole-structure total**: (wall LF removed or reinforced on all floors of that structure) ÷ (existing wall LF on all floors of that structure) × 100.

Apply this three-way rule:

1. Every floor percentage is under 50% → the wall test is **not met**.
2. At least one floor percentage is 50% or more AND the whole-structure total is 50% or more → the wall test is **met**. Both readings agree.
3. At least one floor percentage is 50% or more BUT the whole-structure total is under 50% → **do not decide**. Return **FLAG** (not FAIL, not PASS). State that the June 2026 handout computes walls per floor but does not state how floors combine, and that the City must confirm. Show both numbers.

Use the same rule for the interior + exterior wall formula in the appealable area. When the wall component enters the combined roof/walls/foundation test, compute the combined result with the wall figures both ways. If one reading gives 50% or more and the other gives under 50%, return FLAG and show both results.

## Coastal Zone Appealable Areas Methodology (more restrictive)

City staff applies the more expansive method in the **coastal zone appealable area** because that area is subject to CCC appeal. Verbatim from the handout, "COASTAL ZONE APPEALABLE AREAS" section:

### Foundation

> *"Foundation: (linear feet of foundation to be removed/reinforced) ÷ (linear feet of existing foundation) x 100. For caissons, use the diameter to calculate linear feet and include any associated grade beams, taking care not to count the same linear footage twice where caissons and grade beams overlap."*

- Both removal AND reinforcement count
- Caissons measured by diameter (LF equivalent), grade beams included
- Where a caisson and a grade beam overlap, count that linear footage once

### Interior + Exterior Walls (each floor)

> *"Interior and Exterior Walls: (linear feet of walls to be removed/reinforced on each floor) ÷ (linear feet of existing walls on each floor) x 100.*
> *• All exterior walls will be accounted for in this calculation, whether load-bearing or non-load-bearing. For interior walls, the calculation will only consider the load-bearing walls. Both removal and reinforcement count as demolition.*
> *• The removal of drywall, stucco, or similar does not count as demolition.*
> *• Window and door change-outs within the same window openings do not count towards removal. Filling in window and door openings do not count as demolition unless additional structural support is proposed."*

- Compute one wall percentage for each floor (see Unit of Measurement).

### Roof

> *"Roof: (square feet of roof area to be removed/reinforced, including roof eaves) ÷ (square feet of existing roof) x 100."*

- Both removal AND reinforcement count
- Eaves included

### Major-Remodel Triggers (Coastal Zone Appealable)

Verbatim from the handout:

> *"A Project is Considered a Major Remodel if:*
> *1. Demolition or reinforcement of 50% or more of the exterior walls OR combined roof/walls/foundation of the structure.*
> *2. Addition(s) to an existing structure that individually, or when combined with prior additions, results in greater than a 50% expansion of the existing structure. 'Existing structure' for the purposes of this section means a structure that legally existed on the site on January 13, 1993. The construction of a new structure or major remodel of an existing structure after January 13, 1993 establishes a new baseline for purposes of calculating cumulative additions over time.*
> *3. The construction and remodeling of accessory dwelling units are exempt from major remodel calculations."*

Trigger 1 has two independent prongs:
- 50%+ of **exterior walls alone** (apply the three-way floor rule in Unit of Measurement), OR
- 50%+ of **combined roof/walls/foundation** of the structure

(So a project that hits 30% exterior walls + 60% roof + 40% foundation might trip the combined prong even though no single component hits 50%.)

Trigger 2 is the **expansion** prong — square footage added to that structure vs. its Jan 13, 1993 baseline. The threshold is **greater than** 50%.

## Coastal Zone Non-Appealable Areas Methodology (less restrictive)

City staff applies a less expansive method in the coastal zone non-appealable area because that area is not subject to CCC review. Verbatim from the handout, "COASTAL ZONE NON-APPEALABLE AREAS" section:

> *"City staff will use a less expansive calculation method as these areas are not subject to CCC review."*

### Exterior Walls Only (each floor)

> *"Exterior Walls: (linear feet of walls to be removed on each floor) ÷ (linear feet of existing walls on each floor) x 100. Please note:*
> *1. All exterior walls will be accounted for in this calculation, whether load-bearing or non-load-bearing.*
> *2. The removal of drywall, stucco, or similar does not count as demolition.*
> *3. Window and door change-outs within the same window openings do not count towards removal. Filling in window and door openings do not count as demolition unless additional structural support is proposed."*

Two key differences from the appealable-area methodology:
- **Only exterior walls** are measured. No foundation. No roof.
- **Only removal counts.** Reinforcement does NOT count.

### Major-Remodel Triggers (Coastal Zone Non-Appealable)

Verbatim:

> *"A Project is Considered a Major Remodel if:*
> *1. Demolition of 50% or more of the exterior walls occurs.*
> *2. Addition(s) to an existing structure that individually, or when combined with prior additions, results in greater than a 50% expansion of the existing structure. 'Existing structure' for the purposes of this section means a structure that legally existed on the site up to January 13, 1993. The construction of a new structure or major remodel of an existing structure after January 13, 1993 establishes a new baseline for purposes of calculating cumulative additions over time.*
> *3. The construction and remodeling of accessory dwelling units are exempt from major remodel calculations."*

Same Jan 13, 1993 baseline. Same ADU carveout. Same per-structure and per-floor counting. Just a less expansive demolition test.

## Cumulative Baseline — January 13, 1993

Both methodologies define "existing structure" as one structure, not the whole site. Verbatim (appealable-area section):

> *"'Existing structure' for the purposes of this section means a structure that legally existed on the site on January 13, 1993. The construction of a new structure or major remodel of an existing structure after January 13, 1993 establishes a new baseline for purposes of calculating cumulative additions over time."*

The non-appealable section reads "legally existed on the site up to January 13, 1993". Both sections use the same date.

- **Date:** January 13, 1993 — this is the LCP IP certification date.
- **One structure at a time.** The baseline belongs to each structure. Do not pool the primary dwelling, the garage, and detached structures into one baseline.
- **NOT a rolling 3-year window.** Earlier research drafts cited a "3-year rolling cumulative" framing. That language does not appear in the LCP, the June 2026 handout, or LBMC §25.10.008(O). Use Jan 13, 1993.
- **Reset events:** construction of a new structure OR a post-1993 major remodel of an existing structure. Either creates a new baseline for that structure.
- **Implementation:** for each structure, track cumulative addition square footage from 1993 (or from its reset event) forward.

---

## ADU Carveout (the only carveout)

Verbatim from the handout, both sections, item 3:

> *"The construction and remodeling of accessory dwelling units are exempt from major remodel calculations."*

This is the **only** carveout. ADU square footage is not counted in the expansion prong. ADU demolition/reinforcement is not counted in the demolition prong.

> **Note on ADU coastal exemption pushback (CCC F9a-11-2024):** Even though ADUs are exempt from the **major-remodel** calculation, ADUs in the coastal zone still need a Coastal Development Permit per CCC F9a-11-2024. See `city-cdp-coastal-zone.md`.

## Nonconforming Structures After a Major Remodel

Verbatim from the handout (after each methodology section):

> *"Any legal, non-conforming structure shall be brought into compliance when the threshold for a major remodel is met. When a principal structure undergoes a major remodel, all associated accessory structures and improvements shall be subject to the current zoning regulations and all other applicable requirements, and shall lose their legal, nonconforming status."*

- When a structure meets the major-remodel threshold, it must be brought into compliance with current zoning. Its nonconformities do not carry forward.
- When the **principal structure** is a major remodel, every associated accessory structure and improvement on the site loses its legal, nonconforming status. This applies even when the accessory structure has no work of its own.
- List each nonconforming accessory structure and improvement (for example, a garage in a setback or an over-height wall). Require the plans to show how each one complies with current zoning.
- This handout rule adds to Ch. 25.56. See `city-nonconforming.md`.

## Common Misattributions to Avoid

The following appeared in earlier Laguna research drafts and are **wrong**:

| Misattribution | Correct Citation |
|----------------|------------------|
| "Rolling 3-year window for cumulative tracking" | January 13, 1993 LCP-IP-certification baseline |
| "1,500 sf SFR exception for a non-appealable-area major remodel" | Does NOT exist. 1,500 sf appears in **§25.56.008(C)** as a parking carveout for nonconforming SFRs — different rule. |
| "Major remodel defined in §25.08" | §25.08 is an alphabetical glossary; does NOT contain "major remodel". The on-code definition is **§25.10.008(O)**. |
| "Dual methodology codified in LBMC" | The LBMC backstop (§25.10.008(O)) has only the single-sentence definition. The dual methodology lives in the **LCP + June 2026 handout** as city-staff interpretation of CCC staff's analysis. |
| "City-approved methodology" | The handout itself disclaims: *"these calculation methods have not been approved by the CCC staff"* — frame as **city-staff interpretation**, not approved rule. |
| "Walls are counted across all floors" / "existing structure = principal structure + garage + enclosed detached structures" | That is the August 2025 edition. The June 2026 edition counts walls **on each floor** and runs the test on **each structure independently**. |

## Practical Application — City Cushion Advisory

Verbatim from the handout, paragraph 5:

> *"Although not required, City staff advises applicants to provide a cushion that accounts for unplanned demolition that may sometimes occur during the construction phase of the project — there have been instances where a project was proposed to remain under the 50% demolition threshold, but exceeded 50% due to unforeseen circumstances during construction."*

**Operationally:** any structure, or any floor of a structure, landing within ~5% of the 50% threshold under either methodology should be **FLAGGED** as cushion-needed. This is a corrections-letter recommendation, not a rejection — but it puts the applicant on notice of the mid-construction reclassification risk.

## New-building consequences — §25.56.011 (Ord. 1732)

Urgency Ordinance No. 1732 added §25.56.011 effective July 22, 2026. Once the major-remodel methodology classifies a structure as a major remodel, the code classifies it as a **new building** and subjects it to:

- current development standards for the applicable zone;
- utility undergrounding requirements;
- dedication of required on-site and off-site improvements; and
- new development fees.

The June 2026 handout states the same consequence in its first paragraph: the building or structure "shall be classified as new development subject to current zoning regulations and all requirements applicable to the construction of a new building or structure, including undergrounding requirements, required dedication of on- and off-site improvements, and payment of new development fees."

Keep classification and consequence separate. Section 25.56.011 does not change the 50% definition, dual city-staff methodology, January 13, 1993 baseline, or ADU carveout recorded above. It changes what follows after a project is classified.

## Output Application

For every Laguna residential remodel review:

1. Determine coastal zone appealable vs coastal zone non-appealable status (Step J point-in-polygon or applicant-supplied).
2. Apply the corresponding methodology.
3. List each building or structure on the site. Run steps 4–9 for each one independently.
4. Compute each component for that structure (foundation / walls / roof in the appealable area; exterior walls only in the non-appealable area). Compute the wall percentage **on each floor**.
5. Compare to 50%: apply the three-way floor rule (Unit of Measurement) to the "exterior walls" prong, and to the wall figures in the "roof/walls/foundation" prong in the appealable area. When the two readings disagree, return FLAG with both numbers.
6. Compute cumulative addition to that structure since its Jan 13, 1993 baseline (or its reset event).
7. Compare to >50% expansion.
8. Apply ADU carveout.
9. Output **YES / NO / FLAG** for that structure, with the methodology applied, each component's percentage (walls per floor and the whole-structure wall total), the trigger that fired, and (when quoting the methodology) the handout's own hedge.
10. If the principal structure is YES, list every associated accessory structure and improvement that is legal nonconforming. Each one loses that status (see Nonconforming Structures After a Major Remodel).

When **YES** fires, route the project to **Design Review** under §25.05.040(B)(1)(a) and flag downstream consequences:
- §25.56.011 new-building classification: current zone development standards, utility undergrounding, required on-/off-site dedications, and new-development fees
- Sprinkler retrofit — **two triggers, either one fires** (see `city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`): §1103.5.4 scope (a major remodel likely fires prong 1, 2 or 3) and Ch. 14.52.030 valuation (50% cumulative over any three-year period). **The major-remodel 50% is its own test.** It decides Design Review and CDP tier. It is not the sprinkler test, and a project can fire a sprinkler trigger without being a major remodel.
- §25.05.040(C)(4) Fire Safety Standards Review (Fire Department or Building Division, as applicable)
- Ch. 25.07 CDP (in coastal zone, regardless of E-93-1)
- Nonconforming status ends: the major-remodel structure must be brought into compliance, and when it is the principal structure, all associated accessory structures and improvements lose their legal, nonconforming status (June 2026 handout; see Nonconforming Structures After a Major Remodel)
- §25.56.008(A) full Title 25 conformance for any expansion portion (if existing structure is nonconforming)
- CEBC additions framework (parent skill, if scope adds SF)
- Phase 8 outputs (zoning, structural, energy, MEP) to full plan-review submittal package per Building Plan Check Submittal Requirements
