# Laguna Beach — Commercial TI Coastal Development Permit (CDP)

**Authority:** LBMC Chapter 25.07 (Coastal Development Permit). California Coastal Act, City's certified Local Coastal Program (LCP). California Coastal Commission (CCC) holds appeal jurisdiction for "appealable development."

**Critical context:** Approximately **95% of the City of Laguna Beach** is within the Coastal Zone. Commercial corridors (Downtown, Forest Ave, Heisler Park, Coast Hwy frontages, Laguna Canyon Rd frontages, North Coast Hwy / Village Entrance) are uniformly inside the Coastal Zone.

---

## 1. When a CDP Is Required (§25.07.004)

> "A coastal development permit shall be required for all proposed development within the coastal zone except for development specifically exempted under Sections 25.07.008 and 25.07.010."

If the parcel is inside the Coastal Zone, the **default is "CDP required"** unless an enumerated exemption applies.

---

## 2. "Development" Defined (§25.07.006)

"Development" includes, among other things:

- The construction, reconstruction, demolition, or **alteration of the size of any structure**
- A **change in the density or intensity of use** of land
- Change in the intensity of use of water, or access thereto
- Grading, excavation, or earthmoving
- Placement of solid material or structures

For commercial TI, the two dominant triggers are:

1. **Alteration of the size of any structure** — any addition or removal of floor area, any envelope change (storefront, awning extension, opening alteration), any rooftop equipment, any change in building footprint or volume.
2. **Change in the density or intensity of use** — retail → restaurant, retail → bar/nightclub, office → medical/dental, low-intensity to high-intensity use, parking-deficient conversions.

---

## 3. Improvements to Non-SFR Structures Exemption (§25.07.008(A)(2))

Improvements to existing non-SFR structures (i.e., commercial, industrial, multi-family) are **exempt** from the CDP requirement, EXCEPT when the improvement:

1. Is in the categories listed for SFR exemptions (e.g., between sea and first public road, on a beach, near a coastal bluff, ESHA, highly scenic area); OR
2. **Would result in a change in the intensity of use of the structure** — the principal commercial-TI trigger; OR
3. Is a conversion of a multi-unit or visitor-serving commercial use to fee/long-term-leasehold ownership; OR
4. Involves significant landform alteration.

**Practical commercial-TI triggers** under prong (2):

- Retail/office (Group M / B) → restaurant (Group A-2): always an intensity-of-use change
- Retail/office → bar/nightclub (Group A-2 high-intensity): always an intensity-of-use change
- Daytime retail → 24-hour use: typically an intensity-of-use change
- Single-tenant to multi-tenant subdivision: depends on the resulting parking demand and occupancy load
- Adding outdoor dining: typically an intensity-of-use change (parking, noise, ESHA proximity considerations)

When intensity-of-use is unclear, default to "CDP required" and let the Director determine on de minimis waiver or administrative CDP grounds (§§25.07.009, 25.07.013).

---

## 4. CDP Tiers

| Tier | Mechanism | Notice / Timeline | When applicable |
|------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Exempt** | §25.07.008 / §25.07.010 enumerated | None | Routine interior-only commercial TI with no intensity-of-use change, no envelope/landform alteration, not in restricted areas |
| **De minimis waiver** | §25.07.009 — Director may waive | 10-day public notice; City Council may pull at next public hearing | Non-appealable scope, consistent with LCP, no adverse effect, similar projects routinely approved without conditions or opposition |
| **Administrative CDP** | §25.07.013 — public hearing may be waived | 15-working-day public-hearing-request window; deemed approved if no request | "Minor development," consistent with LCP, no other discretionary approvals needed, no adverse effect on coastal resources/access |
| **Standard CDP (hearing)** | §25.07.012 — Planning Commission hearing | ≥21-day mailed notice (per §25.05.065(D)) | Default for any project not qualifying for the lower tiers; typically heard concurrently with Design Review |

---

## 5. Appealable Development (§25.07.006, §25.07.016)

A CDP for "appealable development" is **appealable to the California Coastal Commission** within **10 working days** of the city's final action. Appealable development includes:

- Development located between the sea and the first public road paralleling the sea (commonly the Coast Hwy / PCH coastal-side parcels)
- Development within **300 feet** of the inland extent of any beach OR of the mean high tide line (MHW) where there is no beach
- Development within **100 feet** of any wetland, estuary, or stream
- Development within **300 feet** of the seaward face of any coastal bluff
- Major public works projects or major energy facilities

For commercial TI, the most common appealable scenarios are:

- Coast Hwy / PCH frontage TIs in Downtown, North Laguna, South Laguna
- Heisler Park-area commercial frontages
- Forest Ave commercial blocks adjacent to Main Beach
- Three Arch Bay coastal blocks (also private-community application overlay)

When in doubt about appealability, default to treating as appealable and run the 10-working-day CCC clock.

---

## 6. Effective Date (§25.07.012)

- **Non-appealable CDP** — effective on adequate notice of final action.
- **Appealable CDP** — NOT effective until the 10-working-day CCC appeal period expires with no appeal filed. If a CCC appeal is filed, the CDP is stayed pending CCC action.

Building permits cannot be issued until the underlying CDP is effective.

---

## 7. Lapse (§25.07.012(I)(3))

CDP lapses after **2 years** if work is not commenced. Extension provisions per §25.07.012 may apply.

| Site posting | Required during notice window per §25.05.065 |
| Director consistency findings | Required for de minimis waiver and administrative CDP tiers |

---

## 9. Concurrent Processing With Design Review

For commercial TI in the Coastal Zone with any exterior or intensity-of-use scope, the CDP and DR applications run concurrently:

1. Single intake at Planning Division
2. Joint completeness review
3. Joint staff report
4. Joint Planning Commission hearing
5. Two appeal windows: 14 calendar days to City Council (DR), 10 working days to CCC (appealable CDP)

The CCC appeal window runs from the city's "final action" date, which is typically the date the appeal period to City Council expires (or the date of any City Council action on appeal).

## 10. Required Findings Output

For each commercial TI CDP review, output:

```markdown
**Coastal Zone status:** In Coastal Zone | Not in Coastal Zone
**CDP track:** Exempt (§25.07.008/.010) | De minimis waiver (§25.07.009) | Administrative CDP (§25.07.013) | Standard hearing CDP (§25.07.012)
**Intensity-of-use trigger present:** Yes (describe) | No
**Appealable to CCC:** Yes (within 300 ft beach/MHW/bluff / 100 ft wetland / between sea and first public road) | No
**CCC appeal window:** 10 working days from final action | N/A
**Effective date:** On final action | After 10-working-day CCC appeal period
**Lapse:** 2 years from issuance to commence work
```

Cite §25.07.004 for the CDP requirement, §25.07.006 for "development" / appealable definitions, §25.07.008(A)(2) for the non-SFR exemption + intensity-of-use trigger, §25.07.009 for de minimis waiver, §25.07.013 for Administrative CDP, §25.07.016 for CCC appeal, and §25.07.012 for effective date and lapse.
