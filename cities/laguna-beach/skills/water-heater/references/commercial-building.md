# Commercial building water heater: counter-permit checklist

Use this checklist instead of the SKILL.md checklist (STEP 3) when the heater serves a nonresidential building (SKILL.md STEP 0). The rest of this skill's references still hold the detail for each topic: `tp-relief-drain.md`, `seismic-strapping.md`, `valves-connections.md`, `venting-gas.md`, `gas-line-sizing.md`.

A water heater swap in a shop or restaurant is still a counter permit: a short set (often one or two sheets with photos, an equipment table, notes and a strap detail). Review it as one. The applicant is usually the installing contractor. The goal is a short list of what to add to the sheets so the plan checker can sign off, not a building review.

**Out of scope.** Do not check egress, fire-resistance, occupancy, accessibility (the CBC 11B-202.4 path-of-travel rule is not triggered by replacing equipment that is not part of the architecture, Exception 7), CALGreen, zoning, or structure beyond the heater's own anchorage. Do not ask for an engineer, a stamped set, or separate M, E or P sheets for a heater swap.

## A. What is being installed?

Read the equipment table, the notes and the photos. Record:
- heater type (gas storage, electric storage, heat pump, tankless), model, storage gallons, input (Btu/h or kW), voltage and phase;
- the heater it replaces, and whether the location, fuel, or size changes. When the new tank is much larger than the old one (for example 50 to 119 gallons), say so in the findings about room size and strapping so the applicant knows why they apply;
- whether the business is a food facility (commercial cooking, food or drink preparation, a kitchen, a coffee bar). Decide from the plans, the photos and the panel directory.

## B. Walk the checklist

| # | Check | Applies to | What to verify | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heater circuit** | Electric, heat pump | Breaker and conductors rated at least 125% of the heater's **nameplate** ampere rating at the building's voltage (storage heaters of 120 gal or less). A manufacturer's published breaker size for a voltage already includes the 125%: accept it. FAIL the breaker only when the plans pair a breaker with a nameplate rating for the **same** voltage and the breaker is under 125% of it. Never recompute from a current the plans do not tie to a voltage. When the wire, ground or conduit size is missing, ask for it "per the heater's nameplate and installation manual at the building's voltage"; do not write example sizes. | CEC 422.10, 422.13, 110.3(B) |
| 2 | **Disconnect** | Electric, heat pump | A disconnect within sight of the heater, or a breaker that can be locked in the open position. When the panel is in another room and neither is shown, ask for one. | CEC 422.31(B) |
| 3 | **Panel capacity** | A new 30A or larger circuit | The panel's name, voltage and phase, bus and main rating, and open spaces, and a load calculation (12 months of maximum demand data, a 30-day recording under the 220.87 exception, or a calculated load) showing room for the new circuit. "Verified" or "see photo" is not a rating. | CEC 220.87, 408.30 |
| 4 | **T&P valve and discharge** | Storage heaters | A listed T&P valve. Discharge pipe full size, draining by gravity through an air gap to the drainage system (for example a floor sink), or outdoors ending 6 to 24 in. above grade pointing down; unthreaded end, readily observable. It does not drain into a pan or share a pipe with condensate. | CPC 504.4, 504.5, 608.4, 608.5 |
| 5 | **Cold-water shutoff** | All | A full-way shutoff valve on the cold supply at the heater. | CPC 606.2, 606.5 |
| 6 | **Thermal expansion** | Storage heaters | Commercial services usually have a backflow preventer or pressure regulator, which closes the system; then an expansion tank or other expansion control on the cold supply. Ask the designer to show it, or to note the system is open. | CPC 608.2, 608.3 |
| 7 | **Drain pan** | Storage heaters | Only where a leak would damage the building (over a finished floor or ceiling). A slab-on-grade floor needs none. | CPC 507.5 |
| 8 | **Strapping** | Storage heaters | Two straps, upper and lower third, the lower one at least 4 in. above the controls, into studs or masonry. When the detail is generic (wood studs assumed, no fastener), ask for the actual wall type and the fastener at each strap, or the manufacturer's seismic kit for the model. See "Anchorage of a large tank" below. | CPC 507.2; H&S 19211 |
| 9 | **Heat pump condensate** | Heat pump | Condensate drains through an air gap or indirect connection to an approved receptor: a floor sink, a mop sink, or outdoors. "Nearest sink" is not enough. In a food facility never a hand sink or a food-prep sink. Size and slope (1/8 in. per ft) or a condensate pump. Cite CPC 814.1 for the receptor and slope and CMC 310.5 for the indirect connection; the food-sink limit comes from the Retail Food Code (HSC 114123). | CPC 814.1; CMC 310.5; HSC 114123 |
| 10 | **Heat pump air and clearances** | Heat pump | The room volume or ducting and the service clearances the manufacturer's installation manual requires for that model, and the room size on a simple plan. | CMC 303.1, 304; manufacturer instructions |
| 11 | **Gas venting and gas line** | Gas heaters | Vent type and size from the listing (CPC 509, 510), combustion air (CPC 506), gas line sized for the total connected load (CPC 1208, 1215). | CPC 506, 509, 510, 1208, 1215 |
| 12 | **Gas to electric** | A gas heater removed | A note that caps the gas outlet at the valve with a listed fitting and seals or removes the abandoned vent. If another appliance shares that vent, the vent is resized for what remains. | CPC 1206.3, 1210.8.1; CPC 507.6, 509 |
| 13 | **Energy certificate** | All nonresidential | Every Part 6 alteration needs a signed Certificate of Compliance. For service water heating that is the **NRCC-PLB-E**, with the heater's CEC listing. Missing it is a FAIL that asks for the form. State it as a requirement; never say some cities accept the listing instead, and never send the applicant to ask. Pipe insulation on new hot-water piping too. | Admin 10-103(a)1; CEnC 141.0(b)2N, 140.5, 110.3, 120.3 |
| 14 | **Heater location plan** | A new circuit, drain or discharge route | A simple partial floor plan (not to scale is fine): the heater, its clearances, the panel, the conduit route, the condensate receptor and the T&P termination. A one-for-one swap in place with no new routes needs only the photos and notes. | CBC 107.2.1 |
| 15 | **Food facility health approval** | Food facility | The county environmental health agency approves the water heater change before the City issues the permit. Write **one FLAG that names that approval only**. Hot-water sizing (recovery against the sinks and dishwasher) is the health agency's plan check, not Building's: never ask for recovery rates or demand numbers on the building plans. Load `commercial-food-facility-health.md` for what the agency checks, so the FLAG can tell the applicant what to bring. | HSC 114380(a), (e) |

## Anchorage of a large tank

The CBC applies ASCE 7 Chapter 13 to equipment in commercial buildings, and a full storage tank of about 40 gallons or more is heavier than the 400 lb exemption. Plan checkers in practice accept the CPC 507.2 straps with the wall type and fasteners named, or the manufacturer's seismic kit. So:
- Grade only check 8 (the straps, the wall type, the fasteners).
- When the tank is large (full weight over 400 lb), add one separate FLAG worded as a heads-up: "The plan checker may ask for an anchorage design for this heater because of its weight. Nothing is required yet." Never write it as a requirement and never cite ASCE 7 in a FAIL.

## C. Output

For each checklist item: PASS (cite the code), FAIL (what is missing and the fix), FLAG (what the plans do not show, or the health approval and the anchorage heads-up above), or N/A (for example venting on an electric heater). Values the requirement turns on come from the plans or the manufacturer's data, never from memory.
