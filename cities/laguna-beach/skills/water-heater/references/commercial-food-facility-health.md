# Food facility: the health department's part of a water heater change

The building review never grades this. It feeds the one FLAG (`commercial-building.md` checklist item 15) that names the health approval, so the applicant knows what to take to the health agency.

## Who approves, and when
- A person who builds or remodels a retail food facility submits plans and specifications to the local health enforcement agency and gets plan approval before work starts. The building department does not issue the building permit for a food facility until it has the health agency's plan approval. (HSC 114380(a), (e).)
- In Orange County the agency is the **Orange County Health Care Agency, Environmental Health** (Food Protection Program plan check). Its construction guide counts installing equipment that changes a facility's configuration or operation as a remodel that needs plan review and a fee, and it says a water heater should not be bought until the agency sets the minimum required energy input. Its plan submittal checklist asks for the **water heater location and proposed energy input**. (OCHCA, *Construction Guidelines for Retail Food Facilities*, F042-15.0637.)
- Elsewhere: name the local health enforcement agency (the county's environmental health department, or the city's own health department where the city has one, as Long Beach, Pasadena, Berkeley and Vernon do). Use the city info skill when it names the agency.

## What the health agency checks (CCDEH *Guidelines for Sizing Water Heaters*, Feb 2020, which OCHCA uses)
- Hot water of at least **120°F** to every sink, janitorial sink and hot-water appliance at all times. Hand sinks may be 100°F through a mixing valve.
- **Recovery** (gallons per hour) of at least **100%** of the hourly hot-water demand when the facility uses reusable dishes and utensils, or **80%** when it uses only single-service items. Prepackaged-food-only facilities need at least a 10-gallon heater.
- Hourly demand is the sum of the fixtures, for example: 3-compartment sink (18 x 18 in.) 42 GPH; hand sink 5 GPH each; janitorial sink 15 GPH; food-prep sink 5 GPH; pre-rinse hand spray 45 GPH; a dish machine at its listed demand.
- Sizing assumes a **50°F rise** (70°F tap water) unless the local agency sets another rise. Electric heaters are assumed 98% efficient, gas 75%, unless listed otherwise.
- Tankless heaters: at least 2 GPM at 120°F to each fixture (hand sinks 0.5 GPM). The agency may limit units with a high minimum activation flow.
- A heat pump heater's recovery is much lower than its tank size suggests, so its listed first-hour or recovery rating is what the agency compares.
- Where feasible, the heater sits away from food and utensil handling areas.

## How the FLAG reads
Name the approval and what to bring, in plain words, with no question to the agency. For example: "Get Orange County Health Care Agency approval for the water heater change before the permit is issued. Bring the heater's recovery rating and the list of sinks and dish equipment it serves."
