# Laguna Beach — Planning Division: applications and the design review process

## What the Planning Division does

- Planning works with the community to shape development of the City while protecting historic resources and minimizing environmental impacts. Key functions: long-range planning, current planning, and protection of established views from vegetation. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning, as of 2026-06-18]
- Long-range work includes amendments to General Plan elements, the Local Coastal Program, Specific Plans, and the Municipal Code. Current planning covers non-residential projects (hotel, commercial, industrial), subdivisions, and residential development. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning, as of 2026-06-18]
- The Planning Division serves the Planning Commission, Design Review Board, Heritage Committee, and Housing and Human Services Committee. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning, as of 2026-06-18]

## Design review process — the City's nine steps

[source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning/design-review-process, as of 2026-02-22]

1. **Pre-submission.** Investigate applicable ordinances and policies before preparing construction plans. Most projects need discretionary approval before structural drawings go to the Building Division. Review applicable Specific Plans, zoning regulations, the General Plan, and the Local Coastal Program. Planning staff are available at the planning counter to help.
2. **Preliminary zoning review.** Submit the preliminary design online for a Commercial or Residential Planning Entitlement through the Public Permit Portal. There is a fee. The application gets a Zoning Plan Check number (ZPC#). A planner reviews and sends a detailed letter listing required discretionary applications, inconsistencies, and staff concerns. **Concluded in approximately 30 days; the Zoning Plan Check fee is good for up to three reviews.**
3. **Early neighbor communication.** LBMC § 25.05.040(C)(1) requires early neighbor communication for all Design Review projects before a scheduled public hearing. The Design Review Board has published Best Practices, and an optional Neighborhood Outreach Report form exists for documenting outreach.
4. **Discretionary entitlement application.** Design Review and companion applications (Coastal Development Permit, Variance, Revocable Encroachment Permit, others) are **accepted only through the online public permitting portal**. Incomplete applications and applications without full fee payment will not be accepted for processing.
5. **Planner review and feedback.** The project is assigned to a planner who checks completeness, performs a detailed plan check, and routes the project to other City divisions and departments. **Within approximately 30 days** the planner sends a status letter saying the application is complete or incomplete. Complete applications are tentatively scheduled for public hearing **approximately 45 days thereafter**, assuming the project qualifies for a CEQA exemption. After an incomplete letter, the applicant has **six months** to correct and resubmit or the application may be deemed abandoned and withdrawn. Some projects also require Public Works, Water Quality, and/or Fire Department review, which issue their own separate corrections.
6. **Hearing preparation.** The planner drafts a Public Hearing Notice and Staff Report. **The notice is posted at the project site and two other places in the City, and mailed to neighboring property owners within 300 feet and tenants within 100 feet.**
7. **Hearing.** Administrative Design Review meetings are conducted by the Community Development Director or designee in the Council Chambers. Design Review Board hearings and Planning Commission hearings each occur **twice a month** in the Council Chambers. Order of business: staff presentation → questions → applicant presentation → questions → public comment → applicant rebuttal → hearing closed → decision or continuance. **The Municipal Code limits a project to three substantive hearings and two non-substantive continuances**, and the City Council has more recently directed decision-makers to decide within two hearings.
8. **Appeal and call-for-review period.** A **14-day** appeal period starts after final action by the Director, DRB, or Planning Commission. During it, required staking must remain in place and **no building permit application may be filed**. Property owners within 300 feet may appeal to the City Clerk for City Council review; a Council member may call the item for review. If appealed, the Code requires a City Council hearing **within 60 days**. Where a Coastal Development Permit is involved and the site is in an appealable area, staff file a Notice of Final Action with the California Coastal Commission; an aggrieved party or the Commission may appeal **within 10 business days** of receiving the NOFA.
9. **Building permit application.** Once the approval is effective, submit a building permit application with construction plans. **Design Review approvals and associated applications are valid for two years**, extensions available for a fee.

## Real property reports / disclosures

- Real Property Disclosure (RPD) requests are submitted through the Public Permit Portal. First-time users register an account (guide: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/22619/638858590567600000), then submit the request (step-by-step RPD submittal guide: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/22621/638858614092300000). [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning/applications-handouts/real-property-reports, as of 2026-07-15]
- **What a real property report carries as of 2026-08-20.** Three Title 12 amendments effective that date add tree and view items to the city-issued report. **Record of views** (12.16.040(b)) and **view restoration or view preservation decisions** (12.16.100) are set forth in any real property report required by **LBMC Chapter 14.76**; view decisions also run with the land and are recorded in Orange County official records to the extent permitted. **Heritage trees** (12.08.030(b)) are noticed in the property file and in the **real property report issued by the city when property is sold** — that subsection names the city-issued report, not Chapter 14.76. See "Views, trees, and hedges" below. [source: the City source document (not included in this plugin) lines 105, 123, 143, effective 2026-08-20]

## Views, trees, and hedges

- **Hedge height claim:** dense vegetation forming a barrier or fence in the front, rear, or side yard setback that diminishes sunlight, obstructs views, or creates an ingress/egress safety hazard may be subject to a hedge height claim. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/views-and-trees, as of 2026-07-15]
- **Heritage trees:** LBMC Chapter 12.08 (Preservation of Heritage Trees) sets a process for designating significant trees. Property owners nominate trees; the ordinance sets evaluation criteria and provides incentives for owners whose trees receive City Council designation. Heritage trees in public areas are maintained by Public Works. Trees on the Heritage Tree List (revised 10-18-2022) are **exempt from view restoration**. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/views-and-trees, as of 2026-07-15]
- ⚠️ **Heritage tree incentives now come with a written agreement — LBMC 12.08.030(b), amended effective 2026-08-20.** The tree is placed on the heritage tree list and a certificate is presented to the owner. Notice of the heritage tree is included in the **property file** and in the **real property report issued by the city when property is sold**. Owners remain eligible for benefits related to potential development improvements on the property, but the amendment adds a condition: **granting any benefit is conditioned on a written agreement between the city and the property owner** ensuring continued preservation and maintenance of the heritage tree(s) on the subject property. That written agreement **must include a provision requiring subsequent heritage tree owners to continue preservation and maintenance**. Plan for the agreement as a step, not a formality — no agreement, no benefit. [source: LBMC 12.08.030(b) as amended, Laguna Beach efficiency ordinance amending LBMC Chapters 7.25, 12.08, 12.16 and 17.50 — introduced 2026-06-23, adopted 2026-07-21, effective 2026-08-20 line 105. eCode360 had not republished Title 12 as of 2026-08-14.]
- **View decisions run with the land — LBMC 12.16.100, amended effective 2026-08-20.** Decisions by city staff and the View Restoration Committee on view restoration and view preservation bind the claimant, the vegetation owner, and their heirs, successors and assigns. To the extent permitted they are recorded in the official records of the County of Orange, they are set forth in any real property report required by **Chapter 14.76**, and each owner should disclose them to prospective purchasers. The record of views itself is likewise noted in any Chapter 14.76 real property report (LBMC 12.16.040(b), as amended). See `submit-view-preservation.md`. [source: the City source document (not included in this plugin) lines 123 and 143, effective 2026-08-20]
- **Hedge height claim filing (August 2026 forms):** the claimant must show they discussed the concern with the owner of the vegetation and that the attempt to resolve it failed. Photos are color copies taken from the claimant's property. Before the City notices the hearing, the claimant posts the property and pays an approved Listing Service, which prepares the mailing list of owners within 100 feet of the claimant's property, mails the notices, and gives the City an affidavit of mailing. The claim fee and the City Council appeal fee come from the fee estimate. The application points to LBMC Chapter 12.14. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/26796/639220323582270000 and https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/home/showpublisheddocument/26794/639220321046400000, "Revised 08/2026" and "Revised 8-2026", as of 2026-10-09]
- **Tree trimming:** parcels with a landscape plan approved through design review with maximum height limits must trim in accordance with the approved plan conditions. The City suggests consulting a certified arborist. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/views-and-trees, as of 2026-07-15]
- The **View Restoration Committee** — five residents appointed by City Council — adjudicates view claims for pre-existing views alleged to be significantly impaired by vegetation, holds public hearings, and may issue a view restoration order with a vegetation maintenance schedule. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/boards-committees-commissions, as of 2024-11-19]

## Historic preservation

- ⚠️ **Mills Act applications are not being accepted this year** while the City undertakes a comprehensive review of the program, to align it with current best practices before re-launch. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning/historic-preservation, as of 2026-06-18]
- Getting on the Historic Register is the **first** step; only once registered can a property be eligible for the Mills Act. The City normally processes Mills Act applications **once a year, with the application process starting in January**. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning/historic-preservation, as of 2026-06-18]
- **There is no application fee for placement on the register.** The owner completes the department's application; the Heritage Committee reviews it and may request additional information, plans for future changes, or a rating assessment (the applicant may use the City's consultant architectural historian). Designation procedures and criteria are in LBMC Chapter 25.45. [source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning/historic-preservation, as of 2026-06-18]

## Boards and committees under Community Development

[source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/boards-committees-commissions, as of 2024-11-19]

- **Design Review Board** and **Planning Commission** (tiles on the page).
- **Heritage Committee** — advises City Council and the design review authority on historic preservation: alterations to historic structures, Historic Register placement requests, and Mills Act contract recommendations. Members serve two-year terms.
- **Housing and Human Services Committee** — assesses housing opportunities and human needs, provides input on the Housing Element and Human Needs Element of the General Plan, and makes recommendations to City Council.
- **View Restoration Committee** — see "Views, trees, and hedges" above.
