# Structural Project Evidence and Coordination Playbook

Reference for California single-family residential permit review.
Source: 2025 California Residential Code, Title 24, Part 2.5.

This playbook applies when a structural check depends on project documents,
not on a universal prescriptive value. It directs the reviewer to compare the
submitted design bases without substituting engineering judgment.

## 1. Source Boundary and Order of Control

Use each source only for the role it can support:

1. **Current code** establishes general compliance, construction-document,
   engineered-design, and minimum-material requirements.
2. **Approved evaluation report or listing** establishes the evaluated
   product's scope, limitations, configurations, and installation criteria,
   subject to building-official approval under CRC R104.2.2.6.1.
3. **Sealed structural calculations** establish the project-specific loads,
   analysis, member or assembly selection, and required design values.
4. **Geotechnical report** establishes the project-specific soil parameters,
   recommendations, and foundation assumptions within its scope.
5. **Drawings, schedules, notes, and details** communicate the coordinated
   design that is to be built.

Code authority does not create a product-specific footing length, caisson
diameter, reinforcement schedule, or concrete strength. Those values must come
from an applicable current report or the responsible design professional's
project documents.

## 2. Applicability Gate

Apply a family check only when the submitted record affirmatively shows its
feature or establishes that the feature is required by the design basis.

- `APPLICABLE`: Plans, calculations, schedules, an evaluation report, or a
  geotechnical report show the feature.
- `NOT APPLICABLE`: Affirmative project evidence establishes that the feature
  is absent from the submitted design.
- `NEEDS INFO`: The record is ambiguous about whether the feature forms part of
  the design. Ask for the missing scope evidence before demanding the full
  evidence package.

## 3. Common Evidence Map

Inspect the applicable subset of:

- structural cover sheet, design criteria, general notes, and sheet index;
- foundation, floor, roof, and lateral plans;
- shear-wall, footing, caisson, reinforcement, and concrete schedules;
- sections, details, connection callouts, and grid references;
- sealed structural calculations and their input/output pages;
- approved product or evaluation report, including report number, current
  scope, limitations, installation conditions, and referenced details;
- geotechnical report, recommendations, design parameters, and addenda; and
- architectural backgrounds where geometry or location must coordinate.

Record exact sheet, detail, schedule, calculation page, report section, and
geotechnical page locators. A document title alone is not evidence that its
requirements appear in the design.

## 4. Shared Review Procedure

1. Establish that the feature is present and identify the responsible design
   source for each project-specific value.
2. Locate the feature consistently by grid, level, mark, type, or detail
   reference across all applicable documents.
3. Compare only like-for-like values and conditions: type, location, geometry,
   loads, material strength, reinforcement, anchorage, connections, and
   governing assumptions.
4. Confirm the drawings communicate the approved report, sealed design, and
   geotechnical requirements that actually apply to the selected condition.
5. Separate an objective document omission or conflict from a question that
   requires new engineering analysis.
6. Record the decision state and every evidence locator used.

## 5. Family Procedures

### 5.1 Proprietary shear-wall report and details

Apply only when a proprietary shear wall or panel is proposed or referenced.
Verify that the submitted evaluation report identifies the product and
configuration, is within the report's scope, and supplies the limitations and
installation criteria used by the design. Compare the report to the sealed
calculations, wall schedule, plan locations, anchorage, boundary conditions,
and referenced details. The reviewer may identify a missing report or a direct
conflict, but must not select a product or redesign the assembly.

### 5.2 Shear-wall plan and calculation coordination

Apply only when shear walls, braced wall lines, or proprietary panels are part
of the design. CRC R106.1.3 identifies the braced-wall information the
construction documents provide when that provision applies. For a prescriptive
braced-wall design, compare wall marks, methods/types, lengths, locations,
story/line assignments, openings, foundation requirements, anchorage, and
connections between the plans, schedule, and details. Do not require sealed
lateral calculations merely because prescriptive braced walls are present.

Compare sealed calculations only when they are required for the applicable
wall system or are submitted as part of its design basis, including an
engineered or proprietary system. `PASS` is available when a compliant
prescriptive braced-wall design provides the applicable construction-document
information and no calculations are required. Use `CORRECTION` for an objective
omission from the applicable construction documents or a direct conflict among
required or submitted sources. Use `NEEDS INFO` when the record does not
establish whether the wall system is prescriptive, engineered, or proprietary,
and `ESCALATE` when resolving a mismatch requires structural reanalysis.

### 5.3 Proprietary shear-wall foundation requirements

Apply only when a proprietary wall's approved report or sealed design contains
foundation, anchorage, reinforcement, or edge-distance criteria. Match the
selected configuration and design forces to the report and calculations, then
compare those criteria to the foundation plan, schedule, and details. Do not
invent a footing length, reinforcement, embedment, or edge distance.

### 5.4 Engineered foundation reinforcement

Apply only when the project includes engineered foundation reinforcement.
Compare bar size, spacing, quantity, grade where stated, placement, laps or
development references, wall/footing type, and detail marks between the sealed
calculations, foundation plan, schedules, and details. The check is for an
objective omission or conflict, not an independent reinforcement design.

### 5.5 Caisson design coordination

Apply only when caissons are shown in plans or calculations, required by the
geotechnical recommendations, or otherwise form part of the submitted
foundation design. Compare:

- count, type, mark, and plan location;
- diameter, depth, and design capacity;
- reinforcement and concrete design strength;
- structural loads and geotechnical assumptions; and
- grade-beam, cap, anchorage, and supported-construction connections.

If affirmative evidence establishes that the project has no caissons, mark the
row `NOT APPLICABLE`. If the record does not establish whether caissons are
part of the design, use `NEEDS INFO`. Never prescribe a universal caisson
dimension, capacity, or reinforcement schedule.

### 5.6 Concrete design-strength coordination

Apply only when structural concrete is specified or used in the submitted
design. First identify the applicable code minimum, including the location and
weathering condition where CRC R402.2 governs. Then compare the project design
strength stated in the sealed calculations, structural notes, specifications,
foundation or concrete schedules, and details. A higher project-specific
strength may control. Correct a value below an applicable minimum or an
objective document conflict; do not replace the engineer's design with a
generic strength.

## 6. Decision States

- `PASS`: The feature is present, the applicable evidence is complete, and the
  submitted sources are materially consistent.
- `CORRECTION`: Applicability is established and a required controlling
  document is missing, a required project value is omitted from the drawings,
  a value falls below an applicable code minimum, or submitted design sources
  materially conflict.
- `NEEDS INFO`: The record does not establish whether the feature exists or
  which document or design condition controls.
- `NOT APPLICABLE`: Affirmative evidence establishes that the feature is not
  part of the project.
- `ESCALATE`: Resolution requires product approval, interpretation of an
  evaluation-report limitation, geotechnical judgment, structural reanalysis,
  or another decision by the building official or responsible design
  professional.

## 7. Issuing Basis and Correction Structure

Identify the actual issuing basis for each finding:

- current code requirement or minimum;
- approved evaluation report or listing;
- sealed structural design;
- geotechnical recommendation; or
- objective inconsistency among submitted project documents.

A safe correction contains:

1. observed evidence with exact locators;
2. the missing or conflicting evidence;
3. the required coordination or resubmittal action; and
4. the code or project-document basis without inventing a value.

Example:

> Foundation plan S1.1 identifies 24-inch caissons while sealed calculation
> page 42 uses 30-inch caissons. Revise the plans and calculations to provide
> one coordinated design and identify the controlling geotechnical and
> structural basis.

## 8. Limitations

- Do not determine a new member, footing, caisson, reinforcing, anchorage, or
  material design.
- Do not treat a generic engineered-design section as proof of a
  product-specific or project-specific value.
- Do not apply an evaluation report to a product, configuration, load, or site
  condition outside its stated scope.
- Do not resolve conflicts between a geotechnical report and sealed structural
  design by choosing one silently; identify the conflict and escalate.
- Do not assume a feature from a benchmark, prior project, or common practice.
