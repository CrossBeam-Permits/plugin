# Fire Protection Systems

California Residential Code, Title 24, Part 2.5 -- 2025 Edition
Sections R309, R310, R311, R317

## 1. Automatic Sprinkler Systems (R309)

### Where Required

| Building Type | Sprinklers Required? | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Townhouses (new) | Yes | CRC R309.1 |
| One- and two-family dwellings (new) | Yes | CRC R309.2 |
| Additions/alterations to existing non-sprinklered buildings | No | CRC R309.1 Exception, R309.2 Exception 1 |
| Qualifying ADU under Government Code 66313, detached and <= 1,200 sf, on same lot as an existing unsprinklered primary residence | No | CRC R309.2 Exception 2 |

### Design Standard
Sprinkler systems must be designed and installed per CRC Section R309 or NFPA 13D
(CRC R309.1.1, R309.2.1). Standalone and multipurpose wet-pipe systems are permitted.
Antifreeze systems are not covered by Section R309.3 (CRC R309.3.1).

### Required Sprinkler Locations (R309.3.1.2)
Sprinklers shall protect **all areas** of a dwelling unit.

**Exceptions -- sprinklers NOT required in:**
1. Attics, crawl spaces, and normally unoccupied concealed spaces without fuel-fired
   appliances. If fuel-fired equipment is present, a sprinkler is required above the
   equipment only.
2. Clothes closets, linen closets, and pantries <= 24 sf with smallest dimension <= 3 ft,
   with gypsum board walls and ceiling.
3. Bathrooms <= 55 sf.
4. Garages, carports, exterior porches, unheated entry areas (mud rooms) adjacent to an
   exterior door, and similar areas.

### Sprinkler Specifications (R309.3.2)
- Must be new, listed residential sprinklers installed per manufacturer's instructions.
- **Temperature rating:** 135 to 225 degrees F generally.
- **Intermediate temperature** (175-225 degrees F) required at exposed skylights, in
  attics, in concealed spaces directly beneath a roof, and near heat sources per Table
  R309.3.2.2:

| Heat Source | Distance Range Requiring Intermediate Temp (in.) |
|---|---|
| Coal/wood burning stove | 12 to 42 |
| Fireplace (front, recessed) | 36 to 60 |
| Fireplace (side, open/recessed) | 12 to 36 |
| Wall-mounted warm-air register (front) | 18 to 36 |
| Heating duct (not insulated) | 9 to 18 |
| Hot water pipe (not insulated) | 6 to 12 |
| Kitchen range top | 9 to 18 |
| Luminaire up to 250W | 3 to 6 |
| Luminaire 250-499W | 6 to 12 |
| Oven | 9 to 18 |
| Ceiling/wall warm-air register (side) | 12 to 24 |
| Vent/chimney connector | 9 to 18 |
| Water heater, furnace, boiler | 3 to 6 |

### Coverage and Obstructions (R309.3.2.4)
- Max coverage per sprinkler: 400 sf.
- Sprinkler discharge must not be blocked by obstructions; additional sprinklers required
  for obstructed areas.
- Pendent sprinklers within 3 ft of center of ceiling fan or luminaire are considered
  obstructed.
- Sidewall sprinklers within 5 ft of center of ceiling fan or luminaire are considered
  obstructed.

### Water Supply (R309.3.5)
The water supply must provide the design flow rate at the required pressure. Where the
supply serves both domestic and sprinkler systems and no provision prevents domestic
flow during sprinkler operation, add 5 gpm at the connection point. In a multipurpose
system, add it at the nearest domestic connection; it may be split 2.5/2.5 gpm as the
section permits (CRC R309.3.5).

**Required capacity (duration):**

| Dwelling Configuration | Min. Duration |
|---|---|
| One story, < 2,000 sf | 7 minutes |
| Two+ stories, or >= 2,000 sf | 10 minutes |

Attached garages, attached open carports, porches, balconies, and patios are excluded
from the dwelling-unit area calculation (CRC R309.3.5.2).

### Pipe Sizing (R309.3.6)
- Minimum pipe from water source to any sprinkler: 3/4 in. nominal.
- Minimum threaded adapter at sprinkler attachment: 1/2 in. nominal.
- Sizing by prescriptive tables or hydraulic calculation per NFPA 13D.

### Backflow Protection (R309.3.5.3)
Required unless the system complies with NFPA 13D or Section R309, uses
potable-water-suitable piping, contains no antifreeze, and has no fire department
connection (CRC R309.3.5.3, R309.3.1.1).

## 2. Smoke Alarms (R310)

### Where Required
- **New construction:** all dwelling units (CRC R310.2.1).
- **Alterations, repairs, additions requiring a permit:** the individual dwelling unit
  must be equipped with smoke alarms per new-construction requirements (CRC R310.2.2).

### Locations (R310.3)
Smoke alarms shall be installed in:
1. Each sleeping room.
2. Outside each separate sleeping area in the immediate vicinity of bedrooms.
3. On each additional story (including basements and habitable attics; excluding crawl
   spaces and uninhabitable attics). In split-level units without an intervening door,
   one alarm on the upper level suffices for an adjacent lower level less than one full
   story below.
4. Not less than 3 ft horizontally from the door of a bathroom with a bathtub or shower,
   unless that distance would prevent a required alarm placement.
5. In the hallway AND in the room open to the hallway where the room ceiling exceeds the
   hallway ceiling by 24 in. or more.
6. Within the room to which a sleeping loft is open, in the immediate vicinity of the
   sleeping loft.

### Placement Restrictions (R310.3.3 / NFPA 72 29.8.3.4)

| Restriction | Min. Distance |
|---|---|
| From permanently installed cooking appliance | 20 ft; 10 ft for ionization with silencing switch or photoelectric; >6 ft for photoelectric only in a qualifying open plan where 10 ft would prevent required placement; or as listed for close proximity |
| From bathroom door (with tub/shower) | 3 ft horizontally, unless that would prevent required placement |
| From supply register of forced-air HVAC | 36 in. horizontally, outside direct airflow |
| From tip of ceiling fan blade | 36 in. horizontally |

Additional rules:
- Not in unfinished attics or garages.
- Not where temps can fall below 40 degrees F or exceed 100 degrees F.
- On tray/coffered ceilings: install on highest portion or within 12 in. vertically
  from highest point.

### Interconnection (R310.4)
Where more than one smoke alarm is required, all alarms in the individual unit must be
interconnected so activation of one activates all. The alarm must be clearly audible in
all bedrooms with intervening doors closed.

**Exceptions** (interconnection not required):
1. Buildings not undergoing any alterations, repairs, or construction.
2. Existing areas where alterations/repairs do not remove interior wall/ceiling finishes
   (unless attic/crawl space/basement provides access for interconnection).
3. Exterior-only work (roofing, siding, windows, doors, porch, deck).
4. Plumbing, mechanical, or electrical work not removing interior wall/ceiling finishes.

### Power Source (R310.6)
- **Primary power:** building wiring from commercial source.
- **Battery backup** required.
- Wiring must be permanent, without disconnecting switch (other than overcurrent
  protection).
- Must emit a signal when batteries are low.
- A smoke alarm with an integral strobe and no battery backup may instead be connected
  to an emergency electrical system under the R310.6 exception.

**Battery-only exceptions:**
1. Existing buildings with no construction taking place.
2. Buildings without commercial power.
3. Existing areas where alterations/repairs do not expose structure, unless an
   attic, crawl space, or basement provides access for building wiring without
   removing interior finishes.
4. Exterior-only work.
5. Plumbing/mechanical/electrical work not exposing structure.

### Combination Alarms (R310.5)
Combination smoke and carbon monoxide alarms may be used in lieu of smoke alarms.
Must be California State Fire Marshal listed and approved.

### Fire Alarm Systems (R310.7)
Fire alarm systems per NFPA 72 may be used in lieu of smoke alarms. Detectors must be
listed per UL 268. Where installed, the system becomes a permanent fixture owned by
the homeowner (CRC R310.7.3).

## 3. Carbon Monoxide Alarms (R311)

### Where Required (R311.2)
CO alarms are required in dwelling units where **either** condition exists:
1. The dwelling unit contains a fuel-fired appliance or fireplace.
2. The dwelling unit has an attached garage with an opening communicating with the
   dwelling unit.

This applies to both existing buildings and new construction (CRC R311.2.1).

For alterations/additions: new CO alarms are required when an addition is made or a
fuel-burning heater, appliance, or fireplace is added to a dwelling not previously
requiring CO alarms. Exterior-surface work, plumbing work, and mechanical work that is
not fuel fired are excepted (CRC R311.2.2).

### Locations (R311.3)
1. Outside each separate sleeping area in the immediate vicinity of bedrooms.
2. On every occupiable level, including basements.
3. Within the bedroom where a fuel-burning appliance is located in the bedroom or its
   attached bathroom.

### Interconnection (R311.5)
Where more than one CO alarm is required, all shall be interconnected so activation of
one activates all in the unit. Listed wireless alarms are acceptable.

**Exception:** Interconnection not required in existing buildings built before
January 1, 2011, where no construction is taking place; where interior work does not
remove finishes and no accessible attic, crawl space, or basement permits wiring;
for exterior-only work; or for qualifying plumbing/mechanical/electrical work
(CRC R311.5).

### Power Source (R311.6)
- **Primary power:** building wiring from commercial source.
- **Battery backup** required when primary power is interrupted.
- Wiring must be permanent, without disconnecting switch (other than overcurrent
  protection).

R311.6 permits battery power where commercial power is unavailable, for alarms installed
under R311.2.2, and in its specified pre-2011 existing-building cases. Group R may also
use another primary power source recognized by NFPA 72. Apply these four actual
exceptions rather than importing the smoke-alarm list by analogy.

### Combination Alarms (R311.4)
Combination CO/smoke alarms may be used in lieu of CO alarms. Must comply with both
R311 and State Fire Marshal listing requirements.

## 4. Garages and Carports -- Fire Safety (R317)

### Floor Surface (R317.1)
Garage floors must be of approved noncombustible material. Parking area floors must be
sloped toward a drain or the main vehicle entry doorway.

### Carports (R317.2)
Must be open on not less than two sides. Carports not open on two or more sides are
considered garages and must comply with garage provisions. Asphalt surfaces are
permitted at ground level in carports (CRC R317.2).

### Fire Sprinklers in Garages (R317.5)
Private garages shall be protected by fire sprinklers where the garage wall has been
designed based on Table R302.1(2), Note a. Sprinklers shall be:
- Residential sprinklers or quick-response sprinklers
- Designed for 0.05 gpm/sf density
- Connected to an automatic sprinkler system per R309
- Garage doors are not considered obstructions for sprinkler placement

### Attached Garages/Carports with Habitable Space Above (R317.5.1)
Must be sprinklered using one of:
1. Residential sprinklers per their listing.
2. Extended coverage sprinklers at Light Hazard flow rate per NFPA 13.
3. Quick-response spray sprinklers at light hazard spacing, 0.05 gpm/sf minimum.

System demand may be limited to the number of sprinklers in the compartment but not
more than two sprinklers for hydraulic calculations (CRC R317.5.1).

Exception: Not required for additions/alterations to existing carports/garages without
existing sprinklers.

### Automatic Garage Door Openers (R317.4)
Must be listed and labeled per UL 325, installed per manufacturer's instructions.
See the current official California statutes cross-referenced by R317.4 for additional
automatic-garage-door-opener provisions; those statutes are not indexed in the committed
Title 24 corpus.

### EV Charging (R317.6)
New one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses with attached private garages must
comply with EV charging requirements per the California Green Building Standards Code
(CALGreen).
