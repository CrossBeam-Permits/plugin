# Residential Lighting Controls Review Playbook

Reference for California single-family residential permit review.
Source: 2025 California Energy Code, Title 24, Part 6, Section 150.0(k).

This playbook converts Section 150.0(k) into conditional document checks. It
does not make every lighting-control family applicable to every project.

## 1. Applicability Gate

First identify whether the submitted scope installs, replaces, or alters
residential luminaires or lighting controls governed by Section 150.0(k).

- `APPLICABLE`: The scope, lighting plans, electrical plans, schedules, notes,
  or energy documents affirmatively show covered lighting work.
- `NOT APPLICABLE`: The submitted scope affirmatively excludes covered lighting
  work. Do not infer applicability merely because the building contains lights.
- `NEEDS INFO`: The record does not establish whether lighting or controls are
  installed or altered. Ask for the lighting scope or plan legend before
  deciding whether a control is missing.

After establishing applicability, evaluate only the control families triggered
by the affected space, luminaire, or control type. A feature absent from the
covered work is `NOT APPLICABLE`, not a correction. Indoor lighting controls
must also comply with Energy Code 110.9. An energy-management or multi-scene
control may satisfy a dimming, occupancy, or lighting-control requirement only
when the documents establish the specified functionality, the physical manual
on/off control in Section 150.0(k)2A, and that no dimmer or occupancy or
vacancy sensor installed to comply with Section 150.0(k)2 is bypassed.

## 2. Evidence Map

Inspect the submitted documents in this order:

1. Permit scope, demolition plan, and project notes for the extent of lighting
   work.
2. Lighting plan, reflected ceiling plan, or electrical plan for luminaire and
   control locations.
3. Luminaire, switch, sensor, and control schedules for device type and
   functionality.
4. Symbol legend, keyed notes, wiring/control diagrams, and specifications for
   the relationship between luminaires and controls.
5. Applicable energy compliance documents for the declared lighting features.

Record the exact sheet, detail, schedule row, or document page used for the
decision. Do not treat an unexplained symbol as proof of a dimmer, sensor,
photocell, or time control.

## 3. Control-Family Checks

### 3.1 Manual on/off controls

Covered indoor lighting has a readily accessible wall-mounted control that
manually turns the lighting on and off. An integrated ceiling-fan light may use
the remote-control exception stated in Section 150.0(k)2A.

### 3.2 Automatic-off controls

These are two independent indoor checks. Do not confine the cabinet rule to
the room list.

- In a bathroom, garage, laundry room, utility room, or walk-in closet, at
  least one installed luminaire is controlled by an occupancy or vacancy
  sensor providing automatic-off functionality.
- Lighting inside drawers or cabinetry with opaque fronts or doors, in any
  covered indoor space including kitchens, uses a control that turns the light
  off when the drawer or door is closed.

Do not convert the room-list rule into a universal demand for every garage
luminaire to have a sensor. Confirm the controlled luminaire and the affected
space.

Lighting in a residential parking garage for eight or more vehicles follows
the separate nonresidential-garage provisions identified by Section 150.0(k)5.
Do not resolve that condition with the ordinary dwelling-garage sensor check.

### 3.3 Habitable-space dimming

Covered lighting in habitable spaces, including living rooms, dining rooms,
kitchens, and bedrooms, has a readily accessible wall-mounted dimming control
that allows manual adjustment up and down. Apply the current exceptions before
issuing a correction:

- integrated ceiling-fan lighting controlled by a remote, and lighting
  integral to kitchen range hoods or bathroom exhaust fans;
- luminaires on a circuit with controlled lighting power below 20 watts, or
  controlled by an occupancy or vacancy sensor with automatic-off;
- navigation lighting below 5 watts; and
- qualifying lighting inside drawers or cabinetry that has the stated
  automatic-off control.

For forward-phase-cut dimmers controlling LED sources in these spaces, verify
the plan or specification identifies compliance with the applicable NEMA SSL
7A requirement rather than inventing a product qualification.

### 3.4 Independent controls

Lighting integrated with an exhaust fan is controlled independently from the
fan. Undercabinet lighting, undershelf lighting, display-cabinet lighting, and
switched outlets are controlled separately from ceiling-installed lighting so
one can operate without the other.

### 3.5 Residential outdoor lighting

Covered outdoor lighting permanently mounted to a residential building or
another building on the same lot has:

1. a manual on/off control that permits the required automatic operation; and
2. one of these automatic-control combinations:
   - photocell plus motion sensor;
   - photocell plus automatic time-switch control; or
   - astronomical time-clock control.

A control that overrides to on is not allowed unless that override
automatically returns the control to normal automatic operation within six
hours. Do not require an override where none is proposed. An energy-management
or other control system may satisfy this family only when the documents
establish equivalent required functionality.

## 4. Review Procedure

1. Establish the covered lighting scope and affected spaces.
2. Separate the review into manual on/off, automatic-off, dimming, independent
   control, and outdoor-control subchecks.
3. For each applicable subcheck, match every affected luminaire or lighting
   group to a control symbol, schedule entry, and stated functionality.
4. Apply the section's exception for that exact subcheck before deciding the
   control is missing.
5. Compare the lighting plan, legend, schedule, specifications, and energy
   documents. Identify an actual omission or conflict; do not infer one from
   silence in an unrelated document.
6. Record one decision state and the exact evidence locator.

## 5. Decision States

- `PASS`: Applicability is established and the plans identify the required
  control type and functionality consistently across the relevant documents.
- `CORRECTION`: Applicability is established and the submitted documents show
  a required control is absent, bypassed, or materially inconsistent, after
  all applicable exceptions are considered.
- `NEEDS INFO`: The record does not establish the lighting scope, control
  symbol, controlled luminaire, device functionality, or applicable exception.
- `NOT APPLICABLE`: Affirmative scope evidence establishes that the lighting
  work or control family is absent.
- `ESCALATE`: A proposed alternative control system or unresolved product
  qualification requires a determination beyond document comparison.

## 6. Issuing Basis and Correction Structure

The issuing basis is 2025 Energy Code Section 150.0(k), applied to the actual
covered lighting shown in the submitted documents, and Section 110.9 for
control-device functionality where that section applies. A safe correction states:

1. the observed lighting scope and location;
2. the missing or conflicting control evidence;
3. the exact control function that must be shown or coordinated; and
4. Section 150.0(k), including the applicable control-family subsection.

Example pattern:

> Sheet E2.1 shows new garage luminaires, but the lighting legend does not
> identify an occupancy or vacancy sensor controlling at least one garage
> luminaire. Revise the lighting plan and legend to identify the applicable
> automatic-off control, or document why the Section 150.0(k)2E requirement
> does not apply.

## 7. Limitations

- Do not assume lighting work from the existence of a room.
- Do not invent wattage, sensor coverage, time delay, product certification,
  or wiring topology absent from the submitted documents or current authority.
- Do not demand every control family where only one family is triggered.
- Do not treat an energy-document note as proof that the plan locates and
  coordinates the control.
- Do not approve an alternative control system unless the submitted material
  establishes equivalent required functionality or the building official has
  accepted it.
