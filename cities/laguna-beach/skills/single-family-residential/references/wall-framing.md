# Wood Wall Framing

> California Residential Code (CRC), Title 24, Part 2.5
> 2025 Edition (Effective January 1, 2026)

This reference covers wood wall framing requirements for single-family residential construction in California, including stud sizing, top plates, headers, fireblocking, cripple walls, wall bracing, and exterior window/door installation.

---

## 1. Stud Size, Height, and Spacing

Stud size, height, and spacing shall be in accordance with Table R602.3(5) (CRC R602.3.1).

### Prescriptive Stud Requirements (Table R602.3(5) — Nonexhaustive Excerpt)

The excerpt below does not reproduce every column or limiting footnote in Table R602.3(5). In particular, the current table also includes a one-floor-only bearing-wall column; 2x3 studs are not permitted in exterior walls; and a footnote separately limits some habitable-attic assemblies supported by 2x4 studs based on roof span. Use the current table, including all footnotes, for final sizing.

| Stud Size | Bearing Wall Max Unsupported Height | Bearing: Roof-Ceiling Only | Bearing: 1 Floor + Roof | Bearing: 2 Floors + Roof | Non-Bearing Max Height | Non-Bearing Max Spacing |
|-----------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| 2x3       | --                                   | --                        | --                       | --                       | 10 ft                 | 16" o.c.               |
| 2x4       | 10 ft                                | 24" o.c.                  | 16" o.c.                 | --                       | 14 ft                 | 24" o.c.               |
| 3x4       | 10 ft                                | 24" o.c.                  | 24" o.c.                 | 16" o.c.                 | 14 ft                 | 24" o.c.               |
| 2x5       | 10 ft                                | 24" o.c.                  | 24" o.c.                 | --                       | 16 ft                 | 24" o.c.               |
| 2x6       | 10 ft                                | 24" o.c.                  | 24" o.c.                 | 16" o.c.                 | 20 ft                 | 24" o.c.               |

Studs shall be minimum No. 3, Standard, or Stud grade lumber (CRC R602.2). Utility-grade studs shall not be spaced more than 16" o.c. and shall support no more than a roof and ceiling. Their maximum height is 8 ft in exterior or load-bearing walls and 10 ft in interior non-load-bearing walls (CRC R602.3.1, Exception 1).

### Prescriptive Height Exceptions

- Where ground snow load is 25 psf or less and ultimate design wind speed is 130 mph or less, 2x6 studs supporting roof only with 6 ft or less tributary length may be up to 18 ft at 16" o.c. or 20 ft at 12" o.c.; studs shall be No. 2 grade or better (CRC R602.3.1, Exception 2).
- Exterior load-bearing studs up to 12 ft may follow Table R602.3(6) only where the building is in Exposure B, roof live load does not exceed 20 psf, ground snow load does not exceed 30 psf, and studs and plates are No. 2 grade or better. Provide full-height studs adjacent to openings as required by R602.7.5 (CRC R602.3.1, Exception 3).

### Alternate Stud Sizes for Taller Walls (Table R602.3(6) Summary)

| Stud Height | Supporting   | 12" Spacing      | 16" Spacing      | 24" Spacing      |
|-------------|-------------|------------------|------------------|------------------|
| 11 ft       | Roof only   | 2x4              | 2x4 to 2x6      | 2x6              |
| 11 ft       | Roof + floor| 2x4 to 2x6      | 2x6              | 2x6              |
| 12 ft       | Roof only   | 2x4 to 2x6      | 2x4 to 2x6      | 2x6              |
| 12 ft       | Roof + floor| 2x4 to 2x6      | 2x6              | 2x6 or DR        |

Note: Values vary by wind speed (115-140 mph) and roof/floor span (12-24 ft). DR = Design Required.

## 2. Top Plates

Wood stud walls shall be capped with a **double top plate** providing overlapping at corners and intersections with bearing partitions. End joints in top plates shall be offset not less than **24 inches** (CRC R602.3.2).

### Single Top Plate Alternative

A single top plate is permitted when all of the following are met (CRC R602.3.2, Exception):

1. Tied at corners, intersecting walls, and in-line splices per Table R602.3.2
2. Rafters or joists centered over studs within 1-inch tolerance
3. Top plate may be omitted over headers if headers are adequately tied

Table R602.3.2 makes single-top-plate tie details conditional. Its first-row details use galvanized steel plates at least 3" x 6" at corners and 3" x 12" at butt joints, with the table-specified nailing. For structures in SDC D0, D1, or D2 where braced-wall-line spacing is at least 25 ft, use a 3" x 8" x 0.036" plate with nine 8d box nails on each side at corners and a 3" x 16" x 0.036" plate with eighteen 8d box nails on each side at butt joints (CRC Table R602.3.2).

### Bearing Studs Below Joists/Trusses

Where joists, trusses, or rafters are spaced more than 16" o.c. and bearing studs below are at 24" o.c., members shall bear within 5 inches of the studs beneath. Exceptions include top plates made of two 2x6 or two 3x4 members, installation of a third top plate, or solid blocking equal in size to the studs that reinforces the double top plate (CRC R602.3.3).

## 3. Drilling and Notching of Studs

Drilling and notching limits for studs (CRC R602.6):

| Condition | Bearing/Exterior Walls | Non-Bearing Partitions |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Maximum notch depth | 25% of stud depth | 40% of stud depth |
| Maximum bore diameter | 60% of stud depth | 60% of stud depth |
| Minimum edge distance for bore | 5/8 inch | 5/8 inch |
| Bore at notch location | Not permitted | Not permitted |

Where bore diameter exceeds 40% in bearing/exterior walls, the stud shall be doubled, and not more than two successive doubled studs may be bored this way. As an exception, approved stud shoes may be installed according to the manufacturer's instructions (CRC R602.6, Item 2).

### Top Plate Drilling

Where piping or ductwork in an exterior wall or interior load-bearing wall requires the top plate to be cut, drilled, or notched by more than 50% of its width, a galvanized metal tie (16 ga, 1-1/2" wide) shall be fastened across the opening with not less than eight 10d nails on each side, extending not less than 6 inches past the opening (CRC R602.6.1). Exception: where the entire notched side is covered with wood structural panel sheathing.

## 4. Headers

Header spans are governed by Tables R602.7(1) for exterior bearing walls, R602.7(2) for interior bearing walls, and R602.7(3) for open porches (CRC R602.7).

### Common Header Sizes — Exterior Bearing Walls, 30 psf Snow Load (Table R602.7(1) Excerpt)

Spans shown for roof-and-ceiling support, building width = 24 ft:

| Header Size     | Span   | Jack Studs Required |
|----------------|--------|---------------------|
| 2-2x6          | 4'-7"  | 1                   |
| 2-2x8          | 5'-9"  | 1                   |
| 2-2x10         | 6'-10" | 2                   |
| 2-2x12         | 8'-1"  | 2                   |
| 3-2x10         | 8'-7"  | 1                   |
| 3-2x12         | 10'-1" | 2                   |
| 4-2x10         | 9'-11" | 1                   |
| 4-2x12         | 11'-8" | 1                   |

### Header Types

- **Built-up headers**: Two or more 2-inch members with 1/2" spacer, face nailed 16d common at 16" o.c. each edge (CRC Table R602.3(1), Item 10).
- **Single-member headers**: Single flat 2-inch nominal member with 2-inch plate on top and bottom, face nailed with 10d box nails at 12" o.c. (CRC R602.7.1).
- **Rim board headers**: Sized per Table R602.7(1), supported at each end by full-height studs (CRC R602.7.2).
- **Wood structural panel box headers**: Per Table R602.7.3, maximum 8 ft span with panels on both sides, 15" depth (CRC R602.7.3).

### Non-Bearing Wall Headers

Load-bearing headers are NOT required in non-bearing walls. A single flat 2x4 is permitted for openings up to 8 ft wide where the vertical distance to the nailing surface above is 24" or less (CRC R602.7.4).

### Header Support

Headers shall be supported at each end by jack studs or approved framing anchors. Minimum full-height studs at each end per Table R602.7.5 (CRC R602.7.5):

| Header Span | < 140 mph Exp B | <= 115 mph Exp B |
|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 4 ft        | 1              | 1               |
| 6 ft        | 2              | 1               |
| 8 ft        | 2              | 1               |
| 10 ft       | 3              | 2               |
| 12 ft       | 3              | 2               |
| 16 ft       | 4              | 2               |

## 5. Interior Non-Bearing Partitions

Interior non-bearing walls may be constructed with (CRC R602.5):

- 2x3 studs at 24" o.c., or
- 2x4 flat studs at 16" o.c. (where not part of a braced wall line)
- Capped with not less than a single top plate
- Fireblocked per R602.8

---

## 6. Fireblocking

Fireblocking shall be provided in accordance with Section R302.11 (CRC R602.8). Key locations include:

- Concealed stud spaces at ceiling and floor levels
- Connections between horizontal and vertical concealed spaces (soffits, drop ceilings, cove ceilings)
- Openings around vents, pipes, ducts at ceiling and floor levels
- Horizontally at intervals not exceeding 10 ft in concealed spaces of walls and partitions; the separate vertical rule applies at ceiling and floor levels

---

## 7. Cripple Walls

Foundation cripple walls shall be framed with studs not smaller than the studding above (CRC R602.9).

- Where exceeding **4 feet in height**, cripple walls shall be framed as an additional story
- Exterior cripple walls with stud height **less than 14 inches** shall be continuously sheathed on one side with wood structural panels or constructed of solid blocking
- Cripple walls shall be supported on **continuous foundations**
- Cripple wall bracing per R602.10.10 applies specific requirements for SDC D0, D1, and D2

### California Seismic Note

In SDC D0 and D1 (and townhouses in SDC C), cripple wall bracing is limited to Methods WSP and CS-WSP, with maximum 14 ft between adjacent braced wall panels (CRC R602.10.10.1). In SDC D2, cripple walls shall be braced per Tables R602.10.3(3) and R602.10.3(4) (CRC R602.10.10.2).

---

## 8. Wall Bracing

Wall bracing is critical for California construction due to high seismic design categories. Buildings shall be braced per Section R602.10 or Section R602.12 (CRC R602.10).

### Braced Wall Lines

- Designated as straight lines in the building plan (CRC R602.10.1)
- Panels may be offset up to **4 feet** from the designated line (CRC R602.10.1.2)
- Maximum spacing between parallel braced wall lines is determined from Table R602.10.1.3; do not treat SDC A-C as one wind-governed category. As a nonexhaustive seismic excerpt, the table provides 35 ft for SDC C townhouses, with an increase to 50 ft where required bracing is adjusted under Table R602.10.3(4), and 25 ft for SDC D0-D2 detached dwellings and townhouses, with an increase to 35 ft where required bracing is adjusted under Table R602.10.3(4). Apply the table's building-type and story conditions to the project.

### Braced Wall Panels

- Full-height sections, no vertical or horizontal offsets (CRC R602.10.2)
- Nearest edge within **10 feet** from each end of braced wall line (CRC R602.10.2.2)
- Maximum **20 feet** between adjacent panel edges along a braced wall line
- In SDC D0-D2, the base rule places panels at each braced-wall-line end. Exceptions allow qualifying WSP, BV-WSP, and continuous-sheathing panels to begin up to 10 ft from an end where the listed corner-panel or hold-down conditions are met; qualifying PFH, ABW, and BV-WSP configurations may also begin up to 10 ft from an end (CRC R602.10.2.2.1)

### Required Bracing Length

Determined from Tables R602.10.3(1) (wind-based) and R602.10.3(3) (seismic-based) with applicable adjustment factors. For SDC D0-D2, use the greater value from wind or seismic tables (CRC R602.10.3).

### Common Bracing Methods (R602.10.4)

| Method   | Description                                           |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| LIB      | Let-in bracing (1x4 diagonal)                         |
| DWB      | Diagonal wood boards                                  |
| WSP      | Wood structural panels (most common)                  |
| SFB      | Structural fiberboard                                 |
| GB       | Gypsum board                                          |
| PBS      | Particleboard sheathing                               |
| PCP      | Portland cement plaster                               |
| HPS      | Hardboard panel siding                                |
| ABW      | Alternate braced wall                                 |
| PFH      | Portal frame with hold-downs                          |
| PFG      | Portal frame at garage opening                        |
| CS-WSP   | Continuous sheathing - wood structural panels         |
| CS-G     | Continuously sheathed wood structural panel adjacent to garage openings |
| CS-SFB   | Continuous sheathing - structural fiberboard          |
| CS-PF    | Continuous sheathing - portal frame                   |

## 9. Window and Door Framing

### Rough Opening Configuration

Configure each opening under the applicable bearing- or nonbearing-wall header provisions rather than using one universal component list (CRC R602.3, R602.7). For headers governed by R602.7.5, provide jack studs or approved framing anchors and a full-height stud adjacent to each header end. Qualifying nonbearing-wall headers under R602.7.4 do not require cripples or blocking above the header. Header sizing and support quantities remain subject to the applicable R602.7 table and alternative.

---

## 10. Exterior Window and Door Installation

Exterior windows and doors shall be installed per manufacturer's written instructions. Openings shall be flashed per Section R703.4 (CRC R609.1).

### Performance Requirements

Exterior windows and doors shall resist the design wind loads in Table R301.2.1(1), adjusted for height and exposure under Table R301.2.1(2), or loads determined under ASCE 7 using its allowable-stress-design load combinations (CRC R609.2).

### Testing and Labeling

Windows and sliding doors shall be tested per AAMA/WDMA/CSA 101/I.S.2/A440 and bear a label from an approved independent laboratory (CRC R609.3). Garage doors shall be tested per ASTM E330 or ANSI/DASMA 108 (CRC R609.4).

### Anchoring

Windows and glass doors shall be anchored per manufacturer's recommendations (CRC R609.7.1). Anchorage details per Figures R609.7.2(1) through R609.7.2(8) address:

- Through-the-frame connections
- Frame clip connections
- Through-the-flange connections
- Masonry/concrete substrate requirements (CRC R609.7.2.1)
- Wood framing connections (CRC R609.7.2.2)

### Windborne Debris Protection

In windborne debris regions, protection per Section R301.2.1.2 is required (CRC R609.6).
