# Electrical Requirements for Additions

Source: 2025 California Electrical Code (Title 24, Part 3); California Residential Code (Title 24, Part 2.5), Sections R310 and R311.

---

## Panel Capacity and Service (CEC Article 220, 230)

### Load Calculation
Before adding circuits, verify that the existing electrical panel has sufficient capacity:

1. **Standard method (CEC 220.82):** Calculate total load based on:
   - General lighting and receptacles: 3 VA per SF of dwelling area
   - Small-appliance circuits: 1,500 VA per circuit (min 2 required)
   - Laundry circuit: 1,500 VA
   - Fixed appliances (range, dryer, water heater, HVAC, etc.)
   - Apply demand factors per CEC 220.82

2. **Common minimums for existing homes:**
   - 100A service: marginal for additions — may need upgrade
   - 200A service: typically adequate for most additions
   - 400A service: needed for large additions with heavy loads (HVAC, EV charging, etc.)

### Panel Upgrade Triggers
A panel upgrade is often required when:
- Existing service is 100A or less and addition adds significant loads
- Addition includes new HVAC system (heat pump: typically 30-50A)
- Addition includes EV charging (typically 40-50A)
- Total calculated load exceeds 80% of existing service capacity

## Branch Circuit Requirements (CEC 210.11)

### Required Circuits by Room Type

| Room/Area | Circuit Requirement |
|---|---|
| **General lighting** | 15A or 20A circuits; 3 VA per SF |
| **Kitchen** | Two 20A small-appliance circuits (countertop receptacles) |
| **Kitchen** | Individual circuit for dishwasher (typically 20A) |
| **Kitchen** | Individual circuit for disposal (typically 15A or 20A) |
| **Bathroom** | One 20A circuit (may serve multiple bath receptacles in same dwelling) |
| **Laundry** | One 20A dedicated circuit |
| **HVAC** | Dedicated circuit per equipment nameplate |
| **Water heater** | Dedicated circuit (HPWH typically 30A/240V) |

### Wire Sizing
- 15A circuit: min 14 AWG copper
- 20A circuit: min 12 AWG copper
- 30A circuit: min 10 AWG copper
- 40A circuit: min 8 AWG copper
- 50A circuit: min 6 AWG copper

## AFCI Protection (CEC 210.12(A))

### Where Required
Arc-fault circuit interrupter protection is required for ALL 120-volt, 15A and 20A branch circuits supplying outlets or devices in the following rooms of a dwelling:

- Kitchens
- Family rooms
- Dining rooms
- Living rooms
- Parlors
- Libraries
- Dens
- Bedrooms
- Sunrooms
- Recreation rooms
- Closets
- Hallways
- Laundry areas
- Similar rooms or areas

### Key Points
- AFCI protection covers virtually all habitable spaces in a dwelling
- For additions: all new branch circuits in these areas must have AFCI protection
- AFCI breakers must be listed to UL 1699
- Combination AFCI breakers (protecting against both series and parallel arcing) are the standard

### Existing Circuits
Existing circuits extended to serve the addition may need to be upgraded to AFCI if the existing breaker is not AFCI-rated and the extension serves a covered area.

## GFCI Protection (CEC 210.8(A))

### Where Required for Dwelling Units
Ground-fault circuit interrupter protection is required for 125-volt, 15A and 20A receptacles in:

| Location | Specific Rule |
|---|---|
| **Bathrooms** | All receptacles |
| **Garages and accessory buildings** | All receptacles at or below grade |
| **Outdoors** | All receptacles (readily accessible, 6.5 ft or less from grade) |
| **Crawl spaces** | All receptacles at or below grade |
| **Unfinished basements** | All receptacles (except dedicated appliance receptacles not readily accessible) |
| **Kitchens** | Receptacles serving countertop surfaces |
| **Sinks** | Within 6 ft of outside edge of sink (measured along wall) |
| **Boathouses** | All receptacles |
| **Bathtubs/shower stalls** | Within 6 ft of outside edge |
| **Laundry areas** | All receptacles |
| **Indoor damp/wet locations** | All receptacles |

### GFCI Devices
- GFCI breakers: protect entire circuit
- GFCI receptacles: protect downstream receptacles
- GFCI class A: trips at 4-6 mA ground fault
- Outdoor receptacles: must also be weather-resistant (WR) rated

## Receptacle Spacing (CEC 210.52)

### General Requirements (CEC 210.52(A))
- Max 12 ft between receptacles along any wall (measured along the wall line)
- No point along the wall should be more than 6 ft from a receptacle
- Any wall space 2 ft or wider requires a receptacle
- Floor receptacles within 18" of wall count toward spacing

### Kitchen Countertops (CEC 210.52(C))
- Max 24" from end of countertop to receptacle
- Max 48" between receptacles
- Receptacle required for each counter space 12" or wider
- Island countertops: one receptacle required if long dimension >=24" and short dimension >=12"
- Peninsula countertops: one receptacle required if long dimension >=24" and short dimension >=12"
- All kitchen countertop receptacles: 20A small-appliance circuit + GFCI

### Bathroom (CEC 210.52(D))
- Min one receptacle outlet within 36" of each basin (measured horizontally from edge of basin)
- Receptacles must be on wall adjacent to basin location

### Outdoors (CEC 210.52(E))
- Min one receptacle at front and back of dwelling (readily accessible, max 6.5 ft above grade)
- Balconies/decks/porches with >=20 SF area: one receptacle required

## Smoke Alarms (CRC R310)

### Where Required (R310.3)
Smoke alarms must be installed in:
1. Each sleeping room
2. Outside each separate sleeping area in immediate vicinity of bedrooms
3. On each additional story (including basements, habitable attics)
4. Not less than 3 ft from bathroom door (with tub/shower)
5. In hallway and room open to hallway where ceiling height difference >24"

### Trigger for Additions (R310.2.2)
When alterations, repairs, or additions requiring a permit occur:
- The **individual dwelling unit** must be equipped with smoke alarms in all required locations
- This applies to the ENTIRE dwelling, not just the addition

### Installation Requirements
- **Listings:** UL 217 (smoke) or UL 217 + UL 2034 (combination)
- **California State Fire Marshal listed and approved** per Title 19
- **Location specifics (NFPA 72 Section 29.8.3.4):**
  - Min 20 ft from cooking appliance (ionization) or 10 ft (photoelectric)
  - Min 3 ft from bathroom door
  - Not within 36" of supply registers or ceiling fan blade tips
  - On sloped ceilings: within 12" vertically from highest point

### Interconnection (R310.4)
Where more than one smoke alarm is required within a dwelling:
- All alarms must be interconnected (activation of one triggers all)
- Alarm must be clearly audible in all bedrooms with doors closed
- **Exceptions:**
  1. Not required in buildings not undergoing alterations
  2. Not required in existing areas when alterations don't remove interior wall/ceiling finishes, UNLESS attic/crawl/basement access is available for interconnection

### Power Source (R310.5)
- Primary power from building wiring (commercial source)
- Battery backup when primary power interrupted
- Permanent wiring without disconnecting switch (except overcurrent protection)
- Exception: Wireless smoke alarms may satisfy interconnection without hardwiring

## Carbon Monoxide Alarms (CRC R311)

### Where Required (R311.2)
CO alarms required in dwelling units where:
1. Dwelling has a fuel-fired appliance or fireplace, OR
2. Dwelling has an attached garage with opening to dwelling

### Trigger for Additions (R311.2.2)
When an addition is made to a dwelling:
- New CO alarms required per R311
- **Exceptions (CO alarms NOT triggered):**
  1. Exterior work only (roofing, siding, windows, doors, porch, deck)
  2. Plumbing work
  3. Non-fuel-fired mechanical work

### Location (R311.3)
1. Outside each separate sleeping area in immediate vicinity of bedrooms
2. On every occupiable level (including basements)
3. Within bedroom if fuel-burning appliance is in bedroom or attached bathroom

### Interconnection (R311.5)
- All CO alarms within a dwelling unit must be interconnected
- Physical interconnection not required if listed wireless alarms are used
- **Exception for pre-2011 buildings:** Interconnection not required if:
  1. Alterations don't remove interior finishes (unless attic/crawl/basement access available)
  2. No construction taking place
  3. Exterior work only
  4. Plumbing/mechanical/electrical work not removing interior finishes

### Power Source (R311.6)
- Hardwired with battery backup (same as smoke alarms)
- **Exception for pre-2011 buildings:** Battery-powered or plug-in with battery backup permitted if:
  - No construction taking place, OR
  - Repairs don't remove interior finishes, OR
  - Exterior work only, OR
  - Plumbing/mechanical/electrical work not removing interior finishes

### Combination Alarms (R311.4)
Combination smoke/CO alarms are permitted in lieu of separate CO alarms. Must comply with both UL 217 and UL 2034.

## Practical Summary for Additions

### What Every Addition Triggers
1. All new circuits in addition must have AFCI protection (virtually all rooms)
2. GFCI protection for wet/damp locations (bathrooms, kitchens countertops, outdoors, laundry)
3. Smoke alarms throughout the ENTIRE dwelling (not just addition) — hardwired, interconnected
4. CO alarms if fuel-fired appliance or attached garage — hardwired, interconnected
5. Panel capacity verification — may trigger panel upgrade

### What Does NOT Trigger Full Upgrade
- Extending a circuit to serve the addition does not require the existing panel to be replaced (but capacity must be adequate)
- Pre-2011 buildings: interconnection of alarms has exceptions when interior finishes are not disturbed
- Existing circuits in unaltered portions of the home are generally not required to be upgraded to AFCI
