# Structural Framing Requirements

Source: 2025 California Residential Code (Title 24, Part 2.5), Chapters 5, 6, and 8.

---

## Lumber Grade and Species Requirements

### General (CRC R602.1)
- All load-supporting lumber must be identified by a grade mark from an accredited lumber grading agency
- Design values must be certified by an accreditation body complying with DOC PS 20

### Studs (CRC R602.2)
- Minimum grade: No. 3, Standard, or Stud grade
- Exception: Non-bearing studs and bearing studs not supporting floors may be Utility grade if spaced per Table R602.3(5)

### Engineered Wood Products
- Prefabricated wood I-joists: per ASTM D5055
- Glued-laminated timbers: per ANSI A190.1
- Structural composite lumber (LVL, PSL, LSL): per ASTM D5456
- Cross-laminated timber: per ANSI/APA PRG 320
- Wood structural panels: per DOC PS 1 or PS 2

### Floor Framing Rules
- Bearing on wood/metal: min 1-1/2" (CRC R502.6)
- Bearing on masonry/concrete: min 3" (CRC R502.6)
- Blocking/bridging: required at supports and at intervals per span
- Subflooring: wood structural panels per Section R503.2
- Framing at braced wall lines: load path for lateral forces required (CRC R502.2.1)

## Wall Framing (CRC R602)

### Stud Sizing and Spacing

| Condition | Stud Size | Max Spacing | Max Height |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-story exterior bearing | 2x4 | 16" OC | 10 ft |
| 2-story exterior bearing (bottom) | 2x6 | 16" OC | 10 ft |
| Interior non-bearing | 2x4 | 24" OC | 10 ft |
| Interior bearing | 2x4 | 16" OC | 10 ft |

Per Table R602.3(5), utility grade studs have reduced heights and spacings.

### Headers (CRC R602.7, Tables R602.7(1) and R602.7(2))

Headers are required over all openings in bearing walls. Common prescriptive header sizes:

| Opening Width | 1-Story (Roof+Ceiling Only) | 2-Story (2 Floors + Roof) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 4 ft | 2-2x6 | 2-2x8 |
| Up to 6 ft | 2-2x8 | 2-2x10 |
| Up to 8 ft | 2-2x10 | 2-2x12 |
| Up to 10 ft | 2-2x12 | Engineered |
| >10 ft | Engineered | Engineered |

*Actual sizes depend on building width, loading, species, and span. Refer to Tables R602.7(1) and R602.7(2) for precise values by species and condition.*

### Key Fastening Schedule (CRC Table R602.3(1))

| Connection | Fastener | Spacing |
|---|---|---|
| Top or bottom plate to stud | 3-16d box or 2-16d common | End nail |
| Top or bottom plate to stud | 4-8d box or 3-8d common | Toe nail |
| Top plate to top plate | 16d common | 16" OC face nail |
| Double top plate splice | 8-16d common | Each side of joint, min 24" lap |
| Bottom plate to joist/blocking | 16d common | 16" OC face nail |
| Bottom plate at braced wall panel | 3-16d box or 2-16d common | 16" OC face nail |
| Stud to stud (at braced panels) | 16d box or 3" nails | 12" OC face nail |

### Built-up Headers
- 2" to 2" header with 1/2" spacer: 16d common, 16" OC each edge face nail

## Roof Framing (CRC R802)

### Rafter Spans

Rafter spans depend on species, grade, spacing, roof slope, and loading. Refer to Tables R802.5.1(1) through R802.5.1(8) in the CRC. Common California values for Douglas Fir-Larch #2:

**20 psf live load (typical for most of CA):**

| Rafter Size | 12" OC | 16" OC | 24" OC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2x6 | 13'-1" | 11'-11" | 10'-5" |
| 2x8 | 17'-4" | 15'-9" | 13'-9" |
| 2x10 | 22'-1" | 20'-1" | 17'-1" |
| 2x12 | 26'-10" | 23'-5" | 19'-2" |

*Values approximate; actual spans depend on dead load and ceiling attachment.*

### Ridge Board
- Min 1" nominal thickness
- Depth equal to or greater than the cut end of the rafter
- When ridge acts as a beam (not supported at both ends): requires engineered design

### Collar Ties (CRC R802.5.2)
- Required in upper third of attic space
- Max 4 ft OC
- 2x4 minimum
- Face-nailed to each rafter: 4-10d box or 3-10d common

### Ceiling Joist-Rafter Connection (CRC R802.5.2, Table R802.5.2(1))
- Ceiling joists must provide a continuous tie across the building when rafters bear on exterior walls
- Number of nails per Table R802.5.2(1) based on rafter span, spacing, and roof slope

### Uplift Connections (CRC R802.11)
- Roof assemblies must be connected to resist uplift forces
- Rafter or truss to plate: 3-16d box (toe nail) or approved connector per design wind speed
- Approved framing connectors (hurricane ties/clips) may be required based on wind speed zone

## Connection to Existing Structure

### New-to-Existing Framing Tie-In
- New top plates must lap existing top plates minimum 24" or be spliced with approved connectors
- New studs adjacent to existing wall: face-nail or use approved connectors
- Roof framing intersections (valley, hip, ridge): proper bearing and fastening required
- Floor diaphragm continuity: blocking and nailing between new and existing joist systems

### Diaphragm Continuity
- Floor and roof diaphragms in the addition must be continuous with the existing structure
- Blocking at diaphragm boundaries between new and existing
- Nailing per Table R602.3(3) for wood structural panel sheathing at boundaries

## Engineered Design Triggers (CRC R301.1.3)

The following conditions require a California licensed architect or engineer:

1. **Any structural element exceeding prescriptive limits** (spans, loads, openings wider than tables permit)
2. **Wood-frame structures >2 stories and basement** (R301.1.3.2) — always requires stamped plans
3. **Non-conventional framing** (R301.1.3.1) — any deviation from substantial compliance with conventional framing
4. **Cold-formed steel, concrete, masonry, or SIPs** (R301.1.3.3) — always requires stamped plans for structural elements
5. **Point loads not directly over bearing walls** — transfer beams, cantilevers, etc.
6. **Seismic Design Category D** — complex irregular configurations may exceed prescriptive bracing

When engineered design is used for specific elements, the design need only demonstrate compliance of the non-conventional elements and must be compatible with the conventional framed system.
