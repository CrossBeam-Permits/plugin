# Laguna Beach Practice — Homes in Commercial Buildings

This file records how the City has actually handled housing in commercial buildings. It is **City practice, not code**. Use it to predict what Planning will ask for and to set expectations. The rules are in `mixed-use-conversion.md`; the code controls where the two differ.

Sources: City letters, redlines and resolutions in the Tyler/EnerGov record, Planning Commission staff reports, and the 2025 Housing Element Annual Progress Report (APR), as collected 2026-10-09. Record ids are given so each fact can be checked. Facts marked "(record)" come from a City document. Facts marked "(reading)" are an interpretation.

## Headline

- As of 2026-10, **no interior office-to-apartment conversion under §25.19.002(DD) (Ord. 1712) had been filed or heard** (record, absence across 2025 to September 2026 PC and DRB agendas and permit records). A conversion would be an early case. There is no approved template and no denial.
- No City letter in these records cites §25.19.002(DD) or Ord. 1712 by number (record). Staff describe the use as allowed: "The C-1 zone allows mixed-use development, including residential uses above ground-floor commercial uses" (1040 S Coast Hwy, PC Resolution for DR-2026-0844).
- The 2025 APR counts **0 units under AB 2011 / SB 6** and reports Housing Element Programs 5.2-1i, 5.2-2a, 5.2-2b and 5.2-2d as implemented through Ord. 1712 (record).
- No Laguna AB 507 ordinance or agenda item, and no adopted mixed-use objective design standards, as of 2026-10 (record, absence). The Coastal Commission's Ord. 1712 modifications set a 2028-01-01 deadline for multifamily objective standards (record).

### Case notes

**1040 S Coast Hwy (C-1).**
- 2023 TDM review (redlines 2023-06-26): staff found "no grandfathered parking" and an 8-space deficit. Staff refused off-site spaces, reading §25.52.006(H) as limited to "an intensification of a non-residential use." That reading predates the Ord. 1712 amendment of (H); see `mixed-use-conversion.md` Section 6.
- 2025 approval: staff accepted 0.5 space per unit for units of 700 sf or less near transit, and kept the commercial requirement at the count set by the 1958 approval (record). Units were approved over a parking shortfall only through the Council TDM CUP.
- A recorded covenant against short-term lodging was a condition (record).
- 2026 modification: front setback of 7 ft 2 in under the §25.96.030(C)(1) waiver; decks of 630 to 961 sf needed the open-space variance. The resolution states that a major remodel "only due to the size of the floor-area addition, without triggering demolition thresholds" may "retain nonconformities associated with existing nonresidential uses" (record).
- 2025 letter: demolition over 50% is a major remodel, and every nonconformity must then comply (record).
- Coastal: non-appealable area; the PC approved the CDP with the Design Review (record).

**1155 S Coast Hwy (C-1).** No units added. The PC hearing was triggered by additions over 15 ft, decks over 3 ft, and a new roof and materials (record). Staff ran a parking table at the new 0.5 rate and credited the site's existing deficit as "grandfathered" spaces: the old requirement less the 2 spaces provided (record). The resolution says "off-street parking will continue to be provided from Gaviota Drive" (record).

**1391 S Coast Hwy (C-1).** New construction, not a conversion.
- The CDP is appealable, for a new structure and intensification of use (letter 2025-12-15, record).
- Staff required a traffic study "because the proposed use is more intense than the existing use," with peer review at applicant cost (record).
- Redlines: "Only 1 space is required per unit" for the 1,030 sf units; "Residential open space is not required" (record).
- Round 4: "The lower level was not approved as office space per City Council Resolution No. 20.069 - Condition No. 50" (record). Old conditions of approval travel with the site.
- Also required: parcel map or lot line adjustment before entitlement, ALTA survey, deposit agreement for consultant review, historic assessment (record).

**670 S Coast Hwy (C-1).**
- The letter of 2026-10-08 found a major remodel (addition over 50% of commercial floor area; over 50% of walls removed over time). So the residential-addition relief for nonconforming parking does not apply. Staff required commercial parking at 1 per 250 sf or in-lieu certificates (record). The letter cites the relief as "25.95.030(C)(3)"; the codified section is §25.96.030(C)(3).
- Redlines: the infill incentive requires a recorded covenant against short-term lodging; ground-floor residential must be separated from commercial; commercial depth over 30 ft (record).
- CDP not appealable, fee $585, under §25.07.008(A)(2)(b) intensity of use; Design Review at the PC (record).
- A $10,000 "Housing Development Project (City Attorney Review)" deposit was charged (record). The 2026 application forms list $5,000 "When requested by CAO"; see `mixed-use-conversion.md` Section 13, item 6.
- 90-day resubmittal clock (record).

**276 S Coast Hwy (DSP, CBD Visitor Commercial).** The concept letter of 2026-08-20 stated (record):
- Nonconforming expansion capped at 10% or 100 sf.
- Second-story residential capped at 50% of floor area.
- A 12 ft height limit.
- About 9 spaces short; open space short.
- At least five variances whose findings staff did not believe could be made.

Staff offered two options: redesign, or a 100% affordable project under State Density Bonus Law. See `dsp-mixed-use.md`.

**2940 S Coast Hwy (C-N).**
- Staff said live/work "is not a use expressly listed in the C-N (Commercial-Neighborhood) zone" and deferred the use path, review body and fees (letter 2026-08-18, record).
- Redlines: "A conditional approval requires a defined use"; a parking table for every use on the site, existing and proposed, with the prior legal use's demand and basis; walking distance to transit; a Tenant Mix Table; covenant questions (no short-term lodging, no separate rental or sale) (record).
- Building-stage items flagged early: meet Building on the occupancy change first, with occupancy separation and egress (live/work case); guardrails, egress, the 20% accessibility upgrade, Fire, and LBMC 14.70 materials (deck case ZPC-2026-1335) (record).
- This is a ground-floor unit, which (DD) allows only behind 30 ft of highway-fronting commercial. It does not test an upper-floor conversion (reading).

**3337 Laguna Canyon Rd (M1B).** Artists' work/live by PC CUP under Ch. 25.16. The 2017 staff report for Unit D (CUP-2017-1814) treated the change as parking **de-intensification**: 1.5 spaces for work/live against about 2.5 at the industrial 1 per 500 sf rate, so no new parking (record). It also required an artist occupancy permit, two-thirds work area in M-1, and no separate rental or sale of the work and living areas (record).

**340 St. Ann's Dr (LBP).** Approved by right under SB 4 with no CEQA, but the City still required a discretionary CDP at the PC with findings under §25.07.012(F),(G) (PC staff report 2026-06-03, record). By-right housing in the coastal zone still gets a CDP here.

**21022 Laguna Canyon Rd (M1B).** Adding residential to a mixed retail and apartment building outside the Ord. 1712 zones opened three discretionary cases at once: PC Design Review, a CDP, and a CUP (record).

## Patterns (City practice)

1. **Pre-application first.** Every case started with a zoning plan check (ZPC/ZPCC) before entitlement filings (record). First rounds came back incomplete at 1391, 670, 2940, 21022, 340 St. Ann's and 240 Thalia (record).
2. **CDP every time.** Adding units was treated as intensification of use, never exempt (670, 1391), and by-right housing still got a CDP (340 St. Ann's) (record).
3. **Planning Commission hears mixed use.** Commercial and mixed-use Design Review went to the PC in every case; the DRB heard no commercial or mixed-use items in 2025-26 (record). Every PC case had new floor area or exterior work. None was interior-only.
4. **No use permit in C-1 for upper-floor units** (1040, 1155, 670). A CUP was needed in M1B (3337, 21022) and for parking relief (1040) (record).
5. **Parking decides the case.**
   - Staff build a table for every use on the site.
   - Credit for a legal nonconforming ("grandfathered") deficit has gone both ways. Staff gave it at 1155 (2025; additions under 50%) and kept the 1040 commercial count at its 1958 ratio (2025; a major remodel by floor-area addition only, §25.96.030(C)(3)). Staff refused it at 670 (2026; major remodel that also crossed the demolition threshold) and in the 2023 TDM review at 1040. No rule settles it; confirm the credit with Planning at pre-application.
   - They accept the 0.5 rate for small units near transit (1040, 1155) and 1 per unit otherwise (1391).
   - They have accepted "homes need less parking than the use they replace" as de-intensification (3337).
6. **Covenant against short-term lodging** wherever a §25.96.030 exception is used (1040, 670; asked at 2940) (record).
7. **Major remodel is the hinge.** A major remodel that crosses a demolition threshold turns off nonconforming parking credit and the §25.96.030(C)(3) relief (670; 1040 letter 2025-03-20) (record). A major remodel by floor-area addition alone kept non-residential nonconformities under (C)(3) (1040 Resolution 2026-0844) (record). Measure it early with the October 2026 guide.
8. **Building items asked at entitlement.** Occupancy separation (CBC 508), CBC 11A adaptable units, egress and accessibility (1391, 670, 2940) (record).
9. **Old approvals carry forward.** Prior conditions of approval and use limits travel with the site (1391, Res. 20.069 Cond. 50) (record). Pull the property's resolution history before filing.
10. **Traffic study when use intensifies** on a larger project (1391) (record). Not seen on a small conversion.
11. **Mis-citations to expect.** City documents cite "25.95.030(C)(3)" for §25.96.030(C)(3) (670 letter; the 1040 2026 resolution per the Tyler record summary) (record).

## Using this file

- Present these as "what the City has done," with the record id. Do not present a pattern as a rule.
- Do not predict an outcome for an interior conversion from these cases. None of them was one.
- Where a case and the code differ (the 2023 reading of §25.52.006(H)), cite the current code and note the case.
