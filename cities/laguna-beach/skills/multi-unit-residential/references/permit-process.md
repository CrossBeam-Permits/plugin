## Permit Process — Plan Review, Streamlining, CEQA, Inspections, and Certificate of Occupancy

### Overview of the Multi-Unit Residential Permitting Path

Multi-unit residential projects (3+ dwelling units, R-2 occupancy) follow a fundamentally different permitting path than single-family or ADU projects. Unlike ADUs, which are ministerially approved by state mandate, multi-unit projects typically require both **planning entitlements** (discretionary or ministerial depending on zoning compliance and streamlining eligibility) and **building permits** (ministerial plan check against the CBC/CRC). The process involves multiple agencies, phased reviews, and correction cycles that can span months to over a year depending on project complexity and streamlining eligibility.

### Standard Plan Review Process

#### Completeness Review (Gov. Code Section 65943)

When an applicant submits a development application for a multi-unit residential project, the lead agency must determine whether the application is **complete** within a statutory timeframe:

- **Planning applications (development permits):** The agency must determine completeness within **30 calendar days** of receipt. (Gov. Code, Section 65943, subd. (a).)
- **Building permit applications:** The Permit Streamlining Act establishes a **15 business day** completeness review period for building permit applications submitted to the building department. (Gov. Code, Section 65943.)

If the agency fails to provide written notice that the application is incomplete within the applicable period, the application is **deemed complete** by operation of law. (Gov. Code, Section 65943, subd. (b).) This is a critical protection for applicants — the clock on the action deadline starts running from the date of completeness (or deemed completeness).

If the application is determined incomplete, the agency must provide a **written notice specifying the information needed** to make the application complete. (Gov. Code, Section 65943, subd. (a).) Upon resubmission, the agency has the same statutory period to review the supplemental information for completeness. However, the agency may only request information it failed to identify in the initial completeness notice if new information submitted by the applicant raises new issues.

#### Action Deadline (Gov. Code Section 65950)

Once the application is deemed complete, the Permit Streamlining Act imposes a deadline on the lead agency to approve, conditionally approve, or deny the project:

| Approval Type | Deadline from Completeness | Citation |
|---------------|---------------------------|----------|
| **Ministerial permits** (building permits, streamlined approvals) | **60 days** | Gov. Code Section 65950, subd. (a) |
| **Discretionary permits** requiring only initial study or negative declaration | **180 days** | Gov. Code Section 65950, subd. (a) |
| **Discretionary permits** requiring EIR | **180 days** from certification of the EIR, or **1 year** from certification if the project involves a General Plan amendment | Gov. Code Section 65950, subds. (a), (b) |

These deadlines may be extended once for **90 days** by mutual written consent of the applicant and the lead agency. (Gov. Code, Section 65957.)

#### Deemed Approved (Gov. Code Section 65956)

If the lead agency fails to act within the deadline established by Section 65950, the project is **deemed approved** — provided the project is consistent with applicable general and specific plans, zoning, and the lead agency's written standards. (Gov. Code, Section 65956, subd. (a).)

However, for discretionary projects requiring CEQA review, the deemed-approved provision does not apply until the CEQA process (negative declaration adoption or EIR certification) is complete. (Gov. Code, Section 65950, subd. (a).)

### SB 35 / SB 423 Streamlined Ministerial Approval (Gov. Code Section 65913.4)

SB 35 (2017) created a streamlined ministerial approval pathway for qualifying multifamily housing developments. SB 423 (2023) extended and expanded this framework effective January 1, 2024, and made it permanent.

#### Qualifying Criteria

A multifamily housing development is eligible for streamlined ministerial approval under Section 65913.4 if **all** of the following are met:

1. **Zoned residential or mixed-use:** The site is zoned for residential use or residential mixed-use development, or has a general plan designation that allows residential use or a mix of residential and nonresidential uses. (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (a)(2).)

2. **Objective standards compliance:** The development is consistent with **objective zoning, subdivision, and design review standards** in effect at the time the application is submitted. Objective standards are those that involve no personal or subjective judgment by a public official and are verifiable by reference to an external and uniform benchmark. (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (a)(5).)

3. **Affordability thresholds:**
   - In a jurisdiction that has **not met its above-moderate-income RHNA:** At least **10% of units** must be affordable to households at or below 80% of area median income (low income). (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (a)(4)(B)(i).)
   - In a jurisdiction that has not met its below-moderate-income RHNA but has met its above-moderate: At least **50% of units** must be affordable, with at least 15% for lower-income households. (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (a)(4)(B)(ii).)

4. **Labor requirements:** For projects with 50 or more units, the development must use a skilled and trained workforce or pay prevailing wages in accordance with Section 65913.4, subdivisions (a)(8) and (a)(9).

5. **Site eligibility exclusions — the site must NOT be:**
   - Within a coastal zone (unless fully urbanized)
   - Prime farmland or farmland of statewide importance
   - Wetlands, very high fire hazard severity zone (unless mitigated), FEMA special flood hazard area
   - On a site with existing affordable housing, rent-controlled units, or housing occupied within the past 10 years
   - On a site listed on the Cortese hazardous waste list
   (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (a)(6), (7).)

#### What Streamlining Eliminates

When a project qualifies under Section 65913.4, the local agency **shall not**:
- Require a conditional use permit, planned unit development permit, or other discretionary local government review or approval (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (c))
- Impose subjective design review standards (objective standards only)
- Require CEQA review — the project is **exempt** from the California Environmental Quality Act (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (d))
- Conduct public hearings or require neighborhood notice as a condition of approval

The local agency retains authority to apply **objective** standards and to conduct building code plan review.

#### Approval Timeline Under SB 35/SB 423

| Project Size | Approval Deadline | Citation |
|-------------|-------------------|----------|
| **150 units or fewer** | **90 days** from submittal of complete application | Gov. Code Section 65913.4, subd. (c)(1) |
| **More than 150 units** | **180 days** from submittal of complete application | Gov. Code Section 65913.4, subd. (c)(2) |

If the local agency fails to approve or deny the application within the applicable deadline, the project is **deemed approved**. (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (c).)

**Design review**, if conducted, must be completed within the same 90/180-day period and must be limited to objective standards only. (Gov. Code, Section 65913.4, subd. (e).)

### CEQA — California Environmental Quality Act

Unless a project qualifies for SB 35/SB 423 streamlining, most multi-unit residential developments with a discretionary approval component require CEQA review.

#### Categorical Exemptions (CEQA Guidelines Section 15300 et seq.)

Small-scale multi-unit residential projects may qualify for a **categorical exemption**:

- **Class 3 (Section 15303) — New Construction:** Construction of up to **6 dwelling units** in an urbanized area or up to **3 dwelling units** in a non-urbanized area, where utilities and public services are available, on a site of no more than 2 acres surrounded by urban uses. (CEQA Guidelines, Section 15303, subd. (b).)

- **Class 32 (Section 15332) — Infill Development:** Projects that are consistent with the general plan and zoning, on a site of no more than **5 acres** that is substantially surrounded by urban uses, with no value as habitat, adequately served by utilities, and that would not result in significant traffic, noise, air quality, or water quality effects. (CEQA Guidelines, Section 15332.)

Categorical exemptions do **not** apply if there is a reasonable possibility that the project will have a significant effect on the environment due to unusual circumstances. (CEQA Guidelines, Section 15300.2, subd. (c).)

#### Infill Exemption (Pub. Resources Code Section 21094.5)

Residential projects located on infill sites within urbanized areas may qualify for a **streamlined CEQA review** under the infill exemption if:
- The project is consistent with a specific plan, community plan, or zoning for which an EIR was certified
- The project site has been previously developed or is surrounded by qualified urban uses
- The project satisfies the applicable performance standards for traffic, air quality, water, and noise
(Pub. Resources Code, Section 21094.5.)

#### Sustainable Communities Strategy Exemption (Pub. Resources Code Section 21159.24)

A residential or mixed-use project qualifies for CEQA exemption if it is:
- Located within a transit priority area (within one-half mile of a major transit stop or high-quality transit corridor)
- Consistent with the applicable sustainable communities strategy or alternative planning strategy
- On an infill site
(Pub. Resources Code, Section 21159.24.)

#### When a Full EIR Is Required

A full Environmental Impact Report is required when there is **substantial evidence** that the project may have a **significant effect** on the environment and no exemption or negative declaration is applicable. (Pub. Resources Code, Section 21080, subd. (d).) For large multi-unit residential projects, typical triggers include:

- **Traffic/transportation** — significant increases in vehicle miles traveled
- **Air quality** — construction or operational emissions exceeding regional thresholds
- **Noise** — project-generated noise exceeding local noise standards
- **Biological resources** — presence of special-status species or sensitive habitats
- **Cultural resources** — historic resources on or adjacent to the site
- **Hazardous materials** — contaminated soil or groundwater requiring remediation

A Draft EIR must be circulated for public comment for a minimum of **30 days** (or **45 days** when submitted to the State Clearinghouse). (CEQA Guidelines, Section 15105.)

### Phased Plan Review

For large multi-unit residential projects, California allows **phased (deferred) plan review** so that site work and foundation construction can begin while the superstructure plans are still under review.

#### Foundations-First Permit (CBC 107.3.4)

A separate permit may be issued for **grading, excavation, and foundation work** prior to approval of the complete building plans, provided:
- A soils report (geotechnical investigation) has been approved
- Foundation plans are reviewed and approved independently
- The applicant acknowledges that the foundation permit does not guarantee approval of the superstructure plans and assumes risk for any changes required

This is standard practice on multi-unit projects where the foundation design is typically independent of the superstructure framing above the podium level.

#### Superstructure Phases

On large projects, the building department may accept plan review in sequential phases:
1. **Phase 1 — Site/civil and foundations:** Grading plan, utility layout, foundation design
2. **Phase 2 — Structural superstructure:** Gravity and lateral framing, shear walls, diaphragms
3. **Phase 3 — Building envelope and life safety:** Fire-rated assemblies, egress, sprinklers, alarms
4. **Phase 4 — MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing):** HVAC, electrical distribution, plumbing risers
5. **Phase 5 — Interior finish and accessibility:** Unit layouts, accessibility compliance, finish details

Each phase must be approved before the corresponding construction work begins. (CBC 107.3.4; local jurisdiction policies.)

### Multi-Agency Coordination

Multi-unit residential projects require concurrent or sequential review by multiple agencies:

| Agency | Review Scope | Typical Timeline |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Planning Department** | Zoning compliance, general plan consistency, parking, density, setbacks, design review (if applicable) | 2-12 weeks (ministerial) or 3-12 months (discretionary with entitlements) |
| **Building Department** | CBC/CRC code compliance: structural, fire/life safety, energy, accessibility, MEP | 4-8 weeks per plan check cycle |
| **Fire Department / Fire Authority** | Fire access roads, hydrant spacing, sprinkler system design, fire alarm, Knox box, FDC location | 2-6 weeks (concurrent with building) |
| **Public Works / Engineering** | Grading, drainage, storm water (SWPPP/WQMP), curb/gutter, sidewalk, street improvements, sewer capacity | 4-8 weeks |
| **Water / Sewer Utility** | Water meter sizing, backflow prevention, sewer lateral capacity, will-serve letter | 2-6 weeks |
| **Electrical Utility** | Transformer sizing and location, service entrance, panel-to-meter clearances | 2-4 weeks |
| **School District** | School impact fee calculation (where applicable) | 1-2 weeks |

Most jurisdictions use a **concurrent review** model where plans are distributed simultaneously to all reviewing agencies after the building department deems the initial submittal substantially complete. Some jurisdictions require **sequential review** (planning approval before building submittal).

### Corrections Process

#### Plan Check Comments

After each reviewing agency completes its review, the applicant receives a **plan check correction letter** itemizing code deficiencies. Common correction categories for multi-unit residential projects include:

- **Structural:** Lateral force resisting system deficiencies, connection details, diaphragm nailing
- **Fire/life safety:** Fire-resistance ratings not shown or incorrect, missing hourly details for party walls, sprinkler coverage gaps
- **Egress:** Exit width, travel distance, dead-end corridors, illuminated exit signs, exit door hardware
- **Accessibility:** Type A/B unit counts, accessible route dimensions, elevator compliance, accessible parking
- **Energy:** Title 24 compliance forms (CF-1R) not matching plans, envelope U-factor errors, HERS verification scope
- **MEP:** Electrical load calculations, panel sizing, HVAC sizing, plumbing fixture counts, venting

#### Resubmittal Cycles

The standard correction-resubmittal process works as follows:

1. **First plan check** — Initial review and issuance of correction letter (4-8 weeks typical)
2. **First resubmittal** — Applicant addresses corrections and resubmits revised plans with a written response to each comment. Building department performs second plan check (2-4 weeks typical)
3. **Subsequent resubmittals** — If corrections remain unresolved, additional cycles occur. Each cycle is shorter as the scope of remaining comments narrows (1-3 weeks typical)
4. **Approval** — When all corrections are resolved across all agencies, the plans are stamped "Approved" and the building permit is issued

Most projects require **2-3 plan check cycles** to reach permit issuance. Complex projects or projects with significant structural irregularities may require more.

Each resubmittal resets that agency's review clock but does **not** reset the Permit Streamlining Act deadlines (Gov. Code Section 65943 et seq.), which run from the date of completeness. However, agencies commonly take the position that plans returned for correction are not a "complete application" until corrections are resolved — applicants should track these deadlines carefully.

### Inspections

Once the building permit is issued and construction begins, the building department conducts inspections at required stages. For multi-unit residential projects, the inspection sequence typically follows this order:

#### Required Inspection Stages (CBC 110.3)

| Inspection | Scope | Timing |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Grading/excavation** | Subgrade preparation, compaction testing, shoring | Before foundation work |
| **Foundation** | Rebar placement, form alignment, anchor bolt layout, hold-down embedment, pre-pour | Before concrete placement |
| **Underfloor/underslab MEP** | Underground plumbing, underslab conduit, grounding electrode | Before slab pour |
| **Framing** | Structural framing, shear walls, nailing, holdowns, straps, headers, blocking, fireblocking | Before concealment |
| **Rough mechanical** | HVAC ductwork, equipment mounting, combustion air, venting, refrigerant piping | Before concealment |
| **Rough electrical** | Wiring, boxes, panel installation, grounding, arc-fault/GFCI protection | Before concealment |
| **Rough plumbing** | Supply and waste piping, venting, water testing, gas piping pressure test | Before concealment |
| **Insulation / energy** | Insulation R-values, air sealing, duct sealing, HERS verification (if required) | Before drywall |
| **Lath / drywall** | Proper installation before exterior stucco or interior finish | Before exterior coating or taping/texturing |
| **Fire-rated assemblies** | Party wall construction, horizontal assemblies, penetration firestopping, draftstopping | Before concealment |
| **Fire sprinkler** | Sprinkler system rough-in, head placement, hydraulic test | Before concealment; final at completion |
| **Fire alarm** | Panel, initiating devices, notification appliances, annunciator | Before concealment; final at completion |
| **Accessibility** | Accessible route, ramps, handrails, unit features (Type A/B), parking, signage | Final inspection stage |
| **Final building** | All work complete, all systems operational, occupancy requirements satisfied | Before Certificate of Occupancy |
| **Final fire** | Sprinkler system acceptance test, fire alarm acceptance test, Knox box, FDC | Before Certificate of Occupancy |
| **Final mechanical / plumbing / electrical** | All MEP systems complete, tested, and operational | Before Certificate of Occupancy |

Each inspection must be **requested by the permit holder** in advance (CBC 110.5). Work may not proceed to the next stage until the inspection for the current stage is approved or the inspector provides written approval to proceed.

**Failed inspections** result in a correction notice. The contractor must correct the deficiency and request re-inspection. Repeated failures may result in a stop-work order. (CBC 110.6, 115.)

### Certificate of Occupancy

#### Requirement (CBC 111)

No building or structure shall be used or occupied, and no change of occupancy shall be made, until the building official has issued a **Certificate of Occupancy** (CO). (CBC 111.1.) For multi-unit residential, the CO confirms that all building, fire, accessibility, energy, and site requirements have been met.

#### Temporary Certificate of Occupancy (CBC 111.3)

A **Temporary Certificate of Occupancy** (TCO) may be issued before completion of the entire project when the building official determines that portions of the building may be **safely occupied** prior to final completion. (CBC 111.3.)

For multi-unit projects, a TCO is commonly issued when:
- Some buildings or phases are fully complete while others are still under construction
- All life-safety systems (fire sprinklers, fire alarms, egress) are operational in the occupied portion
- Remaining incomplete items do not affect the health or safety of occupants (e.g., landscaping, common area amenities)

TCOs are issued for a **limited duration** (typically 90-180 days depending on the jurisdiction) and may be renewed. The permit holder must complete all remaining work and obtain a final CO before the TCO expires.

#### Phased Occupancy

Large multi-unit developments are commonly constructed and occupied in phases. Each phase can receive its own CO or TCO, provided:

1. **Fire separation** between occupied and under-construction phases is maintained (fire walls, barriers)
2. **Fire access** roads and hydrants serve the occupied phase independently
3. **Egress paths** from occupied units do not pass through construction zones
4. **Utilities** (water, sewer, electrical, gas) are independently operational for the occupied phase
5. **Accessibility** requirements are met for the occupied phase, including accessible parking, accessible route, and the required Type A/B units within that phase

(CBC 111.3; local jurisdiction policies.)

#### Final Certificate of Occupancy

The final CO for the entire project is issued when:
- All phases of construction are complete
- All final inspections (building, fire, MEP, accessibility) are approved
- All conditions of approval from the planning entitlement are satisfied
- All offsite improvements (sidewalks, curb ramps, street trees) required by the conditions of approval are completed or bonded
- All utility connections are finalized and meters activated
- Record drawings ("as-builts") are submitted (if required by the jurisdiction)

(CBC 111.1, 111.2.)

### Housing Accountability Act (Gov. Code Section 65589.5)

The Housing Accountability Act (HAA) is one of the most powerful state-level protections for multi-unit residential projects. It severely limits a local agency's ability to deny or reduce the density of a housing development project that complies with applicable objective standards.

#### Core Protections

A local agency **shall not disapprove** a housing development project (including multi-unit residential) or impose a condition that renders the project infeasible at the proposed density if the project complies with **applicable, objective general plan, zoning, and subdivision standards and criteria**, including design review standards, **in effect at the time the application is deemed complete**. (Gov. Code, Section 65589.5, subd. (j)(1).)

A local agency **shall not reduce the density** of a housing development project below the density permitted by the applicable zoning unless the agency makes **written findings**, supported by a preponderance of the evidence on the record, that:
1. The project would have a **specific, adverse impact** upon the **public health or safety**, and
2. There is **no feasible method** to satisfactorily mitigate or avoid the impact other than the disapproval or density reduction.
(Gov. Code, Section 65589.5, subd. (j)(1)(A)-(B).)

"Specific, adverse impact" means a **significant, quantifiable, direct, and unavoidable impact** based on objective, identified, written public health or safety standards, policies, or conditions as they existed on the date the application was deemed complete. (Gov. Code, Section 65589.5, subd. (d)(2).)

#### Remedy for Violations

If a court finds that a local agency violated the HAA, the agency must **approve the project** within 60 days. In egregious cases, the court may impose a fine of **$10,000 per unit** in the housing development project. (Gov. Code, Section 65589.5, subd. (l).)

The Attorney General may also bring an action to enforce the HAA. (Gov. Code, Section 65589.5, subd. (m).)

### 35-Unit Test Case: Typical Timeline from Application to CO

The following timeline represents a typical 35-unit townhome or apartment project in a California city, assuming the project is consistent with zoning and does not require a variance or general plan amendment:

#### Scenario A: Standard Discretionary Approval (no SB 35)

| Phase | Duration | Cumulative |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| **Pre-application / conceptual review** | 2-4 weeks | 1 month |
| **Planning application submittal and completeness review** | 30 days (Gov. Code Section 65943) | 2 months |
| **CEQA review** (assume categorical exemption Class 32 or infill) | 4-8 weeks | 3-4 months |
| **Planning entitlement hearing and approval** | 4-8 weeks | 4-5 months |
| **Building permit submittal and first plan check** | 4-8 weeks | 6-7 months |
| **Corrections / second plan check** | 2-4 weeks | 7-8 months |
| **Permit issuance** | 1-2 weeks | 8 months |
| **Construction** | 12-18 months | 20-26 months |
| **Final inspections and CO** | 2-4 weeks | 21-27 months |
| **Total: Application to CO** | **~21-27 months** | |

#### Scenario B: SB 35 / SB 423 Streamlined Approval

| Phase | Duration | Cumulative |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| **SB 35 application submittal** | — | Day 0 |
| **Streamlined ministerial approval** | 90 days max (Gov. Code Section 65913.4(c)) | 3 months |
| **Building permit submittal and first plan check** | 4-8 weeks | 4-5 months |
| **Corrections / second plan check** | 2-4 weeks | 5-6 months |
| **Permit issuance** | 1-2 weeks | 6 months |
| **Construction** | 12-18 months | 18-24 months |
| **Final inspections and CO** | 2-4 weeks | 19-25 months |
| **Total: Application to CO** | **~19-25 months** | |

SB 35/SB 423 streamlining saves approximately **2-5 months** by eliminating the CEQA process, discretionary hearings, and reducing the entitlement review period to a fixed 90-day window. The building permit and construction phases remain the same regardless of entitlement pathway.
