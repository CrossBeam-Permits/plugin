## Egress Occupant Load — Calculating Design Occupant Load for Multi-Unit Residential

### What Occupant Load Determines

The design occupant load is the foundation of all means of egress calculations. It determines:
- **Number of exits** required from each story, floor level, or dwelling unit (CBC 1006)
- **Exit and exit access width** — total egress capacity sized to occupant load (CBC 1005)
- **Corridor width** minimums (CBC 1020)
- **Stairway width and capacity** (CBC 1011)
- **Whether occupant load posting is required** (CBC 1004.9)

(CBC 1004.1)

### The Basic Calculation

For areas without fixed seating, occupant load is calculated by dividing the floor area by the occupant load factor from Table 1004.5:

> **Occupant Load = Floor Area / Occupant Load Factor**

The occupant load shall be **not less than** the calculated number. The building official may approve the actual number of occupants if it is less than the calculated value, but only by specific approval.

(CBC 1004.5)

### Table 1004.5 — Occupant Load Factors Relevant to Multi-Unit Residential

The following factors from Table 1004.5 are most commonly applicable to multi-unit residential projects:

| Function of Space | Occupant Load Factor (sq ft per occupant) | Gross or Net |
|---|---|---|
| **Residential** | 200 | gross |
| **Dormitories** | 50 | gross |
| **Accessory storage areas, mechanical equipment room** | 300 | gross |
| **Parking garages** | 200 | gross |
| **Business areas** (leasing office, property management) | 150 | gross |
| **Assembly without fixed seats — Unconcentrated** (clubhouse with tables/chairs) | 15 | net |
| **Assembly without fixed seats — Concentrated** (chairs only, meeting room) | 7 | net |
| **Assembly without fixed seats — Standing space** (lobby/lounge standing event) | 5 | net |
| **Exercise rooms** (fitness center) | 50 | gross |
| **Locker rooms** | 50 | gross |
| **Mercantile** (ground-floor retail in mixed-use) | 60 | gross |

(CBC Table 1004.5)

**Gross vs Net distinction:**
- **Gross** floor area = measured between exterior walls; includes corridors, stairs, walls, storage, mechanical — everything within the building footprint
- **Net** floor area = actual occupied area; excludes walls, columns, fixed equipment, corridors, stairs, and other unoccupied space

### How Occupant Load Works for Multi-Unit Residential

#### Per Dwelling Unit

For individual dwelling units in R-2 or R-3, the "Residential" factor of **200 gross sq ft per occupant** applies. A 1,000 sq ft unit = 5 occupants for egress purposes. However, individual dwelling units in R-2 buildings are exempt from requiring multiple exits when they comply with CBC 1006.2.1 (dwelling unit egress requirements).

#### Per Floor / Per Story

When calculating occupant load for a **floor or story** to determine the number of exits from that floor (per CBC 1006.3), the occupant load of the entire story is used — the sum of all spaces on that floor.

**Critical rule — adjacent stories are NOT added together** for egress component sizing, except at convergence points (stairway discharge levels) per CBC 1005.6.

(CBC 1004.2.3)

#### Cumulative Loads Through Intervening Spaces

Where occupants must egress through intervening rooms or spaces, the design occupant load is the **combined** load of all interconnected spaces along the egress path. Egress path capacity must accommodate the cumulative load at each point.

(CBC 1004.2.1)

#### Mezzanines

The occupant load of a mezzanine that egresses through an adjacent level shall be **added** to the occupant load of that level.

(CBC 1004.2.2)

### Multiple Functions — Different Factors for Different Spaces

Where an area contains multiple functions with different occupant load factors, **each function is calculated independently** using its own factor. The results are summed.

Example for a multi-unit residential project:
- First floor: 4,000 sq ft residential units + 800 sq ft leasing office + 1,500 sq ft fitness center + 500 sq ft mechanical room
- Residential: 4,000 / 200 = 20 occupants
- Leasing office (Business): 800 / 150 = 6 occupants (rounded up)
- Fitness center (Exercise): 1,500 / 50 = 30 occupants
- Mechanical: 500 / 300 = 2 occupants (rounded up)
- **First floor total = 58 occupants**

(CBC 1004.3)

### Multiple Occupancies — Most Restrictive Governs Shared Egress

Where a building contains two or more occupancies (common in mixed-use residential), means of egress requirements apply to each portion based on its occupancy. Where different occupancies share portions of the same egress system (common stairs, corridors, exit discharge), the **most stringent** requirements of all served occupancies govern those shared components.

(CBC 1004.4)

### Increased Occupant Load (CBC 1004.5.1)

The occupant load may be **increased** above Table 1004.5 values — for example, a community room designed for higher-density use — provided:
- All other code requirements are met based on the increased number
- The occupant load does not exceed **one occupant per 7 sq ft** of occupiable floor space (absolute maximum)
- An approved aisle/seating/fixed equipment diagram may be required by the building official

(CBC 1004.5.1)

### Outdoor Areas (CBC 1004.7)

Yards, patios, occupiable roofs, courts, and similar outdoor areas accessible to building occupants must have means of egress. The building official assigns occupant load based on anticipated use.

**Key exception for residential:** Outdoor areas associated with **Group R-3** and **individual dwelling units of Group R-2** are exempt from the general outdoor area egress requirements.

This means private patios and balconies serving individual residential units do not add to the building's occupant load calculation for egress.

(CBC 1004.7, Exception 2)

### Posting Requirements (CBC 1004.9)

Occupant load **must be posted** in any room or space used for assembly, classroom, dining, drinking, or similar purposes with an occupant load of **50 or more**.

For multi-unit residential, this typically applies to:
- Clubhouse / community rooms
- Party rooms / event spaces
- Large fitness centers
- Common dining areas

Posted signs must be approved, legible, permanent, placed near the main exit from the space, and maintained by the owner.

Posting is NOT required for dwelling units, corridors, lobbies, or parking areas.

(CBC 1004.9)

### Occupant Load Drives Number of Exits

The occupant load directly determines the minimum number of exits required per story (CBC Table 1006.3.3):

| Occupant Load per Story | Minimum Number of Exits |
|---|---|
| 1 - 500 | 2 |
| 501 - 1,000 | 3 |
| More than 1,000 | 4 |

For R-2 buildings, the occupant load per floor determines how many exit stairways or exit discharges are required. This is the critical link between occupant load and the rest of the egress system.

See also: `egress-exits.md` for full exit number, arrangement, and separation requirements.

### For the 35-Unit Townhome Test Case

For a 35-unit townhome project (R-3, CRC, 3-story, Type V-A, separate means of egress per unit):

**Individual unit occupant load** (assuming ~1,800 sq ft average unit):
- 1,800 / 200 = **9 occupants per unit**

**Per-unit egress:** Each townhouse unit has its own separate means of egress (required for CRC applicability per CRC R101.2). The occupant load of each individual unit determines its own egress requirements — typically two means of egress from each unit (primary door + emergency escape/rescue opening per CRC R319).

**Site-wide occupant load** (for fire access, parking, and CBC site requirements):
- 35 units x ~1,800 sq ft = 63,000 sq ft total residential
- 63,000 / 200 = **315 total occupants**
- With 315 occupants, if this were a single building under CBC, it would require **2 exits** per story. However, since each townhouse unit is an independent structure under the CRC with separate means of egress, the per-building exit count is governed by the individual unit, not the project total.

**Common amenity spaces** (if provided — clubhouse, pool area, etc.) are calculated separately using their applicable occupant load factors and may require independent egress analysis under the CBC.

**Parking garage** (if provided as common structure):
- Calculated at 200 gross sq ft per occupant
- A 35,000 sq ft garage = 175 occupants for egress purposes
- Garage egress is calculated independently of residential units (CBC 1004.2.3)
