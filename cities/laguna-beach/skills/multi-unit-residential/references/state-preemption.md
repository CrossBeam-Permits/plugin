# State Housing Law Preemption — Laguna Beach Multi-Unit

Use when an applicant invokes any state housing law to override Laguna's local restrictions. State preemption frequently overrides Laguna's local rules — **including zone height limits, the 36 ft citywide ceiling, R-3 density limits, multi-family parking ratios, and DRB discretion** — for qualifying projects.

This reference covers the **Laguna-specific interaction** with state law. For the state law mechanics themselves, load the California Multi-Unit Residential rules (Density & Zoning — State Housing Laws for Multi-Unit Residential Projects), which come from the CrossBeam connector (see "California Code Compliance" in `../SKILL.md`) and the California Multi-Unit Residential rules (Permit Process — Plan Review, Streamlining, CEQA, Inspections, and Certificate of Occupancy), which come from the CrossBeam connector (see "California Code Compliance" in `../SKILL.md`).

## Laguna-Specific Notes

### Height Limits Are Override-able

Zone height limits (R-3 30-35 ft by slope, §25.14.008(D); C-N 30 ft and two floors, §25.19.008(E)(2); DSP 12 ft and 1 floor) and the §25.51.010 36 ft citywide ceiling are development standards that can be **waived** under Density Bonus Law (Gov. Code §65915(e), (k)) when the city cannot make a feasibility-justified denial finding. This is the most common state-preempted override in Laguna for ground-up multi-unit. Common pattern:

1. Applicant designs a qualifying affordable project under Ch. 25.96.
2. Requests density bonus + concessions + waiver of the zone height limit (and of the 36 ft ceiling if the design exceeds it).
3. City must either grant the waiver or articulate a specific, feasibility-justified denial finding (e.g., physical/environmental impact that cannot be mitigated, specific public health/safety risk).
4. Laguna's view-corridor concern alone is **not** a sufficient basis to deny a §65915 waiver — must be tied to a specific physical / environmental / health-safety finding.

### RHNA Lag = SB 35/423 Eligibility

Laguna has historically lagged on RHNA progress. The city's 6th-cycle Housing Element (2021-2029, adopted Feb 2022) relies on rezones of church / hospital surface lots + one active condo expansion (Pyne Castle, 770 Hillcrest Dr) to claim it can meet the 394-unit RHNA. Real construction trails the plan. **Confirm current SB 35 / SB 423 eligibility status with HCD** before treating Laguna as ineligible — applicants invoking SB 35/423 should not be turned away on city RHNA grounds without an HCD letter to support that.

### SB 423 Narrowed the Coastal Zone Exemption

SB 35's original Coastal Zone exemption was a routine reason Laguna disqualified streamlined projects. SB 423 (eff. Jan 1, 2024) narrowed that exemption — **some Coastal Zone parcels in Laguna are now SB 35/423-eligible**. Do not auto-disqualify a project for being in the Coastal Zone without running the SB 423 eligibility test.

### SB 4: housing on faith and college land (Gov. Code §65913.16)

Use for any housing proposal on land owned by a church or other religious institution, or by an
independent (nonprofit) college, whether or not the applicant says "SB 4". Run the test in order.

1. **Eligibility (65913.16(c)); all must hold.**
   - Land owned on or before Jan 1, 2024 by a religious institution or an independent institution
     of higher education, including through an affiliated nonprofit ((c)(1)). The applicant must be
     a "qualified developer" ((b)(9)).
   - Infill: a legal parcel in an urbanized area, with at least 75% of the site perimeter adjoining
     urban uses ((c)(2), citing 65913.4(a)(2)(A)-(B)).
   - Site exclusions of 65913.4(a)(6)(B)-(K) ((c)(3)): farmland, wetlands, a very high fire hazard
     severity zone unless the site adopts the state fire mitigation standards (PRC 4290-4291 or Gov.
     Code 51182, CBC Ch. 7A), a listed hazardous waste site, an earthquake fault zone unless built
     to the seismic standards, a FEMA flood zone or floodway (with the stated cures), conservation
     plan lands, protected-species habitat and conservation easements. Subparagraph (A), the
     coastal zone exclusion, is left out on purpose: being in Laguna's coastal zone does not
     disqualify a site.
   - No demolition of rent-restricted, rent-controlled or tenant-occupied (past 10 years) housing,
     no site where tenant housing was demolished in the past 10 years, and no demolition of a
     historic structure on a national, state or local register ((c)(4), citing 65913.4(a)(7)). In
     Laguna, check the City historic register before any building on the site comes down.
   - Industrial and oil buffers ((c)(5)-(8)); 100% of units for lower-income households, except up
     to 20% moderate-income and 5% staff, at affordable cost for 55 years (rental) or 45 years
     (owner) ((c)(9)); every objective City standard not in conflict with the section ((c)(10));
     replacement under Gov. Code 66300(d) when units are demolished ((c)(11)); prevailing wage
     certification ((c)(12)); and the remaining (c) items.
2. **By-right objective review.** A qualifying project is a "use by right": no CUP or other
   discretionary local review, and not a CEQA project ((b)(12), (c)). The City must send written
   notice of any conflict with an objective standard within 60 days of submittal (150 or fewer
   units) or 90 days (more than 150); with no timely notice the project is deemed consistent
   ((l)(1)-(2)). Design review, if any, is objective only and may be held by the Planning
   Commission or an equivalent body; it must finish within 90 or 180 days by the same unit split
   ((l)(5)). Approval life, changes and later permits follow 65913.4(f), (g), (h) ((l)(8), (9),
   (11)). The City may not add fees or inclusionary rules because a project uses SB 4 ((l)(10)).
3. **Density and height (j).** In a zone allowing housing, the Housing Element default density
   for lower-income housing (Gov. Code 65583.2(c)(3)(B)) or the City's higher figure; in a zone
   that does not allow housing, 40 units per acre. Height is one story or 11 ft above the height
   otherwise applicable to the parcel. Density bonus concessions and waivers stay available
   ((j)(1)(C), (j)(2)(C)); in a nonresidential zone they cannot push height past the (j)(2)(B)
   limit. The religious use and any existing permitted use stay on the site by right within their
   existing floor area and conditions ((i)); ground-floor childcare, community and (outside
   single-family zones) by-right commercial uses are allowed ((h)).
4. **Parking cap (k).** The whole development, the religious use included, provides at most one
   space per unit; none may be required within one-half mile walking distance of a high-quality
   transit corridor or major transit stop, or within one block of a car share vehicle.
5. **Church parking (AB 2244, Gov. Code §65913.6).** This also applies outside SB 4, to any housing
   project that qualifies for a density bonus on land wholly owned by a religious institution and
   on, beside, or within 0.1 mile of a parcel holding the religious use's required parking
   ((a)(5)). The City may not require replacement of up to 50% of those religious-use spaces
   ((b)(1)-(2)), may not make the project cure an existing parking deficit ((c)), must count the
   remaining spaces toward the housing's own parking ((d)), and may not deny the project only
   because church parking drops by 50% or less ((e)). Under this section alone the City may still
   require up to one space per unit, except near transit or a car share ((f)); accessible and EV
   space requirements stay ((g)). On an SB 4 project, the (k) cap above also applies.
6. **A CDP is still required.** Section 65913.16 contains no Coastal Act exemption, so the
   project needs a CDP under Ch. 25.07 (25.07.004) unless a 25.07.008 exemption applies; new
   multi-unit housing is not an improvement to an existing structure. **Who decides follows
   25.07.012(D):** the CDP goes to the same body that approves the project's other City permits
   (for example the body holding the objective design review, or a parcel map), processed
   together; when the CDP is the only City permit, the Director decides it at a public hearing
   ((D)(1)). Appealable-area rules and Coastal Commission appeal apply as for any CDP. Lean (City
   practice): the City has sent an SB 4 project's CDP to the Planning Commission as a
   discretionary CDP with findings under 25.07.012(F)-(G), alongside its map; expect the Planning
   Commission when the project also needs a map or Commission design review.

The section sunsets Jan 1, 2036 ((p)). Confirm the current text before relying on a detail; SB 597
(2025) amended it.

### Ch. 25.96 Housing Programs and Incentives (verbatim from ecode360.com/46473710, scraped 2026-05-12)

Ch. 25.96 is titled "Housing Programs and Incentives" and contains six substantive sections plus the local inclusionary requirement. Raw text at the City source document (not included in this plugin).

| Section | Topic | Key parameters |
|---|---|---|
| §25.96.010 | Density bonuses | Pure deferral to state Gov. Code §65915. Coastal Act consistency clause; LCP-deviation findings required in Coastal Zone. |
| §25.96.020 | **Lot consolidation incentives** | Available for 100% affordable OR 100% senior projects (≥55 years) on R-3 / LBP / C-N / C-1 / CH-M / SLV / I parcels, up to 25,000 sf consolidated. Incentives: 5–10 ft setback reduction (10 ft if ≥25% VLI/ELI units), parking reduced to 0.5/unit, 50–70% open-space reduction, full refund of building + planning fees on final inspection. |
| §25.96.030 | Infill / mixed-use exceptions | C-N, C-1 and SLV only. Three exceptions, each requiring a recorded covenant against short-term lodging: (C)(1) added-setback waiver, (C)(2) nonconforming open space, (C)(3) floor-area-only major remodel need not cure nonresidential nonconformities. Full rules: `mixed-use-conversion.md` Section 7. |
| §25.96.040–.060 | Home occupations, SROs, transitional/supportive housing | Standard state-law alignment. |
| **§25.96.070** | **Citywide Inclusionary Housing Program** | See requirements below. |

### §25.96.070 Inclusionary Housing Requirements (the substantive local IZ requirement)

| Parameter | Requirement | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| **Applicability threshold** | **10 or more residential units or lots** — applies to new development, conversion, adaptive reuse, redevelopment, AND subdivision | §25.96.070(C) |
| **For-sale projects** | **15% of total units** affordable to moderate-income households | §25.96.070(E)(1)(a) |
| **Rental projects** | **15% of total units** to moderate-income households, OR **10% of total units** restricted to very-low or low-income households | §25.96.070(E)(1)(b) |
| **Affordability cost cap** | Inclusionary housing cost limited to **30% of household income** | §25.96.070 |
| **Minimum on-site** | At least **1 inclusionary unit** required even when math yields a fraction; fractions above the first whole unit may be satisfied with a fractional in-lieu fee (e.g., a 1.3-unit requirement = 1 on-site + 0.3 fee) | §25.96.070(E)(2) |
| **Income definition (moderate, rental)** | For rental inclusionary units on the same site as market-rate rentals, "moderate income" means ≤80% of area median income (stricter than Cal HSC §50093(b)) | §25.96.070 (definitions) |
| **Affordable housing covenants** | Continued affordability required for the life of the deed restriction (typically 35+ years for lot-consolidation projects per §25.96.020(B)(2); §25.96.070 covenant duration set in affordable housing agreement) | §25.96.070 |
| **In-lieu fee** | Available for fractional units and (subject to Director approval) for whole-project alternatives. Set by Council resolution, deposited in Housing In-Lieu Fund | §25.96.070 |
| **Adjustment / waiver** | Applicant may request via substantial-evidence showing; decided by the planning-permit decision-making body | §25.96.070(L) |
| **Exemption** | Planning permit applications formally submitted and accepted before January 1, 2024 are exempt; if the permit expires or is revoked, requirements apply on resubmittal. Other exemptions: development agreements, adaptive reuse projects filed within two years of the ordinance's effective date | §25.96.070(D)(2)-(4) |

**Density bonus interaction:** A project providing 15% moderate (for-sale or rental) OR 10% very-low/low (rental) at the §25.96.070 baseline can stack a state Density Bonus on top — the inclusionary baseline is a floor, not a ceiling, and density bonus law's §65915(d) "additional bonus for additional affordability" mechanic still applies.

**Coastal Zone interaction:** §25.96.020(A)(3) makes the lot-consolidation incentives a matter of right EXCEPT when a CDP is required under Ch. 25.07 — in the Coastal Zone, the incentives still apply but are subject to CDP findings.

### AB 2011 Commercial-Corridor Pathway

AB 2011 applies to commercial-zoned parcels in Laguna. The DSP commercial frontage along the Coast Hwy / downtown corridor and the Coast Hwy commercial run identified in the Housing Element (305-397 N Coast Hwy) are likely AB 2011 candidates. AB 2011 mandates ministerial approval — **no DRB hearing, no Planning Commission, no CDP discretion beyond Coastal Act findings**.

### SB 9 Doesn't Directly Hit R-2/R-3

SB 9 is a single-family-residential (R-1) preemption pathway. It can produce duplex / 4-plex outcomes by combining lot splits + duplexes, but it does not directly override R-2 / R-3 zoning. If an SB 9 project is presented as "multi-unit," classify the underlying parcel zoning first — if R-1, route through `../../single-family-residential/SKILL.md` skill + Ch. 25.95. If R-2/R-3, SB 9 does not apply.

## Common FAIL / FLAG Patterns

| Pattern | Severity | Citation |
|---------|----------|----------|
| City staff applies a zone height limit or the 36 ft ceiling to deny a qualifying density bonus project without feasibility-justified finding | **FAIL** | Gov. Code §65915(e), (k); Ch. 25.96 |
| Project disqualified from SB 35/423 solely for being in Coastal Zone (no SB 423 eligibility test run) | **FAIL** | Gov. Code §65913.4 as amended by SB 423 |
| SB 4 site disqualified for being in the coastal zone, or sent to a CUP or discretionary design review | FLAG | Gov. Code §65913.16(b)(12), (c)(3), (l)(5) |
| SB 4 project treated as needing no CDP | FLAG | LBMC 25.07.004, 25.07.012(D); §65913.16 has no Coastal Act exemption |
| Church-site housing required to replace up to 50% of religious-use parking or cure an existing deficit | FLAG | Gov. Code §65913.6(b)-(c) |
| AB 2011 project routed to DRB / Planning Commission | **FAIL** | Gov. Code §65912.100-139 — ministerial only |
| DRB applies subjective design findings to deny a density bonus project | **FAIL** | Gov. Code §65915 + §65589.5 (HAA) — only objective standards apply |
| Density Bonus parking treated as base ratio (not §65915(p)) | **FAIL** | Gov. Code §65915(p) — 0.5/studio, 1/1BR, 1.5/2-3BR, 2.5/4+BR |
| Inclusionary fee disputed without checking current Council resolution | FLAG | Ch. 25.96.070 — fee set annually |
| SB 9 invoked on R-2/R-3 parcel | **FAIL** | SB 9 / Ch. 25.95 is R-1 only |
