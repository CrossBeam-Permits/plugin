## Structural Foundation Requirements — Geotechnical Investigations, Shallow Foundations, Prescriptive Footings, Anchorage, and Slab-on-Grade

### When a Geotechnical Investigation Is Required (CBC 1803)

A geotechnical investigation must be conducted where any of the following conditions apply. For multi-unit residential, multiple triggers typically overlap.

**Mandatory triggers (CBC 1803.5):**
- **Seismic Design Categories C through F** (CBC 1803.5.11) — a geotechnical investigation shall be conducted, including evaluation of slope instability, liquefaction, total and differential settlement, and surface displacement due to faulting or lateral spreading. Virtually all California sites fall into SDC C or higher, making this the primary trigger for multi-unit projects.
- **Seismic Design Categories D through F** (CBC 1803.5.12) — investigation shall additionally include dynamic seismic lateral earth pressures on foundation walls, liquefaction potential and consequences (differential settlement, lateral soil movement, reduction in bearing capacity, soil downdrag), and discussion of mitigation measures (foundation type/depth selection, structural systems, ground stabilization).
- **Expansive soils** (CBC 1803.5.3) — in areas likely to have expansive soil, the building official shall require soil tests. Soil is considered expansive if the expansion index exceeds 20 (ASTM D4829).
- **Groundwater** (CBC 1803.5.4) — investigation required where groundwater is above or within 5 feet below the lowest floor level when that floor is below adjacent finished grade.
- **Compacted fill** (CBC 1803.5.8) — where shallow foundations bear on compacted fill more than 12 inches deep, a geotechnical investigation is required covering site prep specifications, fill material specs, compaction testing methods, lift thickness, and in-place density testing.

**Discretionary triggers:**
- **Questionable soil** (CBC 1803.5.2) — the building official may require an investigation where soil classification, strength, moisture sensitivity, or compressibility is in doubt, or where a bearing value higher than Table 1806.2 is claimed.

**Building official waiver** (CBC 1803.2, Exception): The building official may waive the geotechnical investigation requirement where satisfactory data from adjacent areas demonstrates an investigation is not necessary for conditions in Sections 1803.5.1 through 1803.5.6, 1803.5.10, and 1803.5.11.

**California-specific (HCD 1)** (CBC 1803.1.1): Each city/county must require a preliminary soil report by a registered civil engineer for every subdivision where a tentative and final map is required. If that report identifies critically expansive soils or other soil problems, a per-lot soil investigation is required with corrective recommendations.

**Reporting requirements** (CBC 1803.6): The geotechnical report must include a plot of investigation locations, boring/penetration logs, soil profile, water table elevation, recommendations for foundation type and design criteria (bearing capacity, expansive soil mitigation, liquefaction mitigation, differential settlement), expected total and differential settlement, and compacted fill specifications where applicable.

### Presumptive Bearing Capacity (CBC 1806.2, Table 1806.2)

Where a geotechnical investigation establishes site-specific values, those govern. Where presumptive values from Table 1806.2 are used, the following maximums apply:

| Soil/Rock Class | Vertical Foundation Pressure (psf) | Lateral Bearing (psf/ft below grade) | Lateral Sliding Coefficient or Cohesion |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Crystalline bedrock** | 12,000 | 1,200 | 0.70 (friction) |
| **Sedimentary/foliated rock** | 4,000 | 400 | 0.35 (friction) |
| **Sandy gravel and gravel** (GW, GP) | 3,000 | 200 | 0.35 (friction) |
| **Sand, silty sand, clayey sand, silty gravel, clayey gravel** (SW, SP, SM, SC, GM, GC) | 2,000 | 150 | 0.25 (friction) |
| **Clay, sandy clay, silty clay, clayey silt, silt, sandy silt** (CL, ML, MH, CH) | 1,500 | 100 | 130 psf (cohesion) |

(CBC Table 1806.2)

**Key rules:**
- These values shall not be exceeded unless substantiated by data approved by the building official (CBC 1806.2)
- Mud, organic silt/clay (OL, OH), peat (Pt), and undocumented fill have **no presumptive bearing capacity** (CBC 1806.2)
- Lateral bearing pressure increases by the tabular value for each additional foot of depth, up to 15 times the tabular value (CBC 1806.3.3)
- For clay soils, lateral sliding resistance shall not exceed one-half the dead load (CBC 1806.3.2)

**Practical impact for multi-unit:** A 3-story wood-frame apartment building on common Southern California clay soils (1,500 psf presumptive) will typically require wider footings than on sandy gravel (3,000 psf). The geotechnical report almost always provides site-specific values that supersede the table.

### Shallow Foundation Design (CBC 1809)

Shallow foundations must be designed and constructed per Sections 1809.2 through 1809.13 (CBC 1809.1).

**Supporting soils** (CBC 1809.2): Shallow foundations shall bear on undisturbed soil, compacted fill, or controlled low-strength material (CLSM). Compacted fill must be placed per Section 1804.6.

**Minimum depth and width** (CBC 1809.4): Minimum footing depth below undisturbed ground surface is **12 inches**. Minimum footing width is **12 inches**. Frost protection requirements of Section 1809.5 also apply where applicable.

**Stepped footings** (CBC 1809.3): Footing top surfaces shall be level. Bottom surfaces may slope up to 10%. Where grade changes exceed 10%, footings must be stepped. Individual steps shall not exceed **18 inches** in height, and the slope of a series of steps shall not exceed 1:2 (50%) unless otherwise recommended by a geotechnical report.

**Location of footings** (CBC 1809.6): Adjacent footings on granular soil must be positioned so that the line between lower edges does not slope steeper than **30 degrees** from horizontal, unless the higher footing's supporting material is laterally braced or retained.

### Table 1809.7 — Prescriptive Footings for Light-Frame Construction

Where a specific engineered design is not provided, concrete or masonry-unit footings supporting walls of light-frame construction may be sized per the following table:

| Number of Floors Supported by Footing | Minimum Footing Width (inches) | Minimum Footing Thickness (inches) |
|----------------------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **1 floor** (+ roof) | 12 | 6 |
| **2 floors** (+ roof) | 15 | 6 |
| **3 floors** (+ roof) | 18 | 8 |

(CBC Table 1809.7)

**Critical footnotes:**
- **Footnote a:** Depth of footings shall comply with Section 1809.4 (minimum 12 inches below undisturbed ground)
- **Footnote c:** Interior stud-bearing walls may be supported by isolated footings with width and length = **twice** the table width, spaced not more than 6 feet on center
- **Footnote d:** See Section 1905 for additional requirements in Seismic Design Categories C, D, E, or F
- **Footnote f:** Footings supporting a roof in addition to the stipulated floors are permitted; footings supporting roof only are sized as for one floor
- **Footnote g:** Plain concrete footings for **Group R-3** occupancies are permitted to be **6 inches** thick (even at 3 stories). This does NOT apply to R-2 apartment buildings.

**Applicability warning:** Table 1809.7 is prescriptive and applies only where a specific engineered design is not provided. For multi-unit residential buildings in SDC C through F, Section 1808.8.6 directs to Section 1905 for additional seismic requirements, and in SDC C-F the prescriptive concrete foundation wall tables (1807.1.6.2) cannot be used (CBC 1807.1.6.2.1, item 2). In practice, 3-story multi-unit buildings in California nearly always require an **engineered foundation design** that supersedes these prescriptive minimums.

### Continuous Footings for Bearing and Party Walls

Bearing walls (including party walls between dwelling units) must be supported on continuous foundations. Key requirements:

- **All load-bearing walls** must be placed on **continuous concrete footings** bonded integrally with exterior wall footings (CBC 1809.10, item 1). While 1809.10 applies specifically to pier and curtain wall foundations, the principle of continuous footings under bearing walls is a baseline requirement for conventional construction.
- **Party walls** in multi-unit residential serve as both fire separations and bearing walls. They require continuous footings running the full length of the wall. The footing width must account for the combined loading from both sides of the party wall.
- **Pier and curtain wall foundations** are limited to buildings not more than **two stories** above grade plane (in light-frame construction) and are **not permitted** in SDC D, E, or F (CBC 1809.10). This eliminates them for most California multi-unit projects.

**Footing seismic ties** (CBC 1809.13): Where a structure is assigned to SDC D, E, or F and individual spread footings are on Site Class E or F soils, footings shall be interconnected by ties capable of carrying (in tension or compression) a force equal to the lesser of:
- The larger footing design gravity load times SDS divided by 10, or
- 25% of the smaller footing design gravity load

Exception: Equivalent restraint provided by reinforced concrete beams within slabs on grade or reinforced concrete slabs on grade satisfies this requirement.

### Foundation Anchorage — Anchor Bolts and Hold-Downs (CBC 2308.7.1)

Foundation plates or sills resting on concrete or masonry foundations shall be anchored with:

**Standard anchorage (all buildings):**
- Not less than **1/2-inch diameter** steel anchor bolts (or approved equivalent anchors)
- Bolts embedded not less than **7 inches** into concrete or masonry
- Bolts located in the **middle third** of the sill plate width
- Spacing: not more than **6 feet on center**
- Minimum **two bolts per sill piece**, with one bolt not more than **12 inches** and not less than **4 inches** from each end
- Properly sized nut and washer tightened on each bolt

**Enhanced anchorage for buildings over two stories (critical for 3-story multi-unit):**
- Bolts in sill plates of braced wall lines in structures **over two stories** above grade shall be spaced not more than **4 feet on center** (CBC 2308.7.1)

**Seismic Design Category D (CBC 2308.7.1.1):**
- Braced wall line sill plates: minimum **1/2-inch** anchor bolts with **steel plate washers** (not less than 0.229" x 3" x 3") between sill plate and nut
- Plate washer may have a diagonally slotted hole (up to 3/16" wider than bolt, slot up to 1-3/4" long) with a standard cut washer between plate washer and nut

**Seismic Design Category E (CBC 2308.7.1.2):**
- Braced wall line sill plates: minimum **5/8-inch** anchor bolts with same steel plate washer requirements as SDC D

**Hold-downs for lateral loads:**
- Hold-down devices at ends of braced wall panels resist overturning uplift forces from lateral (wind and seismic) loads
- For alternate braced wall panels (ABW) at the first story of two-story buildings: three anchor bolts at quarter points and tie-down device uplift capacity not less than **3,000 pounds** (CBC 2308.10.5.1)
- In engineered design (typical for 3-story multi-unit), hold-down forces are calculated by the structural engineer and specified on the plans, often significantly exceeding prescriptive minimums

### Slab-on-Grade Requirements for Multi-Unit (CBC 1808.6.2)

Where multi-unit buildings are founded on expansive soils, slab-on-ground, mat, or raft foundations shall be designed using one of the following methods:

- **WRI/CRSI** (Wire Reinforcement Institute / Concrete Reinforcing Steel Institute) for nonprestressed slabs
- **PTI DC 10.5** (Post-Tensioning Institute) for post-tensioned slabs
- **Alternative methods** that account for soil-structure interaction, deformed soil support shape, plate or stiffened plate action, and both center lift and edge lift conditions (CBC 1808.6.2)

**Alternatives to designing for expansive soils:**
- Remove expansive soil to a depth of constant moisture content (CBC 1808.6.3)
- Stabilize the active zone by chemical, dewatering, presaturation, or equivalent techniques (CBC 1808.6.4)

**For non-expansive soils:** Slab-on-grade design follows standard engineering practice per ACI 318 (referenced through CBC 1808.8) and the geotechnical report recommendations. The minimum concrete strength requirements of Table 1808.8.1 apply.

### Minimum Concrete Strength for Foundations (CBC 1808.8.1, Table 1808.8.1)

| Foundation Condition | Minimum f'c |
|---------------------|-------------|
| SDC A, B, or C | **2,500 psi** |
| Group R or U, light-frame, 2 stories or less, SDC D/E/F | **2,500 psi** |
| **All other structures in SDC D, E, or F** | **3,000 psi** |

(CBC Table 1808.8.1)

**For multi-unit residential:** A 3-story R-2 apartment building in SDC D (typical California) requires **3,000 psi** minimum. This applies to footings, grade beams, foundation walls, and slab-on-grade foundations. The geotechnical report or structural engineer may specify higher strengths.

**Additional seismic requirements** (CBC 1808.8.6): For SDC C through F, Section 1905 of ACI 318 applies. Provisions of ACI 318 Section 18.13 govern where not in conflict with Sections 1808 through 1810.

### Design for Capacity and Settlement (CBC 1808.2)

Foundations shall be designed so that:
1. The **allowable bearing capacity** of the soil is not exceeded
2. **Differential settlement** is minimized
3. Foundations in areas with **expansive soils** comply with Section 1808.6

The geotechnical report establishes the allowable bearing pressure, which may be higher or lower than the presumptive values in Table 1806.2. The structural engineer sizes all footings based on actual loading from the structure and the site-specific bearing capacity.

### Application to 35-Unit Multi-Unit Test Case

A 35-unit, 3-story wood-frame apartment building in California presents the following foundation considerations:

**Geotechnical report is almost certainly required because:**
1. Virtually all California sites are SDC C or higher (CBC 1803.5.11) — mandatory investigation
2. Most Southern California sites are SDC D or higher (CBC 1803.5.12) — expanded investigation scope including liquefaction, dynamic lateral earth pressures, and mitigation recommendations
3. Three stories with significant dead/live loads warrants site-specific bearing capacity values

**Foundation design will be engineered, not prescriptive, because:**
- SDC C through F triggers additional seismic foundation requirements (CBC 1808.8.6, 1905)
- 3-story R-2 is not eligible for the R-3 footing thickness reduction (Table 1809.7, footnote g)
- Loading from 35 units across 3 stories with party walls requires calculated footing widths

**Key requirements for the structural engineer:**
- **Continuous footings** under all bearing walls, especially party walls separating units — these carry double-sided loads and are critical for structural continuity
- **Minimum 18" wide x 8" thick** footings per Table 1809.7 prescriptive minimums for 3-story light-frame, but engineered design will likely be wider based on soil bearing capacity and actual loads
- **Minimum 3,000 psi** concrete for all foundation elements (Table 1808.8.1, SDC D)
- **Anchor bolts at 4 feet on center** (maximum) for braced wall line sill plates, with 1/2" bolts (SDC D) or 5/8" bolts (SDC E), plus steel plate washers in SDC D and E
- **Hold-down devices** at shear wall ends per the lateral analysis (engineered, not prescriptive)
- **Footing seismic ties** if individual spread footings are used on Site Class E or F soils (CBC 1809.13)
- **Slab-on-grade** designed per WRI/CRSI or PTI DC 10.5 if expansive soils are present, or per ACI 318 / geotechnical recommendations otherwise
- **Stepped footings** on sloped sites: steps not exceeding 18" in height, slope of steps not exceeding 1:2

**Common plan-check flags:**
- Missing geotechnical report or foundation design not consistent with geotech recommendations
- Anchor bolt spacing exceeding 4 feet on braced wall lines (structures over 2 stories)
- Missing plate washers in SDC D or E
- Footings under party walls not sized for combined loading from both sides
- Foundation concrete strength specified below 3,000 psi for SDC D/E/F
- Missing hold-down schedule or hold-down forces not matching the lateral analysis
