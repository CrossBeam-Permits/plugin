# Laguna Beach Downtown Specific Plan — Mixed-Use Residential

Use when a multi-unit project is inside the Downtown Specific Plan (DSP) boundary with residential over commercial (typically 2nd/3rd floor residential above ground-floor retail / restaurant / office). **DSP density rules govern, NOT Title 25 R-3.**

## When This Applies

| Scenario | Use DSP rules? |
|----------|---------------|
| New mixed-use building inside DSP boundary, 2nd/3rd floor residential | **YES** — DSP density |
| Renovation of existing DSP commercial to add upper-floor residential | **YES** — DSP density (per Phase II direction) |
| Residential-only project on a DSP-zoned parcel | Depends — confirm at counter; DSP generally encourages mixed-use, not residential-only |
| Project just outside DSP boundary | NO — Title 25 R-3 / R-2 applies |
| Project inside DSP with commercial-only scope | DSP commercial rules; not in this skill's scope |

## Why DSP Matters for Multi-Unit Volume

The Downtown Specific Plan Phase II Update (adopted 2022) explicitly **increased residential potential on 2nd/3rd floors over commercial**. This is the city's primary infill housing pathway and is a more likely permit-volume source than ground-up R-3 going forward. The city's 6th-cycle Housing Element relies on DSP upper-floor residential as a meaningful component of meeting its 394-unit RHNA.

## DSP-Specific Deltas (vs Title 25 R-3)

| Item | DSP rule | Title 25 R-3 rule |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| **Density** | Set by DSP density tables (typically higher than R-3 due to mixed-use intensification)  | 1 du / 2,000 sf net lot area |
| **Height** | DSP-specific height — typically still constrained but distinct from §25.51.010  | 36 ft citywide cap |
| **Ground-floor use** | Commercial/retail/restaurant REQUIRED at street level (mixed-use mandate) | N/A (residential-only zone) |
| **Setbacks / build-to lines** | Urban / pedestrian-oriented (often build-to-line at sidewalk) | Suburban (20 ft front yard) |
| **Parking** | DSP-specific parking standards — often reduced for mixed-use / shared parking | Ch. 25.52.012 |
| **Design Review** | DRB applies, but design criteria are calibrated to downtown urban character (not single-family neighborhood compatibility) | Standard DRB |

## State Code Application

For the residential portion of a DSP mixed-use building:

- **Occupancy classification**: R-2 (apartments/condos with common corridors) or mixed-use per CBC 508.1. State `california-multi-unit-residential` baseline applies.
- **Mixed-occupancy fire separation**: R-2 over M (mercantile) or R-2 over B (business) requires fire-resistance-rated horizontal assembly per CBC Table 508.4 (typically 1-hour or 2-hour depending on sprinkler status and construction type).
- **Sprinklers**: NFPA 13 (NOT 13R) is the typical baseline for true mixed-use because (a) any portion of the building is non-residential, or (b) the building exceeds 13R height limits when measured to roof above mixed-use ground floor. Confirm at hydraulic design with LBFD.
- **Accessibility**: CBC Ch. 11A residential + Ch. 11B for the commercial portion + accessible path of travel from public way to BOTH the commercial entrance and the residential entrance.
- **Egress**: Separate residential exits typically required (cannot discharge through commercial tenant space).

## CDP Interaction

- DSP is mostly inside the Coastal Zone — CDP required for almost all DSP projects.
- DSP projects on Coast Hwy / first-row downtown often land in the **appealable area** (between sea and first public road).
- View corridor easements and public access dedications routinely conditioned on DSP appealable-area projects.

## DRB Interaction

- DRB applies, but downtown design criteria differ from residential-neighborhood criteria.
- Street-wall continuity, ground-floor transparency (storefront glazing %), pedestrian-oriented signage, awnings, materials, and articulation are emphasized.
- View-corridor analysis still applies (the 36 ft cap may not — DSP has its own height — but downtown view corridors to the ocean are separately protected).

## State Preemption Stack Over DSP

State housing law preemption still applies on DSP parcels:

- **AB 2011** is particularly relevant: it allows ministerial mixed-income or 100% affordable housing on commercial-corridor parcels. DSP parcels often qualify.
- **SB 35 / SB 423** streamlining can apply when project meets prevailing-wage, on-site labor, and affordability prerequisites.
- **Density Bonus Law** layers on top of DSP density.

Route to `state-preemption.md` if any state law is invoked.
