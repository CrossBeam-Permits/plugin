## Density & Zoning — State Housing Laws for Multi-Unit Residential Projects

### Density Bonus Law (Gov. Code § 65915)

The State Density Bonus Law (SDBL) is the primary tool for increasing allowable density on multi-unit residential projects that include affordable units. It applies to projects of **five or more units** and overrides local density limits.

#### Base Density Bonus (as amended by AB 1287, eff. 2024)

A developer who agrees to construct affordable housing units is entitled to a density bonus of up to **50%** above the maximum allowable residential density under the applicable zoning (AB 1287 raised the former 35% base-tier cap). The bonus scales based on the percentage and affordability level of the units provided:

- **Very Low Income (VLI)** — 5% of base units triggers a 20% density bonus, scaling upward to **50%** at 15% VLI units (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (f)(1))
- **Low Income** — 10% of base units triggers a 20% density bonus, scaling upward to **50%** at 24% low-income units (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (f)(2))
- **Moderate Income (common-interest/for-sale)** — 10% of base units triggers a 5% density bonus, scaling upward to **50%** at 44% moderate-income units (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (f)(4))
- **Senior housing** — 35% density bonus for qualifying senior projects (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (f)(3))

Treat the specific percentages as illustrative of the sliding-scale structure — when the exact yield matters, compute against the current §65915(f) table and cite the controlling subdivision.

#### Rounding — Each Component Up, Separately (§ 65915(q), (f)(5))

This is quoted statute, not drafting convention, and it routinely decides whether the last unit fits:

- **§ 65915(f)(5)**: *"All density calculations resulting in fractional units shall be rounded up to the next whole number."*
- **§ 65915(q)** (added by AB 2501 (Bloom, 2016), Stats. 2016, ch. 758 — and declared by its own terms "declaratory of existing law," so the rule predates the 2016 amendment): *"Each component of any density calculation, including base density and bonus density, resulting in fractional units shall be separately rounded up to the next whole number."*
- **§ 65915(o)(6)** defines base density ("maximum allowable residential density") as the **greatest** number of units allowed under the zoning ordinance, specific plan, or GP land-use element — where those conflict, the greater controls.
- Worked example: a site yielding **10.92 base units** has a base of **11** (never 10, and never reported as "~10" — a truncated base is the classic city error that § 65915(q) forecloses). A 20% bonus is then 11 × 0.20 = 2.2 → **3 bonus units** → **14 total**. Round each component on its own; never round only the final product.

**Scope:** these round-up rules govern the density calculation for a project **invoking the State Density Bonus Law**. A project that does not invoke § 65915 is governed by the local zoning rounding rule, under which rounding a fractional zoning cap **down** may be entirely correct (many city codes do exactly that). Always state which regime you applied — a blanket "always round up" is as wrong as a blanket "always round down."

#### AB 1287 Stacked Second Bonus (§ 65915(g))

A project that **maxes out its base tier first** may stack a **second bonus**: up to **+38.75%** for providing up to 10% additional very-low-income units, or up to **+50%** for up to 15% additional moderate-income units. The stacked total can approach **~100%** above base density.

#### Enhanced Bonus for 100% Affordable Projects

Projects that are **100% affordable** (excluding manager units) qualify for an **80% density bonus** — and in qualifying transit/low-VMT areas (within ½ mile of a major transit stop, or in a very low vehicle travel area in a designated county) the agency "shall not impose any maximum controls on density" (§ 65915(f)(3)(D)(ii)–(iii), AB 2334) — **no density limit applies at all** — plus a height increase of up to three additional stories or 33 feet (§ 65915(d)(2)(D)). To qualify:

1. All units (excluding manager units) must be affordable to very low income, low income, or moderate-income households (moderate up to 20%)
2. Moderate-income units may only be included in common-interest (for-sale) developments
3. The project must meet applicable affordability covenants for a minimum of 55 years (rental) or 45 years (ownership)

(Gov. Code § 65915, subds. (b)(1)(G), (f))

#### Concessions and Incentives

In addition to the density bonus itself, qualifying projects are entitled to **concessions or incentives** that result in identifiable, financially sufficient, and actual cost reductions:

| Affordable % | Income Level | Concessions/Incentives |
|--------------|-------------|----------------------|
| 5% VLI | Very Low | 1 |
| 10% VLI | Very Low | 2 |
| 15% VLI | Very Low | 3 |
| 10% Low | Low | 1 |
| 20% Low | Low | 2 |
| 30% Low | Low | 3 |
| 10% Moderate (for-sale) | Moderate | 1 |
| 20% Moderate (for-sale) | Moderate | 2 |
| 30% Moderate (for-sale) | Moderate | 3 |
| 100% affordable | All levels | **5** (AB 1287 raised from 4 — Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (d)(2)(D)) |

Common concessions include:
- **Reduced setback requirements**
- **Increased maximum height allowances**
- **Reduced on-site parking requirements** (see parking minimums below)
- **Reduced lot area or open space requirements**
- **Approval of mixed-use zoning** where not otherwise permitted

A city may deny a concession or incentive **only** if it makes a written finding, based on substantial evidence, that the concession does not result in identifiable cost reductions, would cause a specific adverse public health or safety impact, or would be contrary to state or federal law. (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (d))

#### SDBL Parking Minimums

The SDBL caps parking requirements for density bonus projects regardless of local standards:

- **0-1 bedrooms**: 1 space per unit
- **2-3 bedrooms**: 1.5 spaces per unit (may be rounded down)
- **4+ bedrooms**: 2.5 spaces per unit

These maximums include guest and handicapped parking. For 100% affordable projects, projects within 0.5 miles of a major transit stop, or senior housing: **0.5 spaces per unit**. (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (p))

#### Waivers of Development Standards — Unlimited in Number

A developer may request a **waiver or reduction of any development standard** (height, setbacks, FAR, open space, parking geometry, lot coverage) that would physically preclude construction of the project at the density or with the concessions/incentives permitted. Waivers are **unlimited in number** — this is separate from and in addition to the capped concessions. A city cannot apply any development standard that would physically prevent the project from being built at the permitted density and with the granted concessions. The city must grant the waiver unless it makes a written finding that the waiver would cause a specific adverse impact on public health or safety and there is no feasible method to mitigate or avoid the impact — and an unlawful waiver refusal carries a one-way attorney-fee shift in the applicant's favor. (Gov. Code § 65915, subd. (e))

**Practical significance**: even a small affordability set-aside (e.g., two affordable units in a ~26-unit base project) qualifies the project for the bonus + concession + unlimited-waiver package. Real-world example: a 28-unit mixed-use project on a 5,828 sq ft downtown Huntington Beach parcel (410 Main St) was deemed complete and processed by the city with §65915 waivers (building height 45'→55', parking stall geometry) supported by 2 affordable units, layered on an SB 330 preliminary application.

#### SB 330 Preliminary Applications (Gov. Code § 65941.1)

Filing an SB 330 **preliminary application** (17-item statutory checklist the city cannot add to) **freezes the zoning, design, subdivision standards, and fees** in effect at filing for the life of the entitlement, provided the full application follows within 180 days. Housing projects consistent with objective standards are limited to a **maximum of five public hearings** (Gov. Code § 65905.5). Combined with the HAA (below), this is the vesting-plus-shield package sophisticated applicants file before entitlement review.

### AB 2011 — Affordable Housing on Commercial Corridors (Gov. Code §§ 65912.100-65912.140)

AB 2011 (effective July 1, 2023) creates a **by-right, ministerial approval pathway** for housing on commercial-zoned land along existing commercial corridors. No conditional use permit, no CEQA review, no discretionary design review.

#### Eligibility Requirements

The project site must be:
1. Zoned for **office, retail, or parking** uses (or similar commercial zones)
2. Located on a **commercial corridor** — a lot with frontage on a street at least 70 feet wide (or 100 feet wide for projects not 100% affordable)
3. Not within a coastal zone, very high fire hazard severity zone, wetlands, floodway, conservation lands, farmland, hazardous waste site, or other excluded areas

(Gov. Code § 65912.120)

#### Affordability Component

AB 2011 projects must include affordable units:

- **Mixed-income projects** (on lots fronting streets 100+ feet wide): At least **15% of units affordable to lower-income households** at 80% AMI, or **8% at very low income** (50% AMI)
- **100% affordable projects** (on lots fronting streets 70+ feet wide): All units (excluding manager units) affordable to lower-income households

(Gov. Code § 65912.111)

#### Objective Standards Only

Local agencies may apply **only objective standards** — zoning, design, subdivision, and other standards that involve no personal or subjective judgment by a public official and are uniformly verifiable by reference to an external, uniform benchmark or criterion. Height limits, setbacks, FAR, and density limits from the existing general plan and zoning code apply, but the city cannot impose subjective design review or discretionary findings. (Gov. Code § 65912.114)

#### Labor Requirements

AB 2011 projects must pay prevailing wages and, for projects of 50+ units, use a skilled and trained workforce for specified trades. (Gov. Code § 65912.130)

### SB 423 — Streamlined Ministerial Approval (Gov. Code § 65913.4)

SB 423 (successor to SB 35, effective January 1, 2024) provides a **streamlined, ministerial approval process** for qualifying multifamily housing projects. No CEQA, no discretionary design review, no public hearing required for approval.

#### Qualification Criteria

A project qualifies for SB 423 streamlining when:

1. **Site zoning**: The site is zoned for residential or mixed-use residential (or qualifies under the urban uses standard)
2. **Minimum density**: Two or more residential units
3. **Affordability threshold**: Depends on the jurisdiction's progress toward its RHNA allocation:
   - Cities that have **not approved sufficient housing for above-moderate-income RHNA**: **10% of units** must be affordable to households at or below 80% AMI
   - Cities that have **not approved sufficient housing for below-moderate-income RHNA**: **50% of units** must be affordable to households at or below 80% AMI
4. **Labor standards**: Prevailing wages required; skilled and trained workforce for projects of 50+ units
5. **Site exclusions**: Not in a coastal zone (unless previously developed), wetlands, earthquake fault zone (Alquist-Priolo), 100-year floodplain (unless mitigated), high fire severity zone (unless mitigated with defensible space), hazardous waste site, or conservation/farmland

(Gov. Code § 65913.4, subds. (a), (b))

#### Key Protections

- **No CEQA**: Streamlined projects are exempt from the California Environmental Quality Act
- **No subjective design review**: The city may apply only **objective** zoning, subdivision, and design standards in effect at the time of application submittal
- **Approval timeline**: 90 days for projects of 150 or fewer units; 180 days for larger projects. If the city fails to act within the timeline, the project is **deemed approved**
- **No reduction in density**: The city cannot require the project to be built at a lower density than what the applicant proposes (if consistent with objective standards)
- **Modifications limited**: Design review modifications permitted only where objective standards are not met — not for subjective aesthetic preferences

(Gov. Code § 65913.4, subds. (c), (d))

### Housing Accountability Act (Gov. Code § 65589.5)

The Housing Accountability Act (HAA) is the "anti-NIMBY" law that limits a local agency's ability to **deny, condition to lower density, or render infeasible** a housing development project that is consistent with the applicable general plan, specific plan, and zoning.

#### Core Prohibition

A local agency **shall not disapprove** a housing development project (including emergency shelters) that complies with applicable, objective general plan, zoning, and subdivision standards unless the agency makes **written findings**, supported by a preponderance of the evidence, that the project would have a **specific, adverse impact upon public health or safety** and there is **no feasible method** to satisfactorily mitigate or avoid the impact other than project disapproval. (Gov. Code § 65589.5, subd. (d))

#### Heightened Scrutiny for Affordable/Special Projects

For projects that include at least **20% low-income** or **100% moderate-income** units, the burden on the city is even higher — the adverse impact finding must be supported by **clear and convincing evidence** in the record. (Gov. Code § 65589.5, subd. (d)(2))

This heightened-scrutiny threshold is not the post-AB 1893 Builder's Remedy affordability menu. For Builder's Remedy, use the current state skill/reference (`california-builders-remedy` / `housing-accountability-act.md`) and do not reuse the old 20% lower-income qualifier for post-2025 applications.

#### What Cities Cannot Do

Under the HAA, a city may not:
1. **Deny the project** if it is consistent with objective standards
2. **Reduce the density** below the minimum density allowed by the applicable land use designation
3. **Impose conditions** that make the project infeasible for the proposed use (financially or physically)
4. **Apply subjective standards** as the basis for denial — only objective standards count

#### Penalties for Violation

A court may order the local agency to approve the project and may impose a fine of **$10,000 per unit** for bad-faith violations (minimum fine: the median value of a market-rate unit in the city). Attorney's fees are awarded to the prevailing plaintiff. (Gov. Code § 65589.5, subds. (k), (l))

### Builder's Remedy

The Builder's Remedy is not a standalone statute but an enforcement mechanism within the Housing Element Law (Gov. Code § 65589.5, subd. (d)(5)).

#### When Builder's Remedy Applies

When a city or county does **not have a substantially compliant housing element** (the housing element has expired, has not been certified by HCD, or has been found out of compliance), the city loses the ability to deny qualifying Builder's Remedy projects based on general plan or zoning inconsistency. Specifically:

- The city may **not deny** a qualifying housing development project solely because it is inconsistent with the general plan or zoning when the jurisdiction-status gate is open.
- For applications deemed complete on or after January 1, 2025, AB 1893 replaced the former pre-2025 Builder's Remedy affordability qualifier. Use the current menu in a CrossBeam reference that is not included in this plugin: 7% ELI, 10% VLI, 13% lower-income, 100% moderate, or the small-site exemption for projects of 10 units or fewer meeting the statutory site/density conditions.
- For pre-2025 vested applications, verify whether the applicant elected the post-AB 1893 framework before applying either threshold.
- The city cannot invoke general plan or zoning inconsistency as grounds for denial when its housing element is non-compliant

(Gov. Code § 65589.5, subd. (d)(5))

#### Practical Impact

Builder's Remedy projects have been filed in cities across California where housing elements lapsed or failed HCD certification. Under the post-AB 1893 framework, the jurisdiction's non-compliant housing element opens the Builder's Remedy gate, but the reviewer still has to reason project qualification, affordability tier, density cap, objective standards, site restrictions, and timing. Cities may still apply objective standards that do not preclude the allowable project, and health/safety findings remain a separate HAA constraint.

### SB 9 — Urban Lot Splits and Duplexes (Gov. Code §§ 65852.21, 66411.7)

SB 9 (effective January 1, 2022) allows **ministerial approval** of duplexes and urban lot splits on parcels zoned for single-family residential in urbanized areas.

#### Duplex Provision (Gov. Code § 65852.21)

A local agency must **ministerially approve** (without discretionary review or CEQA) a housing development of no more than **two residential units** on a parcel within a single-family residential zone if:

1. The parcel is located in an **urbanized area** or urban cluster (Census definition)
2. The project does **not** require demolition of existing affordable housing, rent-controlled housing, or housing withdrawn from rental market within 15 years
3. The parcel is not in a historic district, high fire severity zone (unless mitigated), flood zone, conservation/habitat area, or on property with a historic landmark
4. The development meets **objective** zoning, subdivision, and design standards

Local agencies may impose **objective** standards including 4-foot side and rear setbacks, but may not:
- Require a minimum lot size greater than required for the existing zone
- Require more than one off-street parking space per unit (no parking required within 0.5 miles of transit)
- Impose FAR limits below 0.8
- Require lot coverage limits that preclude construction of two 800 sq ft units

(Gov. Code § 65852.21, subds. (a)-(c))

#### Urban Lot Split (Gov. Code § 66411.7)

A local agency must **ministerially approve** a parcel map for an urban lot split creating no more than **two parcels** from an existing single-family residential parcel if:

1. Both resulting parcels are at least **40% of the original lot area** (minimum 1,200 sq ft unless local agency approves smaller)
2. The parcel has not been established through a prior SB 9 lot split
3. Neither the owner nor any person acting in concert has previously subdivided the parcel under SB 9
4. The same site exclusions as the duplex provision apply (historic, fire, flood, etc.)

After a lot split, each resulting parcel may contain up to **two units** (via the duplex provision or ADU law), yielding a potential maximum of **four units** on what was originally a single-family lot. An owner-occupancy requirement applies for 3 years from the date of the lot split. (Gov. Code § 66411.7, subds. (a)-(g))

### Application to a 35-Unit Project

For a 35-unit multi-unit residential project, the following state laws should be evaluated:

**Density Bonus Law (Gov. Code § 65915):**
- At 35 base units, the project exceeds the 5-unit minimum for SDBL
- If the project includes 4 VLI units (11% of 35 = 3.85, rounded **up** to 4 — fractional units in a § 65915 density calculation round up, and each component rounds up separately: § 65915(f)(5), (q)), it sits mid-scale on the § 65915(f)(2) very-low-income table at a **35% density bonus** (11% VLI → 35%; the base-tier maximum of 50% takes 15% VLI): 35 × 0.35 = 12.25 → **13 bonus units** → **48 total units** on the same site
- At 15% VLI (6 units — 15% of 35 = 5.25 → 6) the base tier maxes at a **50% bonus**: 35 × 0.50 = 17.5 → **18 bonus units** → **53 total units**. A 100%-affordable project is on a different, higher tier entirely (§ 65915(f)(3)(D): 80%, or no density cap at all near transit / in a low-VMT area) — see `california-density-bonus`
- At minimum 5% VLI (2 units), the project qualifies for a 20% bonus plus 1 concession/incentive
- Parking capped at SDBL maximums regardless of local code

**SB 423 Streamlining (Gov. Code § 65913.4):**
- If the site is zoned residential or mixed-use and the project meets affordability thresholds (10% or 50% depending on the jurisdiction's RHNA progress), the project qualifies for streamlined ministerial approval
- For a 35-unit project: either 4 affordable units (10% threshold) or 18 affordable units (50% threshold)
- No CEQA, no discretionary design review, 90-day approval timeline
- Confirm the site is not in an excluded zone (coastal, fire, flood, etc.)

**AB 2011 (Gov. Code § 65912.100):**
- Only applies if the site is zoned **commercial** (office, retail, parking) — not applicable to residentially-zoned sites
- If on a commercial corridor with 100+ foot street frontage: 15% affordable to lower-income or 8% VLI required
- If on a commercial corridor with 70+ foot street frontage: 100% affordable required
- By-right ministerial approval, no CEQA

**Housing Accountability Act (Gov. Code § 65589.5):**
- Applies to every housing project — if the 35-unit project is consistent with objective general plan and zoning standards, the city cannot deny it
- If the city's housing element is non-compliant, Builder's Remedy may apply — allowing the project even if inconsistent with zoning, provided it includes an affordable component

**SB 9 (Gov. Code §§ 65852.21, 66411.7):**
- Not applicable to a 35-unit project — SB 9 applies only to single-family parcels and is limited to 2 units per parcel (or 4 units with a lot split)
