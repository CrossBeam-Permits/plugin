## Egress Corridors — Fire-Resistance Rating, Width, Dead Ends, Continuity, and Applicability

### When Corridors Are Required vs Not

A "corridor" under CBC 1020 is an enclosed exit access component serving as part of a means of egress system. Not every hallway in a building triggers these requirements:

**Corridors ARE required (CBC 1020.1):**
- R-2 apartment buildings with common hallways serving multiple dwelling units
- Any building where exit access from occupied spaces passes through an enclosed common hallway before reaching an exit (stairway, exterior door)
- Mixed-use buildings where residential units open onto shared corridors

**Corridors are NOT code corridors:**
- Hallways **within** an individual dwelling unit or sleeping unit — these are exempt from fire-resistance rating requirements (CBC 1020.2, Exception 2)
- Individual townhouse units governed by the CRC — each unit has its own independent means of egress directly to the exterior; there is no common corridor serving multiple units
- Passages within a single tenant space that do not serve as exit access for other tenants

(CBC 1020.1; 1020.2, Exception 2)

**Practical distinction:** In a townhouse development, each unit exits directly to grade or to its own private stair — there is no shared corridor, so CBC 1020 does not apply within units. In an apartment building, units open onto a common corridor that leads to exit stairways, so every provision of CBC 1020 applies to that corridor.

### Table 1020.2 — Corridor Fire-Resistance Rating

The fire-resistance rating required for corridor walls depends on occupancy group, occupant load served, and sprinkler status:

| Occupancy | Occupant Load Served by Corridor | Without Automatic Sprinkler System | With Automatic Sprinkler System |
|-----------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| H-1, H-2, H-3 | All | Not Permitted | 1 hour |
| H-4, H-5, L | Greater than 30 | Not Permitted | 1 hour |
| A, B, E, F, M, S, U | Greater than 30 | 1 hour | 0 hours |
| **R-1, R-2, R-2.2, R-3, R-3.1, R-4** | **Greater than 10** | **Not Permitted** | **1 hour** |
| I-2 | Greater than 6 | Not Permitted | 1 hour |
| I-3, R-2.1 | Greater than 6 | Not Permitted | 1 hour |
| I-4 | All | 1 hour | 1 hour |

(CBC Table 1020.2)

**Critical points for multi-unit residential:**
- R-1 and R-2 occupancies with occupant load >10 served by the corridor require **1-hour fire-resistance-rated corridor walls** even when sprinklered. Unsprinklered R occupancies are **not permitted** to use corridors as exit access at all when occupant load exceeds 10.
- R-3 and R-4 buildings apply to Table 1020.2 only when equipped with sprinklers per Section 903.3.1.3 (NFPA 13D). (Table 1020.2, footnote d)
- The "with automatic sprinkler system" column requires NFPA 13 or 13R systems per Section 903.3.1.1 or 903.3.1.2 for H and I occupancies (Table 1020.2, footnote c). For R-2 apartments, NFPA 13R is the typical system.
- The 1-hour corridor wall is constructed as a **fire partition** per CBC 708 (CBC 1020.2).

**Exception 2 — Dwelling units exempt:** A fire-resistance rating is not required for corridors **contained within a dwelling unit or sleeping unit** in Group R. This means the hallway inside an apartment connecting bedrooms to the living area is not a rated corridor. Only the common hallway outside the unit entry door requires the 1-hour rating. (CBC 1020.2, Exception 2)

### Corridor Width Requirements (Table 1020.3)

Minimum corridor width is determined by Table 1020.3 and Section 1005.1 (capacity calculation). The greater value controls.

| Condition | Minimum Width |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Default (any facility not otherwise listed)** | **44 inches** |
| Occupant load less than 50 | 36 inches |
| Within a dwelling unit | 36 inches |
| Access to mechanical/plumbing/electrical equipment | 24 inches |
| Group E corridor with occupant load ≥ 100 | 72 inches |
| Group I-2 and I-3 areas requiring bed movement | 96 inches |
| Ambulatory care stretcher traffic corridors | 72 inches |

(CBC Table 1020.3)

**For R-2 apartment buildings:**
- Common corridors serving occupant load **greater than 10** (which triggers Table 1020.2 requirements) almost always have aggregate occupant loads well above 50, so the **44-inch minimum** applies.
- Corridors serving occupant load **less than 50** may use the **36-inch minimum** — but this is rare in apartment buildings since a typical floor with even a few units exceeds 50 occupants.
- Hallways **within** a dwelling unit: **36 inches** minimum (this is the CRC/within-unit standard, not a code corridor).

**Capacity calculation (CBC 1005.3):** The corridor width must also be sufficient based on occupant load capacity factors. The base factor for non-stairway egress components is 0.2 inch per occupant (CBC 1005.3.2), reduced to 0.15 inch per occupant in sprinklered buildings with emergency voice/alarm communication (CBC 1005.3.2, Exception 1). For stairways, the base factor is 0.3 inch per occupant (CBC 1005.3.1), reduced to 0.2 inch per occupant under the same conditions. For a corridor serving 200 occupants at the base rate: 200 x 0.2 = 40 inches — still less than the 44-inch table minimum, so 44 inches controls. The capacity calculation only governs when very high occupant loads push the calculated width above 44 inches.

### Dead-End Corridor Limits (CBC 1020.5)

Where more than one exit or exit access doorway is required, dead-end corridors shall not exceed **20 feet** in length.

(CBC 1020.5)

**Exceptions relevant to residential:**
- **Exception 2 — Sprinklered R occupancies:** In Groups R-1, R-2, R-2.1, and R-2.2 (among others), where the building is equipped throughout with an automatic sprinkler system per Section 903.3.1.1 (NFPA 13), the dead-end corridor limit increases to **50 feet**. (CBC 1020.5, Exception 2)
- **Exception 3 — Short dead ends:** A dead-end corridor is not limited in length where the dead-end length is **less than 2.5 times the least width** of the dead-end corridor. For a 44-inch-wide corridor, this means dead ends under 110 inches (about 9 feet 2 inches) are unrestricted regardless of sprinkler status. (CBC 1020.5, Exception 3)

**Practical impact:** Since new R-2 buildings require sprinklers (CBC 903.2.8), most apartment projects qualify for the 50-foot dead-end allowance under Exception 2. However, note that Exception 2 requires NFPA 13 specifically — NFPA 13R does not qualify for this dead-end extension in the literal code text (the exception lists 903.3.1.1 only, not 903.3.1.2). Verify with the AHJ whether 13R triggers this exception, as interpretations vary.

### Corridor Continuity (CBC 1020.7)

Fire-resistance-rated corridors shall be **continuous from the point of entry to an exit** and shall not be interrupted by intervening rooms. Where the egress path within a fire-resistance-rated corridor includes travel along unenclosed exit access stairways or ramps, the fire-resistance rating shall be continuous for the length of the stairway or ramp and for the connecting corridor on the adjacent floor leading to the exit.

(CBC 1020.7)

**Exceptions:**
1. Foyers, lobbies, or reception rooms constructed to corridor standards are not considered intervening rooms (CBC 1020.7, Exception 1)
2. Enclosed elevator lobbies per Section 1016.2, Item 1 are not considered intervening rooms (CBC 1020.7, Exception 2)
3. [SFM] In fully sprinklered office buildings, corridors may lead through enclosed elevator lobbies if all areas have access to at least one required exit without passing through the lobby (CBC 1020.7, Exception 3)

**Practical impact:** In apartment buildings, the 1-hour corridor from dwelling unit doors to the exit stairway cannot pass through an unrated lobby, lounge, or leasing office. Any such space the corridor passes through must be rated to corridor standards or the corridor must route around it.

### Corridor Ceiling Height

Corridors, as exit access components, must comply with the general means of egress ceiling height requirement of **not less than 7 feet 6 inches** (CBC 1003.2). Projections from the ceiling shall not reduce the clear height to less than **6 feet 8 inches** (CBC 1003.3.1).

(CBC 1003.2, 1003.3.1)

### Corridor Obstruction (CBC 1020.4)

The minimum width or required capacity of corridors shall be **unobstructed**. Encroachments complying with Section 1005.7 are permitted (doors reducing width by not more than 7 inches when fully open, nonstructural projections not more than 1.5 inches on each side, handrails per Section 1014.9, etc.).

(CBC 1020.4)

### Air Movement in Corridors (CBC 1020.6)

Corridors shall **not** serve as supply, return, exhaust, relief, or ventilation air ducts.

**Key exceptions for residential:**
- Corridors may serve as makeup air source for exhaust systems in rooms opening directly onto the corridor (toilet rooms, bathrooms), provided the corridor receives outdoor air at a rate exceeding the makeup air drawn from it (CBC 1020.6, Exception 1)
- Within a dwelling unit, corridors may convey return air (CBC 1020.6, Exception 2)
- Within tenant spaces of 1,000 sq ft or less, corridors may convey return air (CBC 1020.6, Exception 3)

**Corridor ceiling plenum (CBC 1020.6.1):** The space between the corridor ceiling and the structure above may serve as a return air plenum only when one of the following is met:
1. The corridor is not required to be fire-resistance rated
2. The corridor is separated from the plenum by fire-resistance-rated construction
3. The air-handling system shuts down on smoke detector activation
4. The air-handling system shuts down on sprinkler water flow (building fully sprinklered)
5. The space is part of an approved engineered smoke control system

(CBC 1020.6.1)

### For the 35-Unit Test Case

**Townhouse units (CRC-governed):**
- Individual townhouse units exit directly to the exterior — no common corridor exists within or serving the units
- Hallways within each townhouse unit are **not code corridors** and do not require fire-resistance rating (CBC 1020.2, Exception 2)
- Interior hallways within units must be minimum **36 inches** wide (within-dwelling-unit minimum per Table 1020.3)
- No dead-end corridor limits, continuity requirements, or air movement restrictions apply within individual townhouse units

**Apartment units (CBC-governed, R-2):**
- Common corridors serving apartment units require **1-hour fire-resistance-rated walls** constructed as fire partitions per CBC 708 (Table 1020.2 — R-2, occupant load >10, sprinklered)
- Unsprinklered apartment corridors in R-2 are **not permitted** where occupant load >10
- Common corridor width: **44 inches minimum** (occupant load almost certainly exceeds 50)
- Dead-end corridors: **20 feet maximum**, extended to **50 feet** with NFPA 13 sprinkler system throughout
- Corridor must be **continuous from unit doors to exit stairway** — no intervening unrated rooms
- Corridor doors from dwelling units into the corridor: **20-minute fire door** (CBC Table 716.1(2) — fire partition corridor wall)
- Corridor ceiling height: minimum **7 feet 6 inches**
- Corridor shall not serve as supply/return duct (with limited exceptions)
