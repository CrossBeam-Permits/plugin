# Laguna Beach Fire Code Amendments (Title 15)

Source: Laguna Beach Municipal Code, Chapter 15.01
Based on: 2025 California Fire Code (2024 IFC)
Adopted: Ord. 1726, 12/18/2025

---

## 1. Automatic Sprinkler Requirements

### Section 903.2 — All New Buildings Require Sprinklers

All new buildings and structures require approved automatic sprinkler systems. This is significantly more restrictive than state code.

> An approved automatic fire sprinkler system meeting the requirements of NFPA 13D, 13R or 13 shall be installed in all new one-family, two-family or multi-family dwellings, regardless of square footage or the number of stories.

**Exceptions:**
1. Open parking structures.
2. Detached private garages and detached carports more than 30 feet from any structure.
3. Accessory structures not intended for human habitation, floor area under 400 sq ft, and more than 30 feet from any habitable structure.

**NFPA standard selection:**
- NFPA 13 — Commercial and general buildings
- NFPA 13R — Low-rise residential occupancies
- NFPA 13D — One- and two-family dwellings and manufactured homes

### Section 1103.5.4 — Existing Group R Retrofit Trigger

Existing Group R (residential) buildings must be retrofitted with sprinklers when:

> Work requiring a building permit that involves alteration of 50 percent or more of a building, which includes or involves work on 50 percent or more of the building's exterior, or which involves the addition of one or more stories.

**Exception:** Work that consists only of the installation of fire-resistant siding. The exception belongs to §1103.5.4 alone; it does not exempt a project from the valuation trigger below.

**This is only one of the two residential retrofit triggers.** The other is **LBMC Ch. 14.52.030** (CRC R309.2 amendment, Ord. 1725, 12/18/2025) — a **valuation** test: cumulative additions, alterations and repairs to a one- or two-family dwelling and attached garage in any three-year period exceeding 50% of the pre-work valuation. **Either trigger firing requires the retrofit.** Run both from the single residential reference, `../../residential-remodel/references/city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`.

**Re-roofing impact:** A full re-roof alone is unlikely to trigger this (it is exterior work but typically not "alteration of 50% or more of a building"). However, if a re-roof is combined with other work that collectively reaches the 50% threshold, sprinkler retrofit is required.

## 2. Defensible Space and Vegetation Management

### Section 304.1.3.1 — Vegetation Clearance

> Weeds, grass, vines or other growth that is capable of being ignited and endangering property, shall be cut down and removed by the owner or occupant of the premises.

Vegetation clearance in WUI areas and Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones (VHFHSZ) must comply with Chapter 49 and the Fuel Modification Guidelines (Resolution 89.104).

### Section 304.1.3.2 — Defensible Space for Existing Structures

All existing habitable buildings in the VHFHSZ must maintain defensible space per the "Defensible Space Guideline for Existing Structures in the Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, dated August 2021."

**Exceptions:**
1. New construction or major remodels in Fuel Modification designated areas follow Section 324 instead.
2. Existing structures built under Fuel Modification Guidelines (Resolution 89.104) maintain per approved plan.

### Section 304.1.3.3 — Documentation of Compliance

Property owners must obtain documentation of compliance from the fire code official upon inspection verification for either the Defensible Space Guideline or Fuel Modification Guidelines.

### Section 324 — Fuel Modification for New Construction / Major Remodel

All new buildings or major remodels in Fuel Modification designated areas must:
1. Submit preliminary fuel modification plans with any tentative map.
2. Submit final fuel modification plans before building permit issuance.
3. Meet criteria in the 2019 Laguna Beach Fire Department Landscape/Fuel Modification Standards and Maintenance Program (Resolutions 89.104, 1640, 1641).
4. Maintain all elements per approved plan.

## 3. Fire Alarm and Monitoring Requirements

### NFPA 13D (One/Two-Family) — Sections 7.6 and 7.6.1

**Exterior alarm:**
> Exterior alarm indicating device shall be listed for outside service and audible and visible from the street from which the house is addressed. Exterior audible devices shall be placed on the front or side of the structure, and the location is subject to final approval by the fire code official.

**Interior alarm (Section 7.6.1):**
> Additional interior alarm devices shall be required to provide audibility throughout the structure. Sound levels in all sleeping areas with all intervening doors closed shall be a minimum of 15 dBA above the average ambient sound level but not less than 75 dBA. Audible devices shall be powered from an uninterruptible circuit (except for over-current protection) serving normally operated appliances in the residence.

**Exceptions:**
1. When an approved water flow monitoring system is installed, interior audible devices may be powered through the fire alarm control panel.
2. When smoke detectors per CFC Section 907 sound alarm upon water flow switch activation with audibility throughout.

### NFPA 13D — Section 7.1.2 — Control Valve Supervision

> The piping system shall not have a separate control valve unless supervised by a central station, proprietary, or remote station alarm service.

### NFPA 13R (Low-Rise Residential) — Section 6.16.1 — Central Station Monitoring Required

> An NFPA 72 fire alarm system with 24-hour UL-certified central station monitoring shall be provided on all sprinkler systems and shall be connected to the building fire alarm or water-flow monitoring system where provided. There shall also be a minimum of one exterior alarm indicating device, listed for outside service and audible and visible from the access roadway that serves that building.

Interior alarm requirements (Section 6.16.1.1) mirror NFPA 13D Section 7.6.1 above (75 dBA minimum in sleeping areas).

### NFPA 72 — Annual Testing and Records

- Section 14.2.2.3.2: Uncorrected defects must be reported to fire code official in writing within 24 hours.
- Section 14.6.2.1: Annual testing records must be sent to the fire authority and retained for 1 year beyond the next testing cycle.
- Section 14.6.2.1.1: A durable tag must be affixed to the main fire alarm panel showing company info, license, service date, and type of service performed.

## 4. Fire Department Connection (FDC) Specifications

### NFPA 13 — Section 16.12.1

- Minimum two 2-1/2" inlets.
- Location no more than 150 feet from a public hydrant.
- May be installed on backflow assembly if acceptable to water authority.
- All FDC piping painted **OSHA safety red**.
- When sprinkler density design requires 500 GPM or greater (including inside hose stream demand), or standpipe is included: **four 2-1/2" inlets required**.
- FDC may be within 150 feet of a private fire hydrant when approved by the chief.

### NFPA 13 — Section 16.12.5.7 — FDC Location

> Fire department connections shall be on the street address side of buildings and shall be located and arranged so that they are immediately adjacent to the approved fire department access road and that hose lines can be readily and conveniently attached to the inlets without interference from nearby objects including buildings, fence, posts or other fire department connections.

### NFPA 14 — Section 6.4.6.1 — Standpipe FDC

- Minimum two 2-1/2" internal threaded (NHS) inlets.
- Additional inlets at 250 GPM per inlet ratio to meet system demand.
- Approved caps required.
- No more than 150 feet from a public hydrant.
- Painted OSHA safety red.

### NFPA 24 — Section 5.9.1.3 — Private Fire Service Mains FDC

- Same 150-foot and two 2-1/2" inlet minimums.
- Section 5.9.1.3.1: Four 2-1/2" inlets when sprinkler density is 500 GPM or greater or standpipe included.
- Section 5.9.1.3.2: FDC may be within 150 feet of private hydrant if downstream of aboveground sprinkler system check valve.

## 5. WUI / Chapter 7A Provisions

The municipal code does not contain explicit amendments to Chapter 7A (Materials and Construction Methods for Exterior Wildfire Exposure) beyond what is addressed through the defensible space, fuel modification, and sprinkler requirements above.

Laguna Beach is **not** citywide VHFHSZ. Use the parcel-specific adopted 2025 LRA determination from site facts and the authoritative baseline in `../../city-reference/SKILL.md`; do not infer designation from a citywide percentage or either legacy City viewer. For parcels where the adopted map resolves Very High, apply:
- Chapter 49 vegetation management compliance
- Defensible space requirements (Section 304.1.3.2)
- Fuel modification plans for new construction/major remodel (Section 324)
- Universal sprinkler requirements for all new residential (Section 903.2)

For WUI roofing material requirements specific to Laguna Beach, see the companion reference: `city-wui-roofing.md`.

## 6. Outdoor Fire Restrictions

### Section 305.6 — Hazardous Conditions

Outdoor fires are prohibited when:
1. Sustained winds exceed 8 MPH and relative humidity is below 25%, within a wildfire risk area.
2. Sustained winds exceed 25 MPH and relative humidity is below 15% and a flag condition has been declared by a fire agency.
3. The fire code official determines conditions are hazardous for open flame.
4. A Red Flag warning has been issued by the National Weather Service.

### Section 308.1.6.3 — Sky Lanterns

No person shall release or cause to be released an untethered sky lantern or similar device.

### Section 5601.1.3 — Fireworks

All fireworks are prohibited except storage/handling per Section 5604 and displays per Section 5608 when authorized by the fire code official.

## 7. Hazardous Materials Provisions

### Section 5001.5.2 — HMMP and HMIS Required

When required by the fire code official, permit applications must include:
- **Hazardous Materials Management Plan (HMMP)** per Section 5001.5.2.1
- **Hazardous Materials Inventory Statement (HMIS)** per Section 5001.5.2.2

Both must be submitted to the fire department for review and approval at the time of permit application.

### Section 5704.2.7.5.8 — Underground Storage Tank Monitoring

All underground storage tank systems must have approved leak detection and monitoring per CUPA/state law. Inventory monitoring and record keeping required.

### Section 6104.2 — LPG Storage Limits

Aggregate capacity of any LPG installation in congested/populated areas: maximum 2,000 gallons water capacity.

## 8. Provisions Affecting Re-Roofing, HVAC, and Electrical Work

### Re-Roofing

- **Sprinkler retrofit trigger — two triggers, either fires:** (a) **§1103.5.4** (scope) — a re-roof alone does not typically fire it, but combined with other alterations reaching 50% of the building or 50% of the exterior, or an added story, sprinklers are required throughout. (b) **Ch. 14.52.030** (valuation) — a re-roof valuation counts toward the 50% / three-year cumulative total, so a re-roof can fire the retrofit through Ch. 14.52.030 even when §1103.5.4 is nowhere near. Run both: `../../residential-remodel/references/city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`.
- **Siding exception:** Installation of fire-resistant siding only is explicitly exempt from the §1103.5.4 scope trigger. It is not exempt from the Ch. 14.52.030 valuation trigger.
- **Defensible space:** Any re-roof project on an existing structure in the VHFHSZ should verify defensible space compliance (Section 304.1.3.2). The fire department may flag non-compliance during plan review.
- **WUI materials:** All roofing in the VHFHSZ must comply with Chapter 7A fire-resistive construction requirements (see `city-wui-roofing.md`).

### HVAC

- **Sprinkler coverage of equipment areas:** NFPA 13D Section 8.3.5.1 requires sprinklers in "concealed spaces dedicated to ventilation equipment" and penthouse equipment rooms.
- **Attic penetrations (Section 8.3.5.2):** All attics require intermediate temperature quick response sprinkler to protect penetrations created by mechanical equipment.
- **Exterior overhangs (Section 8.3.5.3):** Overhangs 48" and greater require fire sprinkler coverage — relevant if HVAC equipment is mounted on or near overhangs.

### Electrical Work

- **Fire alarm circuits:** Interior alarm audible devices must be powered from an uninterruptible circuit (except overcurrent protection) serving normally operated appliances (NFPA 13D Section 7.6.1).
- **Central station monitoring:** NFPA 13R systems require NFPA 72 fire alarm with 24-hour UL-certified central station monitoring (Section 6.16.1).
- **Control valve supervision:** Separate control valves on NFPA 13D systems must be supervised by central station, proprietary, or remote station alarm service (Section 7.1.2).

### Standpipe Systems

- Section 905.3.1: Class I automatic dry standpipe required when highest floor is more than 30 feet above (or lowest floor more than 30 feet below) fire department vehicle access level.

### NFPA 13 — Sprinkler Type for Shell Buildings (Section 9.4.3.1)

When occupancy is undetermined (spec buildings), quick-response type sprinklers are required. Options include quick-response, residential, quick-response CMSA, or ESFR sprinklers.

## 9. Underground Pipe Protection (NFPA 24 Amendments)

Critical for fire service main installations:

- **Section 10.4.1.1:** All bolted joint accessories must be coated with asphalt or corrosion-retarding material prior to polytube and after installation.
- **Section 10.4.1.1.1:** All bolts, washers, and nuts in pipe-joint assembly must be **316 stainless steel**.
- **Section 10.4.1.4:** All ferrous pipes must be coated and wrapped. Fittings protected with loose 8-mil polyethylene tube extending 12" past joints, sealed with 2" tape approved for underground use. Galvanizing does not satisfy this requirement. Exception: 316 or 304 stainless steel pipe and fittings.
- **Section 10.4.3.1:** Pipe entering under building foundation limited to 18" maximum from interior of exterior wall, must be 316 stainless steel with no joints.

## 10. Water Supply for Fire Sprinkler Design (NFPA 13)

### Section 28.1.3

> The available water supply for fire sprinkler system design shall be determined by utilizing the LBFD Hydrant Flow Report and Fire Flow Information form to document a flow test conducted by the water purveyor and approved by the Fire Code Official.

Flow test must be conducted no more than **6 months** prior to working plan submittal unless otherwise approved by the Laguna Beach Fire Department.
