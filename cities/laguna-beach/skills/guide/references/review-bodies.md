## The DRB-versus-Planning-Commission split (since July 22, 2026)

Ord. 1732 §7, new 25.05.040(F)(1):

> "i. The design review board shall conduct a noticed public hearing on design review applications
> for all single-family or two-family developments, including associated public right-of-way
> improvements.
> ii. The planning commission shall conduct a noticed public hearing on all other design review
> applications, including but not limited to: residential development of three-families or more,
> all projects located in the downtown specific plan area, all non-residential development, public
> works projects, and projects for which the planning commission is the primary discretionary review
> authority, such as approval of a conditional use permit."

So a house, a house with an ADU, or a duplex goes to the DRB. A triplex, a mixed-use building, a
shop, or anything downtown goes to the Planning Commission.

**Does an ADU count toward "three-families or more"?** Our reading: no. State law says an ADU "shall
be deemed to be an accessory use or an accessory building and shall not be considered to exceed the
allowable density for the lot" (Gov. Code 66319), and the ADU is approved on its own ministerial ADU
application, outside the Design Review (25.05.040(B)(2)(h)). So a house plus ADU, or a duplex plus
ADUs, stays a single- or two-family development at the DRB. The City has not ruled on a duplex with
ADUs: UNSETTLED, confirm at pre-application. A project with three or more primary units goes to the
Commission whatever its ADUs.

**Variances by project type** (25.05.025(E), (J)): house or duplex, the DRB as Board of Adjustment,
with the Design Review; a project the Commission decides (3+ units, Downtown, nonresidential, public
works, a CUP), the Commission; subdivision, access and substandard-street variances, the Commission.
Detail: `discretionary-permits.md`. The DRB meets the 2nd and 4th Thursday
at 5:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers, with a few exceptions (City web page, as of 2026-06-18).

## Staff and the Director

- **Zone Clearance** is a staff compliance check, not a discretionary decision. It can be a stamp,
  signature, or letter (25.05.045(C)(2)).
- **Administrative Design Review** is the Director's decision after a hearing, or after a
  15-working-day notice with no hearing request (25.05.040(B)(3)(b)-(c)).
- **Referral up.** The Director may refer a Zone Clearance or ADR project to the DRB or Planning
  Commission (25.05.040(A)(2)(d)). Treat it as possible, never predicted.
- **CDP alone.** When a project needs only a CDP, the Director decides after a public hearing
  (25.07.012(D)(1)), or without one under the Administrative CDP waiver for minor development
  (25.07.013). When other permits are needed, the CDP goes to the same body as those permits
  (25.07.012(D)).

## Historic properties

- Major work on a historic resource needs Design Review under (B)(1)(d). Before the DRB or Planning
  Commission hears it, "the Heritage Committee must review the project and make a recommendation"
  (25.45.010(B), as amended by Ord. 1732).
- Minor work listed in (B)(3)(a)(iv) is Director-level ADR, judged against the Secretary of the
  Interior's Standards, with no hearing waiver (25.45.010(A); 25.05.040(B)(3)(b)).
- Demolition of a historic resource: CDP, Heritage Committee recommendation, then the design review
  authority decides; an approval waits 90 days for relocation or documentation (25.45.014).
- Details and ratings: `historic-properties.md`.

## Appeals (who hears what)

- Director or staff decision → Planning Commission or DRB, as applicable. For a ministerial permit
  (such as a Zone Clearance) the only ground is that the decision did not follow the Zoning Code
  (25.05.070(B)(1)).
- DRB or Planning Commission decision → City Council (25.05.070(B)(2)).
- One Council member may call any Director, DRB or Planning Commission decision up for review within
  14 calendar days; no reasons or fee needed (25.05.070(B)(3)).
- CDP exemption determinations and appealable CDPs → Coastal Commission, under Chapter 25.07
  (25.07.008(C); 25.07.016). Deadlines: `process-timeline-appeals.md`.
