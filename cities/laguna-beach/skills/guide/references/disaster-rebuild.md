# Laguna Beach — Rebuilding after a fire, landslide or other disaster (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Authority:** LBMC 25.07.008(A)(5) (certified LCP); PRC 30610(g)
(the City source document (not included in this plugin)); LBMC 25.56.014 (restoration of
nonconforming structures) and 25.56.011 (major remodels, Ord. 1732), texts at
the City source document (not included in this plugin) and
the City source document (not included in this plugin); LBMC 25.05.040(B)(1)(a)-(b), (B)(2)(c)
(Ord. 1732); LBMC Ch. 14.70.
**Use for:** "My house burned down; what do I need to rebuild?", "Can I rebuild the same house?",
"Can I keep my old setbacks?", "Do I need a CDP to rebuild?", "Can I make it bigger while I'm at it?"

Run the normal method in `planning-approvals` for every piece. This file adds the four rules that
change after a disaster.

## 1. Planning list: a replacement house is a new building

- A replacement building is a new building, so it goes to **Design Review** before the DRB (house or
  duplex) or the Planning Commission (other uses), with a landscape plan and exterior lighting plan
  (25.05.040(B)(1)(b), (F)(1)). The list has no disaster exception.
- **Partial damage** repaired in kind (same materials, size and form) can be Zone Clearance as
  "in-kind replacement of improvements for repair and maintenance" (25.05.040(B)(2)(c)). Repair work
  that reaches the major remodel thresholds is Design Review (B)(1)(a) and is treated as a new
  building (25.56.011; `../../residential-remodel/references/city-major-remodel-methodology.md`).
- Whether an exact replica of a destroyed house can use (B)(2)(c) in-kind is not addressed by the
  code. UNSETTLED; the safer reading is (B)(1)(b) Design Review. Confirm with Planning at
  pre-application.
- The other pieces (new pool, decks, grading, retaining walls) are matched on their own items, as
  for any project.

## 2. Nonconforming rights: what you may keep

- **The rebuild right.** "Notwithstanding the extent of damage, any legal nonconforming building,
  structure or improvement which has been damaged by fire, flood, wind, earthquake or other
  disasters may be repaired, restored, replaced or reconstructed up to the original size, placement
  and density within five years of such damage or destruction, notwithstanding any other provision
  of this title" (25.56.014). So a legal nonconforming setback, height or density can be kept, up to
  the original, if the work starts within five years.
- **Limits in the same section:** a multifamily building damaged more than 50% of its value must meet
  the parking chapter (Ch. 25.52) in full; a shore protective device must meet current zoning and
  General Plan policy (25.56.014).
- **Only legal nonconformities.** Space or structures that were never permitted get no rebuild right
  (`unpermitted-work`). Ask for the permit history.
- **Bigger than the original:** the added part must meet current standards (25.56.008), and the
  CDP exemption below is lost if the growth passes 10%.
- **Conflict with Ord. 1732.** 25.56.011 says a major remodel is a new building subject to current
  standards. 25.56.014 says disaster restoration up to the original is allowed "notwithstanding any
  other provision of this title". On the words, 25.56.014 controls for a restoration within its
  limits; 25.56.011 is later. UNSETTLED; give both and confirm with Planning at pre-application.
- Design Review still applies to the replacement (section 1). How far the DRB may change a
  restoration that stays within the original size and placement is UNSETTLED.

## 3. Coastal Development Permit: the disaster-replacement exemption

- **Certified LCP:** replacing a structure "destroyed by natural disaster" is exempt if it is for the
  same use, does not exceed the floor area, height or bulk of the destroyed structure by more than
  10%, and is sited in the same location on the same building site (25.07.008(A)(5)). Public works
  facilities are excluded.
- **Coastal Act:** PRC 30610(g) has the same test for a structure destroyed by "a disaster", meaning
  any force beyond the owner's control, and adds that the replacement must conform to applicable
  existing zoning. "Bulk" means total interior cubic volume measured from the exterior surface
  (30610(g)(2)). The LBMC says "natural disaster"; a fire the owner did not cause fits the state
  definition. Whether a non-natural cause fits the LBMC wording is UNSETTLED.
- **Outside the exemption** (moved footprint, more than 10% larger, a different use): it is new
  development and needs a CDP, decided with the Design Review (25.07.012(D)); appealable in the
  appealable area (`coastal-development-permits`).
- Planning records the exemption; in the appealable area work waits 10 working days after the
  Commission receives it (25.07.008(B)-(C)). In Three Arch Bay, Irvine Cove or Blue Lagoon the
  Commission decides.
- Emergency shoring or slope work right after the event: emergency CDP (25.07.020;
  `coastal-development-permits` "Emergency CDP").

## 4. Fire-zone construction

- The replacement is new construction. It must meet the current WUI building rules (Class A roof,
  ignition-resistant siding, glass skylights, vent limits; LBMC 14.70.015-.040) and the state WUI
  code (state overlay skill), not the rules the old house was built under.
- Design Review of new construction triggers **Fire Safety Standards Review** before the hearing
  (25.05.040(C)(4)), with a fuel modification program if Fire asks (VHFHSZ or FM). On FM land the
  final fuel modification plan is approved before the building permit (14.70.070). Detail:
  `fire-hazard-and-wui`.
- New one- and two-family homes need fire sprinklers (`../../city-reference/SKILL.md` "Sprinkler Retrofit Triggers").
- A destroyed **historic register** structure: emergency demolition is allowed only as far as the
  Building Official or Fire Chief orders (25.45.016); the rebuild goes through the historic route
  (`historic-properties`).
- A burned site in a landslide or liquefaction zone still needs the geotechnical review
  (`grading-drainage`).
- The Art in Public Places requirement does not apply to "remodeling, repair or reconstruction of
  structures which have been damaged by fire, flood, wind, earthquake or other calamity"
  (1.09.040(b)(4)).

## What to tell the resident

1. Planning: Design Review for the replacement house (DRB for a house or duplex); in-kind repair
   of partial damage can be Zone Clearance.
2. Keep what was legal: 25.56.014 lets legal nonconforming size, placement and density come back
   within five years; say the Ord. 1732 conflict is UNSETTLED.
3. CDP: exempt if same use, same spot, within 10%; otherwise a CDP with the Design Review.
4. Fire: current WUI materials, Fire Safety Standards Review, fuel modification, sprinklers.
5. Get the permit history first; unpermitted parts are not restored by right.
