# Laguna Beach — Does my project need Planning approval, and which? (the method)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Use for:** any "what do I need for this project on this lot?" question, including projects no
file names and requests with several parts. This file is a procedure, not a catalog. Run every
step for every project. Every other guide file named here is fetched with the reference file `references/<name>.md`,
never the permit skills: list text `design-review-lists`; bodies `review-bodies`; CDPs
`coastal-development-permits`; filing order, approval life and appeals `process-timeline-appeals`;
variances, use permits, changes to approvals, encroachments `discretionary-permits`; how to weigh
City practice against the code `city-practice`. A file in another skill is fetched by its path,
e.g. `../../residential-remodel/references/city-major-remodel-methodology.md`.
City practice is evidence, never the rule: state the code rule first, then the practice.

**Authority.** LBMC 25.05.040 and 25.05.045 as replaced or added by Urgency Ord. 1732 (effective
2026-07-22; extended by Ord. 1733); LBMC 25.08 definitions as amended by Ord. 1732 §§17-21
(the City source document (not included in this plugin)); the City's review-level
matrix (Design Review Application doc 26714, Aug. 2026, p. 23); City Planning intake rulings
(Sept. 2026), restated in this file where they matter to residents.

## Step 1 — Get the property facts, and know what each one changes

Call the parcel lookup and the record check in the same round,
for every project question, not only status questions (Step 1a). the record check is not in every
chat: when the chat has no record lookup, or the lookup fails, ask the applicant to bring any
existing or pending approvals for the address (Planning case numbers, resolutions, stamped plans)
and say the answer assumes none until they do. If the address does not bind to a
parcel (for example "St" versus "Dr"), say so, give the parcel the lookup suggests, and ask the
resident to confirm before using parcel facts. Say which facts came from the lookup. When two lines
of the lookup disagree (a parcel flag says yes, a map layer says no), say both and name the fact to
confirm; never pick one silently. Lead with a fact only when it changes this project's answer: a
parcel split by a zone, coastal, appealable or ESA line matters only if the work sits on the
affected part or the line changes the route; otherwise mention it once, last.

**Step 1a — Existing approvals and open permits come before routing.** Read the the record check
result for Planning records (DR, ZPC, CDP, VAR, CUP, HCR) and building permits at the address. Then:
1. **Is the approval alive?** Design Review and a City CDP each "lapse and become void two years
   following the effective date" unless the work is "commenced within such two-year period and
   diligently pursued to completion" (25.05.040(K)(2); 25.07.012(I)(3)). A variance lapses in two
   years unless a building permit issues and work is diligently pursued (25.05.025(H)(2)). Count from
   the effective date (14 days after the decision, 25.05.040(K)(1)) and name the lapse date. Take
   the dates from the record's "Decision dates" (hearing held, completion date, approval
   expiration). When the record check says no decision date is published, say the lapse date cannot
   be computed from the public record, and ask for the resolution or Notice of Decision; never
   estimate one from the applied date. A stated approval expiration governs over your own count.
2. **Alive, same scope, more time needed:** an extension, asked in writing before expiry. Design
   Review: two years, then a final one year, granted "through the administrative design review
   process" by the Director (25.05.040(K)(3); ADR item (B)(3)(a)(xvi)), on findings that the rules and
   the neighborhood have not materially changed ((K)(4)). City CDP: same terms, by the approving
   authority (25.07.012(I)(4)). Variance: the Planning Commission or Board of Adjustment grants it
   (25.05.025(H)(2)(b)). City practice has processed a joint Design Review-and-variance extension as
   one ADR; give the code rule first, then that practice.
2a. **Alive, but its term is ending and the use will go on** (a TUP; a CUP, CDP or other permit whose
   conditions say how long a use or structure may stay). Keep two clocks apart:
   - **Lapse clock:** the time to start using the approval. The item 2 extensions move only this. Each
     code grants "an extension of time of the beginning two-year approval period" (25.05.040(K)(4);
     25.07.012(I)(4)(a); 25.05.025(H)(3); 25.05.030(I)(4)). None of them extends a term of use, so
     never call keeping a use going "an extension of the CDP".
   - **Term:** how long the use may run. "Temporary Use Permits may be authorized for a maximum three
     year time period" (25.05.035(I)), and 25.05.035 has no extension clause. A CUP whose conditions
     set a "Duration of use" (25.05.030(G)(1)(m)) "shall be considered to be revoked upon such date of
     expiration without any notification to the owner" (25.05.030(I)(2)). A CDP's term is one of its
     conditions (25.07.012(H)); a TUP's is too ((G)(4) "Regulation of permit duration").

   The method:
   1. Read the term from the approval (the record check, the resolution or Notice of Decision) and name
      the end date.
   2. **Still temporary?** A use that will end on a known date and fits "interim or temporary uses"
      (25.05.035(A)) takes a **new TUP**, decided by the body 25.05.035(F) names: the Director in R-1
      and for minor events of five days or less (never temporary parking lots), the Planning
      Commission for the rest. A change to a live TUP's duration is no different in practice: duration
      is a condition, so it is never a Director minor change (25.05.060(B)(3)(i)(c)); it "may only be
      approved by the original review authority ... through a new permit application" ((C)(1)). The
      code does not bar a second TUP, but the decider must still find the use interim or temporary.
      Lead with the new TUP; a TUP that ran its term is never renewed, and running out of term does
      not by itself make the use a CUP. Go to step 3 only when the applicant describes the use as a
      permanent part of the business.
   3. **Permanent now?** Run the zone's use list (permitted, AUP or CUP; `discretionary-permits`) as a
      new application. A CUP with a duration condition: file the change with the Planning Commission
      before the end date (25.05.060(C)(1)); after it, the CUP is revoked on its own terms
      ((I)(2)) and only a new CUP can authorize the use.
   4. **CDP (coastal zone).** A temporary use that is development needs a CDP of its own
      (25.05.035(F)(2)). The 25.07.012(I)(4) extension cannot carry the use past its term: it reaches
      only "the beginning two-year approval period", and only for a City CDP not appealed to the
      Commission. So the continued use takes a new CDP, or an amendment of the old CDP's time condition
      (certified: "such permit may be amended by the original approving authority", 25.07.022; Ord.
      1732: a new application to the original review authority, 25.07.012(J)(4)). It is processed
      "concurrently" with the new TUP or use permit, and "the approving body for the coastal
      development permit shall be the same as that for the permit required by the underlying zoning
      district" (25.07.012(D)). So: the Director with a Director TUP; the Planning Commission with a
      Commission TUP or a CUP; the Director alone when only a CDP is needed ((D)(1)). A
      Commission-issued CDP: the Coastal Commission ((J)(5)). Beach events: 25.07.008(A)(6).
   5. **The City Council does not decide any of these at first instance.** It hears appeals
      (25.05.035(H); 25.05.070). When the old approval was a Council decision on appeal, whether an
      amendment of that same approval returns to the Council as "original review authority" is
      UNSETTLED; a new TUP or CUP still starts with the body the code names. Confirm with Planning.
   6. **File before the end date.** After it, the use or structure has no approval until the new
      permits issue. Say so plainly. Detail and examples: `temporary-uses.md`.
3. **Alive, and the resident wants to change it** (add a deck, move a wall, enlarge, reduce).
   **First, is the piece already approved?** Read the approval's full description (and the approved
   plans if the resident has them) before calling anything a change. If the description names the
   piece, it is approved: the building permit carries it out, and no change filing is needed. If the
   description is silent or general ("addition and remodel per plans") and does not clearly exclude
   the piece, say it may already be approved, that the approved plans decide, and that the resident
   should check the stamped set or ask the case planner before filing anything. Only a piece the
   description or plans clearly leave out is a change. **Compare the envelope, not the square
   footage.** When the approved addition area differs from a later building-permit area, ask what
   changed on the outside: footprint, walls, roof height or form, openings. An interior conversion
   inside the existing envelope (a storage room, a garage or an attic finished as living space) is
   an interior modification, Zone Clearance (B)(2)(d): "Interior modifications to existing structures
   that do not modify the exterior of such structures" (Ord. 1732). Our reading: it is not new floor
   area that the Design Review approval had to cover, so the gross-area mismatch alone is not a
   change to the approval. The 25.08 "Floor area, gross" definition excludes garage areas and
   accessory-only basement and attic areas, so such a conversion can still raise gross floor area
   for the standards that count it, and it can trigger other rules: required parking
   (25.52.004(C)(2)), a coastal development permit (`coastal-development-permits.md`),
   an ADU (`../../adu/SKILL.md`). Run those as their own checks. A change in the envelope is a change to the approved
   permit, not a fresh application. Design Review, variance, CUP, AUP or TUP:
   LBMC 25.05.060(B)-(C) (Ord. 1732 §9). The Director may approve a minor change without a hearing
   only if it meets the Zoning Code, does not touch a feature behind the CEQA finding or a condition
   "or that was a specific consideration by the applicable review authority", does not expand or
   change the use, and does not alter the CDP findings (25.05.060(B)(3)(i)); the Director may refer it
   to the original body ((B)(3)(ii)). Anything else "may only be approved by the original review
   authority ... through a new permit application" ((C)(1)); the original conditions stay unless that
   body changes them ((C)(2)). A City CDP: the parallel rule is 25.07.012(J) (Ord. 1732 §15, not yet
   certified); the certified rule is that "such permit may be amended by the original approving
   authority" (25.07.022). A Commission-issued CDP is amended only by the Commission
   (25.07.012(J)(5)). New pieces that sit outside the approved plans still run Steps 2-7 for their own
   list item, and the change is filed with them.
4. **Lapsed:** void; it cannot be extended. Refile; the refiled scope runs Steps 2-7 under today's
   lists (City practice has sent an unchanged refiling to ADR; `process-timeline-appeals`,
   "Extending, reviving or changing an older approval"). Conditions of the old approval no longer bind, but recorded covenants do.
5. **Open building permit** for the same structure: the new work is a revision to that permit or a
   new permit; Planning acts first on the changed scope (25.05.045(B)(3)). Never tell a resident to
   refile an approved, unexpired project as new.
6. **Building permit in plan check, but no Planning record for work that needs one.** Building
   reviews only after Planning: "A zoning clearance shall be obtained before the City issues a new or
   modified building permit" (25.05.045(B)(3)), and the City's plan review handout requires
   "Zoning/Planning approval ... for all submittal plan reviews". A zone clearance may also be only "a
   stamp, signature, or other official notation on approved plans" (25.05.045(C)(2)), with no record
   of its own. So when the record check shows a building permit in plan check for Planning-listed work
   and no DR, ZPC or CDP, assume the approval exists under another record: another address spelling,
   another APN (a lot over two parcels, a condominium or common-area parcel), or a combined filing.
   Say so, ask the resident for the Planning record number or the stamped plans, and do not tell them
   to file again. If they find nothing, the next step is to ask the plan checker, not to file anew.
7. **A related case is pending, but the request needs a different kind of entitlement.** Each kind
   is its own application ("Additional applications are required when an applicant is requesting
   additional entitlements", City application forms). A pending case of one kind never carries another: a lot
   line adjustment is exempt from the map rules only where "a greater number of parcels than
   originally existed is not thereby created" (21.08.030(a)), so creating a lot takes a tentative map
   (21.08.070, 21.08.090) even with an adjustment pending; a CDP does not carry a variance; Design
   Review does not carry a use permit. Name the related case, say the new application is filed beside
   it (or after it where the code sets the order, e.g. 25.95.060(J)), and tell the resident to ask
   Planning to coordinate the two. A change to the same approval is item 3, not a new kind.
8. **A Planning case is pending, and the new request is separate work** (a different structure or
   scope the pending case does not describe). Lead with filing the new work on its own, on the list
   item Steps 2-6 give it; the pending case does not carry it. The case planner may fold it into the
   pending application if it is still open and the work is related; say that is the planner's call,
   not the applicant's ("Additional applications are required when an applicant is requesting
   additional entitlements", City application forms). A change to an already approved plan is
   item 3 (25.05.060), not this. Do not branch the answer on whether the planner folds it in.
the record check shows nothing: say the public record shows no live approval, and that older
approvals may exist on paper; ask the resident only for what the record cannot show.

**Reading the record types.** A zoning plan check (ZPC) marked "Cleared" that is followed by a
Design Review (DR) record at the same parcel means "cleared to file Design Review", not approved:
the DR record decides. ADR is filed as "Design Review - Administrative". A variance is usually filed
the same day as a DRB record and decided with it. A record applied for before 2026-07-22 was routed
under the old lists ("City practice the lists do not show", below); never read it as how Ord. 1732 routes the same work.

## Step 2 — Break the request into pieces

Multi-part requests are the norm. List each structure and each scope item as its own piece: for
example "ADU", "second-floor exterior remodel", "detached accessory building", "new deck",
"grading for a pad". Interior and exterior work on the same structure are separate pieces. A
structure with two uses (a garage with an ADU above) is two pieces. Any outdoor equipment the scope
brings (an AC condenser or mini-split for a new room, a heat-pump water heater's split compressor,
pool pumps, a generator) is its own piece and runs the equipment items and the 7.25.130(C) test;
when the applicant has not said whether a unit sits outside, ask, because it changes the filing.
Run Steps 3-6 on each piece. Combine in Step 7.

## Step 3 — Does the piece need a building permit?

Assume yes. Laguna's exemption list is short (LBMC 14.50.022; `building-permit-exemptions.md`):
for example storage sheds and play sets up to 120 sf and under 12 ft, low walls and fences away
from lot lines, finish work. Electrical, gas or plumbing for an exempt item still needs a permit.
No building permit does not mean no Planning approval.

## Step 4 — Find the Planning list for the piece

**4a. Test the piece against every item on all three lists.** Record every item it meets. This
index is complete; the full text is in `design-review-lists.md`.
- **(B)(1) Design Review:** (a) major remodel; (b) new building (except ADUs and Ch. 25.13
  houses); (c) improvement over 15 ft; (d) historic resource, major work; (e) development in an

- **(B)(3)(a) Administrative Design Review:** (i) landscaping 15 ft or more in the buildable area;

  (g) fuel modification; (h) ADU under Ch. 25.17; (i) grading 20 cy or less outside the footprint;
  (j) retaining wall 5 ft or less; (k) hardscape not adding impervious area, or fire-required; (l)

**City projects.** The Director assigns the capital-project tier "in consultation with other
affected department heads", by the project's "environmental, aesthetic, and/or land use effects":
Major (B)(1)(p), reviewed by the Planning Commission "prior to approval by the City Council of
design and/or construction documents" (and (F)(1)(ii) sends public works Design Review to the
Commission); Minor (B)(3)(a)(xvii), the Director; De Minimis (B)(2)(bb), staff. Nobody
self-classifies: give the three tiers, say the Director decides, and name the decider each tier
gives. The tiers never reach work that state law takes off the lists: solar panels and the batteries
installed as their storage on a City building go to Building only (4a-bis), whatever the system
costs. A project by another public agency (school, water or sanitation district, State) may be
outside City zoning under state law; UNSETTLED here, confirm with Planning.

**4a-bis. State law takes some pieces off the lists.** Test this before the lists.
- **Solar and its storage, on any building. Lead line: "Building only (state law), unless the same
  job reroofs or adds exterior equipment."** "A city or county shall administratively approve
  applications to install solar energy systems through the issuance of a building permit or similar
  nondiscretionary permit" (Gov. Code 65850.5(b)). The duty covers homes, businesses and public
  buildings alike, and "solar energy system" takes the Civil Code 801.5(a)(1)-(2) meaning
  (65850.5(j)(4)), a device whose primary purpose is "the collection, storage, and distribution of
  solar energy" (Civil Code text not in our corpus; the Laguna `solar` skill relies on the same
  reading). So rooftop or onsite PV and batteries paired with it go to Building only; no Planning
  list item applies, City project or not.
  - **Batteries installed with the panels as their storage are solar storage, whatever their size.**
    The statute sets no ratio of battery kWh to panel kW, so never test one; a battery installed in
    the same job as the system's storage is part of the solar energy system.
  - **City capital-project tiers ((B)(1)(p), (B)(3)(a)(xvii), (B)(2)(bb)) never apply** to solar and
    its storage on a City or other public building: the state duty to approve "through the issuance
    of a building permit" is not a City capital-project review.
  - **The exceptions are the same-job extras, each on its own list item:** a reroof is (B)(2)(e)
    when like for like, ADR (B)(3)(a)(ix) for a white, light or glare-prone roof and (x) for a change
    in dimensions, area or pitch; a new carport, trellis, shed or enclosure built to hold the system
    runs the catch-alls. The battery cabinets themselves, indoors or out, are part of the solar
    energy system and run no list item. A battery or generator
    **not** part of a solar system runs the equipment items ((B)(2)(v), (w), (B)(3)(a)(v), (xiv));
    `../../solar/SKILL.md` has the rows.
  - **Fire code review of the storage (CFC 1207) is a Building and Fire item**, done in plan check;
    it is not a Planning step and does not change the route.
  - The one discretionary door: a use permit, only if the building official finds "based on
    substantial evidence" a "specific, adverse impact upon the public health or safety"
    (65850.5(b)); that decision is appealable to the Planning Commission ((d)). City practice
    matches on houses and public buildings alike: rooftop PV with paired batteries goes straight to
    Building, with no Planning or Design Review record. Never route it to ADR or the Planning
    Commission by analogy to utility or telecom equipment ((B)(3)(a)(xiii), (xiv)) or to a City
    capital project.
  - **Coastal zone:** rooftop solar and paired storage on an existing building is an improvement to
    an existing structure, CDP-exempt unless one of the listed exceptions applies
    (25.07.008(A)(1)-(2); PRC 30610(a)-(b)). Cost never turns it into a CDP: the "major energy
    facility" definition excludes development "governed by the provisions of" PRC 30610 (25.07.006).
    Detail: `coastal-development-permits`, Step 3.
- **Solar on a historic resource.** 25.45.010 sends a minor alteration that qualifies for ADR
  ((B)(3)(a)(iv)) and a major modification ((B)(1)(d)) to Heritage Committee review first, and the
  code has no solar exception. State
  law reaches further: solar is "a matter of statewide concern", the Legislature names "design review
  for aesthetic purposes" as an unreasonable barrier (65850.5(a)), and review "shall be limited to the
  building official's review of whether it meets all health and safety requirements" (65850.5(b)).
  Our lean: panels and paired storage on a register property are still Building only; the City
  cannot require ADR, Design Review or Heritage Committee review for them, and the only Planning
  door is the health-and-safety use permit above. Still check: a recorded historic
  resource preservation agreement (25.45.006(B)(3)) is the owner's contract with the City, so ask
  about it before saying nothing else applies; any other work in the same job on the historic resource (reroof with a
  new material, new trellis or carport, structural change) runs 25.45.010 on its own; and a CDP is a
  separate question. No City decision on solar on a historic resource is in
  our records; confirm with Planning when the resident says the house is on the register.
- **ADUs** go to the ADU application ((B)(2)(h); Gov. Code 66317(a)(1)); 4e and 4h.

**4b. The catch-alls.** Every piece also gets these tests:
- (B)(1)(a) Is it, with the rest of the work on that structure, a major remodel? Use
  `../../residential-remodel/references/city-major-remodel-methodology.md`. The method depends on the
  coastal fact: appealable area (removed or reinforced walls, or walls plus roof, interior bearing
  walls and foundation), non-appealable area (removed exterior walls only), outside the coastal
  zone (no handout method; 25.10.008(O) floor; UNSETTLED). Each structure is tested on its own;
  ADUs are excluded; walls are counted floor by floor and the handout does not say how floors
  combine (UNSETTLED). A major remodel is treated as a new building (25.56.011).
- (B)(1)(b) Is it a **new building**? "Building" means "any structure built for the support,
  shelter or enclosure of persons, animals, chattels or property of any kind" (25.08). The
  exceptions are ADUs (B)(2)(h) and Ch. 25.13 houses (B)(2)(z).
- (B)(1)(c) Does the improvement **exceed 15 ft**, measured under "Height, building" (25.08, Ord.
  1732: from the roof surface, "including any roof-mounted or rooftop appurtenance", to the lowest
  floor below or to grade, whichever is lower)? "Improvement" means "any building, structure,
  feature, or object constituting a physical addition to real property or a structure on real
  property, or any part of such addition" (25.08.018, Ord. 1732). Step 4f says how to measure
  common cases.
- (B)(1)(e) Is the piece in an ESA (Step 1)?
- Otherwise: (B)(2)(a) alteration, addition or demolition that is not a major remodel, and (B)(2)(b)
  an improvement not over 15 ft, are Zone Clearance.

**4c. A piece on no list.** The lists are closed (Ord. 1732). A piece that no specific item names
still falls under a catch-all: a new structure or feature is an "improvement" ((B)(2)(b) if 15 ft
or less, (B)(1)(c) if over); a change to an existing structure is (B)(2)(a) unless a major remodel;
an enclosed new structure is also a "building" (see 4g). Name the catch-all and quote its words.
If even the catch-alls do not fit (for example work needing no City permit at all, such as
repainting a non-historic house, or a new use with no construction, which takes Zone Clearance at
"initiation" or "change" of use, 25.05.045(B)(1)-(2)), say so and send the question to
pre-application.

**4c-bis. Repair or refinish with no change** (same size, material and operation: patching stucco,
re-coating a deck, replacing rotted boards or a damaged window in kind). In-kind replacement is on a
list, not off the lists: (B)(2)(c) "In-kind replacement of improvements for repair and maintenance"
sits under "Exempt from Design Review (Zone Clearance)", which exempts it from Design Review, not
from Planning. Decide it in three questions:
1. Does it need a building permit (Step 3)? Finish work such as painting, tiling and cabinets does
   not (14.50.022, Building 3). If no permit is needed and nothing is replaced, no list item applies
   and there is no City step (4c), except on a historic resource.
2. Does it need a building permit and touch the exterior? Zone Clearance first: "A zoning clearance
   shall be obtained before the City issues a new or modified building permit" (25.05.045(B)(3)).
   This is usually same-day at the counter (City practice table below). Our records show no exterior
   in-kind work that Building took with no Planning step; do not tell a resident it is Building-only.
3. Interior only? (B)(2)(d); Building accepts it with no prior Planning record (City practice table).
   **Except a conversion of non-habitable space to habitable space** (a garage, storage room, attic
   or basement finished as a bathroom, bedroom or other living space). That is not Building only:
   lead with a Zone Clearance counter check before the building permit (25.05.045(B)(3)), because
   the conversion can change two zoning standards. Parking: required parking "shall not be
   eliminated, reduced or converted in any manner unless equivalent facilities approved by the city
   are provided elsewhere" (25.52.004(C)(2)), so a garage conversion needs replacement spaces or a
   variance. Floor area: "Floor area, gross" (25.08) leaves out garage and accessory-only attic and
   basement areas, so the conversion adds counted floor area, which the planner checks against any
   floor-area limit the zone or specific plan sets (Step 6 item 4). A conversion that creates a unit is an ADU instead
   (`../../adu/SKILL.md`); in the coastal zone, check the CDP exemption too
   (`coastal-development-permits`).
A change in size, material, location or operation is not in kind: 4d rule 2.

**4d. Precedence: which item governs the piece.** Apply these rules in order.
1. **Always apply, whatever else matches:** a major-remodel finding (B)(1)(a) for the structure;
   the ESA push-up (B)(1)(e) (the (B)(2) and (B)(3) preambles, 4e); and the Director's power to refer
   any Zone Clearance or ADR piece to a hearing (25.05.040(A)(2)(d)). Mention referral; never predict it.
2. **In-kind replacement governs a true like-for-like rebuild.** (B)(2)(c) covers replacing an
   existing improvement for repair and maintenance, even one another list names when built new (an
   elevated deck, a retaining wall, a fence, rooftop equipment). Otherwise (c) would be empty for
   those items. "In kind" is shown on the drawings: same size, location, height, type, materials and
   operation. Any change makes it an alteration or new work, and the item for the changed thing
   governs (City intake ruling, Sept. 2026). What breaks it: a visible
   change (taller or different guard, new railing type, larger footprint, new material). What does
   not, on our reading: hidden upgrades the building code requires to rebuild the same thing (deeper
   or engineered footings, new connectors, a code-height guard where the old one was low). Those keep
   the same "size, location, height, type" above grade; a guard raised only to code height is a
   railing, (B)(2)(r), and "Barricades, railings and security fencing required by the building code
   are permitted in any yard" (25.50.012(B)(2)). Excavate-and-refill for a new foundation counts as grading only for what lies
   outside the footprint (`grading-drainage`). The City has not ruled on hidden code upgrades:
   UNSETTLED, confirm at pre-application. **Nonconforming rebuilds:** "If any part of a nonconforming
   portion of the structure is substantially removed or modified in such a way that it compromises
   the structural integrity of the building, that portion must be rebuilt in conformance with zoning
   regulations" (25.56.009). So rebuilding a deck, stair or wall that sits in a setback, past the
   bluff setback or over the height limit loses its nonconforming status once it is substantially
   removed: it must meet today's standard, or get a variance (Step 6). Repair that leaves the
   structure standing keeps it (25.56.002). Disaster damage has its own five-year right to rebuild
   (25.56.014): `disaster-rebuild.md`.
3. **A specific item governs its subject over the general catch-alls.** When a named item describes
   the thing being built (equipment, deck, railing, reroof, roof change, skylight, pool, wall,
   fence, landscaping), that item decides it. The general catch-alls (B)(1)(c) "any improvement" over
   15 ft, (B)(2)(a) and (B)(2)(b) do not override it ("when a general and particular provision are
   inconsistent, the latter is paramount", Code Civ. Proc. 1859). Example of the check: equipment on
   a roof, deck or balcony is not "ground-mounted" (7.25.130(C)(1)), so it is ADR (B)(3)(a)(v), not
   (B)(1)(c), however high it sits. **Use (B)(1)(c) only where the work itself creates new height:**
   a new building, an addition or other new structure whose own roof is over 15 ft, a raised roof,
   or a new exterior elevator or other non-equipment rooftop appurtenance (4f). Work on an existing
   building that stays the same height is never Design Review on height alone; route it by its own
   items. City practice since Ord. 1732 has applied (B)(1)(c) exactly this way. A new building, new
   floor area or a raised roof is a separate piece that still meets (B)(1)(b) or (c).
4. **Two specific items on different lists cover the same thing:** the highest list governs
   ((B)(1) over (B)(3) over (B)(2)). This is City practice (one design-review tier) and the City
   matrix. Where the ordinance itself sorts two items (ADUs and Ch. 25.13 houses carved out of
   (B)(1)(b); (B)(1)(n) trash "except as provided in (B)(2) and (B)(3)"), follow the ordinance.
5. **No specific item covers the thing:** the catch-alls in 4b decide.
6. **City intake practice beyond the ordinance:** a deck on top of a structure, including an ADU
   roof deck, "establishes that Design Review is required"; a pool, a ground-level lounge deck or a
   patio cover alone does not (City Planning intake ruling, Sept. 2026). The ordinance item alone
   would give ADR (B)(3)(a)(iii) for such a deck over 3 ft. The rule reaches only a deck on top of
   enclosed space; an open raised deck (posts, cantilever, nothing enclosed under it) is ADR (iii),
   not Design Review (4f). Headline the ordinance item (Step 7,
   headline tier); list the intake practice as a fact that could move the piece up to Design Review,
   and say no post-Ord. 1732 decision has tested it yet. Confirm at
   pre-application. **First check whether the deck is
   allowed at all:** in the coastal zone an ADU roof deck is prohibited ("a deck above the uppermost
   story of a building, with no access from interior living space at the same floor level",
   25.17.020(c)(5)(B), Ord. 1713); Three Arch Bay bans roof decks (25.44.050(M)); Arch Beach Heights
   bans them above the uppermost story (25.35.080(B)(9)). A ban is a development standard (Step 6b),
   not a review tier.
Each piece keeps its own result; Step 7 combines them.

**4e. ESA overlay on every piece.** The (B)(2) and (B)(3) preambles say the lower process "shall
not be used" for projects "in an environmentally sensitive area" unless a licensed professional
finds, subject to peer review, no impact; such projects go to (B)(1). Two open questions:
- **No ground disturbance** (interior work, an upper-floor remodel) on a lot with 30% slopes. The
  preamble is written by location ("projects that are in"), but (B)(1)(e) says "development in" an
  ESA, and Title 25 does not define "development" outside Ch. 25.07. The no-impact finding is the
  practical route: get a geotechnical or biological letter early. UNSETTLED. This question is only
  about work that adds nothing new: interior work, in-kind repair, window and door swaps in existing
  openings, and re-cladding or re-siding an existing wall with no ground disturbance all add nothing
  new. A new structure, addition, deck or rooftop appurtenance on a lot
  over 30% is Design Review under 25.50.040 whatever ground it disturbs (Step 1, slope row).
- **ADUs.** State law requires ADU permits to be "considered and approved ministerially without
  discretionary review or a hearing, notwithstanding ... any local ordinance" (Gov. Code
  66317(a)(1)), and no local rule outside a conforming ADU ordinance may delay or deny one
  (66317(c)). Ord. 1713 25.17.010(e)(2)(A) repeats this for ADUs without a CDP. So the ESA push-up
  should not turn an ADU into Design Review; coastal habitat issues go through the ADU's CDP
  (66329(a) keeps the Coastal Act), and slope through Building geotechnical review. The City has
  not said. UNSETTLED.

**4f. Measuring height for common cases** (25.08 "Height, building" (a), Ord. 1732).
- **Existing upper floor, no height change.** (B)(1)(c) measures an improvement by "Height,
  building", which runs from a roof surface or rooftop appurtenance to the lowest floor or grade.
  Work that creates no new or raised roof surface and no new floor area, story or rooftop
  appurtenance therefore does not "exceed a height of 15 feet". Do not measure the existing building.
  This covers window and door replacement or resizing, siding and stucco, deck resurfacing, repair or
  like-for-like rebuild, railings and guards, and in-kind roof equipment. Route it by the other lists:
  Zone Clearance under (B)(2)(a), (c), (r), (e) or (v); ADR when a (B)(3) item is present (a new or
  enlarged deck more than 3 ft above grade (iii), a roof pitch, size or dimension change (x),
  equipment failing 7.25.130(C) (v), an unshaded skylight (xii)). City practice since Ord. 1732
  confirms this: such work has gone to the counter, and to ADR only when a (B)(3) item was present
  (an enlarged upper deck or balcony, a roof change), never to the DRB for height.
- **New story below an existing floor** (basement, lower level, walk-out). Height runs to "the
  finished floor surface of the lowest floor measured directly below", but "if the entire lowest
  floor ... is located completely below natural or finished grade ... the building height shall be
  measured to the top of the finished floor of the next level directly above". So a fully
  subterranean level does not change measured height. A walk-out or partly exposed level lowers the
  measuring point, so the building's measured height grows. Whether that makes the new level "an
  improvement that exceeds 15 feet" (the whole building measured to the new floor), or whether only
  the new level's own height counts, is UNSETTLED. City practice has gone both ways: one lower-level
  addition was taken on the Zone Clearance Application, while another went to the DRB without a
  stated trigger. Never call either route near-certain; give both readings and say the measured
  height to the new lowest floor decides. The same measurement feeds the zone height limit (Step 6).
- **Replacement roof no higher than the old one.** The specific roof items govern (rule 3): same
  shape and material, (B)(2)(e) or (c); a change in pitch, area or dimensions, ADR (B)(3)(a)(x). Our
  reading: (B)(1)(c) is not triggered when the new roof is no higher than the old one and adds no
  floor area, because nothing new "exceeds" 15 ft that did not already. Raising any part of the roof
  above its old height and above 15 ft is (B)(1)(c). Lowering part of a roof: City practice has
  cleared one at the counter, which suggests a pure reduction is not treated as an (x) alteration;
  that is a single decision, so treat it as UNSETTLED. Rebuilding the whole roof frame to a new shape above 15 ft but no higher:
  UNSETTLED, confirm at pre-application.
- **Covers hung from or attached to a wall** (patio cover, awning, trellis over a deck): a covered
  structure has a roof surface, so measure from its top to the lowest floor or grade directly below.
  Over 15 ft (a cover over an upper-floor deck usually is) points to (B)(1)(c); no list item names
  patio covers. An awning replaced in kind is (B)(2)(c), at the counter. A covered porch or
  deck is "part of the building" for setbacks (25.50.008(C) note).
- **Access structure that serves only an ADU** (exterior stair, elevator, bridge): read it as part
  of the ADU, reviewed on the ADU application against Ch. 25.17 height and setbacks, since state law
  bars discretionary review of the ADU (Gov. Code 66317(a)(1)). The other reading: a separate
  improvement under (B)(1)(c) if over 15 ft. Exterior elevators serving a house have gone to the DRB
  under (B)(1)(c). UNSETTLED; confirm at pre-application.
- **New roof surface, new story, or addition** with its own roof: measure the new roof's highest
  point to the lowest floor below it or to grade, whichever is lower. Over 15 ft: (B)(1)(c).
- **New rooftop appurtenance.** Mechanical equipment: rule 3 above (equipment items). Other
  appurtenances (elevator or stair overrun, screen wall): a literal reading of the Ord. 1732
  definition makes a top more than 15 ft up (B)(1)(c); exterior elevators have gone to the DRB on
  that reading. UNSETTLED for small appurtenances.
- **New or enlarged deck more than 3 ft above grade, including one more than 15 ft up.** The
  ordinance item is ADR (B)(3)(a)(iii): "decks, slabs, terraces, or patios elevated more than 3 feet
  above the adjacent, legal ground elevation", with no upper limit, and the hearing cannot be waived
  ((B)(3)(b)). Rule 3 of 4d makes that specific item govern over the (B)(1)(c) catch-all. Ask what is
  under the deck; only one answer can bring Design Review in.
  - **Open deck (on posts, on grade fill, or a balcony cantilevered from a wall), nothing enclosed
    under it:** ADR (iii), at any height. Not Design Review: (B)(1)(c) measures by "Height, building",
    which runs from "the finished roof surface, including any roof-mounted or rooftop appurtenance",
    and an open deck has neither. The City's deck-on-a-structure intake rule (4d item 6) does not
    reach it.
  - **Deck on top of enclosed space (a roof deck, or a deck over a room or garage):** headline ADR
    (iii), the ordinance item (4d item 6; Step 7). Then list the two facts that could move it to
    Design Review, and say this is the only deck case where Design Review applies: (1) City intake
    treats a deck on top of a structure as establishing Design Review (Sept. 2026 ruling, untested
    after Ord. 1732); (2) if a guard, stair or elevator housing on the roof
    is a "rooftop appurtenance" (not defined; our reading is that it is), the building's height now
    runs to its top, and a top more than 15 ft up raises the building past 15 ft, which is (B)(1)(c)
    like a raised roof (4d rule 3, last sentence). Confirm at pre-application.
  - **Adding a roof or cover over any deck** gives it a roof surface: measure that (covers bullet
    above).
  - Lot over 30% slope or other ESA: Design Review either way (Step 1).
- **Second-story ADU, or an ADU over a garage.** The ordinance carves ADUs out of (B)(1)(b) only;
  (B)(2)(h) sends ADUs to Zone Clearance "only as set forth in Chapter 25.17". State law requires ADU
  approval "ministerially without discretionary review or a hearing" (Gov. Code 66317(a)(1)). Read
  together, the ADU itself is reviewed on the ADU & JADU Application against Ch. 25.17 height
  standards, not sent to the DRB under (B)(1)(c). The ADU's decks, any garage below, and any
  house addition are separate pieces. Before Ord. 1732, projects mixing a second-floor ADU with a house
  addition went to the DRB as a whole. The City has not ruled since. UNSETTLED.
- **Garage, new or rebuilt, with or without an ADU above:** a detached garage is a new building
  ((B)(1)(b)); an attached one is an addition ((B)(2)(a) unless a major remodel or over 15 ft). The
  ADU portion stays on its own ADU application. How the City splits one structure between the two
  applications is UNSETTLED; confirm at pre-application.

**4g. Known open conflicts.** Mark these UNSETTLED, give both readings, and give the City practice
stated here where there is one:
- **Detached accessory building** (sauna, studio, gym, pool house, enclosed cabana, workshop):
  (B)(1)(b) "all new buildings" (Design Review) versus (B)(2)(b) "improvement that does not exceed
  15 feet" (Zone Clearance). (B)(1)(b) carves out only ADUs and Ch. 25.13 houses, which favors
  Design Review. City practice favors Zone Clearance for small, low ones (a backyard sauna well under
  15 ft); a prefab storage shed is still taken on a full zoning plan check, not at the counter. A
  storage shed or play set that meets (B)(2)(x) is Zone Clearance, because (x)
  would be empty otherwise.
- **Small factory-built or portable enclosed items** (prefab or barrel sauna, cold-plunge or spa
  enclosure, office pod): (B)(2)(x) does not reach them by its words. It covers "Temporary on-grade
  removable accessory structures used as play sets, swing sets, other similar unenclosed recreation
  equipment, and storage sheds", and Building exemption 6 (14.50.022) has the same limits. An
  enclosed item people go into is neither: it is a "building" (25.08) and runs the conflict above.
  Measure it against (x)'s numbers (120 sf or less, under 12 ft, setbacks met) only to say why the
  Zone Clearance reading is stronger for it, never as the rule. It needs a building permit, and an
  electrical permit for a heater. Over 15 ft, or in an ESA: Design Review either way.
- **Open structures** (pergola, trellis, open cabana, outdoor kitchen, fire pit): improvements, not
  enclosures, so (B)(2)(b) or (B)(1)(c) by height. Whether a roofed open structure is a "building"
  is part of the same question.
- **Fire pit or fire ring versus fireplace.** ADR (B)(3)(a)(xi) names "Detached fireplaces". Title
  25 does not define a fireplace; the building code does: "A hearth and fire chamber or similar
  prepared place in which a fire may be made and which is built in conjunction with a chimney" (CBC
  202). So an open gas fire pit, bowl or ring with no firebox and no chimney is not a fireplace: it is
  an improvement, Zone Clearance (B)(2)(b) at 15 ft or less (the gas line takes a building permit). A
  freestanding outdoor fireplace with a firebox and a chimney is (xi), ADR, whatever the fuel. One
  built onto the house is part of that alteration ((B)(2)(a)); its chimney may project 12 in into a
  yard (25.50.008(B)). Borrowing the CBC definition is our reading; confirm at pre-application.
- **Hardscape that adds impervious area**: (B)(2)(k) names only hardscape that does not; (B)(2)(b)
  may still cover it. UNSETTLED.
- City matrix rows that differ from the ordinance: Step 8.

**4h. An ADU project's other pieces.** The ADU unit is (B)(2)(h). Every other piece filed with it
(a deck over 3 ft, added covered or tandem parking (B)(3)(a)(vi), equipment, exterior work on the
host house) runs Step 4 like any other piece, so an ADU project can carry an ADR or Design Review
application beside the ADU application. On a lot that already has two or more houses, City practice
has opened an ADR record beside the ADU without stating why, likely for the non-ADU pieces (parking,
site work, the second house). The ADU's own standards stay ministerial (4e), so that ADR cannot deny
the ADU on design grounds. Say Planning may open one and confirm at pre-application. Never say
flatly "no Design Review" for the whole project.

**4i. Who decides** (25.05.040(A)(2), (F)(1)): Zone Clearance, staff, no hearing; ADR, the
Director, hearing unless waived after a 15-working-day notice (not for decks over 3 ft, historic
work, or a revocable encroachment permit, 11.50.035); Design Review for "all single-family or two-family developments", the **DRB**; for
"three-families or more", Downtown, nonresidential, public works, or a project the Planning
Commission decides for another permit (a CUP), the **Planning Commission**. An ADU does not make a
house or duplex "three-families or more" on our reading: an ADU "shall be deemed to be an accessory
use or an accessory building and shall not be considered to exceed the allowable density" (Gov.
Code 66319), and it is not part of the Design Review application. UNSETTLED for a duplex with ADUs;
confirm at pre-application. Variances: `discretionary-permits` (Board of Adjustment rule).

## Step 6 — Check the zoning standards for the piece (the variance check)

Standards do not set the list, but a piece that fails one cannot be approved as drawn. Run this on
every piece that adds footprint, floor area or height, moves into a yard, or sits on a constrained
lot, including when the resident states a dimension. Test each stated or likely number against the
standard and say plainly when it fails: a stated 3-ft side setback, a 16-ft garage on a 14-ft
street, a roof deck in a zone that bans them. Never leave the variance to "the designer will check".

**Stated-number check, every piece.** Before routing is final, write one line per piece:
- **Coverage.** Compute the limit from lot area and zone: R-1 is 44% at 4,000 sf or less, 35% at
  8,000 sf or more, and 35 + ((8,000 - lot area) x 9) / 4,000 between (25.10.008(E)(1)(c)); other
  zones and specific plans use their own table (6b). Add existing plus new habitable enclosed space
  and covered parking, and compare.
- **Height.** Compare the stated height to the zone limit (item 3) or, for an ADU, Ch. 25.17: 16 ft
  detached (18 ft near major transit, up to 20 ft to match the primary roof pitch, or on a lot with
  a multistory multifamily dwelling); attached,
  25 ft or the zone limit, whichever is lower, and two stories at most (25.17.010(f)(2)(A)-(D), Ord.
  1713), measured from existing legal grade or the lowest floor, whichever is lower, to the peak
  ((f)(2)(E)).
- **Unknown dimensions or a substandard lot.** When an addition grows the footprint or the height
  and the resident has not given the dimensions, or the lot is smaller, narrower or shallower than
  the zone minimum, add one line naming the possible Variance (25.05.025) and the standard it would
  be for ("a Variance if the addition goes into the side setback", "a Variance if the new roof
  passes the zone height"). One line, not a branch; ask for the number that decides it.
- **Result.** "Fits" or "fails by N". When it fails, name the route: redesign; for R-1 coverage, the
  DRB may add up to "five percentage points" on a listed finding (25.10.008(E)(1)(d), Ord. 1732);
  for an ADU standard in 25.17.010(a)-(i), a conditional use permit (25.17.010(j); Planning
  Commission, item 9 and Step 7); otherwise a variance (item 7). If the number is not stated, say
  which number decides and ask for it.

1. **Yards.** Read the zone's setbacks (a through lot has two front yards and no rear yard,
   25.50.018). What may sit in a required yard (25.50.008): eaves, balconies, bay windows and similar
   features may cantilever 20% of a front or rear yard and 40% of a side yard (not balconies), never
   closer than 2.5 ft to a side line ((A)); a chimney 12 in ((B)); uncovered porches, patios, fill
   terraces, landings and unenclosed uncovered stairs, no higher than the first floor and no more
   than 3 ft above natural grade, up to 6 ft into a yard, never closer than 3 ft to an interior side
   line ((C)). A covered or enclosed porch, patio or stair "shall be considered as part of the
   building" and may not enter the yard ((C) note). **Decks and paving in a yard.** Title 25 defines
   neither "patio" nor "deck". Flush paving, walks and patios at grade are not a "structure" (25.08:
   "except outdoor areas such as patios, paved areas, walks, tennis courts and other similar
   recreation areas"), so (C) does not limit them; a yard stays "open from ground to sky, except for
   the projections and/or accessory buildings permitted by this title" (25.08 "Yard"). An uncovered
   on-grade or low deck reads as a patio or terrace under (C) (our reading: the code tests height and
   cover, not material): it may go up to 6 ft into a yard, never closer than 3 ft to an interior side
   line, if it is no higher than the first floor and "not more than three feet above the natural
   grade". A deck more than 3 ft up gets no (C) allowance, and a covered deck is part of the building.
   **A deck past those limits needs a variance (25.05.025; item 7), so when the resident says the deck
   goes into a setback, lead with that test and the variance branch**, then the list item (4f). Pools and spas with their equipment: not in the
   front yard, at least 5 ft from side and rear lines; on lots sloping 25% or more over the rear half,
   the pool may come within 10 ft of the front line (25.50.016). In R-1, Ord. 1732 removed air
   conditioning and pool equipment from the side-yard ban (25.10.008(E)(3)(a)(2)); the general
   side-yard rule still lists them (25.50.004(F)(2)). Which governs: UNSETTLED. Portions over 15 ft
   above natural grade in a front or rear setback add one foot of setback per foot of height
   (25.50.004(D)). Significant watercourse on the City ESA map: 25-ft setback each side of the
   centerflow line, no building or structure in it except access bridges; decks may overhang with no
   ground supports, no closer than 15 ft (25.50.030(D)); an encroachment variance needs two extra
   findings ((D)(4)).
2. **Fences and walls** (25.50.012(B)): front yard 4 ft, corner lot 3 ft; side and rear 6 ft, the 6
   ft also capping a retaining wall that makes a fill terrace ((B)(3)); decorative posts up to 12 in
   over the front-yard limit are ADR (B)(3)(a)(vii); pedestrian entry features up to 8 ft
   ((B)(6), Ord. 1732). A trash screen in the front setback must be 4 ft (3 ft on a corner lot)
   ((B)(3)(a)(xv)), the same as the front-yard cap, so it fits; a taller screen fails 25.50.012(B)(1)
   and needs a variance. Zone overrides: Three Arch Bay front-yard walls 42 in (25.44.050(I)(1)).
3. **Height.** The zone table (R-1 25.10.008(D), from the "Height, building" point in 4f); the
   citywide cap: no building, addition or structure over 30 ft from finished or natural grade,
   "whichever is more restrictive", which the DRB may modify only on unique topography findings
   (25.50.005). Special height area: "no building or structure shall have a height greater than
   fourteen feet above the mean front curb height" (25.10.010(B)); the parcel lookup shows the area.
   Any part over it, a garage roof at the front line included, needs a variance (item 7).
4. **Coverage and floor area.** R-1: 44% at 4,000 sf or less, 35% at 8,000 sf or more, 35 +
   ((8,000 - lot area) x 9) / 4,000 between (25.10.008(E)(1)(c)); existing plus new habitable
   enclosed space and covered parking. Three Arch Bay adds a floor-area cap of 1.5 times the coverage
   percentage (25.44.050(D)) and its own coverage formula (25.44.050(C)). Village Community has its own
   floor-area cap (25.43.070(H)).
5. **Bluff top.** 25 ft from the top of an oceanfront bluff or the stringline, the stricter, for any
   "building, ... structures or improvements", so a garden retaining wall or stair is covered too
   (25.50.004(B)(4); "Improvement", 25.08.018). Is the work a **protective device** ((B)(1)(i),
   Design Review)? Yes when its purpose is to hold the bluff or protect upland structures from erosion
   or sliding ("Shore protective device", 25.08: "any structure or partition to retain or prevent
   sliding of the land, or any device for the purpose of protecting the upland"); a wall built for a
   yard or terrace is a retaining wall under (B)(1)(g)/(B)(2)(j) but still meets the bluff setback.
   Rebuilding bluff stairs or walls that are substantially removed: they must conform (25.56.009),
   so a variance or redesign. A remodel near a bluff: run the major remodel test with the
   roof-walls-foundation combined prong in the appealable area
   (`../../residential-remodel/references/city-major-remodel-methodology.md`).
6. **A standard with its own modification clause:** name the body and the limit. R-1 coverage: the
   DRB may add up to five percentage points on a listed finding (25.10.008(E)(1)(d), Ord. 1732); the
   30-ft cap (25.50.005); a bluff stringline (25.50.004(B)(4)(b)(ii)). That puts the project before
   the DRB even when every piece is Zone Clearance. Beyond the clause's limit, a variance.
7. **A failed standard with no modification clause:** redesign, or a Variance (25.05.025). Who
   decides: for a single-family or two-family project the DRB, sitting as the Board of Adjustment,
   hears the variance with the Design Review (25.05.025(E), (J); 25.05.065(B)); the Planning
   Commission hears the variances 25.05.025(J)(1) gives it and those tied to its own matters
   (`discretionary-permits`). City practice since
   Ord. 1732: a variance takes the whole project to a hearing, with a Design Review record filed the
   same day and decided with it, even when every other piece is Zone Clearance or ADR. So once any
   piece needs a variance, re-pick the hearing body for the whole project (Step 7).
8. **Major remodel:** the structure is treated as a new building and must meet current standards
   (25.56.011). List the existing nonconformities; each one that will stay needs a variance or a
   redesign. Additions to a nonconforming structure that is not a major remodel: the
   addition itself must comply in every respect (25.56.008(A)). Rebuilding a nonconforming part:
   25.56.009 (Step 4d rule 2).
9. **ADU standards** come from the ADU skill (`../../adu/SKILL.md`). An ADU that misses an objective

## Step 7 — Combine the pieces

- **Write the lead route first.** After combining, state the single most likely route in the
  answer's first lines: what to file, with whom, and in what order (for example "File a Zone
  Clearance at the Planning counter, then the building permit"). It comes from the facts given, the
  ordinance default and the usual case for that kind of work. Every other route gets one line,
  "changes to X if Y", and only when a fact could trigger it. A fact that routes the same either way
  (which of two parcels, which of two readings with the same filing) gets no line. Mark UNSETTLED
  only where the answer would change what the applicant files, and ask for a missing fact only when
  the route turns on it. Documents are listed for the lead filing(s) only.
- **Headline tier: the ordinance default.** The tier you lead with comes from the code: the list item
  the precedence rules pick (Step 4), plus any overlay or standard that the facts in hand trigger
  (Steps 5-6). City intake-practice notes and UNSETTLED questions never set the headline. List them
  after it as facts that could move the project up a tier, each with what would decide it ("if the
  City applies its roof-deck intake rule, Design Review instead of ADR"). A default the code itself
  sets on known facts, such as Design Review on a lot screened at 30% slope (Step 1), is the
  ordinance default, not practice.
- **One Design Review Application** covers every Design Review and ADR piece; the City publishes no
  separate ADR form. The highest list among the pieces sets the decider and the hearing. Each piece
  still keeps its own list item; the ordinance has no project-wide merge rule (25.05.040(B)).
- **Re-pick the hearing body after every piece is routed.** If any piece needs a use permit or
  another Planning Commission entitlement, the Commission hears the Design Review too: it takes
  "projects for which the planning commission is the primary discretionary review authority, such as
  approval of a conditional use permit" (25.05.040(F)(1)(ii)); "The Planning Commission shall review
  all Conditional Use Permit applications", and decides the CUP first when the Board of Adjustment
  also acts (25.05.030(E)). The CDP follows: "the approving body for the coastal development permit shall be
  the same as that for the permit required by the underlying zoning district" (25.07.012(D)(1)).
  So a house project at the DRB moves to the Commission once, say, an ADU setback CUP (Step 6 item 9)
  joins it. Name the body only after this check.
- **Add-on applications** ride beside it, never instead of it: "Additional applications are required
  when an applicant is requesting additional entitlements" (City application forms). Variance, use permit, sign
  permit and the like each have their own application; a CDP has its own package and completeness
  check.
- **Zone Clearance pieces** in a project that also files Design Review: show them on the same plans.
  Whether they ride on the Design Review Application or need the Zone Clearance Application is not
  stated. Confirm at pre-application.
- **ADU or JADU**: always its own ADU & JADU Application, filed in parallel and kept separate
  (`process-timeline-appeals.md`, "Several projects at once"). City intake: converting existing
  habitable space needs only the ADU application; converting non-habitable space or adding floor
  area also needs a CDP Application in the coastal zone. As-built floor plans decide which.
- **CDP**: one CDP can cover the non-ADU pieces; when Design Review is required, the same body
  decides the CDP at the same time (25.07.012(D)). How to pick the CDP kind when an ADU is in the
  project: `coastal-development-permits.md`, "Picking the kind for a project with an
  ADU".
- **Building permits**: one Building Permit Application per scope, each with its own valuation (City
  Planning intake ruling, Sept. 2026). A remodel, an ADU and a pool on one building application is
  incomplete even with complete drawings. Grading over 20 cy is its own application.
- Load the permit skill for each piece that has one (list in the guide router) for plan detail.
- **Documents:** after the approvals, call the City checklists once per lane the approvals
  name (planning, building) and give each filing's document list from it.

10. ADU below an objective standard: CUP (25.17.010(j)) or variance (Step 6).
11. A removable shed in a setback through ADR (B)(2)(x)(iii) versus the 25.08 accessory building rule.
12. An ADU in a duplex project and the DRB-versus-Commission split (4i).

## How to write the answer

1. **The lead route** in the first one to three lines: what to file, with whom, in what order
   (Step 7). Then the alternatives, one "changes to X if Y" line each.
2. Address and facts used, naming only the facts that change the route.
3. One short block per piece: building permit; list item, the precedence rule that picked it, and
   who decides; overlays; standards it fails and what that needs (variance, modification, redesign).
   The headline tier is the ordinance default (Step 7); practice and UNSETTLED points that could
   raise it follow it, one line each.
4. The order of steps (`process-timeline-appeals.md`) when there are two or more
   filings.
5. The documents for the lead filing(s), from the City checklists per lane.
6. The UNSETTLED points that would change what the applicant files, phrased as questions for
   pre-application. Leave out open questions that do not change the filing.

## Illustrations of the method

These show the steps, not a catalog. Run Steps 1-8 on the actual project.

**A. One small piece.** Replace windows, same size and operation, non-historic house. Building
permit: yes. Specific item: (B)(2)(c) in-kind, so Zone Clearance. Overlays: CDP exempt for a
single-family improvement unless within 50 ft of a bluff edge, in an ESA or highly scenic area
(25.07.008(A)(1)(b)); WUI window rules. Register historic resource: ADR (B)(3)(a)(iv) after the
Heritage Committee (in-kind work and rated-only properties: `historic-properties.md` section 2).
`../../window-replacement/SKILL.md`.

**B. A piece on no list: a detached accessory building** (sauna, studio, gym, pool house), 11 ft
tall, 150 sf, in the rear yard. Building permit: yes; over 120 sf and not a storage shed, so no
exemption. Specific items: none ((B)(2)(x) covers only temporary sheds and play equipment).
Catch-alls: it encloses persons, so "new building" (B)(1)(b); it is also an improvement under 15 ft
(B)(2)(b). UNSETTLED; City practice favors Zone Clearance for a small, low one (4g). Over 15 ft: Design Review
(B)(1)(c) either way. ESA: Design Review absent a no-impact finding. Standards: 25.08 accessory
building conditions, 10 ft from other buildings, no front or side yards (R-1), coverage if it is
habitable enclosed space, guest-house rule if it has sleeping space. CDP: normally exempt; in the
first-road or 300-ft strip a new detached structure needs one (25.07.008(A)(1)(c)3). Fire: WUI rules
for accessory structures (`fire-hazard-and-wui.md`).

**C. Remodel of an existing upper floor.** New windows and siding on an existing second story, no
roof change. Building permit: yes. List: (B)(2)(a) unless a major remodel; run the per-floor wall
count. Not (B)(1)(c) (4f). A roof pitch or size change adds ADR (B)(3)(a)(x); raising the roof or
adding floor area above 15 ft is Design Review (B)(1)(c). Sprinkler retrofit has its own scope and
valuation triggers (`../../residential-remodel/references/city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`). `../../residential-remodel/SKILL.md`.

**D. A multi-part project on a constrained lot.** ADU, second-floor remodel and a backyard sauna on
an R-1 lot that is coastal (not appealable), VHFHSZ with FM, WQESA, in a CGS landslide zone, with an
estimated 40% slope. Pieces: three. ESA first: if a survey confirms slopes of 30% or more, the
sauna and any ground-disturbing work go to Design Review absent a peer-reviewed no-impact finding,
and whether the upper-floor remodel does too is open (4e). The survey comes first. ADU: (B)(2)(h) on
its own ADU & JADU Application with a parallel CDP, 60-day state clock, not held for the remodel's
hearing (66317(a)(3)). Remodel: Illustration C; if a major remodel, DRB plus Fire Safety Standards
Review, fuel modification plan, a CDP decided with the Design Review (25.07.012(D)), and a variance
for any nonconformity that stays. Sauna: Illustration B. Combined: one Design Review Application if
any non-ADU piece reaches ADR or Design Review. Building: three applications; geotechnical report
with peer review; Public Works water quality (WQESA). Documents: planning and building lanes.

**E. A new house.** Design Review (B)(1)(b) at the DRB with Fire Safety Standards Review, or Zone
Clearance (B)(2)(z) if the applicant elects the Ch. 25.13 objective path at filing and meets both
gates: the Ch. 25.13 development standards (one story, 15 ft or less, setbacks 20% larger, Table A
floor area; 25.13.020(A), 25.13.040) and all 13 General Design Standards in City doc 26790 plus every
standard in the one Form Group the applicant picks (ask; never infer it). Not open to a major
remodel (25.13.030(C)). Coastal zone: CDP either way. `../../single-family-residential/SKILL.md`.
