**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Authority:** LBMC Chapter 25.45 as amended by Ord. 1732 §§40-44 (adopted 2026-07-21), Ord.
1732 changes at the City source document (not included in this plugin)
(the overlay controls);
LBMC 25.05.040(B)(1)(d) and (B)(3)(a)(iv) (Ord. 1732); City historic preservation page.
**Use for:** "Is my house historic?", "What does a K or C rating mean for my remodel?", "Do I
need the Heritage Committee?", "Can I get on the register / Mills Act?", "Can I demolish an old
house?"

## 1. Is my property a "historic resource"?

**No age trigger.** Laguna historic review keys off the register, a state or national listing, the
1981 inventory rating, or substantial evidence, never off a building's age. Chapter 25.45 as amended
has no "50 years or older" rule; any source that says a 50-plus-year-old building needs a historic
assessment for that reason alone is out of date. Age alone does not make a property historic in
Laguna. LBMC 25.45.004 (as amended by Ord. 1732
§40) defines a **historic resource** as a property or structure that:

1. is on the **City's historic register**;
2. is on the **California Register**, or officially found eligible for it;
3. is on the **National Register**, or officially found eligible for it; or
4. the City must treat as historic "based on substantial evidence in light of the whole record"
   (the CEQA standard).

Two Ord. 1732 points:

- The lack of a recorded preservation agreement is **not, by itself, proof** that a property is
  off the register (25.45.004, last paragraph).
- Ord. 1732 §41 deleted the old rule that a property approved for the register is "not formally
  designated" until the agreement is recorded. The City's historic preservation web page (as of
  2026-06-18) still quotes the old sentence. The code controls.

**The 1981 inventory and its ratings.** The City's site data carries an inventory rating
(`historic.rating` from the parcel lookup, letters such as E, K or C) and register facts
(`historic.on_register`, register number, Mills Act). The older inventory "is ineffective for
the purposes of creating a presumption of historicity" (LBMC 25.45.002(H)). So:

- A rating alone does not make the property a historic resource.
- It does flag the property for a possible historic review. For properties on the 1981 Historic
  Inventory, **the City pays** for the study of whether the property is a historic resource under
  CEQA. If it is, and the project needs a Secretary of the Interior's Standards review, the
  applicant pays for that second study. For register properties, the applicant always pays.
  (City Planning intake procedure, quoted in
  a CrossBeam reference that is not included in this plugin "Deposits and the historic-assessment
  cost split.")
- What each letter means is not defined in our code sources. **UNSETTLED** — if a resident asks,
  say the letters are the City's inventory ratings and Planning (Heritage Committee staff liaison)
  can explain theirs.

The Director may require a historic resource assessment before review: City-paid to decide
whether the property is historic; applicant-paid to judge a project's effect on a known resource
(LBMC 25.45.010(C)). The City usually starts the assessment at the first resubmittal, not at
intake, except for truly minor repair-type work (City ruling recorded in
a CrossBeam reference that is not included in this plugin).

**Current project.** The City is reviewing its register. Phase I evaluated 54 register
properties that have no recorded preservation agreement (ASM Affiliates report). A uniform
process for non-register properties with possible significance is coming (City Historic
Property Evaluations Project page, as of 2026-07-14). Expect process changes.

## 2. Which approval does work on a historic resource need?

| Work on a historic resource | Route | Who decides | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor alterations, only the kinds (B)(3)(a)(iv) lists: "replacement or modification of doors and windows; the addition of first floor porches and decks; the replacement of roofing material or building siding; minor improvements to landscaping, hardscape, walls, fences, arbors, and other landscape features; and similar minor site or building alterations" — reviewed against the Secretary of the Interior's Standards. In-kind replacement of doors, windows, roofing or siding is still "replacement", so it is on this row | **Administrative Design Review** | Director, after a **Heritage Committee recommendation**. Always a hearing: the hearing waiver does not apply to this category. | LBMC 25.05.040(B)(3)(a)(iv); 25.45.010(A); 25.05.040(B)(3)(b) |
| Major modifications or other actions that do not qualify as minor | **Design Review** | DRB (single-family and two-family) or Planning Commission (all others), after a Heritage Committee recommendation | LBMC 25.05.040(B)(1)(d); 25.45.010(B); 25.05.040(F)(1) |
| Demolition | Demolition application, **Coastal Development Permit** and CEQA review, Heritage Committee recommendation, then Design Review hearing | Design review authority | LBMC 25.45.014 |
| In-kind replacement of something the ADR item does not list (a water heater, furnace, AC unit or other equipment swapped like for like; repair that alters nothing) | **Zone Clearance (B)(2)(c)** with a historic staff check at the counter. Our reading: it is not a "minor alteration" of the kinds (iv) lists, and the specific in-kind item (B)(2)(c) governs over the (B)(1)(d) "other actions pertaining to historic resources" catch-all (specific over general, `planning-approvals` Step 4d rule 3), so it does not go to the Heritage Committee. The Director may still refer it up (25.05.040(A)(2)(d)) | City Planning staff | LBMC 25.05.040(B)(2)(c); 25.45.010(A)-(B) |
| Interior-only work | Not on the historic lists. Zone Clearance applies to interior work that does not change the exterior (25.05.040(B)(2)(d)). Confirm with Planning whether the Director wants Heritage Committee input. | Community Development | LBMC 25.05.040(B)(2)(d); (A)(2)(d) referral |

**In-kind work, and rated-only properties.** The table applies to a register "historic resource".
A rating alone is not a resource (25.45.002(H)). Reason it in these steps:
1. **Is the property a register resource, or only rated?** Only a register resource carries the
   historic list items ((B)(1)(d), (B)(3)(a)(iv)). For a rated-only property, in-kind repair is
   (B)(2)(c) Zone Clearance like any other house.
2. **Is the work one of the kinds ADR item (iv) lists?** Doors, windows, roofing and siding are
   listed by their "replacement", so on a register resource even in-kind replacement of them is ADR
   (iv), after a Heritage Committee recommendation (25.45.010(A)), with a hearing the Director cannot
   waive ((B)(3)(b)). Lead with that route. Staff have sometimes cleared in-kind work on
   properties marked "Historic" at the counter; mention that only as a one-line lean to confirm with
   Planning, never as the answer.
3. **Not a listed kind?** In-kind replacement of equipment or other repair that alters nothing (a
   water heater, an AC unit or a furnace swapped like for like) is Zone Clearance (B)(2)(c), with a
   historic staff check at the counter.
4. **Before the building permit** for an alteration of a historic resource, the owner, contractor,
   inspector, City staff, architect and any historic monitor meet at a pre-construction meeting
   (25.45.010(D)).

**An ADU on a register property.** The ADU stays on its own ADU application and is reviewed
ministerially. On a California Register property the City's ADU standards add objective placement
and character-defining-feature limits (25.17.010(g)(6); `../../adu/SKILL.md`). City practice since Ord. 1732 opens a
historic review record beside the ADU application, so tell the resident to expect it; it runs in
parallel and does not turn the ADU into Design Review.

Every Design Review decision must be consistent with Chapter 25.45 (LBMC 25.05.040(H)(5)). Before
the building permit issues, a pre-construction meeting is required with the owner, contractor,
inspector, City staff, architect and any historic monitor (LBMC 25.45.010(D)).

The Director may refer any Zone Clearance or Administrative Design Review project to the DRB or
Planning Commission if it is in an unusually sensitive or highly visible area (LBMC
25.05.040(A)(2)(d)).

## 3. Demolishing a historic resource

LBMC 25.45.014:

- File a demolition application. It needs a **Coastal Development Permit** and CEQA compliance
  unless exempt.
- Within 30 days of a complete application, the Heritage Committee reviews it and recommends.
- The design review authority holds a noticed hearing, looks at alternatives and mitigation, and
  approves or denies. It must find either that demolition is consistent with Chapter 25.45 and the
  General Plan, or that there is no reasonable alternative.
- If approved, a **90-day waiting period** applies for relocation or documentation. The
  applicant advertises the demolition (quarter page, with photo) once a month for the first two
  months. The authority may extend the wait up to 180 days if relocation is imminent (25.45.014(E)(3);
  Ord. 1732 did not change 25.45.014).

For this penalty, "demolition" means removing all existing exterior walls, cladding and roof framing
(25.45.022(A)). Demolishing a **register** structure without a permit brings a **five-year stay** on building
permits for new construction on the site and an administrative penalty of up to **$100,000** per
violation (LBMC 25.45.022). Emergency demolition ordered by the Building Official or Fire Chief
is allowed only to the extent needed (25.45.016).

## 4. Getting on the register

- Voluntary. The owner applies on the City's form; **no application fee** (LBMC 25.45.006(B)(2)).
- The **Heritage Committee** decides. It needs criterion (1), owner consent, plus at least one of
  criteria (2)-(11): national or state listing, local heritage, notable person or event, notable
  architect or builder, distinctive style, engineering achievement, iconic location or view
  point, rare remaining example, iconic landscape, or archaeological information (25.45.006(C)).
- After designation, a preservation agreement is recorded with the County and binds later owners
  (25.45.006(B)(3)).
- Removal from the register goes to City Council after a Heritage Committee recommendation and
  CEQA review. Council may require repayment of incentives used (25.45.006(E)-(F)).
- Sellers must give buyers notice that the property is on the register (25.45.012, as amended
  by Ord. 1732 §44). Ord. 1732 deleted the old rule that a current Real Property Report satisfies
  this, and deleted the old penalty subsection.

Application: "Historic Register Placement Application" (`../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`
or `../../city-website/references/forms-index.md` for the link).

## 5. Incentives for register properties (LBMC 25.45.008)

Granted at a public hearing, conditioned on the Secretary of the Interior's Standards and a
recorded agreement. A request with an alteration needs a Heritage Committee recommendation. The
DRB hears most requests; the Planning Commission hears them downtown and where it is the primary
authority (such as a CUP). If the project would not otherwise need Design Review, incentives may
go through Administrative Design Review, except commercial parking reductions.

Incentives listed in the code include:

- parking relief for nonconforming single-family and multifamily homes adding up to 50%
  (25.45.008(A));
- refund of City building and planning fees after final inspection if the work preserves the
  historic character (25.45.008(B));
- California Historical Building Code deviations (25.45.008(C));
- setback flexibility, open-space relief and nonconforming-structure relief (25.45.008(E), (H), (M));
- density credits in R-2, R-3 and LB/P (25.45.008(F));
- priority plan check (25.45.008(J));
- Mills Act eligibility (25.45.008(K)).

**Mills Act:** the City is **not accepting Mills Act applications this year** while it reviews the
program. Normally the cycle opens in January, and the property must be on the register first
(City historic preservation page, as of 2026-06-18).

## 6. Outdoor lighting on a register property

Approved period-appropriate fixtures on a register property may be exempt from the Good Neighbor
Outdoor Lighting rules if they stay at or under 750 lumens and 3,000 Kelvin, with Director
approval (LBMC 7.70.060(k)). See `noise-lighting-equipment.md`.

## What to tell the resident (short form)

1. Run the parcel lookup first. Report what the site data says (register, rating, Mills Act).
2. Say a rating alone is not a historic designation, but it can trigger a City-paid historic study.
3. Lead with the route from the table in section 2: the Heritage Committee recommends first for
   ADR and Design Review items; in-kind equipment and repair the ADR item does not list is Zone
   Clearance with a historic staff check. Mention the pre-construction meeting (25.45.010(D)).
4. Flag demolition and Mills Act timing rules when relevant.
5. Mark the meaning of a specific rating and any study timing as "confirm with Planning."

Ord. 1732 is local law not yet certified by the Coastal Commission; see `design-review-lists.md`.
