# Laguna Beach — Variances, use permits, and other Planning permits

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Use for:** "Can I get a variance?", "Does my use need a CUP?", "Can I change my approved plans?",
"How do I move a lot line?", "Can I split my lot under SB 9?", "Can my wall sit in the city
right-of-way?"

**Sources.** Integrated LBMC Chapter 25.05 (the City source document (not included in this plugin)),
as amended by Urgency Ord. 1732 §§5, 6, 9 (effective dates and new 25.05.060); Chapter 21.08 as
amended by Ord. 1732 (the City source document (not included in this plugin)); Chapter 25.95 (the City source document (not included in this plugin));
Chapter 11.50 (a CrossBeam reference that is not included in this plugin). City application list and
links: `../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`. Fees: call the fee estimate.

These permits stack on top of the review level (`planning-approvals.md`). Each is its own
application. In the coastal zone a CDP may also apply; CDP noticing replaces the Chapter 25.05
noticing when a CDP is required (25.05.020(C), 25.05.025(D), 25.05.030(D)).

## Variance — LBMC 25.05.025

- **What:** relief from a Title 25 standard (setback, height, coverage and so on) because of special
  circumstances of the lot: "size, shape, topography, location and surroundings" (25.05.025(A)).
- **Who decides, by project.** The code names two deciders: "The Board of Adjustment or Planning
  Commission as applicable" (25.05.025(E)(1)). The Planning Commission "shall act as the Board of
  Adjustment" for (a) a variance "in conjunction with any matter pending before the Planning
  Commission for which the Commission has the primary discretionary responsibility"; (b) subdivision
  lot dimension, area, yard and access variances; (c) indirect access; (d) "construction of new
  residential units on lots taking access from streets with less than standard width"
  (25.05.025(J)(1)). Every other variance goes to the Board of Adjustment, and the Design Review
  Board sits as the "design review board/board of adjustment" (25.05.065(B), (F)). So:
  - **House or duplex** (Design Review at the DRB, 25.05.040(F)(1)(i)): the DRB as Board of
    Adjustment, heard with the Design Review, unless (J)(1)(b)-(d) applies. A **new house or new unit
    on a substandard-width street** is a Planning Commission variance under (J)(1)(d).
  - **Three or more units, Downtown, nonresidential, public works, or a project with a CUP**: the
    Planning Commission decides the Design Review (25.05.040(F)(1)(ii)), so the variance is "pending
    before" it and goes to the Commission under (J)(1)(a).
  - **Subdivisions, lot line and access variances**: the Planning Commission.
  City practice since Ord. 1732 matches: a variance on a house project is filed with a DRB Design
  Review record the same day and heard with it, even when every other piece is Zone Clearance or
  ADR (`planning-approvals.md` Step 6 item 7). The Planning variance application in our
  submittal checklist (the City checklists, Variance) speaks of the Planning Commission only;
  follow the code split above.
- Noticed public hearing (25.05.025(E)(1)); CDP noticing replaces it when a CDP is required
  (25.05.025(D)).
- **Findings, all four** (25.05.025(F)): (1) special circumstances of the property deprive it of
  privileges others in the vicinity and zone enjoy; (2) the variance is necessary to preserve a
  substantial property right others have; (3) it is not detrimental to public health, safety,
  welfare or nearby property; (4) it is not contrary to the zoning ordinance or General Plan.
- **Timing:** effective 14 calendar days after the decision unless appealed (25.05.025(H)(1) as
  amended by Ord. 1732 §5). Lapses in two years unless a building permit is issued and work is
  diligently pursued, or the decider grants a two-year and then a one-year extension
  (25.05.025(H)(2)). The extension is granted by "the Planning Commission or Board of Adjustment, as
  applicable" (25.05.025(H)(2)(b)), not by ADR. When one approval combines Design Review and a
  variance, City practice has processed the joint extension as one ADR (`planning-approvals` Step 1a
  item 2); give the code rule first. Watercourse-setback variances need two extra
  findings (25.50.030(D)(4)).
- **Site coverage flex:** in R-1, the design review authority may allow up to five percentage points
  over the coverage limit on stated grounds; beyond that needs a variance (25.10.008(E)(1)(c)-(d) as
  amended by Ord. 1732 §22; the City source document (not included in this plugin)).

## Conditional Use Permit (CUP) — LBMC 25.05.030

- **What:** a use the zone lists as conditional (25.05.030(A)). Whether a use needs one is a zone
  question: call the parcel lookup for the zone, then for a storefront, restaurant or other business
  use `../../commercial-ti/SKILL.md` or `../../restaurant-ti/SKILL.md` and read the use table.
- **Who:** the Planning Commission reviews all CUPs. If a project also needs a Board of Adjustment
  approval, the CUP goes first (25.05.030(E)). The Commission then also hears the Design Review
  (25.05.040(F)(1)(ii)).
- **Findings** (25.05.030(F)): adequate site, adequate street access, no substantial adverse effect
  on abutting property, General Plan consistency, and conditions needed to protect health and safety.
- **Timing:** effective 14 calendar days after the decision unless appealed (25.05.030(I)(1) as
  amended by Ord. 1732 §6); lapses in two years unless established (25.05.030(I)(3)).
- **ADU that misses an objective standard:** "may be allowed by the city with a conditional use
  permit" (25.17.010(j), Ord. 1713). City intake has also taken an ADU setback below 4 ft as a
  variance request. UNSETTLED which route; give both (`planning-approvals` Step 6 item 9).

## Administrative Use Permit (AUP) — LBMC 25.05.020

- For a use Title 25 expressly makes subject to an AUP (25.05.020(A)). The best-known one is
  short-term lodging: `short-term-lodging.md`.
- The Director decides on compliance with the zoning rules. No hearing unless the applicant or a
  noticed person asks for one (25.05.020(D)-(E)). Appeal under 25.05.070.

## Temporary Use Permit (TUP) — LBMC 25.05.035

- Examples in residential zones: temporary construction structures, garages or sheds; parking or
  storing construction equipment during a project; storage for a project; a sales office; events
  that close a street; temporary parking lots (25.05.035(B)(1)).
- The Director decides TUPs in R-1 and "short-term events (five days or less) deemed minor by the
  Director" in any zone, except temporary parking lots, and may refer any TUP to the Commission. The
  Planning Commission decides "all other applications" (25.05.035(F)(1), (3)). So a recurring series
  of events, or any event over five days outside R-1, goes to the Commission.
- Storage containers and construction trailers: a TUP covers temporary structures and storage
  incidental to construction ((B)(1)(a)-(c)); a container kept permanently is an accessory building
  (`planning-approvals.md` Step 6). Detail: `temporary-uses.md`.
- A CDP is also needed when the temporary use is coastal development (25.05.035(F)(2)).
- Maximum three years (25.05.035(I)), with no extension clause. A use continuing past the term
  needs a new TUP (or, if permanent, the zone's use permit) and a new or amended CDP heard by the
  same body, not an extension: `planning-approvals.md` Step 1a item 2a.

## Changing an approved project — LBMC 25.05.060 (new, Ord. 1732 §9)

- Applies to a live Design Review, variance, CUP, AUP or TUP; the work "shall be established only as
  approved" unless a change is approved under this section (25.05.060(B)(1)). Check the record check
  first and confirm the approval has not lapsed (`planning-approvals.md` Step 1a).
- The request is in writing with reasons ((B)(2)). The Director may approve minor changes without a
  hearing if they meet the Zoning Code, do not touch a feature behind the CEQA finding, a condition or
  "a specific consideration by the applicable review authority", do not expand or change the use, and
  do not alter the CDP findings ((B)(3)(i)); the Director may refer the change to the original
  decider ((B)(3)(ii)).
- Anything else "may only be approved by the original review authority ... through a new permit
  application" ((C)(1)); the original conditions stay unless that body changes them ((C)(2)).
- CDPs: changes follow Chapter 25.07 (25.05.060(A)): the same test in 25.07.012(J) (Ord. 1732 §15,
  not yet certified) and, in the certified code, amendment "by the original approving authority"
  (25.07.022). A CDP the Coastal Commission issued (Three Arch Bay, Irvine Cove) is amended by the
  Commission (25.07.012(J)(5)).
- A time extension is not a change: Design Review extensions are ADR (25.05.040(K)(3),
  (B)(3)(a)(xvi)); variance extensions go to the Board of Adjustment or Commission (above). An
  extension moves only "the beginning two-year approval period"; it never lengthens a TUP's term or
  a duration condition (`planning-approvals` Step 1a item 2a).

## Lot line adjustment (LLA) or a map — LBMC 21.08.030 (as amended by Ord. 1732)

Moving, merging or creating lot lines takes one of three instruments. Test the LLA first; only
when it fails pick the map.

**1. Can it be an LLA?** An LLA is "a lot line adjustment between two or more existing building
sites, or between parcels of land contained within an existing building site, where the land taken
from one building site is added to an adjacent building site, or where interior parcel lines are
eliminated for the purpose of consolidation, and where a greater number of parcels than originally
existed is not thereby created" (21.08.030(a)). State law caps it at "four or fewer existing
adjoining parcels" (Gov. Code 66412(d)). Check each condition against the facts:
- **Legal building sites.** "The project site described in the proposal consists of legal building
  sites as defined in Title 25" ((a)(1)) and does not "include any lots or parcels created illegally"
  ((a)(8)). A building site is "a parcel or contiguous parcels of land which was established in
  compliance with the building site requirements of this code", under one ownership, created by a
  recorded tract or parcel map, record of survey, certificate of compliance, or a deed or contract
  recorded before July 19, 1958 (or annexation) that met the zoning then in effect (25.08 "Building
  site" (A), which adds area, yard and 10-ft street frontage tests; an older undersized lot that
  was a legal site under one ownership still counts). Two lots "whose common lot line is crossed by a structure" are one building site until the
  structure is removed (same definition). The title report and grant deeds the LLA application
  requires show this.
- **No new site.** It "does not create one or more building site(s)" and "no additional sites will
  result" ((a)(2)-(3)).
- **Each resulting parcel meets zoning**, or for a legal nonconforming lot does not "significantly or
  adversely increase the extent of such nonconformity" ((a)(6)); minimum lot area and dimensions are
  in the zone chapter (for R-1, 25.10.008).
- **No new improvements, access or easements needed** ((a)(7), (9)); CEQA and General Plan
  consistency ((a)(4)-(5)).

**2. If a condition fails, which instrument?**
- **A parcel is not a legal building site** (created by an unrecorded deed split, or never
  established): an LLA cannot fix it. The owner first asks the City whether the parcel complies with
  the Subdivision Map Act; the City issues a certificate of compliance or a conditional one, with the
  conditions that would have applied (Gov. Code 66499.35(a)-(b)). Then test the LLA again, or go to a
  map. Who in Laguna processes a certificate of compliance is not in our sources; ask Planning.
- **The change creates a lot, or more parcels than before:** a subdivision map. "Tentative Parcel
  Maps shall be required for any subdivision creating four or fewer parcels. Tentative Tract Maps
  shall be required for any subdivision creating five or more parcels" (City Tentative Map
  application, following Gov. Code 66426). "The planning commission shall constitute the 'advisory
  agency' for tentative maps, parcel maps and subdivision maps" (21.08.040), and a tentative parcel
  map is "filed with the planning commission" (21.08.070); "a building site may be created by the
  recordation of a tract map or parcel map after approval has been secured from the city planning
  commission" (25.08 "Building site" (B)). The code also names City Council action on tentative
  tract and parcel maps (21.08.130(j), 21.08.155(d)) and gives the Council map extensions
  (21.08.100(b)). Lean: the Planning Commission hears the tentative parcel map, with the Council
  acting on the dedication and fee items it names; confirm the final decider at pre-application.
  SB 9 urban lot splits are ministerial instead (Ch. 25.95, below).
- **Merging lots** is an LLA when the parcels are legal building sites and the result meets (a)
  ("interior parcel lines are eliminated for the purpose of consolidation"). When it is not (a lot is
  not a legal building site, or more than four parcels), the Map Act lets an owner use "tentative,
  parcel, or final maps which create fewer parcels" or a reversion to acreage (Gov. Code
  66451.10(b)); a reversion is heard by the City Council (21.08.200). When the facts already show a
  failed (a)(1) or (a)(6) test (a City record or the title report says a parcel "is not a designated
  building site", or a lot is under the zone minimum and the merger is meant to cure it), lead with a
  tentative parcel map that creates fewer parcels, heard by the Planning Commission (21.08.040), with
  the CDP decided by the same body; give the LLA only as the branch if Planning confirms the sites are
  legal. Otherwise lead with the LLA.
- **An undersized lot may not shrink.** A legal building site below the zone's minimum area "may not
  be further reduced in area" (25.08 "Building site"), so an LLA that takes area from such a lot fails
  unless it swaps equal area back. Test each lot's resulting coverage, setbacks and area too.
- **Every route in the coastal zone also needs a CDP**: "All lot line adjustments are considered
  development and therefore require a Coastal Development Permit in the Coastal Zone"
  (21.08.030(c)), and a subdivision is development (25.07.006 "Development"). The CDP is decided with
  the map or LLA by the same body (25.07.012(D)).

**3. LLA process.**
- The approval authority is the same body that decides the project's other permits. If the project
  needs only the LLA and a CDP, the Director decides after a noticed hearing (21.08.030(c)).
- Record the approved LLA within 90 days, or ask for one 90-day extension (21.08.030(d)).
- A pending LLA never carries a new lot or a variance; each needs its own application
  (`planning-approvals` Step 1a item 7).

## SB 9 two-unit projects and urban lot splits — LBMC Chapter 25.95

- State law (Gov. Code 65852.21 and 66411.7) makes these ministerial. For the state rules, use
  `../../single-family-residential/SKILL.md` and then a CrossBeam reference that is not included in this plugin; this file gives only the Laguna delta.
- Laguna applies Chapter 25.95 in the R-1, R/HP, Lagunita and Three Arch Bay zones: ministerial, no
  discretionary review or hearing, if the listed site conditions are met (25.95.040).
- Any other permit the project needs (for example Design Review or a CUP) must be obtained **before**
  the SB 9 lot split application (25.95.060(J)).
- The LCP and Coastal Act still apply, but the City holds no public hearing on the CDP
  (25.95.040(F)).
- The City's SB 9 Application (doc 26958) still prints the June 2026 major remodel handout; the
  October 2026 handout governs (`../../residential-remodel/references/city-major-remodel-methodology.md`).

## Revocable Encroachment Permit (REP) — LBMC Chapter 11.50

- Lets an owner put minor private improvements in an unimproved, unused public right-of-way: low
  fences and retaining walls, walkways, stairs, planters, irrigation (11.50.010, 11.50.030).
- It is "a discretionary approval by the Laguna Beach design review approval authority" (11.50.010).
  Alone, it is Administrative Design Review (25.05.040(B)(3)(a)(viii)); with a DRB or Planning
  Commission project, that body decides it.
- **Hearing: always noticed.** "Prior to approval of a revocable encroachment permit by the design
  review approval authority, a noticed public hearing shall be held" (11.50.035, notice under
  25.05.065). That is the specific rule for REPs, so the general ADR waiver ("the Community
  Development Director may waive the requirement for a public hearing", 25.05.040(B)(3)(b)) does not
  reach it (a particular provision controls a general one, Code Civ. Proc. 1859). The City's REP
  application agrees: approval follows "a noticed public hearing". Tell the resident to expect a
  hearing before the Director (alone) or the DRB or Planning Commission (with their project).
- **The REP is the land-use approval only.** Building it in the right-of-way also takes the Public
  Works Right-of-Way Permit: no one may "lay, construct, reconstruct or repair" a curb, sidewalk,
  pavement or "retaining wall" on City property or right-of-way "without first obtaining a permit in
  writing ... from the city engineer" (11.16.010), and digging there needs one too (11.20.010). A
  wall or fence there also needs a building permit: the 14.50.022 exemptions for walls and fences do
  not apply "within three feet of a property line or public right-of-way".
- **A REP after the main project is approved.** Two readings: a new standalone REP is ADR under
  (viii), because it is no longer "associated with" a pending DRB or Commission project; or, while the
  parent approval is live and the REP changes its approved plans, it is a change to that approval
  under 25.05.060 (minor change by the Director, otherwise the original body). UNSETTLED; confirm.
- **Which land.** Chapter 11.50 speaks of "unimproved, unused public right-of-way". In practice the
  City also files planters and similar private items in sidewalk and parkway areas as REPs, so
  improved right-of-way is not a reason to skip the REP. The encroachment area is treated "as an extension of the yard" and must meet that
  yard's rules, such as fence heights (11.50.040). Public Works permit details:
  `right-of-way-utilities.md`.
- The owner of the benefited lot signs a covenant that runs with the land (11.50.020(e)).
