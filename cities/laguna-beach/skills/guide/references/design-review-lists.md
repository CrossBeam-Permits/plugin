## How the lists work

- The old default ("all exterior work gets Design Review unless excepted") is gone. The three
  lists are closed. Match each part of the project to a list item.
- Which item governs a part: the precedence rules in `planning-approvals.md` Step 4d (always-apply
  rules; in-kind replacement; a specific item over the general catch-alls; the highest list only when
  two specific items cover the same thing). The highest list any part reaches sets the project's
  application and decider; other parts stay on their own lists; the ordinance has no project-wide
  merge rule.
- A part no specific item names still meets a catch-all: (B)(1)(a)/(B)(2)(a) (major remodel or
  not), (B)(1)(b) (new building), (B)(1)(c)/(B)(2)(b) (improvement over or not over 15 ft). The
  definitions that decide these ("Building", "Improvement", "Height, building") and the known open
  conflicts (detached accessory buildings, alterations on an existing upper floor) are in
  `planning-approvals.md` Step 4.
- **ESA override.** Neither Zone Clearance nor Administrative Design Review may be used for work in
  an environmentally sensitive area "unless a licensed professional has determined, subject to peer
  review, that the proposed development does not impact the environmentally sensitive area"; such
  work goes to Design Review under (B)(1)(e) (25.05.040(B)(2) and (B)(3)(a) preambles). Under the
  Ord. 1732 definition (LBMC 25.08.010), an ESA includes mapped habitat areas, streams, and
  **"slopes of 30% or greater"**. This definition sets the review list only; Chapter 25.07 keeps its
  own habitat-only ESA definition for CDP exemptions (25.07.006), so a bare 30% slope does not by
  itself defeat a CDP exemption. LBMC 25.50.040 (not amended) separately makes structures on lots over
  30% slope "subject to design review" and requires an open-space dedication offer.
- **Director referral.** The Director may send a Zone Clearance or Administrative Design Review
  project to the DRB or Planning Commission if it could alter significant physical characteristics
  in an unusually sensitive or highly visible area, may affect neighbors, or presents other
  circumstances (25.05.040(A)(2)(d)). This is a City decision; never predict it.
- **CDP is separate.** Every route "may still require a Coastal Development Permit" under Chapter
  25.07 (25.05.040(A)(2)(e); 25.05.045(C)(4)). See `coastal-development-permits.md`.

## (B)(1) Design Review — DRB or Planning Commission, public hearing (16)

(a) Demolition, alteration, or addition that constitutes a major remodel. A landscape plan and an
exterior lighting plan are required.
(b) All new buildings, except as provided in (B)(2) for ADUs and new primary dwelling units.
Landscape plan and exterior lighting plan required.
(c) Any improvement that exceeds a height of 15 feet, measured under the "Height, building"
definition in Chapter 25.08 (from the finished floor or from natural or finished grade, whichever
is more restrictive or lower). Landscape plan and exterior lighting plan required.
(d) Major modifications, alterations, or other actions pertaining to historic resources that do not
qualify for administrative approval under (B)(3).
(e) Any development in an environmentally sensitive area, unless a licensed professional has
determined, subject to peer review, that it does not impact the ESA.
(f) Grading in excess of 20 cubic yards outside the building footprint, except as specified in
Section 22.10.010(e).
(g) Retaining walls in excess of 5 feet in exposed height.
(h) Clearing of native vegetation on undeveloped parcels and undeveloped portions of developed
parcels, and native vegetation restoration plans, except City-required annual weed abatement and
fuel modification.
(i) Shoreline and bluff protective devices.
(j) Portable and in-ground swimming pools and spas more than 3 feet above adjacent, legal ground
elevation, except portable spas that do not exceed 600 gallons.
(k) New vehicular access to streets or alleys, including widening an existing driveway at the
street to a width greater than the minimum required by Section 25.52.008(I)(2).
(l) Commercial or industrial parking and loading areas.
(m) Permanent chain link or similar woven metal fences, and vinyl fences that do not simulate
natural material.
(n) Trash storage or enclosures, except as provided in (B)(2) and (B)(3).
(o) Modification of a Chapter 25.13 primary dwelling unit that conflicts with the Chapter 25.13
objective standards.
(p) Major Capital Improvement Project: "a project undertaken by the City that is determined by the
Community Development Director, in consultation with other affected department heads, to require
review by the Planning Commission prior to approval by the City Council of design and/or
construction documents, based on the project's proposed scope in terms of environmental, aesthetic,
and/or land use effects on the community." Typical examples: (i) new or substantial alterations to
parks, public buildings, community facilities, reservoirs or bridges; (ii) new or substantial
alterations to drainage structures with significant changes to grades, hillsides, habitats or views;
(iii) street improvements with substantial right-of-way expansion, removal of existing facilities or
significant vegetation (unless fuel modification), or major changes to community character; (iv)
design programs for citywide features visible to the public (beach and park accessways, promenades,
bridges, trails, the Festival of Arts property).

## (B)(2) Exempt from Design Review — Zone Clearance (28)

(a) Demolition, alteration, or addition that does not constitute a major remodel.
(b) An improvement that does not exceed a height of 15 feet (same measurement as (B)(1)(c)).
(c) In-kind replacement of improvements for repair and maintenance.
(d) Interior modifications to existing structures that do not modify the exterior.
(e) Re-roofing that does not result in white or light colored roofs, with no change to the
dimensions, square footage, or roof pitch, and that will not result in glare (for example from
asphalt shingles or stainless steel), both at the Director's determination.
(f) Annual weed abatement.
(g) Fuel modification project or program.
(h) Accessory dwelling units, only as set forth in Chapter 25.17.
(i) Grading of 20 cubic yards or less outside the building footprint, except as specified in
Section 22.10.010(e).
(j) Retaining walls 5 feet or less in exposed height.
(k) Hardscape that does not increase impervious surfaces, or that is proposed to comply with fire
prevention standards as determined by the Fire Official, and complies with the rest of the chapter.
(l) Fences, walls and hedges that comply with Section 25.50.012. This excludes the (B)(1)(m) fence
types.
(m) Decks, slabs, terraces, or patios elevated 3 feet or less above the adjacent, legal ground
elevation.
(n) Landscaping within the buildable area that is less than 15 feet at maintained height, if the
plans identify and require maintenance at or below the heights shown.
(o) Landscaping in the rear or side yard less than 6 feet, and in the front yard less than 4 feet,
at maintained height, with the same plan condition.
(p) Artwork approved through Chapter 1.09 (Art in Public Places).
(q) Trash storage within the side yard when screened from public view by a wall or fence at least
4 feet high and not impacting required access.
(r) Railings.
(s) Skylights with operable automatic night shades.
(t) Portable and in-ground swimming pools and spas no more than 3 feet above adjacent, legal ground
elevation, and portable spas that do not exceed 600 gallons.
(u) Water features.
(v) Mechanical equipment, including air conditioners and pool/spa equipment, that meets Section
7.25.130(c).
(w) Above-ground utility structures below 4 feet high, using colors, finishes, and landscaping to
minimize visual impact as the Director determines, with landscaping kept at or below the
structure's height.
(x) Temporary on-grade removable accessory structures (play sets, swing sets, similar unenclosed
recreation equipment, and storage sheds) that do not exceed 120 square feet, are less than 12 feet
above adjacent legal grade, and meet the zone's setbacks unless they receive Administrative Design
Review. (No (B)(3) item names this case, and the 25.08 accessory building rule keeps accessory
buildings out of setbacks: UNSETTLED, `planning-approvals.md` Step 6.)
(y) Certain telecommunication facilities subject to Chapters 11.06 and 25.55.
(z) New primary dwelling unit, only as set forth in Chapter 25.13.
(aa) Modification of a Chapter 25.13 primary dwelling unit that still meets the Chapter 25.13
objective standards.
(bb) De Minimis Capital Improvement Projects: City capital projects "deemed de minimis and subject to
zone clearance ... based on the project's proposed scope in terms of environmental, aesthetic, and/or
land use effects on the community", or emergency projects the Director finds necessary for health,
safety or welfare. Examples: (i) fuel modification; (ii) emergency projects; (iii) roadway or trail
improvements within existing rights-of-way; (iv) storm drain and sewer pipelines, minor utility
alterations; (v) "alterations and repairs to parking lots"; (vi) small habitat restoration (CEQA
Guidelines 15333); (vii) same-size sign replacements with similar materials, at the Director's
determination; (viii) in-kind replacement for repair and maintenance; (ix) similar projects the
Director determines.

## (B)(3)(a) Administrative Design Review — Director or designee (17)

(i) Landscaping within the buildable area that is 15 feet or higher at maintained height.
(ii) Landscaping in the rear or side yard 6 feet or higher, and in the front yard 4 feet or higher,
at maintained height.
(iii) Decks, slabs, terraces, or patios elevated more than 3 feet above adjacent, legal ground
elevation.
(iv) Minor alterations to historic resources: replacement or modification of doors and windows;
first-floor porches and decks; replacement of roofing material or siding; minor landscaping,
hardscape, walls, fences, arbors and similar features; and similar minor alterations, each
reviewed for conformance with the Secretary of the Interior's Standards.
(v) Mechanical equipment, including air conditioners and pool/spa equipment, that does not meet
Section 7.25.130(C).
(vi) Additional covered or tandem parking as specified in Section 25.52.012(G).
(vii) Decorative features such as fence posts, brick or stone columns, which may extend up to 12
inches above the maximum allowable front-yard height.
(viii) Revocable Encroachment Permits not associated with a DRB or Planning Commission project.
(ix) Re-roofing that results in white or light colored roofs and may result in glare, at the
Director's determination.
(x) Roof alterations that change the dimensions, square footage, or roof pitch. (Whether a pure
reduction in roof height is an (x) alteration: `planning-approvals.md` Step 4f.)
(xi) Detached fireplaces.
(xii) Skylights without operable automatic night shades.
(xiii) Telecommunication facilities under Chapters 11.06 and 25.55 unless otherwise exempt.
(xiv) Above-ground utility structures 4 feet or higher.
(xv) Trash storage within the front yard setback, screened by a 4-foot wall or fence (3 feet on a
corner lot), not impacting required access.
(xvi) Extensions of time for design review permits. (The Director grants a two-year and then a
final one-year extension, asked in writing before expiry, on findings that the rules and the
neighborhood have not materially changed: 25.05.040(K)(3)-(4).)
(xvii) Minor Capital Improvement Project: "a project undertaken by the City that may be reviewed and
approved administratively, based upon a determination by the Community Development Director, in
consultation with other affected department heads", on the same effects test. Examples:
alterations to existing facilities resulting in exterior design changes; roadway or trail extensions
within existing rights-of-way; replacement or renovation of damaged facilities with exterior design
changes; small additions to existing structures with exterior design changes; construction or
demolition of small structures with site or building design changes; slope and wall repair for
public infrastructure.

**Capital projects: who picks the tier, and who then decides.** The Director assigns the tier, "in
consultation with other affected department heads", on the project's "environmental, aesthetic,
and/or land use effects" ((B)(1)(p), (B)(2)(bb), (B)(3)(a)(xvii)). Major: Planning Commission
review before City Council approval of design or construction documents ((B)(1)(p);
(F)(1)(ii)). Minor: the Director, by ADR. De Minimis: staff, by Zone Clearance. Nobody
self-classifies: give all three and say the Director decides. The listed examples are typical, not
binding: the Director may place work the ordinance gives as a De Minimis example (a parking lot
alteration, for instance) in a higher tier when its effects warrant, so never pick the tier from
the example alone. Solar on a City building is not a capital-project question at all: state law requires
administrative approval through a building permit (Gov. Code 65850.5(b);
`planning-approvals.md` Step 4a-bis).

**Hearing waiver.** Except for (iii) elevated decks and (iv) historic alterations, the Director may
waive the hearing when no other entitlement is required. The City mails and publishes a notice
(posted in three public places including the site, or in a newspaper). Anyone may ask for a hearing
within 15 working days. If no one asks, the permit is deemed approved (25.05.040(B)(3)(b)). If a
hearing is held, the Director decides after it (25.05.040(B)(3)(c)). A revocable encroachment permit
(viii) always gets "a noticed public hearing" (11.50.035, the specific rule for REPs).

## Zone Clearance procedure — LBMC 25.05.045

- Purpose: staff check that a use or structure meets the zone's allowed uses and standards
  (25.05.045(A)).
- When: at initiation of a use, at a change or intensification of use, and before the City issues
  a building, grading, or other construction permit (25.05.045(B)(1)-(3)).
- Staff issue it after finding the request complies with all Zoning Code provisions. It may be a
  plan stamp, a letter, or another certification (25.05.045(C)(1)-(2)).
- It is appealable under 25.05.070 (25.05.045(C)(3)). A CDP may still be required (25.05.045(C)(4)).
- The City's Zone Clearance Application (doc 26718, "Updated July 2026") describes itself as the
  form "for projects that DO NOT require a discretionary planning entitlement, such as Design Review
  or a Coastal Development Permit." Link: `../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`.

## Coastal zone caveat — the lists are not certified

- Ord. 1732 amends the Implementation Plan of the Local Coastal Program. The ordinance itself
  recites that the Coastal Act "requires that the Coastal Commission review and certify the DR
  Efficiency Ordinance before it becomes effective" (PRC 30514(a); LBMC 25.07.026(E)). The City
  adopted it as an urgency measure to apply the new rules while the Commission reviews them.
- As of 2026-10-01 no Commission action on the package appears on any agenda
  (the City source document (not included in this plugin) section 4). The Commission
  found the City's submittal incomplete because it came as an ordinance, not a resolution. The City
  Council agenda for 2026-09-22 (consent item 20) carried a PRC 30510(a) resolution affirming the
  City will carry out its certified LCP in full conformity with the Coastal Act, so the package can
  be resubmitted (City Council staff report, 2026-09-22). We have not checked the minutes for the vote.
- A CDP must be found consistent with the **certified** LCP (PRC 30604(b); LBMC 25.07.012(G)(1)).
  The certified Chapter 25.05 is the pre-Ord. 1732 text (the City source document (not included in this plugin)).
- **UNSETTLED.** The City applies Ord. 1732 citywide today. Whether a coastal-zone project that
  needs a CDP is also held to the older, certified design review rules, or whether the Commission
  would see the new lists differently on appeal, the City has not said. Tell the resident: "In the
  coastal zone, confirm the review level with Planning at pre-application."
