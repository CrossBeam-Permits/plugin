## The steps, in order

1. **Look up the property and the rules.** the parcel lookup; then `planning-approvals.md` for the
   review level and add-ons.
2. **Preliminary zoning review (pre-application).** The City's process page: submit the preliminary
   design on the Public Permit Portal as a Residential or Commercial Planning Entitlement. It gets a
   Zoning Plan Check (ZPC) number; a planner sends a letter listing the applications needed and any
   concerns, in about 30 days; the fee covers up to three reviews (City design review process page,
   as of 2026-02-22). This is where UNSETTLED questions get answered.
3. **Early neighbor communication (Design Review only).** Before a hearing is scheduled, the applicant
   notifies owners within 300 ft by a mailing prepared by a professional listing service, with a
   certified list (25.05.040(C)(1)).
4. **File the applications.** One form per entitlement; the universal Planning Application is
   retired (City notice, 2026-07-22). File on the Public Permit Portal (EnerGov). Links:
   `../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`. The City's FAQ also mentions appointments; give
   both routes as that file says. **Counter or full plan check (City practice since Ord. 1732).**
   Ask whether staff can judge the piece from the drawings in one sitting, with nothing new to
   measure against a standard. Repair, in-kind replacement and work that adds no area, height or
   new structure is usually cleared the same day at the counter as a "Zoning Plan Check -
   Residential - Over The Counter Review", next to the over-the-counter building permit: reroofs,
   window and door replacement (including changed or infilled openings), siding and stucco, railings,
   deck repair, ground-mounted AC, in-kind rooftop equipment, code-compliant fences, retaining walls
   5 ft or less, hardscape, a portable spa, a pool remodel in its footprint, shaded skylights,
   interior work, an EV charger. A piece that adds something staff must check against setbacks,
   height, coverage or a list threshold takes a full Zoning Plan Check, which may lead to ADR or
   Design Review: new or enlarged decks, any addition (however small), ADUs, new pools, sheds and
   other accessory structures, equipment on a roof or deck, exterior remodels with deck or roof
   changes, and encroachment permits. Interior-only work behind an exterior case can be split off to
   the counter.
5. **Completeness, 30 days.** The City decides complete or incomplete within 30 calendar days, and
   again within 30 days of each resubmittal (25.05.015(B)(1), (3); Gov. Code 65943(a)-(b)). An
   application is not complete until the fee is paid (25.05.015(D) as amended).
6. **Resubmit within 90 days.** If the applicant does not provide requested information within 90
   days of an incomplete determination, the application expires and a new application and fee are
   needed, unless state law says otherwise (25.05.015(G), Ord. 1732 §4). The City's 2026-07-22 notice
   said files found incomplete before that date keep six months; the signed ordinance text does not
   say this (the City source document (not included in this plugin)). UNSETTLED for
   pre-July files; confirm with the planner. The City's process page still says "six months"; that
   is out of date.
7. **Staking (story poles).** Design Review: poles and ribbons showing the full building, certified by
   a licensed surveyor or civil engineer, at least 21 calendar days before the first hearing; ADR: 14
   days, only if the Director requires staking; re-staking for redesign: 14 days. Poles come down
   within 20 days after the final decision (25.05.040(C)(2)). Zone Clearance has no staking.
8. **Notice.** Mailed at least 21 calendar days before a Design Review hearing, 14 days before an ADR
   hearing, 10 days for most others, to owners within 300 ft, tenants within 100 ft, and HOAs within
   300 ft (25.05.065(D)). Posted in three public places, including the site, at least 10 days before
   (25.05.065(E) as amended). CDP notice follows 25.07.014 instead.
9. **Hearing.** Design Review Board and Planning Commission each meet twice a month; the DRB on the
   2nd and 4th Thursday at 5:00 p.m. (City web pages). The City's page says complete applications are
   tentatively scheduled about 45 days after completeness when the project is CEQA-exempt, and the
   Code limits a project to three substantive hearings and two non-substantive continuances (City
   design review process page, as of 2026-02-22).
10. **Decision.** Written notice within 14 calendar days (25.05.040(F)(5)). Design Review needs both
    findings: Code compliance and consistency with the 11 criteria (access, design articulation,
    environmental context, General Plan, historic preservation, landscaping, lighting and glare,
    neighborhood compatibility, pedestrian orientation, privacy, view equity) (25.05.040(H)-(I)).
11. **Appeal window, 14 days.** The decision takes effect 14 calendar days later unless appealed
    (25.05.040(K)(1)). During the window, staking stays up and no building permit application may be
    filed (City process page).
12. **Building permit.** File the construction set after the Planning approval is effective. Building
    returns a set that reaches the counter before its required Planning approval
    (the City checklists, lane building).

## Several projects at once (for example ADU + remodel + accessory building)

Find each piece's list first (`planning-approvals.md`, Steps 2-7). Then:

1. **One pre-application for the whole project.** Show every piece on the preliminary plans so the
   ZPC letter lists every application. Ask every UNSETTLED question there.
2. **Planning applications, filed together:**
   - one **Design Review Application** for all Design Review and ADR pieces; the highest list sets
     the decider and the hearing, and each piece keeps its own list item (25.05.040(B); City
     forms: "Additional applications are required when requesting additional entitlements");
   - Zone Clearance pieces shown on the same plans; whether they need the separate Zone Clearance
     Application is not stated (UNSETTLED; confirm at pre-application);
   - the **ADU & JADU Application** for the ADU, with a **CDP Application** in the coastal zone
     unless the ADU converts habitable space inside the house (25.07.008(A)(1)(a));
   - when any piece needs ADR or Design Review, expect **one CDP** for the ADU and the other work,
     decided by that hearing body at the same time (25.07.012(D); `coastal-development-permits`,
     "Picking the kind for a project with an ADU");
   - any variance, use permit or other add-on.
3. **The ADU keeps its own clock.** Completeness within 15 business days (Gov. Code 66317(a)(2)(A));
   decision within 60 days of a complete application (66317(a)(3); Ord. 1713 25.17.010(e)(2)(B)).
   The City may delay the ADU only when it is filed with an application "to create a new
   single-family or multifamily dwelling" (66317(a)(3); 25.17.010(e)(2)(B)(ii)). A remodel or an
   accessory building is not a new dwelling, so the ADU is not held for the remodel's hearing.
   Whether a major remodel (treated as a new building for zoning, 25.56.011) counts as "a new
   single-family dwelling" here is not addressed; our reading is no, because the dwelling already
   exists. Confirm with Planning.
4. **The ADU-only CDP is the alternative, not the default.** A CDP that covers only the ADU is
   decided within 60 days, concurrently with the ADU, with no hearing required, and is not
   appealable to the Coastal Commission (Gov. Code 66329(a), (c)). The applicant may ask at
   pre-application to file the ADU's CDP on its own; Planning decides. A CDP that also covers other
   work follows the normal hearing and appeal rules (`coastal-development-permits.md`).
5. **Building permits per scope.** One Building Permit Application per scope, each with its own
   valuation; grading over 20 cy is its own application (City Planning intake ruling, Sept. 2026; Building ePlan
   requirements). Each scope goes to Building after its own Planning approval is effective, so an
   approved ADU can go to plan check while the remodel waits for its hearing.
6. **Phasing does not reset the tests.** The major remodel expansion prong counts all additions
   since January 13, 1993 (October 2026 handout), and the coastal 10% floor-area exception counts
   earlier exempt additions (25.07.008(A)(1)(c)1). Splitting work into later permits does not avoid
   them.

Typical order: survey (topography, slope, walls floor by floor) and any geotechnical or biological
letter → pre-application → file ADU (+ its CDP) and Design Review (+ CDP) together → staking,
notice, hearing → 14-day appeal window → building permits per scope (fuel modification plan
approved first on FM lots, 14.70.070; geotechnical peer review where required).

## Statutory decision deadlines (Permit Streamlining Act)

After an application is complete, the City must approve or disapprove within 60 days of finding the
project CEQA-exempt or adopting a negative declaration, or 180 days after certifying an EIR (Gov. Code
65950(a)(1), (4)-(5)). Some housing projects have shorter limits (65950(a)(2)-(3)). Missed deadlines
are handled under Gov. Code 65956 (cited in LBMC 25.07.014(D)(2)). Housing-law questions:
`../../multi-unit-residential/SKILL.md`, which loads the state entitlement index.

## Appeals — LBMC 25.05.070 (replaced by Ord. 1732 §12)

| Decision by | Appeal to | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Staff or Director, ministerial (for example Zone Clearance) | Planning Commission or DRB, as applicable | Only ground: the decision did not follow the Zoning Code (25.05.070(B)(1)(i)) |
| Director, discretionary (for example ADR) | Planning Commission or DRB | 25.05.070(B)(1)(ii) |
| DRB or Planning Commission | City Council | 25.05.070(B)(2) |
| Any of the above | City Council review on one member's request | Filed with the City Clerk within 14 days; no fee or reasons (25.05.070(B)(3)) |
| City CDP in the appealable area, or an appealable exemption | Coastal Commission | 10 working days after the Commission receives notice (25.07.016(B); 25.07.008(C)) |

- **Who may appeal a City decision:** the ordinance names no class of appellant; the City's process
  page says owners within 300 ft may appeal. Confirm standing with the City Clerk.
- **How:** on the City form, within 14 calendar days, with the basis and the part of the decision
  appealed, and the fee (25.05.070(C)(1)-(2)). Filing suspends the decision (25.05.070(C)(3)).
- **Hearing:** scheduled within 90 days; de novo but limited to the stated grounds, with a presumption
  the decision was valid (25.05.070(C)(5), (D)). The City's older page says 60 days; the ordinance
  says 90.
- An incomplete determination may be appealed by the applicant (25.05.015(B)(4); Gov. Code 65943(c)).
- For a Coastal Commission appeal, local appeals usually must be used first, unless the City charges
  an appeal fee or another listed exception applies (25.07.016(A)).

## How long an approval lasts

| Approval | Life | Extensions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design Review (DRB, PC, ADR) | 2 years from effective date unless used or construction is diligently pursued | 2 years by ADR, then 1 final year | 25.05.040(K)(2)-(4); (B)(3)(a)(xvi) |
| CDP | 2 years | City CDPs not appealed to the Commission: 2 years, then 1 | 25.07.012(I)(3)-(4) |
| Variance | 2 years unless a building permit issues and work is diligently pursued | 2 years, then 1, by the Board of Adjustment or Planning Commission | 25.05.025(H)(2)-(3) |
| CUP | 2 years unless established | per 25.05.030(I) | 25.05.030(I)(3) |
| Temporary Use Permit | up to 3 years | None; a use going on past the term needs a new TUP or use permit, and a new or amended CDP (`planning-approvals` Step 1a item 2a) | 25.05.035(I) |
| Lot line adjustment | record within 90 days | one 90-day extension | 21.08.030(d) |

Extensions need findings that the rules and the neighborhood have not materially changed
(25.05.040(K)(4); 25.07.012(I)(4)(a)). They extend only "the beginning two-year approval period",
the time to start using an approval, never a term of use set by a TUP or a duration condition. The City's process page notes extensions carry a fee.

### Extending, reviving or changing an older approval

First call the record check for the parcel's Planning and building records; never rely
on the resident's memory of a case number. The full method is `planning-approvals.md`
Step 1a.

1. **Still alive** (inside its life or an approved extension): ask for the extension in writing
   before it expires (25.05.040(K)(3)). A Design Review extension is ADR (B)(3)(a)(xvi); City
   practice runs a joint Design Review-and-variance extension as one ADR too. The findings above
   apply, so a rule change since the approval can block the extension.
2. **Lapsed** (two years passed without use or diligent construction, 25.05.040(K)(2)): it is void
   and cannot be extended. Refile, naming the prior case number on the application. The refiled
   scope is routed under today's lists (Ord. 1732 for anything filed on or after 2026-07-22): run
   each piece through the `planning-approvals` method as if new. City practice has sent an
   unchanged refiling of an expired approval to ADR even where a piece reads lower under the lists,
   so give the code reading of each piece, say ADR is possible, and confirm at pre-application.
3. **Approved before Ord. 1732, not yet built:** the approval stands on its own terms for its life.
   A resubmittal or a new filing is reviewed under the current lists, not the lists in force when
   the first approval was granted.
4. **Changing approved plans:** a change to a live Design Review, variance or CUP is a change to that
   approval, not a fresh application: the Director may approve a minor change without a hearing on
   the 25.05.060(B)(3) findings, otherwise the original body decides on a new application
   (25.05.060(C)(1)). A City CDP: 25.07.012(J) (Ord. 1732) and certified 25.07.022, amended by the
   original approving authority; a Commission CDP, by the Commission. Details:
   `discretionary-permits.md`. A correction to the approved staking or roof
   elevations is a change too; City practice has handled one as ADR. New pieces outside the approved scope still run the
   `planning-approvals.md` method and are filed with the change. Never tell a
   resident to refile an approved, unexpired project as new; the approval and its CDP stay in force.
5. **The CDP and any variance run their own clocks** (table above); check each one, not only the
   Design Review.

## Fees

Never quote a fee from memory. Call the fee estimate with the application or permit type. The FY
2026-27 Master Fee Schedule took effect 2026-07-01 (Community Development fees 2026-07-12)
(the City source document (not included in this plugin)). Appeal fees are set by the
fee resolution (25.05.070(C)(2)).
