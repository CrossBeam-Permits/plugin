# Laguna Beach — Trees, views and hedges (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Use for:** "Can I cut down my tree?", "Can I remove the tree in front of my house?", "My
neighbor's trees block my ocean view", "How tall can my hedge be?", "Is my tree a heritage tree?",
"Does new landscaping need Planning approval?", "Will my addition block my neighbor's view?"

## 1. Which trees need a Tree Removal Permit? (LBMC Ch. 12.06)

**Lead line when someone wants to remove trees: "File a Tree Removal Permit application with
Community Development; the planner sets the category."** The code has staff classify each
application: it "shall be assigned one of the following category classifications" (12.06.040(b)),
and the category sets who decides. Give the category tests below as the reasons behind that line,
not as the opening. Do not open with "it depends where the trees stand". If the planner finds a
tree fits no category, it needs no TRP (12.06.080(f)); say that in one line after the lead.

"No person shall remove a heritage tree, a tree approved on a landscape plan approved through the
design review process or a privately maintained tree located in the public right-of-way without
first obtaining a tree removal permit (TRP) from the city" (LBMC 12.06.030). Everything else on
private property is outside the TRP: "Trees located on private property that cannot be classified
under Category I, II or III of this chapter" are exempt (12.06.080(f)).

Routing method. Ask in this order:

| Step | Question | If yes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the tree on the City's Heritage Tree List? | **Category I.** City Council decides by majority vote after notice to owners within 300 ft (12.06.040(c); 12.08.070). See section 2. |
| 2 | Is it a City-maintained tree (park, City property, City street tree)? | Not a TRP. City-maintained vegetation is exempt from Ch. 12.06 (12.06.020). Work on any tree in a park or public street needs a written permit from the City's parks foreman under Ch. 12.04 (12.04.080); removal permits are valid only with the City Manager's signature (12.04.040). Who staffs this today is not in the code: **UNSETTLED** — ask Public Works. |
| 3 | Is it in the public right-of-way (including a "paper street") but maintained by the owner, not the City? | **Category III.** The adjacent owner responsible for it applies. The Director of Community Development decides after consulting the Director of Public Works and holding a noticed on-site meeting at least 10 days after notice (12.06.040(e)). The tree is posted and marked with a yellow ribbon (12.06.090). |
| 4 | Is it shown on a landscape plan approved in Design Review? | **Category II.** 12.06.040(d) names the Design Review Board, with notice under 25.05.065 (12.06.090). Since Ord. 1732 the DRB hears Design Review only for single-family and two-family development (25.05.040(F)(1)); on any other property (campus, commercial, multifamily) expect the Planning Commission. UNSETTLED until the City conforms ch. 12.06; confirm with Planning. |
| 5 | None of the above | No TRP (12.06.080(f)). Check section 5 (native vegetation, Coastal Zone) before cutting. |

**Exemptions even for Category I-III trees** (12.06.080): trunk under 6 in. diameter at 4.5 ft
above grade; a tree an ISA certified arborist finds structurally unsound, dead, dying or diseased
(in writing to the City **before** removal); a tree in a utility easement an arborist finds a
hazard to the lines (in writing, before removal); an imminent threat as determined by the City
Manager, the Director of Community Development, the Director of Public Works, the Fire Chief, the
Police Chief or the Chief of Marine Safety; a tree
under a View Restoration Committee order. A heritage tree may also be removed in an emergency by
order of the police or fire department (12.08.050).

**Application** (12.06.040(a)): owner and agent details, a plot plan locating each tree, species
and approximate trunk diameter, a current photo, suggested replacement tree and size, and the
reason. A request based on tree health needs an arborist report.

**Decision criteria.** Category III uses the seven criteria in 12.06.050(c) (tree unsuited to the
planting area, damage to public improvements, overplanting, topography, no adverse effect on
neighbors, streetscape or canopy, suitable replacement). Category I uses 12.08.070. Category II
points to a Design Review subsection that Ord. 1732 replaced when it rewrote 25.05.040: which
criteria now apply is **UNSETTLED** — confirm with Planning at pre-application.

**Replacement trees** (12.06.060): the decision body sets the species and size. Minimum 15-gallon;
planted within 30 days after removal. Not required where it would hit utilities, the site is
overcrowded or unsuitable, or it lies in a significant watercourse or its 25-ft buffer.

**Timing.** Work must be done within 30 days of issuance unless extended (12.06.030); nesting birds
can extend it up to 90 days after a biologist finds the nest inactive. A permit not acted on within
one year is void (12.06.070). Once an application is filed, the tree may not be altered until the
decision (12.06.090).

**Appeals.** Category II (DRB) and Category III (Director) decisions go to the City Council under
25.05.070, with the City Manager setting the hearing date (12.06.040(d), (e)(3)). Category I is
decided by Council. Ord. 1732 replaced 25.05.070, so confirm the filing deadline with Planning.

**Penalties** (12.06.100(b)): removal without or in violation of a permit costs $1,000 to $15,000
per tree for Category II and III, double for heritage trees, by trunk size and repeat count. The
City can also withhold final building permits until the TRP conditions are met (12.06.100(a)(3)).
All removal costs and any damage to the right-of-way fall on the owner (12.06.130).

**City practice.** The Tree Removal Permit is filed as its own Planning case with its own fee, even
when it supports a larger project (for example trenching for utilities); the project's Design Review
or Zone Clearance does not grant it. The coastal question is different: in the coastal zone, removing
major vegetation is "development" (25.07.006), so when the removal is part of a project that has a
CDP, that CDP (or the project's exemption) describes and covers the removal with the rest of the
work; the TRP still runs as its own case. A removal on its own, with no project, takes its own CDP
determination from Planning (section 5). Fees: the fee estimate ("Tree Removal Permit — Category I,
II, III").

**Builders:** during construction, street trees near the work must be guarded against injury
(12.04.120).

## 2. Heritage trees (LBMC Ch. 12.08)

- **Eligibility** (12.08.020): one of: over 50 years old and historically significant;
  distinctive form, size or shape; tied to a person or event of community-wide significance; a
  large remnant of an original native stand of California live oaks, sycamores and toyons; or scenically prominent
  from public view corridors.
- **Listing** (12.08.040): the owner applies to the Director of Community Development. Notice goes
  to owners within 300 ft. Council lists the tree by majority vote. The tree may not be altered
  after the application until the decision.
- **What the owner gets** (12.08.030): a certificate and a note in the property file; City
  maintenance of a heritage tree in an unimproved part of an accepted street easement, on request;
  reimbursement of the public-notice cost; one waiver per year of Planning, Design Review and
  building permit application fees, up to $500 a year in total; and setback flexibility: an
  addition placed to save the tree may keep setbacks up to the line of existing encroachments. Any
  benefit requires a written agreement, binding later owners, to keep preserving and maintaining
  the tree (12.08.030(b)).
- **Owner duties** (12.08.050): maintain the tree's health and avoid unreasonable view impacts on
  neighbors. Removing, destroying or substantially altering it without a permit is a misdemeanor.
- **Substantial trimming or new construction within 15 ft of the trunk** needs a permit from the
  Director (12.08.060), judged on the tree's condition, nearness to structures and the trimming
  detail. Repair or replacement of existing construction is exempt. Appeal to Council in writing
  to the City Clerk by 5 p.m. on the 10th business day after the decision (12.08.080).
- **Removal** needs Council approval (12.08.070; Category I TRP).
- A neighbor cannot use a hedge claim or a view claim against a heritage tree: hedge concerns go
  only through the 12.08.070 process, and heritage trees are exempt from Ch. 12.16 (12.14.040(c);
  12.16.080(d)).
- Design Review also tells reviewers to protect existing heritage trees (LBMC 25.05.040(H)(3),
  Ord. 1732).
- Fee: the fee estimate ("Heritage Tree - Application").

## 3. A neighbor's vegetation blocks my view (LBMC Ch. 12.16)

Ch. 12.16 covers views blocked by **vegetation** only, not buildings. "Vegetation" means woody
plants or grasses taller than 6 ft; it excludes heritage trees, City-maintained vegetation, and
plants under a Design Review height limit or a finalized hedge or view claim (12.16.020). A "view"
is seen from the claimant's principal residential structure and "does not mean an unobstructed
panorama" (12.16.020).

**Two routes:**

1. **View preservation (record of views)** (12.16.040). Staff visit, photograph the unimpaired view
   from the locations you pick, keep the record in the property address files, and mail notice to
   vegetation owners within 500 ft. The record is a benchmark, not a height limit. If the view is
   later significantly impaired, staff give the vegetation owner 30 days, then may order
   restoration within 90 days; non-compliance can be abated as a nuisance. Appeal of a staff
   determination: City Council, within 14 calendar days of the notice to the vegetation owner.
   Filing walkthrough: `../../city-website/references/submit-view-preservation.md`.
2. **View restoration** (12.16.050). Restores "pre-existing views": views that "existed on or after
   either the date of acquisition of the claimant's property or November 4, 2003 ... whichever is
   earlier" (12.16.020). The baseline date is always the earlier of the two, so for anyone who
   bought after November 4, 2003 the baseline is still November 4, 2003: a view that existed on or
   after that date counts, and photos or testimony from before the purchase (a seller's, a
   neighbor's, a listing's) can prove it. Only an owner who bought before that date gets an earlier
   baseline, the purchase date. The claimant carries the burden of proof, with date-stamped photos
   or written testimony from people who saw the view (12.16.020 "Burden of proof"); proof of a view
   from a guest house or second unit cannot predate its certificate of occupancy. Limits: the vegetation
   must be 6 ft or taller and within 500 ft of your property line, and not already acted on for
   the same claimant without changed circumstances. Steps:
   - talk to the vegetation owner first;
   - file a notice of intent; the City invites the owner to a mediation meeting (owner has 14
     days to respond). If **you** refuse mediation, the City will not process a claim. If the
     owner refuses or mediation fails, you may file a claim;
   - the five-member **View Restoration Committee** visits and holds a noticed hearing, applies
     the 11 criteria in 12.16.030, and may order trimming or removal only on the findings in
     12.16.050(c)(3)(D) (removal only if lesser actions fail; minimum action needed);
   - orders carry a long-term maintenance schedule; work is due within 90 days;
   - the decision takes effect 14 days after the resolution; appeal by either party to Council
     within 14 calendar days (12.16.050(c)(4)-(5)).

**Who pays** (12.16.060): the claimant pays the mediator and, generally, the initial restoration
work (the Committee may split it); the vegetation owner pays later maintenance. Replacement trees
are generally 15-gallon to 24-in. box.

**Exempt vegetation** (12.16.080): under 6 ft or more than 500 ft away; City-maintained; heritage
trees; plants with Design Review height limits; plants approved in a hedge claim; view claims
decided before December 17, 2014; plants under a court settlement.

**Decisions run with the land**: staff and Committee decisions bind both owners and later owners
and are recorded with the County where permitted (12.16.100).

**Not a City matter:** branches that merely overhang your property line are a civil matter
between neighbors (City views-and-trees page). Fees: the fee estimate.

## 4. Hedges

**Height in the setbacks (zoning).** Fences, walls, hedges, latticework and screens may be:

- up to **4 ft** in the front yard (3 ft for a fence or wall in the front yard of a corner lot);
- up to **6 ft** in the side and rear yards.

Hedges **may exceed** those heights **unless** a hedge height claim has been filed and the City
finds the hedge is a safety hazard or blocks views or sunlight to a neighbor (LBMC
25.50.012(B)(1), (B)(3), citing Ch. 12.14). Corner cutback limits still apply (25.50.012(B)(1)(a)).

**Hedge height claim (LBMC Ch. 12.14).**

- Covers a hedge in a front, side or rear yard within **100 ft** of your property line that causes
  a safety hazard or blocks your views or sunlight (12.14.040(a), 12.14.070).
- Not available for City trees, heritage trees, or hedges with a Design Review height limit
  (12.14.040(b)-(d)).
- Filing (12.14.050): claim form; five sets of a dimensioned plot plan; five sets of photos taken
  from your property; proof you tried to resolve it with the hedge owner; a 100-ft radius mailing
  list from a professional listing service with stamped envelopes; the fee.
- The Director (or designee) visits, holds a noticed public hearing, and decides. The hedge may
  not be cut down to below the fence heights in 25.50.012(B) unless it is a safety hazard
  (12.14.060(f)). The hedge may not be altered while the claim is pending (12.14.060(c)).
- Appeal by either party to Council under 25.05.070 (12.14.060(h)). If you win, half the claim
  fee is refunded and the owner has 30 days to comply (12.14.060(i)-(j)).

## 6. New landscaping — does it need Planning approval?

Under Ord. 1732, landscaping is routed by **maintained height** and **location**
("buildable area" = the lot minus required setbacks, LBMC 25.08.004 as added by Ord. 1732):

| Planting | Route | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| In the buildable area, kept under 15 ft | Zone Clearance (plans must show and require the maintained height) | LBMC 25.05.040(B)(2)(n) |
| In the buildable area, 15 ft or taller | Administrative Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(3)(a)(i) |
| Side or rear yard under 6 ft; front yard under 4 ft | Zone Clearance (same plan condition) | 25.05.040(B)(2)(o) |
| Side or rear yard 6 ft or taller; front yard 4 ft or taller | Administrative Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(3)(a)(ii) |
| Any of the above in an environmentally sensitive area (incl. slopes of 30% or more) without a peer-reviewed no-impact finding | Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(1)(e), (B)(2), (B)(3)(a) |
| Landscape plan as part of a major remodel, new building, or improvement over 15 ft | Required with the Design Review application, plus an exterior lighting plan | 25.05.040(B)(1)(a)-(c) |

A landscape plan approved in Design Review with height limits binds later owners: trim to those
heights (25.05.040(F)(6), Ord. 1732), and a tree on that plan needs a Category II TRP to remove
(12.06.030). Such plants are also outside hedge and view claims (12.14.040(d); 12.16.080(e)).

Landscaping in the Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone also has to meet the Fire Department's
fuel modification and defensible-space rules. See `fire-hazard-and-wui.md`. Water-efficient
landscape (WELO, LBMC Ch. 19.01) is a building-side submittal; the City checklists.

## 7. Will my project block my neighbor's view? (view equity in Design Review)

There is no general private right to a view from new buildings in Title 25; Ch. 12.16 covers
vegetation only. But when a project is in **Design Review**, the reviewer weighs **view equity**:
the project and its landscaping "shall be designed to protect existing views from neighboring
properties without denying the subject property the reasonable opportunity to develop" (LBMC
25.05.040(H)(11), Ord. 1732). Landscape design must also treat view equity as "an important
consideration" (25.05.040(H)(6)).

- View equity is a **criterion**, not a veto. It applies only to projects that reach Design
  Review or Administrative Design Review.
- A project that only needs Zone Clearance is checked against zoning standards, not the design
  criteria (LBMC 25.05.045(C)(1)).
- Staking (story poles) lets neighbors see the envelope before the hearing. See
  `process-timeline-appeals.md`.

## What to tell the resident (short form)

1. Tree removal: lead with "file a Tree Removal Permit; the planner sets the category"
   (12.06.040(b)). Then run the section 1 table to say which category the facts point to (or that
   none fits, so no TRP), any exemption that fits, who decides and how to appeal.
2. For heritage trees, hedge claims and view claims, give the steps and limits above with the
   Title 12 citation; offer the municipal code for the full text.
3. Say UNSETTLED only where this file does: who staffs street-tree permits under Ch. 12.04, the
   Category II removal criteria after Ord. 1732, and appeal deadlines under the replaced 25.05.070.
   Point those to Planning at (949) 497-0713 or Public Works.
4. Mention the Coastal Development Permit check and fire rules when the lot is in the Coastal
   Zone or the Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone (the parcel lookup).
