# Laguna Beach — Grading, drainage, hillsides and soils reports (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Use for:** "Do I need a grading permit?", "How much dirt can I move?", "Do I need a soils
report?", "I'm on a steep lot — what changes?", "Does a retaining wall need Design Review?"

## Source limits (read first)

- **LBMC Title 22 (Excavating, Grading and Filling) is in the code lookup** (the municipal code (LBMC 22.10)
  etc.). LBMC 22.10.010(a) requires a grading permit above 20 cubic yards. Its exceptions in
  22.10.010(e) say "A grading permit shall not be required for any of the following conditions, but
  in all other respects the provisions of this title shall apply", and include (1) footing and wall
  excavation for a building under a valid building permit (but not fill made from it), (2) "An
  excavation for a swimming pool if authorized by a valid building permit or valid swimming pool
  permit", (3)-(4) small landscaping fills and cuts under 20 cy within the stated slope and height
  limits, (5) emergency work, and (6) "Backfill for a swimming pool if authorized by a valid
  building permit" (added by Ord. 1732 §54). 22.10.010(d) bans dumping pool or foundation spoil in
  an uncontrolled way. Ch. 22.06 governs erosion control.
- **Building's 20 cu yd count** is settled by a Building Division ruling (section 2).
- **Planning's 20 cu yd test excludes the 22.10.010(e) work.** (B)(1)(f) and (B)(2)(i) both say
  "except as specified in Section 22.10.010(e)". On the plain text, pool excavation and pool backfill
  under a valid permit are therefore outside the Planning count. The City has not ruled on this in a
  record we have: say so and "confirm at pre-application", but lead with the plain-text reading.
- What we do have: the Ord. 1732 Planning thresholds, the Building Division's 2026 handouts, and
  the City's grading-permit portal configuration
  (`../../city-website/references/submit-grading.md`).

## 1. Planning: which review does grading need?

Ord. 1732 sets the Planning route by volume **outside the building footprint**:

| Grading | Route | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **20 cu yd or less** outside the building footprint | Zone Clearance | LBMC 25.05.040(B)(2)(i) |
| **More than 20 cu yd** outside the building footprint | **Design Review** (DRB for single-family and two-family; Planning Commission for others) | 25.05.040(B)(1)(f); (F)(1) |
| Either one, "except as specified in Section 22.10.010(e)" | Pool excavation and pool backfill under a valid permit are excluded on the plain text (22.10.010(e)(2), (e)(6)); also footing excavation (e)(1) and small landscaping cuts and fills (e)(3)-(4). Confirm at pre-application | 25.05.040(B)(1)(f), (B)(2)(i) |
| A pool or spa itself | Over 3 ft above adjacent legal grade: Design Review (B)(1)(j); 3 ft or less: Zone Clearance (B)(2)(t). The ordinance decides: an in-ground pool at or below 3 ft is Zone Clearance. The City's review-level matrix lists built-in pools at DRB/PC; name that conflict, and confirm at the counter (no post-Ord. 1732 new-pool decision yet). On a 30% slope the ESA rule decides first | 25.05.040(B)(1)(j), (B)(2)(t) |
| Any grading in an environmentally sensitive area (including **slopes of 30% or more**) | Design Review, unless a licensed professional finds, with peer review, no impact | 25.05.040(B)(1)(e), (B)(2); ESA definition, LBMC 25.08.010 (Ord. 1732) |

**Counting the cubic yards (driveway, patio, yard or pad work).** Work it out; do not guess.
1. Draw the building footprint. Only grading outside it counts for (B)(1)(f) and (B)(2)(i): both
   items say "outside the building footprint". Cut and fill under the footprint (excavate-and-refill
   for a foundation, a basement) is not counted, for Planning by those words and for Building by its
   ruling (section 2).
2. For each area outside: cubic yards = area (sf) x average depth (ft) / 27. A 10 x 40 ft driveway
   regraded 6 in deep is 400 x 0.5 / 27, about 7.4 cu yd. Removing and replacing base under new
   paving is excavation ("any mechanical act by which earth ... is cut into, dug ... removed",
   22.04), so count it.
3. Keep cut and fill separate. Building counts the greater of the two (section 2). Planning's items
   say "grading", which is "any excavation, filling or combination thereof" (22.04), and do not say
   whether cut and fill are added: when the two readings straddle 20 cu yd, give both and confirm.
4. Take out what 22.10.010(e) excludes: footing excavation under a building permit, pool excavation
   and backfill, and small landscaping cuts or fills (under 20 cu yd, no more than 3 ft, kept 2 ft
   from lot lines).
5. 20 cu yd or less: Zone Clearance (B)(2)(i). Staff often clear minor grading with the Zone
   Clearance for the work it serves, at the counter; tell the resident to confirm at the counter.
   More than 20: Design Review (B)(1)(f) and its own grading permit (section 2).
Slopes of 30% or more turn any of this into Design Review first (row above).

Related Planning triggers that often come with grading:

| Work | Route | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Retaining wall **5 ft or less** exposed height | Zone Clearance | 25.05.040(B)(2)(j) |
| Retaining wall **more than 5 ft** exposed height | Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(1)(g) |
| Clearing native vegetation on undeveloped land | Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(1)(h) |
| Shoreline or bluff protective device | Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(1)(i) |
| New street or alley access, or widening a driveway at the street beyond the minimum | Design Review | 25.05.040(B)(1)(k) |

Design reviewers are told that existing terrain should be used and "grading should be minimized
except when necessary to balance other design review criteria" (LBMC 25.05.040(H)(3)).

Use `../../retaining-wall/SKILL.md` for wall details and `../../coastal-development/SKILL.md` for
the CDP check. Grading and landform alteration are common CDP triggers in the Coastal Zone.

Ord. 1732 is local law not yet certified by the Coastal Commission; see `design-review-lists.md`.

## 2. Building: the grading permit

- **How Building counts the 20 cubic yards (Building Division ruling, 2026-09-22).** Take the
  greater of cut or fill **outside the building footprint** only. Grading inside the footprint (a
  basement, a deepened crawl space, excavate-and-refill under the house) never counts toward the 20
  cu yd and never needs its own application, though it still gets grading review and a fee. More
  than 20 cu yd outside the footprint: its own Building Permit Application, valuation and permit, off
  the same plan set. 20 cu yd or less: it rides on the application it serves, with the quantity stated
  in the description. Show a grading table splitting cut and fill inside and outside the footprint
  (the City checklists, lane building). This matches the Planning count in section 1, so one
  number answers both.
- **Pool or spa excavation.** 22.10.010(e)(2) and (e)(6) exempt pool excavation and pool backfill
  under a valid building or pool permit from the grading permit, so on the plain text they are out of
  the Planning count too (section 1). Building's grading table tracks pool or spa quantities in their
  own column, apart from the outside-footprint count, and the pool is its own building application.
  Other grading on the same job (terraces, walls, yard cut and fill) still counts. No City record we
  have rules on this directly: give the plain-text reading and "confirm with the Building counter at
  pre-application".
- **Terraced retaining walls:** the 5-ft Planning items speak of a wall's "exposed height"
  ((B)(1)(g), (B)(2)(j)). Whether closely spaced terraces are measured wall by wall or as one wall
  is not stated in the code; UNSETTLED, confirm at pre-application.
  (Building Division "ePlan Digital Submittal Requirements," received 2026-08-12,
  the City source document (not included in this plugin).)
- The City files it as **Grading Permit, EnerGov type 214**. Plans are required. Final issuance is
  in person at the Building counter (`../../city-website/references/submit-grading.md`).
- **Grading review** is triggered, per the Building Division's matrix (effective 2026-08-13), by:
  new buildings, new detached ADUs, additions over 1,000 sq ft, basement excavation, retaining-wall
  cuts and backfill, cut-and-fill, slope restoration or repair, landslide repair, tract grading, and
  any plan set that includes civil drawings
  (Building Division grading, residential and fire review matrix, Aug. 2026).
- **Work hours:** grading Monday-Friday **8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.**; hauling **9:00 a.m. to 3:00
  p.m.**; no weekends or federal holidays (City building-permits page; Building Division
  construction staging handout; LBMC 7.25.080).
- Fees: the fee estimate.

## 3. Soils (geotechnical) reports

The Building Division handout "When Is a Geotechnical/Soils Report Required?" (effective
2026-06-17) says a report by a licensed geotechnical engineer, civil engineer or geologist is
required for:

1. any new construction (not an addition or alteration);
2. single-family additions that add more than **1,200 sq ft**, add a **second or upper floor**, or
   increase floor area by **50% or more**, or when the Building Official requires it;
3. retaining walls **over 5 ft** from the bottom of footing, or any wall with a surcharge;
4. shoring for cuts **over 6 ft**;
5. lots steeper than **1 vertical : 2 horizontal**;
6. any construction on beach or oceanfront lots (plus a **wave run-up study**), coastal bluff tops,
   steep hillsides, or liquefaction zones;
7. sites with past soil or structural failure;
8. non-conventional foundations (piles, caissons, deepened footings, post-tensioned slabs);
9. new swimming pools on ungraded lots, in a floodplain, or on a coastal bluff top;
10. whenever the Building Official requires it.

**Peer review** by a City consultant is required for shoring over 6 ft, beach/oceanfront, bluff
top, steep hillside, liquefaction zone, **expansive soil**, **potential landslide**, past failures,
non-conventional foundations, or when the Building Official requires it. The peer-review deposit
is a separate check (the fee estimate; the New-Major handout states $5,000).

Source: the City source document (not included in this plugin).
The report and the peer-review lists are different. Do not merge them.

**State seismic hazard zones.** When the parcel lookup reports a California Geological Survey
earthquake-induced landslide or liquefaction "zone of required investigation", the Seismic Hazards
Mapping Act (Public Resources Code 2690 et seq.; 2697) requires a site investigation and
geotechnical report before a city approves a project in the zone. Its text is not in our corpus.
The City handout already requires a report for new construction, liquefaction zones and steep
hillsides, and peer review for "potential landslide" and liquefaction sites. Tell the resident to
expect a geotechnical report with City peer review for new construction or additions in a CGS
landslide zone, and to confirm the scope with the Building Division. A City landslide layer hit
points the same way. Neither one is an ESA under 25.08.010, so neither changes the Planning list.

Two copies, bound, dated within one year of submittal, for a paper submittal (same handout).

## 4. Drainage and water quality

Grading submittals usually include (Grading Permit type 214 file set; New-Major handout):

- grading and drainage plans and erosion control plans (BMPs);
- hydrology calculations, wet-stamped;
- a **Water Quality Management Plan (WQMP)** where required, with a third-party review deposit and
  processing fee; otherwise the **LID checklist**;
- a site survey with existing and proposed grades.

At the Planning stage, a WQMP is an intake item only for a new home or a major remodel on a slope
over 25 percent (the City checklists lane "planning"). Water quality rules sit in LBMC
Ch. 16.01.

**Water Quality ESA (WQESA).** The City's WQESA map, adopted by reference in Ch. 16.01, shows
water-quality sensitive areas (Aliso Creek, the ocean coastline, marine refuges, NCCP areas) and
the land within 200 ft of them (16.01.020 "Priority development project" (9)). When
the parcel lookup reports a WQESA hit:
- the project routes to **Public Works (Water Quality)**, which decides whether a WQMP or the LID
  checklist is needed (City planning worksheet routing; 16.01.050(a));
- a WQMP is required for a priority development project, which includes **2,500 sq ft or more of
  impervious surface**, or a **10% or greater increase** in impervious surface over the natural
  condition, within, within 200 ft of, or draining directly to a sensitive area (16.01.020 PDP (9));
  other PDP categories include hillside development over 5,000 sq ft grading slopes of 25% or more
  (PDP (6)) and 10,000 sq ft of new impervious surface (PDP (1));
- the City may require independent review of the WQMP at the applicant's cost (16.01.050(a)).
WQESA is a Title 16 map. It is **not** the "environmentally sensitive area" of LBMC 25.08.010
(Ord. 1732), so it does not by itself push a project to Design Review.

## 5. Hillside lots

- **R/HP zone (LBMC Ch. 25.15):** Ord. 1732 narrowed the R/HP design criteria. It removed the
  former architectural-style and grading criteria and kept mass and scale, building size,
  landscaping, fuel modification and an amended building-site criterion
  (the City source document (not included in this plugin)). Uses and development
  standards in 25.15.006-25.15.010 still apply.
- **Slopes of 30% or more** are an environmentally sensitive area under Ord. 1732. That moves most
  work there into Design Review absent a peer-reviewed no-impact finding. LBMC 25.50.040 (not amended
  by Ord. 1732) also makes structures on lots over 30% slope subject to design review and requires an
  offer to dedicate an open-space deed restriction or easement (LUP Policy 14-J). This slope ESA
  governs the review list only; it is not the Chapter 25.07 habitat ESA that defeats a CDP
  exemption (`coastal-development-permits.md`).
- The LCP Open Space/Conservation Element policy 10-E requires a geological and soils report
  "pursuant to Title 22" for development in Hillside Management/Conservation areas or mapped
  geologic hazard areas, and a deed-restricted waiver of liability where hazards are confirmed
  (the City source document (not included in this plugin)).
- Fuel modification and fire access often shape hillside site work; see `fire-hazard-and-wui.md`.

## 6. Floodplain

Development in a mapped flood area (new construction, fill, improvements, watercourse changes)
needs a **Floodplain Development Permit** (LBMC Ch. 25.38; City application "Floodplain
Development Permit Application for Residential Development"). Use the parcel lookup for the
flood zone.

## What to tell the resident (short form)

1. Ask or estimate the cubic yards **outside the building footprint** (greater of cut or fill).
   Give the 20 cu yd Planning split with the 25.05.040 letter; Building uses the same count.
2. Say grading over 20 cu yd outside the footprint needs its own building permit application
   (Grading Permit 214). Pool excavation and backfill under a valid permit are outside the count on the plain text of 22.10.010(e)(2), (e)(6); say to confirm.
3. Run the soils-report list against the project and the parcel (the parcel lookup: slope, bluff,
   liquefaction, CGS landslide zone, flood). Flag WQESA for Public Works water quality.
4. Flag the slope-30% ESA rule and the CDP check.
5. Title 22 specifics: cite the chapter from the municipal code; bonds and fees: "confirm with Building."
