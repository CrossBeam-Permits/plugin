# Laguna Beach — Running a business from home (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Authority:** LBMC 25.96.040 Home occupations (Ord. 1712, 2025-03-11);
LBMC Chapter 25.16 Artists' work/live; City FAQ and building-permits pages.
**Use for:** "Can I run my business from home?", "Do I need a permit to see clients at my
house?", "Do I need a business license?", "Can I teach music lessons at home?", "Can I sell the
art I make at home?"

## Source limits

- **LBMC Title 5 (Business — Taxation, Licensing) is not in the repo.** We know from the code and
  the City that a business license is required, but not the license rules, fees or exemptions.
  For those, send the resident to the City's business license page:
  https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/do-business-here/business-license/starting-a-business-in-laguna-beach
- The Home Occupation Permit fee is in the fee schedule; quote it only through the fee estimate.
- **No Home Occupation Permit form or link is in our sources.** The City's Planning applications list
  (`../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`) and forms index do not include one. The code says the
  form and contents are "as specified by the Community Development Director" (25.96.040(D)(1)). Tell
  the resident to ask the Community Development counter or Planning for the current form; do not
  invent a link or a portal permit type.

## 1. What approvals a home business needs

| Approval | When | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Home Occupation Permit** (Community Development; Planning and Building approval; may need a safety inspection) | For a business activity run by the residents of a dwelling as an accessory use | LBMC 25.96.040(D)(1) |
| **City business license** | Must be kept current the whole time the home is used for the business | LBMC 25.96.040(D)(2); City FAQ ("a business may not be established without first receiving a business license") |
| **Building permit** | Only if you build, alter, convert, repair or demolish anything. Accessibility upgrades may be required if the business is open to the public or non-resident employees. | 25.96.040(D)(3) |
| **Coastal Development Permit** | If the home occupation project in the Coastal Zone counts as "development" under 25.07.006 | 25.96.040(D)(4); `../../coastal-development/SKILL.md` |
| **Temporary Use Permit** | For special or temporary events tied to the business | 25.96.040(I)(13); LBMC 25.05.035 |
| **Design Review route for any exterior change** | Exterior or interior alterations follow Title 25 like any other project (Zone Clearance, Administrative Design Review or Design Review) | 25.96.040(I)(16)(c); `planning-approvals.md` |

The **Director decides** the Home Occupation Permit. The Director must deny it if the business
and its location are reasonably likely to cause fumes, odor, dust, smoke, gas, noise, vibration,
traffic or parking problems (25.96.040(E)). Appeals follow LBMC 25.05.070 (25.96.040(F)).
A change in operations or business type needs an **amended** permit (25.96.040(G)).

The application includes a business narrative, an interior floor plan marking the business area,
total living area, living area used for the business, and any building or site work
(25.96.040(D)(1)(a)).

**What the code requires, step by step (25.96.040(D)):**
1. File a Home Occupation Permit application with the Community Development Department, with the fee
   set by Council resolution ((D)(1)).
2. Include a business operations narrative, an interior floor plan showing where the business goes,
   the total living area and the area used for the business, and any building or site work
   ((D)(1)(a)).
3. Planning and Building both approve it; a safety inspection may be required ((D)(1)).
4. Hold a current City business license the whole time ((D)(2)).
5. Get a building permit for any construction, and a CDP if the project is coastal development
   ((D)(3), (D)(4)).

## 2. Allowed business types (25.96.040(B))

Home occupations are limited to:

1. art and craft work (ceramics, painting, photography, sculpture); retail sales on site **by
   appointment**;
2. art, music and similar fine-art lessons, and academic tutoring;
3. cottage food operators (pickup at the home by appointment; the home keeps a residential look;
   food is eaten off site);
4. hair, nail, facial and similar personal care;
5. massage therapy;
6. office uses (accountant, architect, attorney, consultant, insurance agent, licensed
   therapist, technical advisor, writer);
7. physical therapists and personal trainers;
8. psychology and psychiatric offices;
9. other uses the Director finds similar and not harmful to the neighborhood.

The home must be a conforming or legal nonconforming residential use.

## 3. Not allowed in a dwelling (25.96.040(C))

Adult businesses; animal hospitals, kennels, breeding, grooming or training; industrial assembly;
carpentry and cabinet making (a hobby is fine); construction or landscaping businesses that store
goods, equipment or materials on site; gun sales; hazardous uses; medical clinics, labs and
pharmacies (except the listed therapy uses); manufacturing; marijuana growing, processing or
sales; vehicle repair, painting, storage or washing; self-storage; direct retail with stock on hand
(except as allowed above); schools (lessons and tutoring are allowed); tattoo shops; welding and
machining; and other uses the Director finds incompatible.

## 4. Operating rules (25.96.040(I))

| Topic | Residential zone | Mixed-use or commercial zone |
|---|---|---|
| Share of the home used for business | At most **half** of the habitable area | Same |
| Non-resident employees on site at once | **1** (plus one on-site parking space per employee, on private property; may be uncovered or tandem, in the front or side setback) | **2** |
| Customer vehicle trips per day | **12** (6 car visits) | **24** (12 car visits) |
| Business visitors at one time | **2**, by appointment, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. | **4**, same hours |
| Signs | **None**, on or off site | One vinyl window decal up to 10% of the window and 10 sq ft (5 sq ft downtown), letters 8 in max; directory listing on multi-tenant sites |
| Commercial vehicle | None; only residents' and employees' passenger cars | One licensed car, pickup or van, not parked on the street |

Also:

- The operator must **live in the home**. Work and living areas may not be leased separately.
- There must be direct inside access between the business area and the living space.
- All work is **indoors** unless the permit allows outdoor activity the Director finds compatible.
- The business and its storage must not be visible from the street or from neighbors.
- No change to the fire safety or occupancy classification. No commercial-scale utility service;
  the Building Official may allow 400-600 amp electric service.
- Deliveries no more frequent than a normal home receives.
- Interior changes may not make the home unsuitable to live in. Exterior changes may not add
  features unusual for a house.

## 5. Artists who want a work/live unit

A home occupation is the light-touch path for artists working at home. A true **artists'
work/live unit** (work comes first, living is secondary) is a different use. It needs a
**Conditional Use Permit** (Planning Commission) for the unit and an **Artist Occupancy Permit**
for each artist, reviewed by the artist review panel after the Director confirms the art type fits
the zone (LBMC 25.16.020, 25.16.030). A CDP may also be required (25.16.035). For the
full chapter, call the municipal code (LBMC 25.16); residents can use it. Give these points and
send the resident to Planning for the application.

## 6. Short-term rental is not a home occupation

Renting rooms or the home for 30 days or less is short-term lodging under Chapter 25.23, with its
own permit and zoning limits. See `short-term-lodging.md`.

## 7. Opening a business outside the home

For a storefront or office, the business license counter routes the applicant to Planning to
confirm the zone allows the use and whether a CUP or parking fix is needed (City FAQ). Use
`../../commercial-ti/SKILL.md` for the use and zoning table.

## What to tell the resident (short form)

1. Check the zone (the parcel lookup). Say whether the business type is on the allowed list.
2. Name the two approvals: Home Occupation Permit (Director; form from Community Development, not
   in our sources) and City business license.
3. Give the limits that matter to them (employees, visitors, signs, half the floor area).
4. Add a building permit or CDP only if they are changing the building or the Coastal Zone rule is
   triggered.
5. Business license details: "confirm with the City's business license office."
