# Laguna Beach — Temporary uses, events, containers and temporary structures (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Authority:** LBMC 25.05.035 Temporary Use Permits (not changed by Ord. 1732); LBMC 25.05.040(B)(2)(x)
(Ord. 1732); LBMC 25.07.008(A)(6); LBMC 25.10.005(B), 25.12.005(B); LBMC Ch. 1.09, Ch. 11.60; City
Planning practice since Ord. 1732 where noted.
**Use for:** "Do I need a permit for an event?", "Can I put a storage container in my driveway?",
"A recurring event or activity at a business, with no construction", "Temporary office or sales trailer", "Event on a
street, beach or park", "Who decides a temporary use permit?"

## The method

1. **Is it temporary, or a new permanent use? Lead with the TUP for anything the applicant calls
   occasional.** A Temporary Use Permit (TUP) is for "interim or temporary uses" that are desirable
   for the community or needed while a permitted use is set up or a public facility is built
   (25.05.035(A)), and the non-residential list names the usual occasional activities at a business
   by name: concerts (live music), art shows, sidewalk sales, tents, sporting events and the like
   ((B)(2)). So when a business asks about occasional events (live music some evenings, a seasonal
   market, a sale), lead with a TUP decided by the body 25.05.035(F) names (step 3), not a CUP. Use
   the zone's use list (permitted, AUP or CUP; `discretionary-permits`) only when the applicant
   describes an ongoing, permanent use: a standing part of the business with no end, such as nightly
   entertainment as part of how the place operates. One "changes to a use permit if it becomes a
   permanent part of the business" line covers the other branch. A TUP lasts at most three years
   (25.05.035(I)).
2. **Is the use on the TUP list for the zone?** (25.05.035(B))
   - **Residential zones:** temporary construction structures, garages or sheds; parking or storing
     construction equipment during a project; storing materials for a public works project,
     subdivision or construction; a tract or lot sales office; events that close a public street;
     temporary parking lots; and others the Planning Commission adds by resolution ((B)(1)).
   - **Non-residential zones:** outdoor art and craft shows; carnivals; Christmas tree sales;
     concerts; farmers markets; horse shows and animal exhibitions; outdoor merchandise and display
     stands; sporting events; street vendors, sales booths and sidewalk sales; tents or canvas or
     plastic enclosures; the residential-zone list; and others the Commission adds ((B)(2)). Uses in
     industrial zones need special care to fit the zone.
   - Not on the list and not added by resolution: UNSETTLED; the Commission can add similar uses by
     resolution ((B)(1)(g), (B)(2)(l)). Confirm with Planning.
3. **Who decides?** (25.05.035(F))
   - **Director:** every TUP in the R-1 zone, and short-term events of **five days or less** that the
     Director deems minor, in any zone. Temporary parking lots are excluded. Decision within 20
     business days unless referred to a hearing ((F)(1)).
   - **Planning Commission:** everything else, with General Plan consistency findings ((F)(3)),
     after public notice under 25.05.065 ((E)) and posted notice (25.05.065(E), Ord. 1732). A recurring
     series is not one minor event of five days or less, so outside R-1 it goes to the Commission,
     even when each occurrence is short.
   - The Director may refer any TUP to the Commission ((F)(1)). A Commission TUP takes effect 14
     calendar days after the decision unless appealed to the City Council ((I)); appeals follow
     25.05.070 ((H)).
4. **Does it also need a CDP?** In the coastal zone a temporary use that is "development" also needs
   a CDP ((F)(2)), noticed under 25.07.014 instead ((E)). Events on beaches have their own exemption
   test (25.07.008(A)(6); `coastal-development-permits` "Temporary events"). **Exclusive use of a
   public street or parking area points to a CDP.** The LCP's "temporary event" is development of
   limited duration that places non-permanent structures "and/or involves exclusive use of a sandy
   beach, parkland, filled tidelands, water, streets or parking area which is otherwise open and
   available for general public use" (25.07.008(A)(6)(d)), and the only event exemption is for
   events on beach areas ((A)(6)(a)). So an event that closes a public street or parking area to the
   public has no listed exemption; a recurring series beyond "limited duration" (two weeks straight
   or four months intermittent) is not even a temporary event. When the event takes a public street,
   City lot, beach or park, lead with "expect a CDP, decided with the TUP by the same body
   (25.07.012(D))". On a private lot or inside a private building that is not otherwise open for
   general public use, the definition does not plainly reach it: say Planning decides whether a CDP
   applies (25.07.012(B)) and ask at pre-application; do not lead with a CDP.
5. **Conditions to expect** ((G)): hours, a bond for cleanup or removal, restoring the site, permit
   length, sign, lighting and sound limits, nuisance limits, and possibly referral to Design Review.
6. **Building and Fire.** Tents, stages, temporary structures and temporary power can need Building
   or Fire permits on their own (`../../temporary-power/SKILL.md` for power).
7. **The TUP is ending and the use will continue.** There is no extension of a TUP's term:
   "Temporary Use Permits may be authorized for a maximum three year time period" (25.05.035(I)),
   and the section has no extension clause. The two-year "extension of time" rules elsewhere
   (Design Review, CDP, variance, CUP) extend only "the beginning two-year approval period", the
   time to start using an approval, never the term of a use. So:
   - **Lead with a new TUP.** A TUP that has run its maximum term cannot be renewed or extended;
     continuing the same occasional or interim activity takes a **new TUP** application, decided by
     the body in step 3 (Director in R-1 or for a minor event of five days or less; Planning
     Commission otherwise). The code does not bar a second TUP; the decider must again find the use
     interim or temporary. A change to the live TUP's duration is a condition change, which only the
     original review authority can approve "through a new permit application" (25.05.060(B)(3)(i)(c),
     (C)(1)), so it lands with the same body.
   - **A CUP only if the applicant now describes a permanent use:** then the zone's use list
     (permitted, AUP or CUP) as a new application (`discretionary-permits`). Do not move an
     occasional activity to a CUP just because its TUP ran out.
   - **CDP:** a new CDP, or an amendment of the old CDP's time condition (25.07.022; Ord. 1732
     25.07.012(J)(4)), filed with the new TUP or use permit and decided by the same body
     (25.07.012(D)). Not an "extension of the CDP": 25.07.012(I)(4) does not reach a term of use.
   - The **City Council** hears appeals (25.05.035(H)); it is not where a renewal is filed.
   - File before the end date; after it the use has no approval.
   General method for any time-limited approval (TUP, CUP or CDP with a term):
   `planning-approvals.md` Step 1a item 2a.

## Common cases

| Case | Route | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Shipping or storage container on a residential lot | TUP (City practice routes it here, not as an accessory building). Director in R-1; Planning Commission in other zones. Three years max. A container meant to stay with no end date is a building: `planning-approvals` Step 6, accessory space | 25.05.035(B)(1)(a)-(c), (F), (I) |
| Construction trailer, equipment or material storage during a project | TUP in a residential zone | 25.05.035(B)(1)(a)-(c) |
| Prefab shed, play set or similar, on grade and removable, 120 sq ft or less and under 12 ft | **Zone Clearance**, not a TUP, if zone setbacks are met; ADR if it does not meet setbacks | 25.05.040(B)(2)(x) |
| Block party or other event that closes a public street | TUP (the street closure itself also needs Public Works or Police approval; ask the City). In the coastal zone an event that takes exclusive use of a public street or parking area, especially a recurring one, points to a CDP as well (step 4) | 25.05.035(B)(1)(e), (F)(2); 25.07.008(A)(6)(d) |
| Occasional live music, concerts, sidewalk sales, tents at a business | TUP in a non-residential zone (concerts and sidewalk sales are on the list); Director for a minor event of five days or less, Planning Commission for a series. A CUP only if the activity becomes a permanent part of the business | 25.05.035(B)(2)(d), (i), (j), (F) |
| Event on the sand between Memorial Day and Labor Day with paid admission | Likely needs a CDP as well as City event approval | 25.07.008(A)(6) |
| RV, boat, trailer or van over 20 ft long and 6 ft high stored at home | Not a TUP. Administrative Use Permit in R-1 and R-2, with conditions (owner's vehicle, 5 ft from lines, not in front or side yard, not lived in, screened) | 25.10.005(B); 25.12.005(B) |

## Events on parks, beaches and other City property

- A TUP is filed by "the property owner or an authorized agent" (25.05.035(C)). On City property the
  City is the owner, so the event organizer needs the City's permission as owner first. The City's
  special-event and park-use rules are not in our sources; say so and send the resident to the City.
- Artwork in a public place: Zone Clearance applies to "artwork approved through the procedures
  outlined in Chapter 1.09 (Art in Public Places)" (25.05.040(B)(2)(p)). Chapter 1.09 is the
  percent-for-art program for permanent art on large projects: new commercial or industrial
  development, commercial remodels over $225,000, residential subdivisions of more than four lots or
  units, and City parks and public works over $225,000 (1.09.040(a)). Fire, flood and other disaster
  repairs, street and sidewalk repairs, underground public works, tree planting, low-income housing
  and nonprofit facilities are excepted (1.09.040(b)). The Arts Commission reviews the art, before
  structural plan check is accepted (1.09.050). Chapter 1.09 does not set rules for temporary art or
  events; for those, UNSETTLED, confirm with the City's arts staff or Planning. Full text:
  the municipal code (LBMC 1.09).
- Street performers on public sidewalks and the Forest Avenue Promenade follow their own chapter
  (LBMC Ch. 11.60; the municipal code (LBMC 11.60)), not a TUP.
- Large City-owned event sites (for example the Festival of Arts grounds) fall under the Major
  Capital Improvement Project rules only for physical work (25.05.040(B)(1)(p)(iv)); the event itself
  is not a Design Review item.

## What to tell the resident

1. Whether the use is temporary (TUP) or a new permanent use (zone use list).
2. Whether it is on the TUP list for the zone, and who decides (Director or Planning Commission) with
   the five-day and R-1 rules.
3. Whether a CDP is also needed (coastal zone; beach events).
4. Fees: the fee estimate. Anything outside LBMC Title 25 (park permits, street closures): say it is
   not in our sources and give the City contact.
