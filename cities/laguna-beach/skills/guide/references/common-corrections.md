# Laguna Beach — Common plan-check corrections: check the drawings before the City does

**Law as of:** 2026-10-13
**Use for:** a drafted plan set, after the route and the intake check are settled, when the
applicant wants to know what Laguna's plan check is likely to send back. Also for "what does the
City usually correct on a new house / addition / ADU / pool?" This file is a method: for each
recurring correction it says where to look on the drawings and what a sheet that passes shows. It
lists no past cases.
**Not for:** which application to file (`planning-approvals.md`), whether the
packet matches that route (`before-you-file.md`), or what the counter needs to
accept a filing (the City checklists). It is not a code review and it does not certify
anything.

**Authority and evidence.** The rules come from the LBMC, the City's handouts and checklists, and
the California codes, cited by section. Which items come up most often comes from an aggregate read
of the City's own correction documents from late 2025 to fall 2026: first-review Building plan-check
letters (the plan-check consultant's list plus the City's quality-control comments), Building
completeness emails, Planning intake markups, one complete Planning plan-check letter, and how many
review rounds each Building permit type took. Practice, not law. Where an item rests only on those
letters and on no City handout, this file says "City plan-check letters".

## The three stages: keep them apart

Laguna sends a filing back at three separate points. This file covers the third.

| Stage | Who | What comes back | Where to check it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning intake | Planning intake staff | The application checklist with failed rows marked | the City checklists (planning lane) |
| Building intake | Permit technicians | A short email: Planning approval first, one application per scope, wrong lane | `before-you-file.md`; the City checklists (building lane) |
| **Plan check** | Building: the City's plan-check consultant plus the City's own quality-control comments; Planning: the case planner | A numbered correction list, redlines, an Incomplete Letter | **This file** |

A plan-check finding is a preview. It is never an intake defect: the counter does not return a filing
for it. Report it in its own section, **Likely plan-check corrections**, separate from any intake fix
list. Never move an intake item here to make the intake list look shorter, and never move a
plan-check item into the intake list.

## The method

1. **Settle the route and the scope first.** Run `planning-approvals.md` on every
   piece. Write the scope as a list of physical elements (rooms, decks, walls, pool, grading,
   equipment, ADU), each with the sheet that shows it. The permit types below follow that list; a
   project can hit several (a new house with a pool and grading runs three sections).
2. **Run Part A on every Building set**, then the Part B section for each permit type in the scope,
   then Part C for every piece that adds floor area, footprint or height, moves into a yard, removes
   structure, adds equipment, or sits in the fire zone.
3. **Look at the page, not the title.** For each item, find the sheet that should show it and say
   what you see: **shown** (with the page), **missing**, or **unclear** (with the fact that would
   settle it). A sheet titled "Fire Notes" that does not name the fire zone does not name it.
4. **Unknown stays unknown.** Never fill a missing input (lot area, a survey elevation, a wall
   length) with an estimate and then report a pass or a failure on it. Report the input as missing.
5. **Weigh, do not inflate.** Each item below carries how firm its evidence is. An item seen in
   every letter is a strong preview; an item seen once, or never, is a "may ask". Say which.
6. **Write each finding as one line:** what plan check is likely to ask for, the page and sheet, and
   the basis (City handout, LBMC section, California code section, or "City plan-check letters").

## Where the evidence is thin (read before using Part B)

- **Building plan-check letters cover residential hillside projects only:** new houses, additions
  and remodels with grading, ADUs, one pool. All were in the fire zone, several on landslide or steep
  sites, so fire, geotechnical and grading items weigh heavier there than they will on a flat-lot
  remodel.
- **No plan-check letter** in the evidence covers commercial tenant improvements, reroofs, solar,
  windows, fences and walls, decks or hardscape. For those types this file has intake evidence and
  review-round counts only, and says so.
- **Planning plan check after intake** rests on one complete City Incomplete Letter (a new hillside
  house at the Design Review Board level) and one partial ADU letter.
- **Equipment noise** (the 7.25.130(C) test) never appeared as a correction in any source. Part C
  teaches the check; do not claim the City corrects it often.
- **How often each Building type needed a second round** (an indicator, not a cause; a second round
  can be an intake resubmittal): new houses every time, often five rounds or more; additions,
  detached ADUs, commercial tenant improvements, solar filed outside SolarAPP+, attached ADUs and
  pools about half or more; hardscape and grading about one in four; remodels, alterations,
  SolarAPP+, reroofs, windows, fences and walls, and demolition rarely.

## Part B — By permit type

Run Part A too. Each section names the types' own recurring items.

### B1. New single-family house

The type that returns most often. Evidence: two Building letters and two grading letters on new
hillside houses, plus the complete Planning plan-check letter.

- **Grading, drainage and erosion package.** See B5. On a new house it is always in play.
- **Foundations near slopes.** On every section through a footing near a slope steeper than 3:1,
  look for the dimensioned distance from the footing to daylight. Passing: the distance dimensioned
  and checked against CRC R403.1.7 / CBC 1808.7. (City plan-check letters.)
- **Shoring.** A basement, or a cut near a property line: shoring plans and calcs in this submittal,
  surcharge from neighbors addressed. (City plan-check letters; `grading-drainage.md`
  section 3 for when a report and peer review are needed.)
- **Fire zone landscape and fuel modification.** Landscape sheet. Passing: tree and shrub spacing
  dimensioned from the structure and from each other, the ember-resistant zone next to the house
  marked, fence materials near the house shown, and on City fuel-modification land the zone widths
  dimensioned (Part C, "Fire zone (WUI)"). (City quality-control comments.)
- **Alternative materials and methods (AM&M).** If the design relies on one: updated to the 2025
  Fire and WUI Codes, and every measure it requires (a heat-deflecting wall, for example) drawn on
  the site and landscape plans. (City plan-check letters; `fire-hazard-and-wui.md`.)
- **Fire access and water.** A fire access site plan when access, water supply or addressing
  changes; a hydrant flow report when asked. The access path and hose-distance values the Fire
  Department applies are not in our references: get them from Fire Prevention.
- **Life safety on the floor plans.** Egress windows and doors tagged; smoke and CO alarms in every
  bedroom, outside sleeping areas and on each level, interconnected; garage separation and door
  ratings matching the schedule; the aging-in-place blocking detail (CRC R327).
- **Backwater valve** on the plumbing plan where fixtures sit below the upstream manhole rim (LBMC
  17.20.090; City plan-check letters).
- **Elevator** (when present): manufacturer's drawings and the installer certification note.
- **Encroachment permit** for any work past the property line, before issuance
  (`right-of-way-utilities.md`).
- **Planning plan check** (Design Review): Part B9.

### B2. Addition or remodel (including upper levels and decks)

Evidence: two Building letters (both with grading sections), Building completeness emails, Planning
intake markups.

- **The 50% tests decide what the project is.** Run the major remodel test and both sprinkler
  triggers (Part C) before anything else, and recompute after every redesign. A major remodel is
  checked as a new house (B1).
- **Demolition plan per floor.** Passing: a legend for walls that remain, are rebuilt, are removed
  and are new; a tally of linear feet per floor; roof and foundation shares where the parcel is in
  the coastal appealable area. Planning intake asks for it; Building plan check uses it.
- **Grading package** when there is site work (B5); **slope setbacks and shoring** as in B1.
- **Fire zone construction for what stays.** Plan check has asked how the existing roof and walls
  meet the fire-zone rules when the project touches them (Part A).
- **Smoke and CO alarms, egress, garage separation** as in B1.
- **Decks:** height above grade at the outer edge dimensioned on the elevation or section (it decides
  the Planning list and the yard allowance, Part C "Setbacks"); fire-zone decking listed.
- Evidence is lighter for a single-story remodel on a flat lot: the letters we have are all hillside.

### B3. ADU (attached, detached, conversion)

Evidence: two ADU Building letters (a detached unit over a garage, and one attached to a new
house); ADUs were nearly half of all Planning intake asks.

- **Separate utility controls.** Plumbing and electrical plans: accessible water and gas shut-offs
  serving the ADU and an ADU subpanel. This is control, not a separate meter. (City quality-control
  comments.)
- **Separate heating, cooling and water heating.** Mechanical plan: the ADU's own system and water
  heater with make, model and efficiency (CRC R303.10; CMC).
- **Fire and sound separation from the house or garage.** Sections: the rated assembly with its
  listing, its sound rating, and how far it runs; rated walls under rated floors identified (CRC
  R302.3, R302.6).
- **A path to the ADU entry** drawn on the site plan. (City quality-control comments.)
- **Sprinklers only if the house has them.** Cover sheet: say whether the primary house is
  sprinklered. The ADU never triggers a retrofit of the house
  (`../../residential-remodel/references/city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`).
- **Electrical load** for the house and ADU together, with panel locations (CEC Art. 220).
- **Address and unit identification** visible from the street, located on an elevation or the site
  plan.
- Smoke and CO alarms, the kitchen hood and the fire-zone note as in Part A and B1.
- ADU height and setbacks: Part C. ADU standards that fail have their own route
  (`planning-approvals.md` Step 6, item 9).

### B4. Pool or spa

Evidence: one pool letter, pool items inside a remodel letter and a grading letter, and Planning
intake records. Thin: treat as "may ask".

- **Barrier and a second drowning-prevention feature.** Site plan: the barrier line, gate swing
  (outward), and a cover note naming the second feature; door alarms on house doors that open to
  the pool area (Health & Safety Code 115922-115923; CBC 3109;
  `../../swimming-pool/SKILL.md`).
- **Pool sheet:** an enlarged plan and two sections, fully dimensioned.
- **Equipment:** location, size, access and ventilation of the pad or room; the heater's make,
  model, input rating and listing; heater venting to the outside. Equipment siting and noise: Part
  C, "Mechanical equipment".
- **Energy:** a pool cover note for a heated pool and the pool energy forms (California Energy Code
  110.4).
- **Plumbing and electrical notes:** backwash to the sewer through a trap; two main-drain suction
  outlets apart; a hydrostatic relief valve; deck drainage away from the pool; bonding; GFCI
  protection; the gas line sized and sleeved.
- **Safety glazing** for glass near the water.
- **City notes** seen in a City plan-check letter: gunite is not permitted in Laguna Beach;
  equipment bolted down; exposed PVC painted.
- **Pool near a descending slope:** a section from the pool shell to slope daylight, and the
  soils engineer's review of the pool foundation.
- **Planning sheets:** the pool rows of the Project Summary Table filled in, and the pool security
  fence on the site plan.

### B5. Grading (and site work on any project)

Evidence: four grading sections in the Building letters, each with dozens of entries.

- **Drainage traced end to end.** On the grading sheet, follow water from every roof and yard area
  to a named discharge point. Passing: spot elevations, flow arrows and the discharge point shown;
  ground falling away from the foundation as CRC R401.3 / CBC 1804.4 require (5% for the first
  10 ft, 2% on paving); no flow across a property line, onto a neighbor, or sheet-flowing over the
  sidewalk.
- **Property-line sections**, the limit of grading, easements, and the soil over-excavation limits
  with their depth.
- **Special inspection table** for grading and soils work.
- **Erosion and sediment control sheet:** the emergency contact, perimeter control, a stabilized
  entrance, and the winter-season note (October 15 to April 15).
- **Water quality:** the WQMP or the LID checklist as Public Works decides, BMPs routed on the plan,
  hydrology and any bio-retention sized (`grading-drainage.md` section 4).
- **Street work:** curb, gutter and sawcut details; utilities crossing the right-of-way at right
  angles; the encroachment permit.
- **Notice to neighbors** before excavation near a property line (City plan-check letters).
- **The 20 cubic yard count** decides whether grading is its own application: Part C, "Grading
  quantity".

### B6. Hardscape (patios, walks, driveways, site walls)

**Thin evidence: no plan-check letter.** Hardscape needed a second Building round about one time in
four. What the evidence supports:
- Planning sign-off before Building when the work changes the exterior (Building completeness
  emails; `before-you-file.md`).
- Wall and fence heights dimensioned above the grade on both sides, with the distance to the
  property line (Part C, "Setbacks", for the limits).
- Drainage of new paving traced as in B5; hardscape that adds impervious area may bring a water
  quality review (`grading-drainage.md` section 4).
- In the fire zone, landscape and hardscape go to Fire review (`fire-hazard-and-wui.md`).
- Grading over the 20 cubic yard count: B5.

### B7. Solar, and other small scopes (reroof, windows, walls)

**Thin evidence: no plan-check letter.** These types rarely need a second round; when they come
back, it is almost always at intake (wrong lane, Planning sign-off missing, application wording).
Check `before-you-file.md` first. Drawing items seen at intake or in one letter:
- **Solar:** the roof plan with the ridge and pathway setbacks dimensioned (CFC 1205);
  spec sheets as unlocked PDFs. A system SolarAPP+ covers goes through SolarAPP+.
- **Reroof:** a product sheet with its evaluation report and Class A rating (LBMC 14.70.020).
- **Windows and doors:** the schedule states the glazing (dual-pane, one pane tempered) and the
  energy values.
- **Walls and fences:** a wall elevation with the height above grade on both sides and the distance
  to the property line.

### B8. Commercial tenant improvement

**The thinnest type: no Building plan-check letter for commercial work in the evidence.** Tenant
improvements needed a second Building round about six times in ten, but nothing in the evidence says
why. What it supports:
- Planning sign-off first for any exterior change, change of use, sign or outdoor area. This was the
  most common commercial intake return (`before-you-file.md`).
- The application matching the plans, and the code-cycle block (Part A).
- From one commercial Planning letter: on Coast Highway frontage, the ultimate right-of-way
  dimensioned and setbacks measured from it; color and material board, renderings, extra building
  sections; a landscape plan meeting the scenic-highway landscape zone and the water-efficient
  landscape rules.
Say plainly that this is a short list because the evidence is thin, not because commercial plans
come back less.

### B9. Planning plan check after intake (Design Review projects)

**Thin evidence: one complete City Incomplete Letter.** Its items fall into two groups:
- **Numeric standards** the drawings must prove: front setback (the one that needed a variance),
  bluff-top setback, grading and fill limits in the side and rear setbacks, height envelope with
  its code section cited on the elevations, landscaped open space. Run Part C on each.
- **Documents:** the Project Summary Table complete; easements on the plans; a demolition
  clarification; the staking plan; the floor-area exhibit method; pool security fencing; exterior
  lighting aimed down (`noise-lighting-equipment.md` section 4); and peer review of
  the soils, bluff-edge and coastal-hazards studies.

## Part C — Checking the City's numeric standards on the drawings

The values live in the files named here. Do not copy them into the answer as a table; read the value
for this lot, show the arithmetic, and cite the section. When a value is not in our references, say
so and ask the City; never supply one from memory.

**The check, for every standard:**
1. **Read the governing value for this lot** from the named reference: zone, lot size, slope, coastal
   area and specific plan change it (the parcel lookup for the parcel facts).
2. **Find each input on the drawings:** the survey for grades and lot area, the site plan for
   dimensions, the summary table for areas. Name the page.
3. **Compute and show the arithmetic** in one line.
4. **Result:** "fits", "fails by N", or "unclear: the set does not give X". A failure goes in
   Likely plan-check corrections with its route: redesign, a modification the code allows, or a
   variance (`planning-approvals.md` Step 6, items 6-7).
5. **Cross-check:** the same number must agree on every sheet that states it (summary table, floor
   area exhibit, site plan, elevations).

### Height

- **Values:** zone limit and how height is measured, `planning-approvals.md` Step 6
  item 3 and Step 4f; the R-1 slope table, rear-lot and through-lot caps and the sloping
  front-and-rear setback,
  `../../single-family-residential/references/zoning-r1-development-standards.md`;
  ADU heights, Step 6 "Stated-number check"; Three Arch Bay, Step 6b.
- **Check:** the survey must carry a fixed benchmark, and every elevation and section must use it.
  On each elevation and each perpendicular site section, find existing grade (dashed) and proposed
  grade (solid); measure from the more restrictive of the two (the code's "finished or natural
  grade, whichever is more restrictive") to the highest point directly above, ridge or rooftop
  appurtenance included. Take the lot slope from the survey, not an estimate, to pick the limit.
  Then check the 30-ft citywide cap and, where the parcel is in the special height area, the cap
  above front curb height.
- **Passing sheet:** the maximum-height envelope drawn as a dashed line on every elevation and
  section, with its code section noted; floor, plate, ridge and deck elevations labelled to the
  survey datum; both grade lines.
- **Evidence:** height and the envelope were among the most frequent Planning intake asks, and the
  envelope citation appeared at Planning plan check.

### Setbacks and yards

- **Values:** `planning-approvals.md` Step 6 item 1 (projections, decks, pools and
  equipment in yards, the bluff top, watercourses) and item 2 (fence and wall heights); R-1 yards,
  shallow-lot and steep-front-yard reductions in
  `../../single-family-residential/references/zoning-r1-development-standards.md`;
  the bluff setback, Step 6 item 5.
- **Check:** compute the average lot width and depth first and show them on the site plan; they set
  the side yards and the shallow-lot rule. Dimension each setback at its narrowest point, to the wall
  face and, separately, to each projection (eave, balcony, bay, chimney, stair). For a deck or patio
  in a yard, read its height above natural grade at the edge. For a portion over 15 ft in a front or
  rear setback, check the added sloping setback. On an oceanfront lot, dimension the bluff-top
  setback or the stringline. On Coast Highway frontage, measure from the ultimate right-of-way.
- **Passing sheet:** every setback dimensioned at its narrowest point, projections dimensioned
  separately, every wall and fence with its height and material, the average lot width and depth
  stated.

### Lot coverage and landscaped open space

- **Values:** the R-1 coverage formula, `planning-approvals.md` Step 6 item 4 and
  the "Stated-number check"; the oceanfront lot-area rule and the landscaped open space table,
  `../../single-family-residential/references/zoning-r1-development-standards.md`;
  Three Arch Bay and Village Community add their own caps (Step 6 item 4).
- **Check coverage:** numerator = habitable enclosed area plus covered parking, existing plus new;
  denominator = lot area (landward area on an oceanfront lot). The summary table's figures must
  equal the dimensioned floor-area exhibit.
- **Check landscaped open space** (new houses and major remodels): only living plant areas count,
  each at least 3 ft in its smallest dimension. Hatch them and total them on the site or landscape
  plan.
- **Passing sheet:** a geometric floor-area exhibit with every area dimensioned; the summary table
  filled in, existing and proposed columns included; the open-space hatch with its total.

### The major remodel 50% test

- **Values and method:**
  `../../residential-addition/references/city-major-remodel.md` (the October 2026
  City handout); the full method,
  `../../residential-remodel/references/city-major-remodel-methodology.md`.
- **Check:** take coastal appealable or non-appealable from the parcel facts; the two areas count
  different things (the appealable area adds roof and foundation, and counts reinforcement as
  removal). Run the test for each structure on its own. From the demolition plan, tally existing and
  removed (and, in the appealable area, reinforced) linear feet of wall on each floor, then the
  whole structure; take roof area and foundation length where they count. For the expansion prong,
  measure floor area from the face of the structure, leave out an attached garage, and add earlier
  additions back to the structure's baseline. ADU area is exempt. When a floor reaches 50% but the
  whole structure does not, say the City has not ruled how floors combine, and show both numbers.
- **Passing sheet:** a demolition plan per floor with a legend and a tally table (existing, removed,
  reinforced, percentage), plus the roof and foundation shares where they count. City staff advise
  a margin below 50%, because demolition found during construction counts.
- **Evidence:** the demolition tally is a frequent Planning intake ask; without a wall inventory
  the coastal test cannot be computed at all.

### Sprinkler retrofit triggers

- **Values:** `../../city-reference/SKILL.md` "Sprinkler Retrofit Triggers" and
  `../../residential-remodel/references/city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`. Two triggers,
  valuation and scope; either one requires the retrofit; an ADU never triggers it for the house.
- **Check:** the valuation on the application against the dwelling's value, over the three-year
  window; the scope from the demolition plan and any added story. When a trigger fires, look for
  the Part A sprinkler items.
- **Evidence:** raised on every house-family project with a letter. Older letters cite an earlier
  section number; cite the current one from the reference.

### Mechanical equipment (air conditioners, heat pumps, pool and spa equipment)

- **Values:** the six-part test, `noise-lighting-equipment.md` section 3; noise
  limits by zone, section 2. In R-1, whether equipment may sit in a side yard is UNSETTLED
  (`planning-approvals.md` Step 6 item 1).
- **Check:** on the site plan, the unit's location (ground or wall at ground level, not on a roof,
  deck or balcony), the distance to every property line, and that it is outside the front and side
  setbacks and does not block the clear path around the building. Then the spec sheet's sound
  rating, the City's sound-level form, and two sound-attenuation measures.
- **Passing sheet:** the unit drawn and dimensioned to the property lines, the spec sheet and the
  sound-level form in the set, the two attenuation measures noted. Failing any part is not a
  correction: it moves the equipment to Administrative Design Review.
- **Evidence:** none. No correction in any source named it. Present it as a check, not a likely
  correction.

### Fire zone (WUI)

- **Values:** the Laguna amendments (Class A roofs and reroofs, vent opening size, exterior wall
  covering, vinyl window frames, glass-only skylights) and the fire review steps,
  `fire-hazard-and-wui.md` sections 2-3; fuel modification zone widths on City
  fuel-modification land,
  `../../single-family-residential/references/fire-wui-local-amendments.md` (a
  transcription; confirm with Fire Prevention); accessory structures, decks and the rest of the
  California WUI Code, the state WUI overlay (`../../wildfire-wui/SKILL.md`, which loads
  with the permit skills).
- **Check:** each exterior product on the elevations and details against those rules, existing
  surfaces the work touches included; vent sizes against the opening limit and the underfloor vent
  calculation; on fuel-modification land, the zone widths dimensioned from each combustible
  structure on the landscape plan.
- **Not in our references:** the tree, shrub and fence spacing the City's quality-control comments
  ask to see dimensioned, the ember-resistant zone next to the house, and the fire access path and
  hose-distance values. Say so, and get them from the state WUI overlay or Fire Prevention.

### Grading quantity

- **Value and method:** `grading-drainage.md` section 2 (the greater of cut or fill
  outside the building footprint; pool excavation tracked apart).
- **Check:** the earthwork table splits cut and fill inside and outside the footprint. Over the
  threshold, grading is its own application and the B5 package applies in full.

### Construction hours

- **Values:** `../../city-reference/SKILL.md` "Construction Hours"; `noise-lighting-equipment.md`
  section 1.
- **Check:** the note on the cover or staging sheet states the weekday construction window, the
  narrower grading and hauling windows, and no work on weekends or federal holidays. Passing: all
  four, matching the City's hours.

## Writing it up

Put these findings under their own heading, **Likely plan-check corrections**, after the intake
fix list and never inside it. One line each:

| # | Likely correction | Where | Basis | How firm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the 2025 Fire Code, the WUI Code and the LBMC in the code block; the structural calcs cite an older edition | p1, T-1; calcs p3 | 2025 cycle; City plan-check letters | Seen on nearly every project |
| 2 | Draw the height envelope on the rear elevation; the ridge is not labelled to the survey datum | p9, A3.2 | `planning-approvals.md` Step 6 item 3 | Frequent at Planning |

- Lead with the strong items (Part A, and Part B items seen on every project of that type).
- A failed numeric standard gives its arithmetic and its route in the same line.
- Items with thin evidence go last, marked "may ask".
- Close by saying this is a preview: the City's plan check can raise other items, and a set that
  clears every line here can still get corrections.
