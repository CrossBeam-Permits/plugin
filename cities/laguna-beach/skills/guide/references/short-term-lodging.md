## 1. What counts as short-term lodging

"Short-term" means **30 consecutive calendar days or less**. It also covers a longer master lease
when the tenant sublets for stays of 30 days or less (LBMC 25.23.020).

- **Home sharing** (the host lives on site for the whole stay) is still short-term lodging and
  needs the same permit (25.23.020).
- A **home exchange** (simultaneous swap with an owner in another city, no money, at least one week,
  no more than twice a year, written agreement) is **not** short-term lodging (25.23.020,
  25.23.030(E)).

## 2. Where it is allowed

| Zone | New short-term lodging? | Permit | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-1, R-2, R-3, Village Community (VC) | **No.** No new short-term lodging may be established. | — | LBMC 25.23.030(A), 25.23.060(A) |
| LB/P, C-N, C-1, CH-M | Yes, within the caps | **Administrative Use Permit** | 25.23.030(A) |
| Downtown Specific Plan: CBD-1, CBD-2, CBD Central Bluffs, CBD Office, CBD Visitor Commercial | Yes, within the caps | **Administrative Use Permit** | 25.23.030(A) |
| SLV (South Laguna Village) | Yes, within the caps | **Conditional Use Permit** | 25.23.030(A) |
| Any other zone | No. An owner may not rent a unit for 30 days or less. | — | 25.23.030(A) |

Use the parcel lookup to get the zone. The City's map of permitted districts is linked on its
short-term lodging page (document 9104).

**Caps** (25.23.030(B)-(C)):

- No more than **20%** of allowable rental units in the permitted districts may convert, with a
  citywide maximum of **300** short-term lodging units (not counting home-share units).
- An extra **165 home-share units** may be approved in single-family, duplex and triplex dwellings
  in the permitted districts. **Use permit fees are waived** for home-share units.
- A property with **five or fewer units** may convert **one** unit. A property with more than five
  may convert up to 20%, rounded down.

**Never allowed** (25.23.030(D)): units restricted by covenant for affordable, senior or disabled
housing.

## 3. Units that carry a "no short-term rental" restriction

**ADUs and JADUs: never short-term, in any zone.** Laguna's ADU ordinance says: "No ADU or JADU may
be rented for a term that is shorter than 30 days. This prohibition applies regardless of when the
ADU or JADU was created" (25.17.010(f)(4), Ord. 1713). It is a flat ban: an old ADU, a new one, a
converted garage and a JADU are all covered, and no Administrative Use Permit or CUP under Ch. 25.23
can lift it, even in a zone where short-term lodging is otherwise allowed. Lead with "No: an ADU or
JADU in Laguna cannot be rented for less than 30 days." (Ord. 1713 frames subsection (f) as applying
to ADUs approved under (d)(2) or (d)(3); a CDP-approved ADU is bound by the certified LCP instead,
which says the ADU or JADU "shall not be rented for a period of less than 31 consecutive calendar
days" (certified 25.17.050(K)).)

| Unit | Restriction | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Residential units built with the infill and mixed-use exceptions in C-N, C-1, SLV | Recorded covenant that the units will not be converted to short-term lodging, binding on future owners, recorded before building permits | LBMC 25.96.030(C) |
| SB 9 units and lots | Rent only for terms **longer than 30 days**; City deed restriction recorded before building permits | LBMC Ch. 25.95 |
| ADUs whose owner takes the affordable-ADU fee refund | Deed restriction that includes the short-term rental prohibition, at least 10 years | LBMC 25.17.070(B) (certified LCP text; the current ADU urgency text, Ord. 1713, may differ, so confirm with Planning) |
| State-law ADUs | Rental terms longer than 30 days for units created under Gov. Code 66323 | Gov. Code 66323(e); `../../adu/SKILL.md` for the state floor |

In R-1, R-2 and R-3 the zoning ban in 25.23.030(A) also stops a new short-term rental of an ADU; the ADU ordinance's own ban (above) applies first.

## 4. The permit: Administrative Use Permit (or CUP in SLV)

**Process (AUP, LBMC 25.05.020):** filed by the owner or agent with Community Development; public
notice under 25.05.065; **no hearing unless the applicant or a noticed person asks for one**; the
Director approves or denies based on zoning compliance; appeal under 25.05.070. A CUP in SLV goes
to the Planning Commission, which reviews all CUPs (25.05.030).

**What the application must include** (25.23.030(G)), in short:

- owner, operator and a **24/7 local contact** who can respond within **60 minutes**;
- the days and months the unit will be offered and the minimum stay advertised;
- bedrooms, overnight and daytime occupancy, on-site parking count;
- a scaled site plan and floor plan showing trash storage hidden from view and parking;
- signed receipt of the City's Good Neighbor brochure;
- proof the unit **passed a Laguna Beach Fire Department safety inspection**;
- proof of liability insurance of at least **$500,000**;
- an estimated daily rate, the fee (waived for home-share), and a public notice package from an
  independent vendor.

The City asks for a completed Planning Application filed through the online permit portal (City
page). Application form: "Short-Term Lodging – Administrative Use Permit Application"
(`../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`).

**Portal filing type.** New short-term lodging is allowed only in the commercial districts and the
Downtown Specific Plan (AUP) and in SLV (CUP) (25.23.030(A)), so every new application, renewal or
new-owner permit is on a commercially zoned property and files as **Commercial Planning
Entitlements (type 116)**, which has a "Short-Term Lodging Application" upload slot
(`../../city-website/references/submit-commercial-planning-entitlements.md`). Not Residential Planning
Entitlements, and not a building permit. A pre-2020 nonconforming unit in R-1, R-2, R-3 or VC
(section 6) has no new-permit path in a residential zone; for paperwork on one, ask Planning which
type to use.

**Building permit and inspections.** The use needs no building permit by itself; the code asks for
none. It does need "Evidence that the residence/premises passed a safety inspection conducted by the
Laguna Beach Fire Department" (25.23.030(G)), so expect a Fire inspection before approval. A
building permit is needed only for physical work (a new bedroom, a conversion, a deck), and that
work runs the normal Planning lists first (`planning-approvals`). Our lean: the bedroom count on the
application sets the occupancy cap (25.23.040), so it should match the bedrooms shown on permitted
plans; an unpermitted bedroom is a legalization question (`unpermitted-work`).

**Coastal Development Permit.** Ch. 25.23 does not mention a CDP. What decides it is whether the
conversion is "development": the definition includes "a change in the density or intensity of use
of land" (25.07.006), and for a building other than a single-family home, improvements that "would
result in a change in the intensity of use of the structure" are not exempt (25.07.008(A)(2)(b)).
Ch. 25.23 is itself part of the certified LCP (it was certified as an LCP amendment, 25.23.090,
LCP-5-LGB-19-0074-1) and sets the AUP or CUP as the permit for the use. Our lean: a conversion to
short-term lodging with no physical work needs the AUP or CUP, not a separate CDP; physical work
that comes with it (new floor area, a new unit, an exterior change) is development and runs the
normal CDP test (`coastal-development-permits`). Not decided by the City in our records; confirm
with Planning.

## 5. Standard conditions (LBMC 25.23.040)

- **Occupancy:** 2 overnight guests per bedroom; daytime guests 7 a.m.-10 p.m. up to another 2 per
  bedroom, total cap 20 (a studio or 1-bedroom: 2 overnight, 4 daytime).
- **Parking:** guests may park only in on-site spaces. No guest parking on residential streets.
  Existing nonconforming parking may stay as is.
- **Noise:** no outdoor amplified sound audible at neighbors from **10 p.m. to 10 a.m.** Comply with
  the noise ordinance (LBMC Ch. 7.25).
- **No** weddings, receptions, large parties or commercial events. **No** on-site rental signs.
- Every ad must show the **City business license** and **AUP or CUP number**.
- Post the permit inside near the entrance and the conditions outside in public view.
- Hold a **City business license** and a **Transient Occupancy Registration Certificate**; file a
  quarterly TOT return every quarter, even with no rentals; keep stay records.
- Trash out by 7 a.m., in by 6 p.m. on pickup day.
- Three confirmed large or unruly gatherings bring an automatic revocation hearing.

**Term and transfer:**

- A new permit is valid for **3 years**, then must be renewed (25.23.040(Q)).
- A change in owner, operator or material facts needs a **new** permit, filed within 14 days
  (25.23.040(R)).
- The permit is **personal to the owner** and does not run with the land. It cannot be transferred
  to another person or property (25.23.050).

## 6. Existing permits from before October 2020

- A legal short-term lodging unit permitted before October 2020 in **R-1, R-2, R-3 or VC** may
  continue as a **legal nonconforming** use. That right **runs with the land**, so a buyer can keep
  it if the business license, TOT filings and permit conditions stay current (25.23.060).
- The right **lapses** if the use stops for **12 consecutive months** or more (25.23.060(C)).
- Pre-2020 units in the commercial districts continue as conforming uses on the same terms
  (25.23.065).

The City's "Approved Short-Term Permits" list (revised 3/25/26) is linked on its page.

## 7. The 2025 license layer (Chapter 5.84, City summary)

The City says operators now need **all four**: a Short-Term Lodging Unit License, a City business
license (renewed every year), the AUP or CUP, and TOT registration. Its summary also lists:

- license term up to three years, matching the use permit;
- license number on every listing;
- a nuisance response plan, a 24/7 contact who responds within 60 minutes, and, for new
  applications, notice to residences and businesses within 200 feet at least 30 days before the
  license issues;
- 12% TOT plus 2% Laguna Beach Tourism Marketing District assessment;
- platforms must show the license number, collect and remit taxes where they take payment, and
  remove unlicensed listings;
- after revocation, no new license for at least three years.

## 8. Enforcement

Advertising a short-term rental without a permit is a violation (25.23.080(G)). Administrative
citations are **$1,000 per violation**, each day separate. Violations are misdemeanors and public
nuisances. After a revocation the owner must wait **three years** to reapply (25.23.080). Platforms
may not complete a booking for a unit that is not on the City's registry (25.23.070(C)).
Unpermitted rentals are a Code Enforcement matter, (949) 497-0301 (City page).
