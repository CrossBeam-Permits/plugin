# Laguna Beach — Work in the street, utilities, wireless, Caltrans and sites outside City limits (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Authority:** LBMC 25.05.040(B)(1)(k), (B)(1)(p), (B)(2)(w), (y), (B)(3)(a)(viii), (xiii), (xiv),
(xvii) (Ord. 1732); LBMC Ch. 11.16, 11.20 (street work, excavations); Ch. 11.50 (revocable encroachment); LBMC Ch. 25.55
and Ch. 11.06 (wireless); LBMC 11.02.010;
LBMC Ch. 21.24 (underground utility districts); LBMC 25.07.006, 25.07.008(A)(4), (A)(7); City
building-permits page (Caltrans); `../../city-website/references/submit-right-of-way.md` (Public Works permit).
**Use for:** "Do I need a permit to work on the sidewalk / parkway / driveway apron?", "The utility is
putting a box or pole in front of my house", "A cell antenna on a street light", "Can we underground
the wires on our street?", "My lot fronts Coast Highway / Laguna Canyon Road", "My address says
Laguna Beach but I'm in Emerald Bay".

## Step 1 — Where is the work, and whose land is it?

- **Outside City limits?** A mailing address of "Laguna Beach" does not make a lot City land.
  Emerald Bay and other unincorporated County areas are governed by **Orange County** for land use,
  building and any local coastal permit. the parcel lookup prints "OUTSIDE CITY LIMITS" when it sees
  this. Then stop: do not apply LBMC, City fees or City steps; send the resident to the County. The
  County's rules are not in our sources.
- **In the public right-of-way?** The right-of-way is any public highway, street, alley, sidewalk or
  parkway owned, operated or controlled by the City, County or State, or subject to a public easement
  or dedication (25.55.004 "Public right-of-way"). The planted strip between a lot and the curb is
  usually right-of-way, not the owner's yard. the parcel lookup binds to the nearest private parcel;
  it does not report the right-of-way itself. Say the parcel facts are for the neighboring lot and
  that the City GIS shows the right-of-way line; a survey decides.
- **On a State highway?** Coast Highway is State Route 1 and Laguna Canyon Road is State Route 133.
  Work on or along them may also need Caltrans (Step 4).

## Step 2 — Which City permit for work in the right-of-way

| Work | Permit | Who decides | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trenching, pavement cuts, curb and gutter, sidewalk, driveway approach, potholing, boring, traffic control | **Public Works Right-of-Way Permit** (EnerGov type 315, online) | Public Works | Ch. 11.16 (building and paving in public places), Ch. 11.20 (excavations); `../../city-website/references/submit-right-of-way.md` |
| New vehicular access, or widening a driveway at the street beyond the minimum | **Design Review** for the private-side change, plus the Public Works permit for the apron | DRB (house or duplex) or Planning Commission | 25.05.040(B)(1)(k) |
| Private improvements kept in an unimproved, unused right-of-way (low walls, stairs, walkways, planters, irrigation) | **Revocable Encroachment Permit** (the land-use approval), plus the **Public Works Right-of-Way Permit** to build it (11.16.010 names curbs, sidewalks, pavement and "retaining wall" on City right-of-way), plus a **building permit** for any wall or fence (no 14.50.022 exemption within 3 ft of the right-of-way) | REP alone: ADR (Director), always after "a noticed public hearing" (11.50.035; the ADR hearing waiver does not reach it). With a DRB or Planning Commission project: that body | Ch. 11.50; 25.05.040(B)(3)(a)(viii). Detail: `discretionary-permits` |
| Street trees | Tree rules under Title 12 | Public Works or Planning | `trees-views-hedges` |
| City's own projects (parks, streets, beach access) | Major Capital Improvement Project to the Planning Commission, or minor one by ADR | Planning Commission or Director | 25.05.040(B)(1)(p), (B)(3)(a)(xvii) |

City practice: private sidewalk planters and parkway work are filed as encroachment permits on a
full zoning plan check, not at the counter. A private owner working in the right-of-way needs the
Public Works permit even when no Planning approval applies.

## Step 3 — Utility and telecom installations

**Utility boxes, pedestals, transformers, pump stations (above ground).**
- Under 4 ft above adjacent legal grade, with colors, finishes and landscaping that minimize visual
  impact: **Zone Clearance** (25.05.040(B)(2)(w)).
- 4 ft or higher: **Administrative Design Review** (25.05.040(B)(3)(a)(xiv)); a telecom facility
  that is not exempt is ADR too ((xiii)). In the coastal zone the CDP rides beside it; City practice
  has used an administrative CDP for a utility structure rebuilt at ADR (`coastal-development-permits`).
  Neither item reaches solar or paired batteries: `planning-approvals` Step 4a-bis.
- Who applies: the utility or agency doing the work, with the owner's authorization where it sits
  on private land. A neighbor cannot file for the utility.

**Wireless (cell) facilities in the right-of-way: LBMC Chapter 11.06.**
- Chapter 25.55 does not apply in the right-of-way; Chapter 11.06 does (25.55.006(A)(6);
  11.06.030(a)). Not covered: amateur radio, over-the-air reception antennas, the City's own
  facilities, and entities exempt under state or federal law (11.06.030(b)).
- No wireless facility may be deployed in the right-of-way without a **wireless facility permit**
  plus any other related permits (11.06.040):
  - **Planning Commission wireless facility permit:** the default for all new facilities,
    collocations, modifications, and new aerial fiber or coax (11.06.040(a)). Mailed notice at least
    21 calendar days before the hearing (11.06.060).
  - **Administrative (Director) permit:** a "small wireless facility" that is not in a restricted
    location and whose new cable beyond the pole is underground ((b)(1)); or an "eligible facilities
    request" under 47 U.S.C. 1455 and 47 C.F.R. 1.6002 that does not defeat existing concealment
    ((b)(2)). The Director must make written findings, including "least intrusive means", and may
    refer any application to the Commission ((b)(3)-(4)).
  - **Restricted locations** the Director cannot approve (an exception is needed): within 100 ft of a
    residential building in a residential zone (R-1, R-2, R-3, R/HP, LAG, VC, TAB, DCSP and similar),
    within 100 ft of a school, park or medical facility, or on a new pole that does not replace an
    existing one (11.06.200).
  - **Right-of-way work permit** from Public Works, only after the entitlements are approved
    (11.06.040(c)).
- **Appeals:** a Commission decision to the City Council; a Director decision to the Planning
  Commission. The applicant or an owner within 300 ft files in writing with the City Clerk within 14
  calendar days, with the fee (11.06.060).
- The design review lists also name these facilities: Zone Clearance for "certain telecommunication
  facilities subject to the provisions of Chapters 11.06 and 25.55" ((B)(2)(y)) and ADR for others
  "unless otherwise exempt" ((B)(3)(a)(xiii)). Which item applies to a given site is not spelled
  out; give the Chapter 11.06 permit as the controlling path and confirm the design review item
  with Planning.
- Federal shot clocks and eligible-facility rules limit what the City may deny (11.06.060;
  47 U.S.C. 1455(a)). Do not promise a resident the City can block a qualifying modification.
- Coastal: a facility mounted on an existing structure, or on a replacement within 3 ft, is
  CDP-exempt unless it affects public access, scenic resources, ESHA or other coastal resources
  (25.07.008(A)(7)); otherwise a CDP, noticed under Chapter 25.07 (11.06.060).
- Off the right-of-way (on private property): Chapter 25.55 applies (CUP or AUP, with exemptions);
  the municipal code (LBMC 25.55). Full text of either chapter: the municipal code (LBMC 11.06).

**Coastal permits for utility work in general.** Every project under the streets title also needs
the Chapter 25.07 CDP test (11.02.010). Utility lines, poles and pipes are "structures";
utility facilities are "public works" (25.07.006). A connection between an existing facility and an
approved project is exempt ((A)(4)). A utility project over $100,000 (indexed) is a major public
works project, appealable to the Coastal Commission anywhere. Detail:
`coastal-development-permits` "Public works, utilities and energy facilities".

## Step 4 — Caltrans (State Route 1 and State Route 133)

- The City says work on Pacific Coast Highway or Laguna Canyon Road "may also require a Caltrans
  permit", and final drawings can be submitted with the plan-check fee while the State reviews (City
  building-permits page, `../../city-website/references/building-permits.md`).
- A Caltrans encroachment permit is a State permit, in addition to the City's permits. Caltrans
  rules and fees are not in our sources; send the resident to Caltrans (District 12 covers Orange
  County).
- Typical triggers: a driveway or curb cut on the highway, utility trenching in the highway, work on
  the highway sidewalk, construction staging or temporary power in the State right-of-way
  (`../../temporary-power/SKILL.md` lists the temporary-power case).

## Step 5 — Undergrounding overhead utilities

- **City district (Ch. 21.24).** The City Council may hold a hearing and declare an underground
  utility district by resolution (21.24.020-.040). After that, new overhead lines there are unlawful
  (21.24.050). The utility furnishes its share under its tariffs on file with the CPUC (21.24.090);
  each property owner builds the service connection on their own lot to the house (21.24.100). Ask
  the City whether a district covers the street.
- **Utility tariff programs** (the CPUC's Rule 20 allocations for electric undergrounding) are
  utility and CPUC matters, not LBMC. Our sources do not hold them; send the resident to the City's
  Public Works department and the utility.
- **Electric utility facilities regulated by the CPUC:** siting of the utility's own electric lines
  and substations is governed by CPUC General Order 131-D, which limits local permitting of those
  facilities. Not in our sources; say the City's role is limited and confirm with Planning.
- **Owner-triggered undergrounding:** a major remodel is treated as a new building, including
  "undergrounding requirements" (25.56.011, Ord. 1732). Expect to underground the service to the
  house on a new house or major remodel; Building and Public Works set the detail.
- Planning list: buried lines are not on any design review list; above-ground pads and boxes follow
  Step 3. Removing a tree for the trench is its own Tree Removal Permit case
  (`trees-views-hedges`).

## What to tell the resident

1. Whether the site is inside City limits, in the right-of-way, or on a State highway.
2. The City permit for the work (Public Works Right-of-Way Permit, Revocable Encroachment Permit,
   Design Review for new access).
3. For utility equipment, the list item and the CDP test. For a cell facility in the right-of-way,
   the Chapter 11.06 wireless facility permit (Planning Commission unless it qualifies for the
   Director) and the 100-ft restricted locations.
4. Caltrans in addition on SR-1 or SR-133.
5. Outside City limits: the County, not the City.
