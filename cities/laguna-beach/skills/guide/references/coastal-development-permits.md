# Laguna Beach — Coastal Development Permits (resident guide)

**Law as of:** 2026-10-10
**Use for:** "Do I need a CDP?", "Is my house in the coastal zone?", "What's the difference between
appealable and non-appealable?", "Can the Coastal Commission appeal my approval?", Three Arch Bay /
Irvine Cove filings, beach events, public works and energy projects, emergency CDPs, changing an
approved CDP
**Go deeper:** `../../coastal-development/SKILL.md` for plan-review detail. The certified code text
is a CrossBeam reference that is not included in this plugin and
the City source document (not included in this plugin).

A CDP is separate from Design Review or Zone Clearance. It is not a substitute for either
(25.05.040(A)(2)(e)). It is a parallel package with its own completeness check (City Planning intake
ruling, Sept. 2026).

## Step 1 — Is the lot in the coastal zone, and which part?

Call the parcel lookup. It reports:
- coastal zone yes/no;
- **appealable area** (25.07.006): between the sea and the first public road paralleling the sea,
  or within 300 ft of the beach or mean high tide line (whichever is farther); within 100 ft of a
  wetland, estuary or stream; within 300 ft of the top of a coastal bluff. Major public works and
  major energy facilities are appealable anywhere;
- a Coastal Commission area (for example Three Arch Bay), where the Commission, not the City,
  issues the CDP (25.07.012(A)(1); see "Three Arch Bay, Irvine Cove and Blue Lagoon" below).

**Which basis makes the lot appealable matters.** The appealable area has two geographic parts
(25.07.006 "Appealable development" (1)(a) and (1)(b)):
- **(1)(a) the shoreline strip:** between the sea and the first public road paralleling the sea, or
  within 300 ft of the inland extent of the beach (or of the mean high tide line where there is no
  beach), whichever is greater;
- **(1)(b) resource buffers:** within 100 ft of a wetland, estuary or stream, or within 300 ft of the
  top of the seaward face of a coastal bluff.

Both make a City CDP appealable. Only the (1)(a) strip turns on the single-family exceptions in
25.07.008(A)(1)(c): the 10% floor-area test, the 10% height test and the detached-structure test. A
lot that is appealable only because of a stream or bluff buffer does not trip (c); test it under (b)
and (g) instead (within 50 ft of a bluff edge, in a stream, in an ESA). If the parcel lookup says
"appealable" without the basis, give both branches and say the basis decides. The City GIS
"Coastal Appealable" layer and the Commission's post-certification map show it; confirm with
Planning.

Not in the coastal zone: no CDP. Stop here. Outside City limits (for example Emerald Bay, which
mails as Laguna Beach but is unincorporated): the City issues nothing; see `right-of-way-utilities`.

## Step 2 — Is the work "development"?

Development includes placing or building any structure, grading, demolition, changing the size of a
structure, a change in density or intensity of use, lot splits and lot line adjustments, and removing
major vegetation (25.07.006 "Development"; 21.08.030(c)). Development in the coastal zone needs a
CDP unless exempt (25.07.008) or categorically excluded (25.07.010) (25.07.004).

## Step 3 — Exempt? (LBMC 25.07.008(A))

**Start here: improvements to an existing structure are exempt** unless a listed exception applies
(PRC 30610(a)-(b); 25.07.008(A)(1) for a single-family home and its lot, (A)(2) for any other
structure that is not a public works facility). An improvement adds to or changes what is already
built: an addition, new windows or doors, re-siding, a reroof, equipment, fixtures attached to the
building. So name the exemption first and then test only the exceptions below that the facts touch.
- **Rooftop solar with batteries paired as its storage, on any existing building** (house,
  commercial, civic): an improvement, exempt unless a listed exception applies (for a
  house, (A)(1)(b): beach, wetland or stream, within 50 ft of a bluff edge, ESA, highly scenic area,
  or (f) when the original CDP required permits for future improvements; for another building,
  (A)(2)(a)-(d)). **Cost never makes it a CDP.** A "major energy facility" is
  one costing over the indexed $100,000 "except for those governed by the provisions of Public
  Resources Code Section 30610" (25.07.006); work the 30610 improvement exemption governs is outside
  the definition, so it is not appealable development by cost either. The (A)(2) exemption does not
  cover a "public works facility" as 25.07.006 defines it (utility production, storage and
  transmission facilities of a public agency or PUC-regulated utility, public transportation
  facilities, publicly financed recreational facilities, special-district development). A fire
  station, City hall or library is not on that list; a park building, a water or sanitation district
  facility is: there, ask Planning for the exemption determination (25.07.012(B)).
- The building permit carries the solar; there is no Planning list item (Gov. Code 65850.5(b);
  `planning-approvals` 4a-bis).

**Improvements to a single-family home** (including garages, pools, fences, sheds and landscaping on
the same lot) are exempt **unless** one of these applies (25.07.008(A)(1)(a)-(g)):
- (a) a guest house or self-contained ADU, except converting habitable area inside the walls of the
  main house;
- (b) the structure or work is on a beach, in a wetland or stream, seaward of mean high tide, within
  50 ft of a coastal bluff edge, in an ESA, or in an area the certified Land Use Plan calls highly
  scenic;
- (c) in the strip between the sea and the first public road (or within 300 ft of the beach), the
  work adds 10% or more internal floor area (counting earlier exempt additions), raises any
  structure more than 10%, or builds a detached structure such as a garage, fence, shoreline
  protection or dock;
- (d) a new or expanded well or septic system;
- (e) an area the Commission has found critically short of water (pools, irrigation);
- (f) the original CDP said future improvements need a CDP;
- (g) significant landform change, including vegetation removal on a beach, wetland or dune, within
  50 ft of a bluff edge or stream, or in natural vegetation or an ESA.

**Default for oceanfront and bluff lots: lead with "a CDP is likely needed" for work that adds
something new outside the existing envelope:** additions, new structures (detached or not), grading,
new footings, retaining walls and landscape changes. When the lot fronts the ocean, a beach or a
coastal bluff (the parcel lookup coastal and bluff facts, an oceanfront or bluff setback on the
plans, or the resident's description), and that work is not plainly far from the edge, the exemption
is the exception, not the rule.
**Work inside the existing envelope** (replacing doors, windows or skylights in existing openings,
re-cladding or re-siding, a reroof with no height change, interior work) adds no floor area, no
height and no detached structure, so the (c) tests cannot trip. Lead with "exempt", then name only
the listed exceptions that could still apply: (b) when the house itself sits within 50 ft of a bluff
edge, on the beach, in an ESA or a highly scenic area; (f) when the original CDP required permits
for future improvements. In-kind replacement is also repair and maintenance ((A)(3)), unless it uses
mechanized equipment or stores materials within 50 ft of a bluff edge ((A)(3)(d)).
For new work, say first: "A CDP is likely needed; whether this work is
exempt depends on its distance to the bluff edge" (and, on the shoreline strip, on the (c) tests).
The text behind the default:
- (b) removes the exemption for improvements "located on a beach, in a wetland or stream, seaward of
  the mean high tide line within fifty feet of a coastal bluff edge" (25.07.008(A)(1)(b); the
  certified text has no comma after "line"; our reading treats "within fifty feet of a coastal bluff
  edge" as its own trigger, as (g) does for landform work);
- (g) removes it for "any significant alteration of land forms ... within fifty feet of the edge of
  a coastal bluff" (grading, excavation, footings, retaining walls, landscaping changes);
- every oceanfront lot is "between the sea and the first public roadway paralleling the sea", so (c)
  applies too: an addition bringing internal floor area up 10% or more (counting earlier exempt
  additions), a height increase over 10%, or any detached structure "such as a garage, fence,
  shoreline protective works, or docks" needs a CDP;
- for a building that is not a single-family home, the landform test reaches 100 ft from the bluff
  edge (25.07.008(A)(2)(d)).
Ask for the one number that decides it: the distance from the nearest part of the work (including
footings, grading and staging) to the bluff edge, from the survey or site plan. Say "exempt" only
when the plans show the work more than 50 ft from the edge (100 ft for (A)(2)(d)) and none of (c)
applies. Where the bluff edge is will be fixed by the surveyor and Planning, not by us. The separate
bluff-top building setback (25.50.004(B)(4), 25.50.020) and the ADU 25-ft bluff setback
(25.17.020(c)(1)(C)) still apply whether or not a CDP is needed.

**Other exemptions:**
- improvements to non-single-family buildings, unless they change the intensity of use or fall in
  the listed sensitive spots ((A)(2)); a change in the number of units is a change in density
  (`unit-removal`);
- repair and maintenance that does not enlarge the thing repaired, except listed shoreline and
  sensitive-area work ((A)(3)). Under the Commission's rule, replacing 50% or more of a single-family
  home or other structure is a replacement structure, not repair (14 CCR 13252(b); state skill
  `coastal-development` `cdp-when-required`). Test a near-total rebuild as new development or under
  the (A)(1) improvement exemption, not as repair;
- utility connections ((A)(4));
- replacing a structure destroyed by natural disaster: same use, same spot, not more than 10% bigger
  in floor area, height or bulk ((A)(5)). Detail: `disaster-rebuild`;
- temporary events on beach areas ((A)(6)), below;
- a wireless facility in the public right-of-way mounted on an existing structure or a replacement
  within 3 ft of the existing location, unless it would affect public access, scenic resources,
  ESHA or other coastal resources ((A)(7)). Detail: `right-of-way-utilities`.

**Temporary events (25.07.008(A)(6)).** A temporary event on a beach area is exempt unless it meets
all three tests: held between Memorial Day weekend and Labor Day; on all or part of a sandy beach;
and charges for general public admission or seating where none is charged now (booth and entry fees
do not count) ((6)(a)). Even then the Director may exclude it if more than 75% of seating is free,
the beach is remote with no resource effect, it lasts less than a day, or it repeats an event that
already had a CDP on the same terms ((6)(b)). The Director may require a CDP for an otherwise exempt
event that shuts the public out of a recreation area for a significant time, affects sensitive
resources, restricts roads or beach parking in summer, or has needed a CDP before ((6)(c)). A
"temporary event" is development of limited duration (up to two weeks straight, or four months
intermittent) that places non-permanent structures (stages, tents, bleachers) or takes exclusive use
of a sandy beach, parkland, street or parking area open to the public ((6)(d)). (A)(6) exempts only
events on beach areas; an event elsewhere that is development has no listed exemption. An event
that takes exclusive use of a public street or parking area, especially a recurring one, points to a
CDP decided with its TUP (25.07.012(D)); Planning makes the determination (25.07.012(B)). The City permit for the event itself:
`temporary-uses`.

**How an exemption is given.** The code requires the Director to "maintain a record of all
development within the coastal zone that were exempted", and, for exempt appealable development, to
notify the Commission's Executive Director within seven days (25.07.008(B)). In the appealable area
an exempt project cannot start until 10 working days pass after the Commission receives a complete
exemption record with no appeal (25.07.008(C)). The code sets no separate exemption application.
Outside the appealable area the resident files nothing extra: Planning logs the exemption with the
Planning or building filing. Say "exempt; Planning logs the exemption", not "file a CDP exemption".
In the appealable area the record and the Commission notice are required, so the start waits out
the Commission's 10-working-day window; the City's reroof handout tells applicants there to "file a
CDP Exemption and wait out a 10 working day appeal" (`../../reroof/SKILL.md`). Say that.

**Detached accessory buildings** (sauna, studio, shed, cabana) on a single-family lot are
"structures ... normally associated with a single-family dwelling" and exempt unless an exception
applies. The detached-structure exception, (c)3, applies only in the first-road or 300-ft strip;
(b) and (g) apply anywhere.

**Two exceptions this guide cannot resolve from data:**
- **"ESA" in Chapter 25.07 is its own definition**: an area where "plant or animal life or their
  habitats are either rare or especially valuable" (25.07.006). The Ord. 1732 definition that adds
  "slopes of 30% or greater" is in 25.08.010 and drives the design review lists. On the text, a
  steep slope alone does not defeat the CDP exemption under (b) or (g); mapped or assessed habitat
  does. Ord. 1732 is also uncertified, so it cannot change the certified Chapter 25.07. Confirm
  with Planning.
- **"Highly scenic" areas** are those "designated as highly scenic in the certified Land Use Plan"
  ((A)(1)(b)). We hold no map of them and the parcel lookup does not report them. For every
  single-family exemption answer, give the branch: "If the lot is in a highly scenic area under the
  certified Land Use Plan, the work needs a CDP. UNSETTLED; confirm with Planning at
  pre-application."

**Categorical exclusion (Order E-93-1).** Development in a categorical exclusion area is exempt
from Chapter 25.07 except the notice rules of 14 CCR 13248 (25.07.010). The order excludes "single
family residential development" in eleven areas. Its Special Condition 8 requires conformity with
the LCP or the order, whichever is more restrictive
(the City source document (not included in this plugin) section 1).
- **What it reaches on its face:** single-family residential development, which on the words
  includes a new house, in the eleven listed areas only. It does not reach multifamily, commercial,
  public works, subdivisions or work outside those areas.
- **What it does not clear:** an ADU. In October 2024 the Executive Director found the LCP
  exemption list (25.07.008) controls for an ADU added to a house (Commission report F9a, Nov. 2024).
  Do not tell a resident E-93-1 clears an ADU.
- **How Special Condition 8 works:** the LCP is stricter wherever it requires a CDP. The LCP exempts
  only improvements to an existing house (25.07.008(A)(1)); it has no exemption for a new house. So
  run the 25.07.008 test as if there were no exclusion: if the LCP exempts the work, no CDP; if not,
  the exclusion does not clear it.
- **New house in an exclusion area:** the test above gives a CDP (the LCP has no new-house
  exemption), and 25.07.012(D) has it decided with the Design Review. City practice has done the
  same: a regular CDP heard with the DRB, not an exclusion. Tell the resident to expect that;
  confirm with Planning at pre-application.

**Major remodel.** A major remodel in the coastal zone needs a CDP; it is treated as new development
(`../../residential-remodel/references/city-cdp-coastal-zone.md`; 25.56.011).

## Step 4 — Which kind of CDP

| Kind | When | Decider and hearing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| De minimis waiver | Not appealable, not in an appeal area, no local hearing required, no possible effect on coastal resources, no violation | Director, written waiver | 25.07.009 |
| Administrative CDP | "Minor development": consistent with the LCP, needs no other discretionary approval, no adverse effect | Director; hearing waived unless someone asks within 15 working days of the notice | 25.07.013 |
| CDP with other permits | The project also needs Design Review, a variance, CUP, etc. | The same body as the other permit, at the same time | 25.07.012(D) |
| CDP alone | Only a CDP is needed | Director, at a public hearing | 25.07.012(D)(1) |
| Appealable CDP | In the appealable area or major public works | At least one public hearing; then open to Coastal Commission appeal | 25.07.012(E); 25.07.016(B) |

**ADU-only CDP (state law).** A City CDP that covers only an ADU is decided within 60 days and
concurrently with the ADU; no hearing is required, and it cannot be appealed to the Commission (Gov.
Code 66329(a), (c)). A CDP that also covers other work follows the normal appeal area. Details:
`../../adu/SKILL.md`.

**Picking the kind for a project with an ADU** (code first, then City practice since Ord. 1732):
- **ADU filed alone:** an administrative CDP (25.07.013) or a CDP waiver (25.07.009), as practice
  has used both. Say "usually administrative; Planning picks the kind".
- **ADU in a project where any other piece needs ADR or Design Review: lead with one CDP decided
  with that review.** The code default: CDP review "shall be reviewed in conjunction with whatever
  other permits are required for the project ... all such permits shall be processed concurrently and
  the approving body for the coastal development permit shall be the same as that for the permit
  required by the underlying zoning district" (25.07.012(D)). So the headline is one CDP covering the
  ADU and the other work, decided by the body hearing that piece: the Director at the ADR hearing,
  the DRB (or the Planning Commission when it is the Design Review body, 25.05.040(F)(1)) at the
  Design Review hearing. Practice agrees: where the non-ADU pieces drew a hearing (a variance, a
  historic resource, large additions), the ADU's CDP was decided by the DRB with the project. What
  sets the hearing body is always the non-ADU pieces, never the ADU. This CDP is appealable if the
  lot is in the appealable area.
  - Then, as the alternative that could change it: the ADU may be filed as its own project with its
    own CDP, decided within 60 days with no hearing and no Commission appeal (Gov. Code 66329(a),
    (c)), and the other work gets its own CDP or exemption record. Planning
    picks at intake; say "confirm with Planning at pre-application". Do not present the two as equal
    branches.
  - When the only other pieces are Zone Clearance items, there is no hearing body to join; the ADU's
    CDP follows "ADU filed alone" above, and the other pieces take their own CDP or exemption.
- **Non-single-family lot** (duplex, R-2 lot with two houses, multifamily): improvements are exempt
  only if they do not change the intensity of use ((A)(2)). A new ADU adds a unit, so expect a CDP
  unless the ADU's own conversion exemption applies (25.17.010; `../../adu/SKILL.md`). With nothing
  else needing a hearing, that CDP is usually administrative, as for an ADU filed alone. On such a
  lot staff may also open an ADR record beside the ADU for its non-ADU pieces (`planning-approvals`
  4h).

**SB 9.** The City does not hold public hearings on CDPs for SB 9 projects (25.95.040(F)).

## Three Arch Bay, Irvine Cove and Blue Lagoon (deferred certification)

The certified LCP does not cover these areas, so the Coastal Commission keeps original permit
jurisdiction. The City's CDP application names "Blue Lagoon, Lower Irvine Cove, and Three Arch Bay"
(City CDP Application, page 1); the Commission's LCP status chart also lists Hobo Canyon and Parking
Lot 16 (the City source document (not included in this plugin) section 1; UNSETTLED for those two, confirm with Planning).
the parcel lookup reports the deferred-certification area; it decides who issues the CDP. Where
they are: Three Arch Bay and Blue Lagoon are gated communities in South Laguna (Blue Lagoon is an
oceanfront condo complex north of Treasure Island); Lower Irvine Cove is the gated area at the north
city edge. When an applicant gives only a street name near one of them, ask which community before
naming who issues the CDP.

The order of steps:
1. **Association review (Three Arch Bay).** Before City review, and to the extent permitted by law,
   the Three Arch Bay Community Association reviews the plans and sends the City a letter of comment
   (25.44.060, as amended by Ord. 1732). Irvine Cove and Blue Lagoon have no such step in the LBMC; a
   private association may still require its own review under its CC&Rs.
2. **City review.** The City still runs its own Planning review (Zone Clearance, ADR or Design
   Review) and Building plan check. The City does not issue a CDP or a CDP exemption there
   (25.07.012(A)(1)). City practice: the CDP item "will get filled out during plan check"; it does
   not block City intake (City Planning intake ruling, Sept. 2026).
3. **Coastal Commission filing.** The applicant files directly with the Commission's South Coast
   District office (25.07.012(B)(2) names that office). The City's instruction: "Complete and submit
   all Appendix B forms and send the required application fees directly to the California Coastal
   Commission" (City Reroofing Guide, "Deferred Certification Zone", 2026-08). The Commission decides
   whether the work is exempt (14 CCR 13250 for single-family improvements; PRC 30610) or needs a
   Commission CDP. Get the exemption letter or CDP before the City issues the building permit.
4. **Later changes and extensions** go to the Commission, not the City (25.07.012(J)(5), Ord. 1732;
   14 CCR 13169 for extensions, asked for before expiry).

## Public works, utilities and energy facilities

- **Public works** means public or PUC-regulated water, sewer, telephone and similar utility
  facilities; public transportation facilities (streets, roads, highways, public parking); and
  publicly financed recreation facilities and special-district development (25.07.006 "Public
  works").
- **Appealable anywhere:** approval or denial of a **major public works project** or a **major
  energy facility** is appealable development wherever it is (25.07.006 "Appealable development"
  (2)). "Major" means an estimated construction cost over $100,000, indexed to the Engineering News
  Record Construction Cost Index, except work governed by PRC 30610, 30610.5, 30611 or 30624
  (25.07.006 "Major energy facility", "Major public works project"). A major energy facility
  includes any facility generating, storing or transmitting electricity over that cost, so a new
  standalone battery or solar facility (a solar farm, a utility battery yard) can qualify. Rooftop
  solar and paired storage on an existing building cannot: it is an improvement governed by PRC
  30610, which the definition excludes (Step 3). A denial of one of these is also appealable, on the
  ground that it does conform (25.07.016(B)(2)).
- **The non-single-family improvement exemption excludes public works facilities** (25.07.008(A)(2)),
  and the disaster-replacement exemption excludes them too ((A)(5)). Repair and maintenance
  ((A)(3)) and utility connections ((A)(4)) still apply.
- For new public works or energy facilities that are not 30610 improvements, cost decides the
  route. Read the stated valuation; if none, ask for it. Do not guess.
- Utility undergrounding, Caltrans and work in the street: `right-of-way-utilities`.

## Emergency CDP (25.07.020)

- In a verified emergency (a sudden, unexpected event needing immediate action to prevent loss or
  damage to life, health, property or essential services; 25.07.006 "Emergency"), the Director may
  authorize remedial work before a full CDP. Apply by letter, in person or by phone, reporting the
  nature, cause, location and needed work within three days (25.07.020(A)).
- **Limit:** the Director may not grant an emergency CDP in a Commission-jurisdiction area or for any
  appealable development. There the request goes to the Coastal Commission (25.07.020(B)). For work
  needed to protect life or public property, the Commission's Executive Director may waive the
  emergency permit (25.07.020(B)(1); PRC 30611). The City's own clearance of the work can then be
  conditioned on the Commission's emergency CDP coming first, so in the appealable area tell the
  resident to go to the Commission before expecting a City sign-off.
- An emergency permit lasts 60 days. Before it ends, the owner must file a regular CDP application,
  even if only to remove the emergency work and restore the site (25.07.020(E)).

## Changing an approved CDP

- **Certified rule:** an application to amend a valid CDP goes to the original approving authority
  and follows the Chapter 25.07 procedures (25.07.022).
- **Ord. 1732 adds 25.07.012(J)** (not yet certified by the Commission; see the certified-LCP
  question below). The Director may approve a minor change without a hearing only if it is
  consistent with the certified LCP; does not touch a feature behind the CEQA finding, a condition, or
  a specific consideration of the decider; does not expand or change the use; and does not alter the
  25.07.012(G) findings ((J)(3)(i)). The Director may refer any change to the original decider
  ((J)(3)(ii)). Anything else needs a new application to the original decider; all prior conditions
  stay unless changed ((J)(4)). Changes to a Commission-issued CDP go to the Commission ((J)(5)).
- After a Commission appeal, the Director may make ministerial changes to the related City
  entitlements if they stay consistent with the Commission's CDP and do not enlarge the project
  (25.07.016(C)).
- Whether the uncertified (J) test or the certified 25.07.022 governs a given change is UNSETTLED;
  give both and confirm with Planning. Changes to the Design Review itself: 25.05.060
  (`discretionary-permits`).

## Step 5 — Notice, decision, effective date

- Appealable: mailed notice to owners and residents within 100 ft and to the Commission, within 10
  days of accepting the application or at least 10 days before the hearing (25.07.014(B)).
- Not appealable: mailed notice at least 10 days before the hearing to owners within 300 ft,
  residents within 100 ft, and the Commission (25.07.014(C)).
- Notice of action goes to the Commission within 7 days of the decision (25.07.014(D)).
- Appealable CDPs take effect 10 working days after the Commission receives that notice, if no one
  appeals. A Commission appeal must be filed in that window, on the ground that the project does not
  conform to the certified LCP or Coastal Act access policies (25.07.012(I)(1); 25.07.016(B)).
- No work starts until every appeal period ends (25.07.016).
- A CDP lapses two years after it takes effect unless used; a two-year and then a one-year extension
  are possible for City CDPs not appealed to the Commission (25.07.012(I)(3)-(4)).

## The certified-LCP question (2026)

A CDP must conform to the **certified** Local Coastal Program (PRC 30604(b); 25.07.012(G)(1)). Ord.
1732's new design review lists are not yet certified (see `design-review-lists.md`, Coastal zone

October 2026 handout uses the stricter method there. Whether the uncertified lists change the

## What to tell the resident

1. Whether the lot is in the coastal zone and appealable area (cite the parcel lookup).
2. Whether the work is exempt, and which exception defeats the exemption if not.
3. Which kind of CDP, who decides, and that it runs alongside the Design Review or Zone Clearance.
4. The CDP Application is a separate filing on the Public Permit Portal
   (`../../city-website/references/planning-applications.md`). In Three Arch Bay, Irvine Cove or Blue Lagoon the
   filing goes to the Coastal Commission instead.
5. For an ADU filed alone, name the kinds: usually an administrative CDP, sometimes a waiver. For an
   ADU in a project with an ADR or Design Review piece, lead with one CDP decided by that hearing
   body (25.07.012(D)); name the separate ADU-only CDP (Gov. Code 66329) only as the alternative
   Planning may choose at intake.
