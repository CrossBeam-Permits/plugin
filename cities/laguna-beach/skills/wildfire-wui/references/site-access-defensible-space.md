## Site Requirements — Defensible Space, Vegetation, Access & Water

### 1. Defensible Space Zones

Hazardous vegetation and fuels must be managed around all buildings. Defensible space has two regulatory zones. (CWUIC §§ 603, 604; PRC § 4291.)

| Zone | Distance | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1** (Lean, Clean, Green) | 0–30 ft from structure | Remove all dead/dry vegetation; manage plants to minimize fire spread; shrub groupings limited; no single tree drip line within 10 ft of structure; ground cover managed |
| **Zone 2** (Reduced Fuel Zone) | 30–100 ft from structure (or to property line) | Reduce fuel volume and continuity; fire-smart vegetation preferred; trees spaced so crowns do not touch; dead vegetation removed |

**Who is obligated:** All buildings and structures in:
1. All unincorporated lands designated as SRA
2. Land designated as VHFHSZ by a city or local agency
3. Land designated in a city/local agency ordinance as WUI area
(CWUIC § 604.2.)

**Governing laws for maintenance:**
- PRC § 4291 (SRA)
- 14 CCR § 1299.03
- Gov. Code § 51182 (LRA VHFHSZ)
- 19 CCR § 3.07

> Defensible space maintenance is an **ongoing property owner obligation**, not typically enforced as a plan-check item — but a fire protection plan for new subdivisions must address it, and building departments may note it in their approval conditions.

### 2. Vegetation — New Planting Requirements

All **new plantings** of vegetation for landscaping in SRA and LRA FHSZ must use fire-smart vegetation, with specific placement rules. (CWUIC §§ 603.2–603.4.)

**What is "fire-smart vegetation"?** Must meet at least one of:
- Identified as fire-smart in an approved book, journal, or listing (e.g., UCCE publications)
- Identified by a licensed landscape architect with supporting justification
- Approved by the local enforcing agency

#### Shrub Rules (§ 603.4.1)
- Maximum height: 6 feet
- Maximum aggregate diameter of grouping: 10 feet
- Grouping-to-grouping separation: ≥15 feet
- Grouping-to-structure separation: ≥30 feet
- Where shrubs are within/below a tree's drip line: lowest branch must be ≥3× height of understory shrubs OR ≥10 feet, whichever is greater

#### Tree Rules (§ 603.4.2 — within 30-ft zone)
- New trees: drip line at maturity ≥10 feet from any combustible structure
- Horizontal distance between new tree crowns and adjacent tree crowns: ≥10 feet
- Existing trees: trimmed to ≥10 feet away from chimney and stovepipe outlets (14 CCR § 1299.03)

#### Non-Fire-Smart Trees (§ 603.4.2.1)
Conifers, palms, pepper trees, eucalyptus, and similar species may be planted if:
- Drip line at maturity ≥30 feet from applicable building; **OR**
- Drip line ≥10 feet from combustible structure AND tree is well-pruned and maintained to not form a fire-transmission path to/from structure

#### Outbuildings (§ 604.4)
Outbuildings (structures <120 sq ft, not for human habitation) must have:
- Minimum 10-foot clearance to bare mineral soil in all directions
- Vegetation more than 10 feet but less than 20 feet from outbuilding must be fire-smart

### 3. Combustible Storage on Site

**Firewood and combustible material (§ 607.1):**
- **NOT permitted:** Storage under buildings/structures, on decks, or under eaves/canopies/overhangs
- Firewood piles: **≥30 feet from structures** unless completely covered by fire-resistant material
- Exposed wood piles within defensible space: minimum 10-foot clearance to bare mineral soil in all directions

**LP-gas tanks (§ 606.3):**
- Must be located within defensible space (CFC compliant)
- Minimum 10-foot clearance to bare mineral soil in all directions
- Vegetation 10–20 feet from tank must be fire-smart

### 4. Building Setbacks from Property Lines and Roads

**Minimum 30-foot setback** for all buildings from:
- Property lines, AND
- Center of a road
(CWUIC § 608.2; 14 CCR § 1276.01(a).)

**Setback reduction (§ 608.2.1):**
A reduction below 30 feet may be approved based on:
- Practical reasons (parcel dimensions, topographic limitations, density requirements, sensitive habitat, other site constraints)

Mitigation required when setback reduced:
1. Noncombustible block walls or fences
2. Noncombustible material extending 5 feet horizontally from the furthest extent of the building
3. Hardscape landscaping
4. Reduced exposed windows on the side with reduced setback
5. Most protective requirements in Chapter 5 applied

### 5. Access Roads and Driveways

Fire apparatus access is required for buildings in WUI areas. (CWUIC §§ 402, 403.)

#### Key Access Standards (14 CCR §§ 1273.01–1273.09)

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Two-way road width** | ≥2 ten-foot traffic lanes (20 ft total), not including shoulder; provides two-way traffic for emergency/evacuation |
| **One-way road width** | ≥1 twelve-foot lane; must connect at both ends to two-lane road; max 10 residential units; max length 2,640 ft; turnout at midpoint |
| **Driveway width** | ≥1 ten-foot traffic lane, 14 ft unobstructed horizontal clearance, 13'6″ vertical clearance |
| **Road structural load** | Roads: ≥75,000 lbs fire apparatus; driveways/structures: ≥40,000 lbs |
| **Maximum grade** | ≤16% (may exceed up to 20% with local jurisdiction approval and mitigation) |
| **Horizontal curve radius** | Inside radius ≥50 ft; add 4 ft width for 50–100 ft curves; 2 ft for 100–200 ft curves |
| **Vertical curve length** | ≥100 ft exclusive of drainage structures |

#### Turnarounds (§ 403.1.6; 14 CCR § 1273.05)
- **Required on:** All driveways and dead-end roads
- **Minimum turning radius:** 40 feet (not including parking); OR hammerhead/T with ≥60 ft top length
- **Driveways >150 ft, <800 ft:** Turnout required near midpoint
- **Driveways ≥800 ft:** Turnouts ≤400 ft apart
- **Driveways >300 ft:** Turnaround required within 50 ft of building

#### Dead-End Road Length Limits (§ 403.1.9; 14 CCR § 1273.08)

| Parcel Zoning | Maximum Cumulative Dead-End Length |
|---|---|
| <1 acre | 800 feet |
| 1–4.99 acres | 1,320 feet |
| 5–19.99 acres | 2,640 feet |
| ≥20 acres | 5,280 feet |

Measured from edge of road surface at intersection to farthest point. Where zoning changes along the road, use the shortest applicable limit.

#### Gate Entrances (§ 403.1.10; 14 CCR § 1273.09)
- Minimum clear width: 14 ft horizontal, 13'6″ vertical
- Must be at least 2 ft wider than the traffic lane served
- Located ≥30 ft from roadway, opening to allow vehicle to stop without blocking road
- Security gates: require approved emergency operation means (Knox box or equivalent)

#### Address Signing (§§ 403.2.4, 403.2.5)
- All buildings must have assigned address, plainly legible and visible from fronting road
- Address signs on private roads: additional sign at public way intersection
- Letters: minimum 4-inch height, ½-inch stroke, reflectorized, contrasting background
- Residential addresses must be reflectorized

### 6. Water Supply

Buildings and structures in WUI areas must be provided with an approved water supply. (CWUIC §§ 402.2.2, 404.)

**General standard:** Sufficient fire flow for structural fire protection AND wildland fire exposure, per CFC Section 507 and applicable fire flow tables (CFC Appendices B or BB, C or CC). (CWUIC § 404.2.)

**Draft sites:** Natural water sources used for fire protection require approved draft sites with emergency vehicle access per § 403. Pumper access point ≤150 ft off access road. (§ 404.3.)

**Hydrant spacing:** Per CFC Appendices C or CC, as applicable. (§ 404.4.)

**Standby power (§ 404.10.3):** Water supply systems within WUI areas that depend on electrical power require standby power capable of ≥2 hours of operation.
- Exception 1: Not required if primary power service is underground (with code official approval)
- Exception 2: Not required for systems serving only one single-family dwelling

**Identification (§ 404.8):** Fire hydrants and water supply access points must be marked with reflectorized blue markers (≥3-inch dimension) on post or driveway sign.

### 7. Fire Protection Plan

The code official may require a **fire protection plan** for projects in WUI areas. (CWUIC § 602.1.)

**Required to be prepared by:** Registered design professional, qualified landscape architect, qualified fire safety specialist, or similar specialist acceptable to the code official.

**Preliminary plan must include (§ 602.3.1):**
1. Total project size
2. Adjoining property information (land use, structures, vegetation, roads)
3. Map with boundary lines, property lines, slope contours, structure footprints, roads/driveways, fuel modification zones

**Final plan also includes (§ 602.3.2):**
1. Plant map for fuel modification zones with species legend (life-form, scientific/common name, mature height/width)
2. Identification of irrigated and non-irrigated zones
3. Vegetation reduction requirements for access/evacuation routes
4. Equipment/personnel access points for vegetation maintenance
5. Legally binding maintenance responsibility statements (HOA, CC&Rs)

**When most commonly required:** New subdivisions or large new residential projects in SRA or LRA VHFHSZ. San Diego County unincorporated areas often require this as part of the tentative map process.

### 8. Ridgeline Protection (§ 609.1)

Local jurisdictions must identify **Strategic Ridgelines** where development restriction would reduce fire risk. New residential units are **prohibited** within or at the top of drainages or topographic chimneys on identified strategic ridgelines. (CWUIC § 609.1(c)(1).)

> San Diego County context: The county has adopted strategic ridgeline protections in its General Plan. Check the applicable community plan area's policies for specific ridgeline mapping.

### 9. Fuel Breaks and Greenbelts (§§ 609.2–609.2.7)

When an application involves ≥3 new parcels, a change to higher-density zoning, or a more intensive use permit, the local jurisdiction (in consultation with the fire authority) must determine whether fuel breaks are required. (§ 609.2; 14 CCR § 1276.03.)

Fuel breaks must:
- Reduce radiant/convective heat and ember exposure to access routes and buildings
- Have at least one point of entry for firefighters and fire apparatus
- Be completed **prior to commencement of permitted construction** (§ 609.2.4)
- Have legally binding maintenance mechanisms (binding on the property; HOA, assessments, or other)
