# Laguna Beach WUI Code -- Fire-Resistive Exterior Wall Covering Requirements

Source: LBMC Ch. 14.70 (Wildland-Urban Interface Code), Ch. 14.50 (Building Code amendments), Ch. 25.05 (Design Review)
Based on: 2025 California Wildland-Urban Interface Code, 2025 California Building Code
Adopted: Ord. 1725, 12/18/2025

This file extracts the WUI code amendments and related city regulations most relevant to siding and exterior wall covering replacement in Laguna Beach.

---

## 1. VHFHSZ Applicability

Laguna Beach is predominantly designated **Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone (VHFHSZ)** per CAL FIRE maps; the citywide coverage figure is in `../../city-reference/SKILL.md`. Essentially the entire city is subject to WUI fire-resistive construction requirements.

**Scope (Section 14.70.015, amending WUI Code 101.2):** The WUI code applies to building materials, systems, and assemblies used in the exterior design and construction of new buildings, additions, alterations, AND REPAIRS within the WUI area.

Siding replacement is an "alteration" or "repair" to exterior construction -- it falls squarely within scope.

---

## 2. Exterior Wall Covering Requirements (Section 14.70.030, amending WUI Code 504.5.2)

The exterior wall covering, or wall assembly including insulation, must comply with ONE of the following:

### Option 1: Noncombustible Material
Materials that do not ignite, burn, or release flammable vapors when exposed to fire or heat.

**Compliant examples:**
- Stucco (cement plaster)
- Metal siding (steel, aluminum)
- Stone or masonry veneer
- Concrete panels

### Option 2: Ignition-Resistant Building Material (Labeled for Exterior Use)
Materials tested and labeled as ignition-resistant per the applicable standard (30-minute ASTM E84 Steiner tunnel test with flame spread index <=25 and smoke-developed index <=450).

**Compliant examples:**
- Fiber cement lap siding (e.g., James Hardie HardiePlank)
- Fiber cement panels (e.g., James Hardie HardiePanel)
- Fiber cement shingle siding

### Option 3: Fire-Retardant-Treated (FRT) Wood (Labeled for Exterior Use)
Wood that has been pressure-treated with fire-retardant chemicals per CBC Section 2303.2.

**Requirements:**
- Must be labeled for exterior use
- Must comply with CBC 2303.2 testing and labeling
- Not commonly used for siding -- fiber cement is the dominant replacement for wood

### Option 4: Fire-Retardant-Treated Wood Shingles/Shakes
FRT wood shingles/shakes qualified as "Class B" roof covering per CBC Section 1505.6.

**Requirements:**
- Must be installed over solid sheathing
- Acceptable as wall covering (not just roofing)
- Less common than Options 1-3

## 3. Prohibited Materials in VHFHSZ

The following materials do NOT comply with Section 14.70.030:

| Material | Why Prohibited | Common Replacement |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Vinyl siding** | Combustible -- melts and ignites at ~700F | Fiber cement lap siding |
| **Untreated wood siding** (cedar, redwood, pine) | Combustible -- ignites readily | Fiber cement simulating wood grain |
| **Polypropylene siding** | Combustible polymer | Fiber cement or metal |
| **Untreated wood shingles/shakes** | Combustible unless FRT and Class B qualified | FRT shakes over solid sheathing, or fiber cement shingles |
| **Hardboard siding** (Masonite) | Combustible wood composite | Fiber cement panels |

## 5. Minor Repair Exception (Section 14.70.015, Exception to WUI Code 101.2)

> With the approval of the building official minor repairs or replacement of exterior materials may be done with the same materials as exists on the building.

**Key limitations:**
- Requires **building official approval** (not automatic -- contractor must request)
- Applies only to **minor repairs or replacement**
- Only permits **same-as-existing** material (cannot upgrade to a different non-compliant material)
- Full re-clad of an entire building does NOT qualify as "minor"
- Building official has discretion to deny

**Practical application:**
- Patching a small damaged section of existing vinyl siding: may qualify
- Replacing a few damaged wood shingles on one wall: may qualify
- Replacing siding on an entire wall or whole house: does NOT qualify
- Changing from one non-compliant material to another: does NOT qualify

## 6. CBC Section 705.1 Amendment (Section 14.50.112)

Laguna Beach amends CBC 705.1 to read:

> 705.1 General. The requirements of Chapter 5 of the California Wildland-Urban Interface Code are applicable for all buildings located in Fire Hazard Zones. Section 705 shall apply to exterior wall coverings; balconies and similar projections; and oriel and bay windows constructed of combustible materials. **The most restrictive requirements of Section 705 and Chapter 5 of the California Wildland-Urban Interface Code shall govern.**

**Impact on siding replacement:** Exterior wall coverings must satisfy BOTH:
1. CBC Section 705 fire-separation distance requirements (fire-resistance ratings based on proximity to property line)
2. WUI Chapter 5 material requirements (noncombustible/ignition-resistant per Section 14.70.030)

The more restrictive of the two governs. In practice, the WUI requirements are typically the controlling factor for siding material selection.

## 7. Current Planning Route After Ord. 1732

Urgency Ordinance No. 1732 repealed the former siding-specific partial-facade and wildfire-resistant-material exemptions. Current classification is:

- in-kind replacement → Zone Clearance under §25.05.040(B)(2)(c);
- other non-major alteration/addition and improvements no more than 15 ft → ordinarily Zone Clearance under (B)(2)(a)-(b), unless another trigger applies;
- major remodel → Design Review under (B)(1)(a);
- improvement over 15 ft → Design Review under (B)(1)(c); and
- ESA development → Design Review under (B)(1)(e), absent the enacted peer-reviewed professional determination.

WUI compliance remains a separate, mandatory material standard. Do not cite repealed `(B)(2)(d)` or `(B)(2)(w)` as current Design Review exemptions.

## 8. Sprinkler Retrofit -- Fire-Resistant Siding Exception

### Title 15 Section 1103.5.4 -- Group R Retrofit Trigger

Sprinklers required when alteration of 50% or more of a building includes work on 50% or more of the building's exterior.

**Exception: Work that consists only of the installation of fire-resistant siding.**

This means:
- Full re-siding with fire-resistant material (fiber cement, stucco, metal) as a standalone project does NOT trigger sprinkler retrofit
- If combined with other work (windows, roofing, interior remodel) that collectively reaches the 50% threshold, sprinklers ARE required
- The exception protects contractors doing siding-only fire-safety upgrades — but it reaches §1103.5.4 only, not the Ch. 14.52.030 valuation trigger below

### Ch. 14.52.030 -- Cumulative Valuation Trigger (the second residential trigger)

**Either trigger firing requires the retrofit.** Ch. 14.52.030 (CRC R309.2 amendment, Ord. 1725, 12/18/2025) requires sprinklers throughout the dwelling and attached garage when the cumulative value of additions, alterations and repairs in any three-year period exceeds 50% of the pre-work valuation. Siding replacement contributes to that running total, **and the fire-resistant-siding exception does not apply to this trigger** — it belongs to §1103.5.4 alone. If the owner has done other permitted work in the past three years, verify the combined valuation. Valuation is per the City's building-permit fee resolution.

CBC §903.6, as amended by LBMC §14.50.104, expressly contains Group R clauses for CBC-track buildings. Do not substitute it for the specific Ch. 14.52.030 valuation rule on a CRC-track house. Full rule: `../../residential-remodel/references/city-sprinkler-retrofit.md`.
