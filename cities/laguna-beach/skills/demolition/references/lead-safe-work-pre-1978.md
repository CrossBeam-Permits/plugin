# Lead-Safe Work Practices for Pre-1978 Demolition

The federal residential lead-based paint ban took effect January 1, 1978. Any residential structure built **before 1978** is presumed to contain lead-based paint (LBP) under both federal (EPA RRP) and California (CDPH Title 17 / Cal/OSHA T8 §1532.1) regulatory schemes unless tested clear by a certified inspector/risk assessor.

> **Key threshold:** 1978 -- *not* 1980. (Asbestos uses the pre-1980 practical threshold; lead uses the pre-1978 regulatory threshold. Easy to confuse; get this right.)

---

## EPA RRP Rule (40 CFR Part 745 Subpart E)

### Scope -- "Target Housing" and "Child-Occupied Facilities"
The RRP Rule applies to renovation, repair, painting, **demolition**, window replacement, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, carpentry, and most trade work that disturbs >6 sq ft interior or >20 sq ft exterior of painted surface in:

1. **Target housing** -- any housing constructed before 1978 (with limited exemptions: 0-bedroom dwellings, housing for elderly/disabled with no resident <6 years old).
2. **Child-occupied facilities (COF)** -- pre-1978 buildings visited by the same child ≤6 years old on at least 2 days/week for ≥3 hours/visit, ≥6 hours/week, ≥60 hours/year (daycares, preschools, kindergartens).

> Note: The RRP Rule's "renovation" definition explicitly includes most demolition activities that disturb painted surfaces. However, **whole-structure demolition that completely removes the structure** is partially carved out -- the activity falls into Cal/OSHA Title 8 §1532.1 (worker exposure) and waste-handling jurisdictions rather than RRP-Certified-Renovator work practices. California-licensed contractors should default to assuming RRP applies until they've confirmed the carve-out, because misapplication has stiff EPA penalties.

### Certified Firm + Certified Renovator
- **Certified Firm:** company must be EPA Lead-Safe Certified (one-time application, 5-year renewal).
- **Certified Renovator:** at least one person on the job site holds a current 8-hour EPA-accredited RRP training certificate; this person trains other workers on-site and oversees lead-safe work practices.

### Required Lead-Safe Work Practices (40 CFR §745.85)
1. Post warning signs at work areas.
2. Contain dust within work area (plastic sheeting, sealed openings).
3. Use HEPA-equipped tools; prohibit open-flame burning, power sanding without HEPA, high-temp heat guns (>1100 °F).
4. Daily cleanup; final HEPA vacuum + wet wash.
5. Verify cleaning visually (white-glove or dust-wipe in COF / target housing).
6. Provide "Renovate Right" pamphlet to owner and occupants.
7. Recordkeeping for 3 years.

### Records
Pre-renovation testing (or presumption) results, certification numbers, training records, the signed Renovate Right pamphlet receipt, cleaning verification, and final compliance certificate.

## California Overlay -- CDPH Title 17 CCR §35001 et seq.

California operates its own lead-related construction-work regulation through the Department of Public Health (CDPH) at 17 CCR §§35001–36100 ("Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention" + "Accreditation, Certification, and Work Practices for Lead-Based Paint and Lead Hazards"). This program is California's EPA-authorized RRP equivalent for accreditation and certification.

### Key CDPH-Certified Categories (17 CCR §35040)
- **Lead Inspector / Assessor** -- conducts lead inspections, risk assessments, and clearance testing.
- **Lead Project Designer** -- writes abatement specifications.
- **Lead Supervisor** -- supervises abatement crews.
- **Lead Worker** -- performs abatement work.

> Demolition of a pre-1978 structure where lead-based paint is present in disturbed surfaces requires CDPH-certified personnel for the lead portion of the work.

### California Presumption Rule
California law (Health & Safety Code §17920.10; 17 CCR §36100) directs that **all paint in pre-1978 structures shall be presumed to contain lead unless tested and certified lead-free by a CDPH-certified Lead Inspector/Assessor.** Contractors may not bypass lead-safe practices on the basis of age alone -- they must either test clear or treat as lead-containing.

## Cal/OSHA Title 8 §1532.1 -- Worker Lead Exposure

For demolition crews exposed to lead during demo of pre-1978 structures:

- Initial determination / exposure assessment.
- Engineering / work-practice controls (HEPA, wet methods).
- Respiratory protection where exposure exceeds the PEL (50 µg/m³ TWA, action level 30 µg/m³).
- Medical surveillance for workers exposed above the action level for ≥30 days/year.
- Training (8-hour initial, annual refresher).
- Records (exposure monitoring, medical, training).

> Cal/OSHA Title 8 §1532.1 applies to the workers regardless of whether RRP applies to the project. It is a separate, parallel obligation enforced by DOSH (Division of Occupational Safety and Health).

## HUD Lead Disclosure Rule (24 CFR Part 35 Subpart A)

Sellers and lessors of pre-1978 housing must disclose known LBP to buyers/tenants and provide the EPA "Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home" pamphlet. **Not a building-code item, but verify a disclosure record exists when the demo is part of a sale/conversion** -- gaps here are red flags for downstream litigation.

## Common Failure Patterns

1. **"It's a teardown, RRP doesn't apply."** Wrong. RRP covers most demolition that disturbs painted surfaces; only full structure-removal carve-outs are narrow and must be documented. Default to RRP-Certified Firm + Certified Renovator for pre-1978 work.
2. **Confusing the 1978 (lead) and 1980 (asbestos) thresholds.** They are different. A 1979 building requires the lead workflow but does not have a regulatory asbestos age threshold (which is "all demolitions, no age exemption").
3. **Testing by an uncertified person.** Lead testing must be done by a CDPH-certified Lead Inspector/Assessor or a CDPH-certified Lead Sampling Technician. EPA RRP test kits cannot be used to declare a surface lead-free; they can only be used by a Certified Renovator to declare a surface lead-positive (or inconclusive).
4. **No Cal/OSHA plan** -- the project has RRP coverage but no worker-exposure compliance plan. Cal/OSHA §1532.1 is parallel, not subsumed.
5. **Lead-painted debris mixed with general demolition debris.** Lead-containing demo debris may be RCRA-hazardous if TCLP exceeds 5 mg/L Pb; treat as California hazardous waste until characterized.
