# Tenant Relocation Noticing for Demolition Projects

The California Relocation Assistance Act (Gov. Code §§7260–7277) and a patchwork of city ordinances govern when occupants must be noticed and provided relocation assistance before a demolition can proceed. Misclassifying this dimension is one of the most common ways a demo permit lands in litigation, so the building department's role is **flagging** rather than enforcing -- the legal trigger is statutory and city-specific.

> **Building-department rule of thumb:** if any portion of the structure is or recently was occupied by tenants AND the project is publicly assisted, federally assisted, or located in a city with a relocation/just-cause ordinance, **stop and route to planning / city attorney** before issuing the demo permit.

---

## The State Statute (Gov. Code §§7260–7277)

### Trigger -- "Displaced Person" (§7260(c))
A "displaced person" includes any person displaced as a direct result of:
- Rehabilitation, demolition, or other displacing activity,
- Of real property on which the person is a residential tenant or conducts a business or farm operation,
- Under a program or project **undertaken by a public entity**,
- Where the public entity determines the displacement is permanent.

### Core Threshold -- "Undertaken by a Public Entity"
This is the most consequential phrase in the statute. The Act applies when a **public entity** (state, city, county, redevelopment successor, transit agency, school district, etc.) is causing the displacement -- whether through direct acquisition, eminent domain, public works, federally funded redevelopment, or other public action.

> Pure private-party teardowns (owner buys property, demolishes building, builds new structure on own funds) generally **do not trigger §7260** at the state level. They may trigger city ordinances -- see below.

### Required Public-Entity Actions
1. **Plan early to minimize displacement** (§7260.5(b)(2)).
2. **Provide a relocation plan** to the public entity's governing board before any acquisition that would cause displacement (25 CCR §6038).
3. **Provide advisory services** (relocation counseling, replacement-housing referrals) (§7261).
4. **Pay relocation benefits** -- moving expenses, replacement-housing differential payments, downpayment assistance for displaced homeowners (§7262, §7263, §7264).
5. **90-day minimum displacement notice** to residential tenants before required vacancy (§7267.2, 25 CCR §6042) -- with 30-day specific-date notice to follow once a comparable replacement is available.
6. **Replacement-housing plan** for multi-family demolition affecting low-income tenants (§7260.5(b)(3); §7265 for last-resort housing).

---

## When the Statute Does NOT Apply

- Owner-occupied detached single-family teardown.
- Vacant structures (no current occupants displaced by the demo).
- Purely private commercial demolition with private financing and no public-entity nexus.
- Tenant who has been determined to be in unlawful occupancy (§7260(c)(3) exclusion).

## Local Ordinance Layer (Where Cities Often Add Requirements)

Many California cities have their own relocation, just-cause, or tenant-protection ordinances that apply to **private-party** demolitions when occupied units are removed from the rental market. Examples (verify current law at city level):

| City | Trigger | Typical Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Los Angeles | RSO unit demolition / Ellis Act withdrawal | 120-day notice + relocation payment ($9k–$24k+/unit) |
| Oakland | Just Cause for Eviction (Measure EE) | 60-day notice + relocation payment per Oakland Muni Code 8.22 |
| Berkeley | Demo of rent-controlled unit | Relocation payment + replacement-housing requirement |
| Santa Monica | Rent Control Charter | Demo permit conditional on Rent Control Board approval |
| San Francisco | Rent Ordinance / Costa-Hawkins-protected units | Wrongful eviction protections + relocation under Admin Code §37.9C |
| West Hollywood | Rent Stabilization Ordinance | Pre-demo approval + relocation |
| Pasadena | Tenant Protection Ordinance (2023) | Relocation payment for no-fault termination |
| Beverly Hills | RSO | Demo permit conditioned on Rent Stabilization compliance |

This is illustrative, not exhaustive. **For any project demolishing rented residential units, the city overlay skill should flag this dimension.**

## Ellis Act Sidebar (Gov. Code §§7060–7060.7)

Statewide statute permitting landlords to withdraw rental units from the market (often as precursor to demolition or conversion). Procedure:
1. Notice of intent to withdraw filed with city (where local ordinance requires).
2. Notice to tenants -- typically 120 days; 1 year for elderly/disabled tenants.
3. Withdrawal recorded against title.
4. Restrictions on re-rental for 5–10 years post-withdrawal.

Cities with rent control administer Ellis filings -- demo permits are usually gated on Ellis compliance.

## Substandard Housing -- Health & Safety Code §17975

Where a building is demolished after being declared substandard, **the displacing entity (often the city itself) must pay relocation benefits to lawful occupants.** Doesn't apply to most demo permits but is a known sleeper provision for code-enforcement-driven demolitions.

---

## Federal Layer -- Uniform Relocation Act (42 USC §§4601–4655)

Where federal funds touch the project (CDBG, HUD, FHWA, FTA, etc.), the federal Uniform Relocation Assistance and Real Property Acquisition Policies Act (URA) applies. California's Act incorporates URA's standards for federally assisted projects (§7260.5(d)).

## Common Failure Patterns

1. **Treating a private demo as automatically exempt from §7260.** Correct -- §7260 itself only applies when a public entity is involved. WRONG: ignoring local relocation ordinances and just-cause protections.
2. **"The tenant moved out before we filed" workaround.** Tenants displaced *in anticipation* of demolition can still be eligible -- Gov. Code §7262.5 and city ordinances often look back 6–12 months.
3. **Demolishing a long-term SRO / residential hotel without §17969 / city SRO ordinance review.** California has special protections for residential hotels (§7280, H&S Code §17995); many cities have additional preservation ordinances.
4. **Missing Ellis Act filing in rent-controlled cities.** The demo permit can be issued only after the Ellis withdrawal period expires and the city housing department signs off.
5. **Building department issues permit without flagging to planning.** Best practice -- if any unit on the property is or recently was rented, route to planning/housing for relocation determination before stamping the demo permit.

## How to Cite in Corrections Output

> **FLAG -- Item 15 (Tenant relocation noticing):** Permit application indicates structure is currently tenant-occupied. Confirm: (1) whether the project involves a public-entity nexus triggering Gov. Code §7260 et seq.; (2) whether the city of [city] has a rent stabilization or just-cause ordinance imposing relocation requirements; (3) Ellis Act compliance if applicable. Route to city housing/planning department for relocation determination before issuance. Building department cannot independently waive these requirements.
