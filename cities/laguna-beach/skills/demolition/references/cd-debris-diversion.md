# CALGreen Construction & Demolition Waste Diversion

Source: 2025 California Green Building Standards Code (Title 24, Part 11), Chapter 4 (Residential Mandatory Measures) §4.408 and Chapter 5 (Nonresidential Mandatory Measures) §5.408.

The CALGreen 65% C&D waste diversion mandate is one of the few requirements that applies to *every* California demolition permit regardless of jurisdiction. Cities may impose more-stringent local ordinances but cannot go lower than 65%.

---

## Core Mandate

**Recycle and/or salvage for reuse a minimum of 65 percent of the nonhazardous construction and demolition waste** generated during the project (CALGreen §4.408.1 / §5.408.1).

This is a percentage of the project's nonhazardous C&D waste stream by weight OR volume (not both -- pick one and document it consistently).

> **For full demolition:** the entire structure is the C&D waste stream. 65% of the lumber, drywall, concrete, metals, roofing, fixtures, flooring, glass, etc. must be diverted from landfill via recycling or salvage.

### Local Ordinance Rule
CALGreen §4.408.1 / §5.408.1 expressly defer to a more-stringent local ordinance. Many California cities (Berkeley, Oakland, San Francisco, San Jose, Palo Alto, Santa Monica, etc.) have local C&D ordinances at 75%, 85%, or "to the extent feasible." Always check the city overlay.

---

## Three Compliance Pathways (§4.408.2–4.408.4 / §5.408.1.1–5.408.1.3)

### Pathway A -- Construction Waste Management Plan (CWMP)
Submit a plan that:
1. Identifies the C&D materials to be diverted.
2. Determines if materials will be **source-separated** (sorted on-site) or **bulk mixed** (single-stream MRF).
3. Identifies the diversion facilities (named recyclers, MRFs, salvage operations).
4. For a residential plan under §4.408.2, identifies construction methods used to reduce generated waste.
5. Specifies diversion calculation by weight OR volume (not both).

### Pathway B -- Waste Management Company (§4.408.3 / §5.408.1.2)
Use a waste-management company that can provide verifiable documentation of diversion percentage. Common in jurisdictions with franchised C&D haulers (LA, OC cities) that issue compliance certificates.

### Pathway C -- Waste Stream Reduction Alternative
- **Nonresidential (§5.408.1.3):** Combined weight of new-construction disposal not exceeding **2 lb/sf of building area** is deemed to meet the 65% minimum.
- **Residential (§4.408.4):** Disposal not exceeding **3.4 lb/sf of building area** is deemed to meet the 65% minimum. Section 4.408.4.1 provides the listed 2 lb/sf alternative where that provision applies.

> These weight-per-sf alternatives are **per-square-foot of building area**, calculated at the end of the project against the disposal weight tickets. Useful when on-site separation isn't practical.

### Exceptions

Residential exceptions are listed under §4.408.1. The nonresidential exceptions
following §5.408.1.2 apply to §§5.408.1.1 and 5.408.1.2; they do not rewrite the
scope of the new-construction-only §5.408.1.3 alternative.

---

## Documentation (§4.408.5 / §5.408.1.4)

The CWMP shall be:
- Submitted to the enforcing agency for approval (typically with the permit application).
- Available on-site during construction for examination.
- **Updated as necessary** -- final reconciliation at end of project.
- Backed by records that demonstrate compliance with the selected pathway.

---

## Universal Waste — Nonresidential Additions and Alterations (§5.408.2)

For a nonresidential addition or alteration that meets the scoping provisions in
§301.3, §5.408.2 requires verification that prohibited Universal Waste is
properly disposed of and diverted from landfill. The section expressly names
fluorescent lamps and ballasts and mercury-containing thermostats and requires a
list of prohibited Universal Waste materials in the construction documents.

Chapter 4 does not contain a parallel residential §4.408.2 Universal Waste rule;
§4.408.2 is the residential construction waste management **plan**. Determine
residential hazardous/universal-waste duties from the applicable current DTSC
and project-specific authorities rather than importing §5.408.2.

---

## Hazardous Material Carve-Out

The 65% diversion calculation applies to **nonhazardous** C&D waste. The following are excluded from the calculation but must be handled separately and properly:
- Asbestos-containing materials (Class II landfill or approved ACM facility per SCAQMD R1403 / BAAQMD 11-2)
- Lead-painted demolition debris (TCLP testing; California hazardous waste if Pb >5 mg/L)
- Treated wood waste (CCA, ACQ, creosote -- 22 CCR §67386 special handling)
- Mercury, PCBs, refrigerants, fluorescent lamps (Universal Waste -- diverted but tracked separately)
- Petroleum-contaminated soil from underground storage tank removal
- Sandblast grit, fluorescent ballast residue

## Common Failure Patterns

1. **65% claimed by hand-wave, no facilities named.** Pathway A requires named diversion facilities. Reject; require specific MRFs / recyclers.
2. **Applicable nonresidential Universal Waste review omitted.** Where §5.408.2 applies, verify the required construction-document list and proper disposal/diversion rather than importing the section into residential scope.
3. **Excavated soil counted toward the 65%.** Soil is excluded from the calculation (§5.408.1.2 Exception 1). Don't let an applicant inflate diversion with soil.
4. **No compliance records.** The 65% commitment was made at submittal but the documentation required by §4.408.5 or §5.408.1.4 was not provided.
5. **Confusing CALGreen with CalRecycle.** CalRecycle is the state oversight body; CALGreen is the code. Cities enforce CALGreen via the building department, CalRecycle audits the city's overall jurisdiction-wide diversion separately.

---

## How to Cite in Corrections Output

> **FAIL -- Item 11 (65% C&D waste diversion):** No Construction Waste Management Plan is submitted. CALGreen §5.408.1 requires a written plan identifying (1) materials to be diverted, (2) source-separated vs. single-stream method, (3) named diversion facilities, and (4) weight-or-volume measurement method. Provide a CWMP committing to ≥65% diversion, naming specific California-permitted MRFs or recyclers, before permit issuance.
