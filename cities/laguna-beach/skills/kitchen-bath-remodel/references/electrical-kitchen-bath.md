# Electrical Requirements — Kitchen & Bathroom Remodel

> Reference file for the California Kitchen & Bath Remodel skill.
> Sources: CEC (Title 24, Part 3 — California Electrical Code), Energy Code (Title 24, Part 6), 2025 Edition

---

## Kitchen Electrical Requirements

### GFCI and AFCI Protection
- All kitchen countertop outlets shall be **GFCI protected** (CEC 210.8(A)(6))
- All kitchen countertop outlets shall be **AFCI protected** (CEC 210.12(A))
- Arc-Fault Circuit Interrupter protection shall be installed in a readily accessible location (CEC 210.12)

### Tamper-Resistant Receptacles
- All new receptacles shall be listed as **tamper-resistant** (CEC 406.4(D)(5))

### Countertop Outlet Requirements
- Countertops **12 inches or wider** require an outlet (CEC 210.52(C))
- Outlets required within **24 inches** of any location along the countertop (CEC 210.52(C)(1))
- Kitchen outlets positioned a maximum **20 inches above countertop** (CEC 210.52(C)(3))
- Outlets positioned no more than **6 inches** below countertop when wall-mounted below, and no more than **12 inches** below countertop when face-mounted in cabinet (CEC 210.52(C)(3))
- Appliances and sinks break up the countertop run; each segment must comply individually (CEC 210.52(C))
- Requirements include **islands, peninsulas, kitchen desktops, wet bars, and serving bars**
- A large window or lack of backsplash does NOT exempt countertop from outlet requirements
- Acceptable outlet types: drop-front cabinet face, under-cabinet plug strip, pop-up, or tombstone type receptacle (CEC 210.52(C)(2), (3))

### Island and Peninsula Outlets
- Island countertop: at least one outlet required when equal to or greater than 12 inches wide and 24 inches long
- If island gap (between ends) is less than 12 inches, outlets required at both ends
- Peninsula countertop: at least one outlet required when 12 inches or wider and 24 inches or longer (CEC 210.52(C))

### Small Appliance Circuits
- **Two (2) small appliance outlet circuits** required for kitchens, **20 amps each** (CEC 210.52(B)(1), (2))
- Circuits shall be balanced and have **no other outlets** outside the kitchen/dining area
- Outlets no more than **48 inches on center** along the wall (CEC 210.52)

### Dedicated Appliance Circuits
- Individual **dedicated circuits** required for all major appliances (CEC 210.11(C)(1); CEC 422.10(A)):
  - Dishwasher
  - Garbage disposal
  - Built-in microwave
  - Trash compactor
  - Refrigerator (if cord-connected)
- **Exception:** A single 20 amp circuit is allowed for both dishwasher and disposal when the total rated amperage is less than 10 amps (CEC 210.23). This receptacle shall be GFCI and AFCI protected.

### Appliance Cord Requirements
- **Garbage disposal:** cord and plug connected, 18 to 36 inches long (CEC 422.16(B)(1))
- **Dishwasher:** cord 18 to 36 inches long. Romex installed with a plug is NOT an approved flexible cord (CEC 422.16(B)(2))
- **Range:** maximum 6-foot listed gas flexible connector with shutoff for freestanding range

## Bathroom Electrical Requirements

### GFCI Protection
- Provide a **20 AMP GFCI protected** electrical outlet within **36 inches** of the outside edge of each bathroom sink basin (CEC 210.8(A)(1))
- Outlet shall be located on a wall or partition adjacent to the basin, or on the side or face of the basin cabinet not more than 12 inches below countertop
- For double sinks: a single duplex outlet located between the sinks will supply both sinks provided the 36-inch maximum distance is met

### Branch Circuit
- Minimum of **(1) 20 amp branch circuit** required for bathrooms (CEC 210.11(C)(3))
- Such circuits shall have **no other outlets** (bathroom receptacles only)
- This circuit **may serve more than one bathroom**

### Electrical Exclusion Zone (Tub/Shower)
- **No** cord-connected or hanging pendant, track light, or suspended ceiling fan fixtures within the zone:
  - **3 feet horizontally** from the edge of the tub or shower
  - **8 feet vertically** above the bathtub rim or shower flood level
- This is the "Designated Zone" per CEC 410.10

### Luminaires in Tub/Shower Area
- Approved luminaires located within the actual outside dimensions of the tub or shower, up to 8 feet above the rim or threshold:
  - Must be marked as suitable for **damp locations** (or wet locations if subject to shower spray)
  - Must be provided with a **solid lens**
  - Must be **GFCI protected** (CEC 410.10)

### Whirlpool/Spa Electrical
- Accessible disconnects and GFCI protection required for whirlpool (spa) pump, aerator, and heater (CEC 210.8)

## Lighting (Energy Code)

### Kitchen Lighting
- Residential kitchen lighting must meet energy efficiency standards (Energy Code 150.0(k))
- All luminaires shall meet minimum efficacy of **45 lumens per watt** (JA8 compliant) or be controlled by a vacancy sensor
- High efficacy lighting required; non-high-efficacy permitted only if controlled by vacancy sensor

### Bathroom Lighting
- Bathroom lighting shall be **high efficacy luminaires** or controlled by a **vacancy (occupancy) sensor** certified to comply with Section 150.0(k)
- Vacancy sensor must be **manual on, auto off** -- automatic on/off or devices with override switch position are NOT approved
- High efficacy lighting, incandescent lighting, and exhaust fans are required to be **switched separately**

### Recessed Lighting
- Recessed luminaires installed in an insulated ceiling shall be:
  - **IC rated** (Insulation Contact / zero clearance)
  - **AT rated** (Air Tight)
  - Sealed with gasket between ceiling and housing
- For occupancies with rated horizontal separations (floor/ceiling assembly), recessed fixtures shall be protected to the rating of the separation (1 hour) or listed for the required protection
- This typically applies to condominium construction where units are above or below other units

## Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarms

### When Triggered
- Kitchen and bathroom remodels (projects over **$1,000**) require smoke and CO alarms for the dwelling to meet current code (CRC R314 and R315)

### Smoke Alarms
- Required in all sleeping rooms
- Outside each sleeping area in the immediate vicinity of the bedrooms
- On each floor level including basements and habitable attics (not crawl spaces or uninhabitable attics)

### Carbon Monoxide Alarms
- Required in dwelling units when:
  - Fuel-burning appliances are installed, OR
  - Dwelling unit has an attached garage
  - Either condition triggers the requirement

### Interconnection
- When more than one alarm of either type is required, alarm devices shall be **interconnected** so activation of one activates all others
- In existing conditions, alarms may be **battery-operated** when repairs or alterations do not result in removal of wall and ceiling finishes, or there is no access via attic, basement, or crawlspace

### Multipurpose Alarms
- Combined smoke/CO alarms shall comply with both CRC R314 and R315 and be listed by the Office of the State Fire Marshal
