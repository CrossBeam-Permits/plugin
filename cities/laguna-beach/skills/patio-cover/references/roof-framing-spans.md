# Patio-Cover Roof Framing and Coverings

Source: 2025 California Residential Code (Title 24, Part 2.5), Appendix BF and
Chapters 8 and 9.

This reference provides state-code selection routes. Appendix BF does not
contain patio-cover rafter, beam, cantilever, connection or sheathing tables.

## Design Loads

BF105.1 requires every patio cover to sustain:

- actual dead load under R301.4;
- vertical live load of at least 10 psf;
- higher snow load where applicable; and
- minimum wind loads under R301.2.1.

Appendix BF does not create separate open-lattice/solid-roof or light/heavy
assembly classes and does not impose a separate 20-psf patio-cover category.
Calculate the actual weight of roof covering, sheathing, ceiling and other
materials. Determine wind, snow and other project parameters from the actual
site rather than generic Southern California assumptions.

## Rafter Selection

R802.4.1 measures rafter span along its horizontal projection and uses Tables
R802.4.1(1) through (8). Select a table and row only after matching all material
conditions, including:

- roof live and dead load;
- rafter spacing and horizontal span;
- lumber species and grade; and
- whether a ceiling is attached.

For other grades, species or loading conditions, R802.4.1 routes to AWC STJR.
Framing outside an applicable prescriptive path is designed under R802.2/AWC
NDS or accepted engineering.

Do not use the inherited attached/freestanding tables or “frost-zone/no-frost-
zone” labels. A local 12-foot rafter cap may be useful only when it satisfies
the complete local/external source gate below.

## Beams

R802 contains no matching statewide patio-cover beam-span table. Select beams
under R802.2/AWC NDS or an accepted engineered design using the actual:

- tributary roof width and span between supports;
- dead, live/snow, wind and other applicable loads;
- species, grade, size, ply configuration and moisture condition;
- bearing, lateral restraint and connection conditions; and
- deflection criteria.

Do not use the inherited sawn-lumber or glulam tables without a source that
expressly covers the project.

## Cantilevers, Bearing and Connections

The old six-times-depth/48-inch and backspan-divided-by-five rafter formulas are
not general CRC patio rules. R802.7.1.1 permits no more than 24 inches only for
its specifically notched cantilevered-rafter condition; it does not create a
general 24-inch patio cantilever allowance. Use an applicable design source for
the actual condition.

Apply R802.6 bearing where it governs. Under R802.11.2, individual rafters attach
to supporting wall assemblies for uplift forces from Table R802.11 or accepted
engineering, and roof-system beam connections are designed by accepted
engineering practice.

Appendix BF does not supply a universal continuous matching-depth ledger,
18-gauge clip, 3-8d toenail, blocking, hurricane-tie or hanger schedule. Exact
hardware and fasteners must come from an applicable CRC table, accepted design,
or listed-product instructions that satisfy the source gate below. Do not import
R507 deck-ledger details into a patio roof without a source that expressly
covers that application.

## Roof Sheathing

R803.2.2 limits wood structural panel roof-sheathing spans to Table
R503.2.1.1(1) or APA E30. Select the panel only after checking its rating,
thickness, edge support, load, continuity and strength-axis orientation.
Installation follows R803.2.3 and the applicable fastening row in Table
R602.3(1) or other approved attachment path.

For 3/8-inch through 3/4-inch wood structural panel roof sheathing, the listed
roof rows in Table R602.3(1) use 8d common or RSRS-01 nails at 6 inches at panel
edges and intermediate supports, subject to the table notes. Note `f` requires
4-inch spacing at gable-end roof framing and intermediate supports within
48 inches of roof edges and ridges under its high-wind, exposure and
framing-specific-gravity conditions.

Do not use the inherited 24/0–60/48 mini-table or a universal 6-inch-edge/
12-inch-field schedule in place of the complete tables and notes.

## Roof-Covering Slopes

| Covering | Minimum design slope | Additional condition | CRC authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asphalt shingles | 2:12 | From 2:12 to under 4:12, use an applicable underlayment path in Table R905.1.1(2), preserving its qualifying alternatives | R905.2.2, R905.1.1 |
| Clay or concrete tile | 2.5:12 | From 2.5:12 to under 4:12, use an applicable underlayment path in Table R905.1.1(2), preserving its qualifying alternatives | R905.3.2, R905.1.1 |
| Built-up roof | 1/4:12 | Coal-tar built-up roofing may use 1/8:12 | R905.9.1 |
| Modified bitumen | 1/4:12 | Install under R905.11 and approved manufacturer instructions | R905.11.1 |

Apply the complete R905 section and approved manufacturer instructions for the
selected covering. “Cap sheet” alone is not enough to select a code path.

## Fire Classification and Material Weight

Outside a Fire Hazard Severity Zone or Wildland-Urban Interface area,
R902.1.2 requires at least Class C fire classification for a new structure's
roof covering. In an FHSZ or WUI area, R902.1.1 also routes the roof to Part 7.
If a project is governed by the CBC rather than CRC, CBC Table 1505.1 varies the
minimum classification by construction type; it is not a universal Class B
rule.

Use actual tile and assembly weight under R301.4. The CRC does not impose a
statewide 10-psf tile cap or prohibit tile on all standard-plan patio covers.
Any local prohibition must pass the provenance gate below.

## Flashing and Drip Edges

Use the covering-specific R905 provisions and approved manufacturer
instructions. For asphalt shingles:

- R905.2.8.1 governs base and cap flashing at wall and roof intersections; and
- R905.2.8.5 requires drip edges at eaves and rake edges and supplies their
  material, overlap, projection and underlayment relationship.

Other coverings use their applicable R905 section. Do not apply a universal
caulking, wall-flashing, drip-edge or ledger-boundary-nailing recipe to every
roof covering.

## Local and External Source Gate

An inherited rafter, beam, cantilever, ledger, clip, fastener, blocking,
sheathing, tile or boundary-nailing detail may be retained only with:

1. named jurisdiction, agency, manufacturer or standards publisher;
2. current document, product-instruction or standard title and edition or
   effective date;
3. exact page, table, section or detail locator;
4. geographic and project-type scope; and
5. confirmation that the source remains current.

Without all five, flag the missing design source rather than applying the
number as a statewide rule.

## Common Review Findings

1. **Rafter inputs incomplete:** Obtain the actual loads, span, spacing, species,
   grade and ceiling condition before selecting an R802.4.1 table row.
2. **Beam source missing:** Require an applicable NDS/accepted design or fully
   provenanced standard plan.
3. **Uplift path incomplete:** Verify the R802.11.2 rafter and beam connection
   capacity for the actual project.
4. **Sheathing table notes omitted:** Check the complete R503.2.1.1(1) and
   R602.3(1) conditions, including high-wind fastening where triggered.
5. **Covering path incomplete:** Verify slope, underlayment, fastening, flashing,
   fire classification and manufacturer instructions for the selected covering.
