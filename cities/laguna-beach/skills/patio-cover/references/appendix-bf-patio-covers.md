# CRC Appendix BF — Patio Covers

Source: 2025 California Residential Code (Title 24, Part 2.5), Appendix BF.

Appendix BF supplies scope, use, definition, wall/opening, height, design-load
and limited slab-support rules. It does **not** contain statewide open-lattice
and solid-roof classes or footing, post, brace, beam, rafter, connection,
material, inspection, or engineering-trigger tables.

## Scope, Definition, Use and Height

### BF101.1 and BF102.1

A patio cover is a structure with open or glazed walls used for recreational,
outdoor living purposes associated with a dwelling unit. Patio covers within
Appendix BF must conform to BF101 through BF106.

### BF101.2 — Permitted uses

An attached or detached patio cover may be used only for recreational outdoor
living. It cannot be used as a:

- carport;
- garage;
- storage room; or
- habitable room.

### BF104.1 — Height

The structure is limited to one story and no more than 12 feet in height. A
structure above that limit is outside Appendix BF and must be routed to the
applicable alternate code path and project-specific design documentation
accepted by the authority having jurisdiction. Appendix BF does not call this
an engineering exception.

## Exterior Walls, Openings and Egress

### BF103.1 — Enclosure walls

Enclosure walls may use any configuration if the open or glazed area of the
longer wall and one additional wall is at least 65 percent of the area below
6 feet 8 inches of each wall, measured from the floor.

Openings may be enclosed with:

1. insect screening;
2. approved translucent or transparent plastic no more than 0.125 inch thick;
3. glass conforming to R324; or
4. any combination of those materials.

Appendix BF therefore does not prohibit framed glazing that complies with the
applicable glass and wall-opening provisions. If a BF103 condition is not met,
the design is outside that Appendix BF enclosure provision; the failed
condition alone does not automatically classify the project as a habitable room
addition.

### BF103.2 — Light, ventilation and emergency egress

Required exterior light and ventilation openings must remain unenclosed when
they serve sleeping-room emergency escape or rescue openings. If exterior
openings serve as an exit from the dwelling unit, the patio structure must
remain unenclosed or provide exits conforming to R318.

## Structural Provisions

### BF105.1 — Design loads

Every patio cover must be designed for:

- actual dead loads;
- vertical live load of at least 10 psf;
- snow load where it exceeds that minimum; and
- the minimum wind loads in R301.2.1.

There is no separate Appendix BF 20-psf “solid roof” category. Roof-covering,
ceiling and other material weights belong in the actual dead load.

### BF105.2 — Limited slab-without-footings option

In an area with zero frostline depth under Table R301.2, columns may be
supported on a slab-on-grade without footings only when:

- the slab conforms to R506;
- it is at least 3.5 inches thick; and
- total live plus dead load does not exceed 750 pounds per column.

Otherwise, size column footings from tributary load and allowable soil pressure
under R403.1.1. Appendix BF contains no 12-by-12 or 18-by-18 footing-area table,
circular-equivalence shortcut, or universal reinforcement recipe.

## Materials, Framing and Connections

Use the main CRC provisions and the actual project conditions:

- actual material weights under R301.4;
- concrete strength for the applicable exposure under R402.2;
- R304 decay protection where its conditions are present;
- grade-marked load-supporting sawn lumber under R802.1;
- rafter-table selection under R802.4.1 using the matching span, load, spacing,
  species, grade, dead-load and ceiling conditions;
- R802.2/AWC NDS or accepted engineering for framing outside an applicable
  prescriptive path; and
- connections and a lateral-force-resisting system designed to transfer the
  applicable gravity, wind and uplift loads.

Do not infer a statewide Douglas Fir-Larch No. 2 rule, 3,000-psi concrete rule,
6-foot-8-inch beam-clearance rule, knee-brace schedule, or fixed engineering
trigger from Appendix BF.

## Fire Hazard Severity Zone and WUI Routing

R902.1.1 routes roofing in a Fire Hazard Severity Zone or Wildland-Urban
Interface area to Part 7 in addition to the applicable roof provisions. Outside
those areas, R902.1.2 requires at least Class C roof covering for a new
structure. Appendix BF does not categorically prohibit wood lattice or require
noncombustible construction.

## Local Standard Plans and Inspections

A local standard plan may provide useful prescriptive details, and a local
inspection authority may publish a sequence. Keep either as a local rule only
when the source supplies all five provenance elements:

1. named jurisdiction or agency;
2. document title and edition or effective date;
3. exact page, table or detail locator;
4. geographic and project-type scope; and
5. confirmation that the source remains current.

Without all five, do not use inherited footing, post, brace, span, material,
inspection, tile, WUI or engineering recipes as operative requirements.

## Common Review Findings

1. **Use outside BF101.2:** Vehicle parking, storage, garage or habitable use is
   not permitted under Appendix BF.
2. **BF103.1 area not demonstrated:** Obtain dimensions for the longer wall and
   one additional wall and calculate the open or glazed area below 6 feet
   8 inches.
3. **Required egress affected:** Apply BF103.2 and R318 rather than relying only
   on the wall-area calculation.
4. **Height above 12 feet:** Route outside Appendix BF; do not invent an
   Appendix BF engineering exception.
5. **Incomplete load path:** Verify actual dead load, live/snow load, wind,
   framing, connections and foundation through an applicable code or approved
   design source.
