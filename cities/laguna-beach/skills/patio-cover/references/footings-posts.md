# Patio-Cover Footings, Posts and Connections

Source: 2025 California Residential Code (Title 24, Part 2.5), Appendix BF and
Sections R304, R402, R403 and R802.

Appendix BF provides design loads and one narrow slab-without-footings option.
It does not provide statewide footing-area, post-size, post-base, post-cap,
knee-brace or beam-cantilever schedules.

## Foundation Decision Path

### 1. Establish the column load

Under BF105.1, include:

- actual dead load;
- at least 10 psf vertical live load;
- higher snow load where applicable; and
- the applicable R301.2.1 wind load and resulting load path.

Do not split the foundation design into unsupported statewide “open 10 psf” and
“solid 20 psf” categories.

### 2. Check the BF105.2 slab-only option

In an area with zero frostline depth under Table R301.2, a column may bear on a
slab-on-grade without a separate footing only if:

1. the slab complies with R506;
2. the slab is at least 3.5 inches thick; and
3. total live plus dead load does not exceed 750 pounds per column.

This is not a footing-size table. If every condition is not demonstrated,
continue to the applicable footing design path.

### 3. Size and detail the footing under R403

R403.1.1 sizes footings supporting piers and columns from tributary load and
allowable soil pressure under Table R401.4.1(1). Apply the minimum width,
thickness, projection and other R403 conditions that govern the selected
footing. Where R403.1.4 applies, an exterior footing is at least 12 inches below
undisturbed ground and must also meet the applicable frost-protection path.

Select concrete strength from Table R402.2 for the actual location and
weathering exposure. Apply R403 seismic reinforcement only when the applicable
provision and construction condition require it. R403.1.3.6 permits isolated
plain concrete footings in its stated qualifying construction.

The CRC does not support the inherited patio-cover tables that paired 12-by-12
or 18-by-18 footings with fixed tributary areas, assumed 1,500-psf soil,
universal 3,000-psi concrete, or a universal one-#4-each-direction recipe.

## Posts and Decay Protection

### Structural sizing

Size each post for the project loads, unsupported length, material, grade,
bracing and end conditions. Load-supporting sawn lumber must be grade marked
under R802.1. Do not import the deck-specific R507.4 post table as a
patio-cover post table or treat 4-by-4/4-by-6 as universal patio minima.

BF104.1 limits the **entire patio-cover structure** to one story and 12 feet; it
does not establish a separate 12-foot prescriptive post-height table or a
6-foot-8-inch beam-clearance rule.

### R304 decay-protection triggers

Use naturally durable or AWPA U1 preservative-treated wood when R304.1 requires
it, including these distinct conditions:

- wood columns closer than 8 inches to exposed ground;
- exterior siding, sheathing or wall framing closer than 6 inches to ground or
  2 inches above weather-exposed concrete steps, porch slabs, patio slabs or
  similar horizontal surfaces;
- structural supports exposed to weather without an adequate roof, eave,
  overhang or other covering that prevents moisture accumulation; and
- ground-contact or qualifying concrete-embedded wood under R304.1.2.

Field-treat cuts, notches and drilled holes under R304.1.1. Required treated
wood bears the R304.2 quality mark, and fasteners/connectors in treated wood
comply with R304.3.

R304.1.2 permits properly approved ground-contact-treated wood in its stated
embedded conditions. The CRC therefore does not support a categorical patio
rule that every post must use a one-inch-standoff product or that direct
embedment is always prohibited.

## Post, Beam and Foundation Connections

The approved design must transfer applicable gravity, lateral and uplift loads
from roof framing through beams, posts and connections to the foundation. Exact
hardware, fasteners, bolts, embedment and installation details must come from:

- an applicable CRC prescriptive provision;
- a listed product's approved installation instructions;
- an accepted engineered design; or
- a fully provenanced local standard plan that expressly covers the project.

Appendix BF does not establish a universal named-product post base, one-half-
inch anchor, 12-gauge strap, one-half-inch machine-bolt, filled-fastener-hole,
or “toenailing always fails” schedule.

## Lateral System and Knee Braces

Provide a lateral-force-resisting system sized for the applicable wind and
other project loads. Knee braces may be one component of an approved system,
but Appendix BF does not require a brace at every post or supply statewide
4-by-4/4-by-6 sizes, lag-bolt recipes, or 60/35/22-square-foot tributary limits.

## Beam Cantilevers and Property Setbacks

Do not use the inherited one-third-of-span/48-inch beam cantilever or 3-foot
property-line setback as a statewide patio rule. Size a cantilever under an
applicable framing/design source. A setback requires the governing land-use or
building source for the project location.

## Local and External Source Gate

A local standard-plan or external footing, post, base, cap, strap, brace,
cantilever, slab-drainage, setback or connection recipe—including listed-product
instructions—may be retained only with:

1. named jurisdiction, agency, manufacturer or standards publisher;
2. current document, product-instruction or standard title and edition or
   effective date;
3. exact page, table, section or detail locator;
4. geographic and project-type scope; and
5. confirmation that the source remains current.

Without all five, flag the missing design source rather than applying an
inherited numerical recipe.

## Review Output

- **PASS:** The load, soil, exposure, member, decay and connection inputs are
  demonstrated against an applicable source.
- **FAIL:** A demonstrated project condition contradicts an applicable cited
  requirement.
- **FLAG:** A load, soil value, design detail, product instruction or local
  source is missing.
- **N/A:** A conditional path, such as BF105.2 slab-only support, is not used.
