# Coastal Bluffs, Hazards, and Erosion (PRC §30235, §30236, §30253, §30270)

## Bluff and Cliff Development — §30253(a)-(b)

New development shall:
- **Minimize risks** to life and property in areas of high geologic, flood, and fire hazard
- **Assure stability and structural integrity**, and neither create nor contribute significantly to erosion, geologic instability, or destruction of the site or surrounding area
- **Not require construction of protective devices** that would substantially alter natural landforms along bluffs and cliffs

> This is a critical provision: if a new project would need a seawall or bluff retention to be safe, it may be denied under §30253(b) because the protective device itself would alter natural landforms.

## Shoreline Protective Devices — §30235

Revetments, breakwaters, groins, harbor channels, seawalls, cliff retaining walls, and other shoreline-altering construction shall be permitted **when:**
1. Required to serve coastal-dependent uses, OR
2. Required to protect **existing structures** or public beaches in danger from erosion

**AND** designed to eliminate or mitigate adverse impacts on local shoreline sand supply.

> Key tension: §30235 allows protective devices for existing structures, but §30253 prohibits new development that would require them. New development on a bluff that would later need a seawall = problem.

Existing marine structures causing water stagnation/pollution/fishkills should be phased out or upgraded where feasible.

## Stream and River Alterations — §30236

Channelizations, dams, or other substantial alterations of rivers and streams:
- Must incorporate **best mitigation measures feasible**
- Limited to:
  1. Necessary water supply projects
  2. Flood control where **no other method** protects existing structures in the flood plain (and necessary for public safety or existing development)
  3. Developments where primary function is fish/wildlife habitat improvement

## Sea Level Rise — §30270

The Commission shall take into account the effects of **sea level rise** in coastal resources planning and management policies and activities to identify, assess, and to the extent feasible, avoid and mitigate adverse effects.

> This 2022 addition (SB 1) codifies the Commission's existing practice of requiring sea level rise analysis in CDP reviews for coastal bluff and shoreline projects.

## Application to Development Review

For development on or near bluffs, in hazard areas, or requesting shoreline protection:

1. **New development on a bluff:** Will it need protective devices now or in the future? If yes → likely denied under §30253(b).
2. **Existing structure needing protection:** Does it qualify under §30235? Must be an existing (not new) structure in danger from erosion. Must mitigate sand supply impacts.
3. **Stream/river work:** Is it one of the three permitted categories under §30236?
4. **Sea level rise:** Has the applicant addressed projected sea level rise impacts on the project and surrounding area?
5. **Geologic hazard:** Is there a geotechnical report demonstrating the project minimizes risks and assures structural stability?
