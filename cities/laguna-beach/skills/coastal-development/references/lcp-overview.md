# Local Coastal Programs (LCPs)

## What Is an LCP?

An LCP is a local government's package of land use plans, zoning ordinances, zoning maps, and other implementing actions that, together, implement the Coastal Act at the local level. (§30108.6)

Each local government with territory in the coastal zone must prepare an LCP. (§30500(a))

## LCP Components

An LCP consists of two parts:
1. **Land Use Plan (LUP)** — the policy document (like a general plan element for the coastal zone)
2. **Implementation Plan (IP)** — the zoning ordinances, regulations, and other implementing actions

Both must be certified by the Coastal Commission.

## Certification Process

### Preparation — §30500-30504
- Local government prepares the LCP in full consultation with the Commission and public participation
- Must include a specific **public access component** (§30500(a))
- Commission establishes common methodology and procedures (§30501)

### Submittal and Review — 14 CCR §13518-13520
- Local government submits by resolution
- Commission staff reviews for completeness
- Public hearing scheduled

### Commission Action — §30512-30514
The Commission certifies the LCP (or portions thereof) if it finds the LCP meets the requirements of and conforms with the policies of Chapter 3.

- **Land Use Plan:** Commission may certify, certify with modifications, or deny
- **Implementation Plan:** Commission certifies if adequate to carry out the certified LUP

### Transfer of Development-Review Authority — §30519
After the LCP or applicable portion is certified **and all affected implementing actions have become effective**, development-review authority transfers to the implementing local government. Earlier delegation can occur under §30600.5 during its certified-land-use-plan/interim period. The Commission retains:
- **Appeal jurisdiction** over specified areas (see appealable-areas.md)
- **Permit jurisdiction** described in §30519(b), including development on tidelands, submerged lands, and public-trust lands, subject to that subdivision's port/harbor exception and other stated rules

## Interim Delegation — §30600.5

After certification of a land use plan and before the complete LCP transfer, §30600.5 can delegate permit authority. Under §30600.5(c), the local government—or Commission on appeal—uses conformity with the certified land use plan. Section 30600.5(d) routes local appeals under §30602.

## Post-LCP Transfer: How CDPs Work

Once the §30519(a) certification-and-effective-implementing-actions conditions are met:
- **Local government generally issues CDPs** under its certified LCP, subject to Commission-retained permit jurisdiction under §30519(b)
- Standard of review under §30604(b): conformity with the certified LCP
- A qualified appellant may appeal local actions in the §30603 categories; the Commission hears an appeal if the substantial-issue gate is met
- On appeal, §30604(b) continues to require certified-LCP conformity. For development between the nearest public road and the sea or the shoreline of any body of water in the coastal zone, §30604(c) additionally requires a specific finding of conformity with Chapter 3's public-access and public-recreation policies.

## LCP Amendments — §30515

A certified LCP may be amended by the local government, but no amendment takes effect until certified by the Commission.

### Types of Amendments — 14 CCR §13554-13555
- **Minor amendments:** Changes that do not raise significant conformity issues — streamlined review
- **Major amendments:** Substantive changes to policies, zoning, or land use designations — full review

### Amendment Standard
An LCP amendment must be found consistent with the Coastal Act (Chapter 3 policies). The Commission may:
- Certify as submitted
- Certify with suggested modifications
- Deny

## ADU Guidance for LCPs — §30500.5

By July 1, 2026, the Commission (with HCD) must develop guidance for local governments to facilitate LCP amendments that clarify and simplify the permitting process for ADUs and JADUs within the coastal zone. (Added by SB 1077, effective January 1, 2025.)

## Application to Development Review

1. **Is the LCP or applicable portion certified, and are all affected implementing actions effective? Has authority instead been delegated under §30600.5?** These facts determine the issuer and review path.
2. If the §30519(a) conditions are met: the local government generally issues CDPs under the LCP, subject to retained Commission jurisdiction. Review standard = conformity with the certified LCP.
3. If NOT certified: determine whether the local government is exercising §30600(b), has delegated authority under §30600.5, or the Commission is the issuer under §30600(c); also test the additional Commission-permit categories in §30601. The §30604(a) findings apply before LCP certification.
4. Any LCP amendment must be certified by the Commission before it takes effect.
