# Marine Resources, ESHA, and Wetlands (PRC §30230-30240)

## Environmentally Sensitive Habitat Areas (ESHA) — §30240

**This is one of the most restrictive Coastal Act provisions.**

**(a)** Environmentally sensitive habitat areas shall be protected against any significant disruption of habitat values, and **only uses dependent on those resources shall be allowed** within those areas.

**(b)** Development **adjacent to** ESHA and parks/recreation areas shall be sited and designed to prevent impacts that would significantly degrade those areas, and shall be compatible with their continuance.

> ESHA includes but is not limited to: wetlands, coastal sage scrub, riparian corridors, tide pools, kelp beds, sea grass beds, nesting/roosting sites, and rare plant communities.

## Marine Resources — §30230-30232

### General Protection (§30230)
Marine resources shall be maintained, enhanced, and where feasible restored. Special protection for areas and species of special biological or economic significance. Uses must sustain biological productivity and maintain healthy populations.

### Water Quality (§30231)
Biological productivity and quality of coastal waters, streams, wetlands, estuaries, and lakes shall be maintained and where feasible restored through:
- Minimizing adverse effects of wastewater discharges and entrainment
- Controlling runoff
- Preventing depletion of groundwater supplies
- Preventing substantial interference with surface water flow
- Encouraging wastewater reclamation
- Maintaining natural vegetation buffer areas protecting riparian habitats
- Minimizing alteration of natural streams

### Hazardous Materials (§30232)
Protection against spillage of crude oil, gas, petroleum products, or hazardous substances. Effective containment and cleanup facilities required.

## Wetlands and Fill — §30233

**Diking, filling, or dredging** of open coastal waters, wetlands, estuaries, and lakes is permitted ONLY where:
1. No feasible less environmentally damaging alternative exists
2. Feasible mitigation measures are provided
3. Limited to these enumerated uses:
   - New/expanded port, energy, and coastal-dependent industrial facilities
   - Maintaining existing navigation channel depths
   - New/expanded boating facilities in open coastal waters (not wetlands)
   - Incidental public service (burying cables/pipes, pier inspection)
   - Mineral extraction (not in ESHA)
   - Restoration purposes
   - Nature study, aquaculture, or similar resource-dependent activities

**Coastal wetlands** identified by CDFW: alterations limited to very minor incidental public facilities, restorative measures, nature study, and limited commercial fishing facilities.

## Shoreline Protection — §30235

Revetments, breakwaters, groins, seawalls, cliff retaining walls, and similar construction that alters natural shoreline processes shall be permitted **when required to:**
- Serve coastal-dependent uses, OR
- Protect existing structures or public beaches in danger from erosion

**AND** designed to eliminate or mitigate adverse impacts on local shoreline sand supply.

## Streams and Rivers — §30236

Channelizations, dams, or other substantial alterations limited to:
1. Necessary water supply projects
2. Flood control where no other method protects existing structures (and necessary for public safety or existing development)
3. Developments where primary function is fish/wildlife habitat improvement

Must incorporate best mitigation measures feasible.

## Application to Development Review

1. **Is the site in or adjacent to ESHA?** If yes, only resource-dependent uses allowed within ESHA; development adjacent must be sited/designed to prevent degradation.
2. **Will the project affect coastal waters, streams, or wetlands?** Water quality must be maintained per §30231.
3. **Does the project involve fill in wetlands?** Must meet the strict 3-part test of §30233.
4. **Is shoreline protection proposed?** Only allowed to protect existing structures or coastal-dependent uses, with sand supply mitigation.
