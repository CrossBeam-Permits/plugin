# Categorical Exclusions (PRC §30610(e), 14 CCR §13240-13249)

## What Are Categorical Exclusions?

Under PRC §30610(e), the Commission may, after public hearing and by **two-thirds vote** of its appointed members, exclude from CDP requirements:
- Any **category of development**, OR
- Any category of development **within a specifically defined geographic area**

The Commission must find that there is **no potential for any significant adverse effect**, either individually or cumulatively, on coastal resources or on public access to or along the coast.

**Pre-LCP certification additional requirement:** The exclusion must not impair the local government's ability to prepare an LCP.

## How Exclusions Work — 14 CCR §13240-13249

### Request Process — §13241
Any person or organization may request a categorical exclusion. The request must describe:
- The category of development to be excluded
- The geographic area (if geographically limited)
- Basis for finding no potential adverse effect

### Commission Action — §13243
The Commission holds a public hearing and may:
- Grant the exclusion (by 2/3 vote)
- Deny the exclusion
- Modify the scope of the exclusion

### Order Content — §13244
The exclusion order must specify:
- The category of development excluded
- The geographic area to which it applies
- Any conditions or limitations
- Findings supporting the no-adverse-effect determination

### Effect — §13247
Development within the scope of a categorical exclusion order does not require a CDP. However:
- Local governments must still **notify the Commission** of development approvals within the exclusion area (§13248)
- Other permits (building, grading, etc.) are still required

### Termination — §13249
An exclusion order may be terminated by the Commission if:
- Changed circumstances create a potential for significant adverse effects
- The exclusion is no longer consistent with the Coastal Act
- An LCP is certified that addresses the same development category

## De Minimis Waiver (Alternative) — PRC §30624.7

Separately from categorical exclusions, the executive director may waive individual CDP requirements for development that is **de minimis** — involving no potential for any adverse effect on coastal resources and consistent with Chapter 3 policies.

Key differences from categorical exclusions:
- De minimis waivers are **case-by-case** (not category-wide)
- Issued by **executive director** (not full Commission vote)
- Can be blocked by **1/3 of Commission members** at next meeting

## Application to Development Review

1. Is there an active categorical exclusion order covering this type of development in this area? (Check with local planning department or Commission)
2. If excluded: no CDP required, but building permit still needed and local government must notify Commission of approval
3. If not excluded: check other §30610 exemptions or proceed to CDP application
4. For individual minor projects: consider requesting a de minimis waiver under §30624.7

> Categorical exclusions are relatively rare. Most development in the coastal zone proceeds through standard CDP or exemption pathways. The most commonly used alternative is the de minimis waiver.
