# Public Access Policies (PRC §30210-30214)

These policies implement Article X, Section 4 of the California Constitution, which guarantees public access to navigable waters.

## Core Rule

**Maximum access and recreational opportunities shall be provided for all the people** consistent with public safety needs and the need to protect public rights, rights of private property owners, and natural resource areas from overuse. (§30210)

## Key Requirements

### No Interference with Existing Access (§30211)
Development shall not interfere with the public's right of access to the sea where acquired through use or legislative authorization, including dry sand and rocky coastal beaches to the first line of terrestrial vegetation.

### New Development Must Provide Access (§30212)
Public access from the nearest public roadway to the shoreline and along the coast shall be provided in new development projects, **except where:**
1. Inconsistent with public safety, military security, or fragile coastal resource protection
2. Adequate access exists nearby
3. Agriculture would be adversely affected

**"New development" does NOT include:**
- Replacement structures under §30610(g)
- Demolition/reconstruction of SFR if within 10% of former floor area, height, and bulk, and sited in same location
- Improvements that don't change use intensity, increase floor area/height/bulk by more than 10%, block access, or encroach seaward
- Seawall repair/reconstruction (if not seaward of former location)

### Lower Cost Facilities (§30213)
Lower cost visitor and recreational facilities shall be protected, encouraged, and where feasible provided. Developments providing public recreational opportunities are preferred.

The Commission shall NOT:
- Require fixed overnight room rental prices for private hotels/motels
- Establish methods to identify low/moderate income persons for room rental eligibility

### Balancing Test (§30214)
Public access policies must be implemented considering:
- Topographic and geologic site characteristics
- Site capacity and sustainable intensity of use
- Appropriateness of limiting access to right to pass and repass (fragile resources, residential proximity)
- Need for access area management (privacy, aesthetics, litter)

The Legislature intends these policies be carried out balancing individual property rights with the public's constitutional right of access.

## Application to Development Review

When reviewing a CDP for development near the shoreline:
1. Does the project maintain existing public access?
2. If new development, does it provide public access (or qualify for an exception)?
3. Are lower-cost visitor facilities protected?
4. Is the access provision appropriate given site characteristics?
