# When a CDP Is Required (PRC §30600, §30610)

## Default Rule — §30600

**Any person wishing to perform or undertake development in the coastal zone shall obtain a coastal development permit**, subject to the emergency-project exceptions in §30600(e) and the exemption, exclusion, and effective-waiver routes below.

"Development" is broadly defined (PRC §30106) and includes: construction, grading, land division, change of use, and any activity that changes the intensity of use or access to coastal waters.

## Exemptions — §30610

No CDP required for:

### (a) SFR Improvements
Improvements to existing single-family residences — **BUT** the Commission has specified by regulation (14 CCR §13250) classes that DO require a permit:

Per **14 CCR §13250(b)**, a permit IS required for SFR improvements:
1. If the structure/improvement is on a **beach, wetland, seaward of mean high tide, in ESHA, in a highly scenic area (per certified LUP), or within 50 feet of a coastal bluff edge**
2. Any significant alteration of landforms on a beach, wetland, sand dune, within 50 ft of bluff edge, or in ESHA
3. Expansion/construction of water wells or septic systems
4. Between the sea and first public road (or within 300 ft of the beach/mean high tide, whichever is greater), or in a significant scenic resource area designated by the Commission or a regional commission: an improvement that increases internal floor area by **10% or more** (including an additional increase of 10% or less after a prior §30610(a) improvement), increases height by **more than 10%**, or constructs a significant non-attached structure
5. In an area that the Commission or a regional commission has declared by public-hearing resolution to have a critically short water supply that must be maintained for coastal resources or public recreational use: specified major water-using development not essential to residential use, including pools and landscaping irrigation systems
6. Where the original permit required future permits for improvements

The executive director may waive the permit requirement even for (b) categories if the impact is insignificant — but 3 commissioners can object to restore the requirement. (§13250(c))

### (b) Non-SFR Structure Improvements
Improvements to structures other than SFR or public works — unless they involve adverse environmental effect, affect public access, or change use contrary to Coastal Act policies.

Per **14 CCR §13253(b)**, test every listed permit-required class directly; it is not the same list as the SFR rule in §13250. The classes cover the locations specified in §13253(b)(1) (including its wetland, stream, and lake scope), significant landform alteration, well/septic expansion or construction, the cumulative floor-area and height tests in the shoreline band, critically short water-supply areas, an original permit's future-permit condition, changes in intensity of use, and specified conversions to fee ownership or long-term leasehold.

### (c) Maintenance Dredging
Existing navigation channels, with Army Corps permit.

### (d) Repair/Maintenance
Activities that don't enlarge or expand the object of repair — unless the Commission has specified "extraordinary methods" by regulation.

Per **14 CCR §13252**, a permit IS required for repair/maintenance involving:
- An extraordinary method enumerated in §13252(a), applying its exact shoreline-work, material/equipment, dredging, and location conditions rather than a generic "sensitive area" shortcut
- Replacement of 50 percent or more of an SFR, seawall, revetment, bluff retaining wall, breakwater, groin, or other structure, unless destroyed by natural disaster; §13252(b) treats that work as a replacement structure rather than repair/maintenance

Also apply §13252(c)-(e), including the stated routine-government/utility discretion and the executive-director waiver process, where relevant.

### (e) Categorical Exclusions
Development categories excluded by two-thirds vote of the Commission after finding no potential for significant adverse effect on coastal resources or public access. If the exclusion precedes certification of the applicable LCP, the Commission must also find that it will not impair the local government's ability to prepare the LCP.

### (f) Utility Connections
Installation, testing, placement in service, or replacement of a necessary utility connection between an existing service facility and development approved under the Coastal Act. The Commission may impose reasonable conditions where necessary to mitigate adverse impacts on coastal resources, including scenic resources.

### (g) Disaster Replacement
Replacement of a structure other than a public-works facility destroyed by disaster — must conform to existing zoning, retain the same use and location, and not exceed the former floor area, height, or bulk by more than 10 percent.

### (h) Timeshare Conversions
Conversion of an existing multi-unit residential structure to a time-share project, estate, or use. An improvement that is otherwise exempt remains exempt despite being made in connection with the conversion. Division into condominiums is not a time-share conversion for this exemption.

### (i) Temporary Events
Events the executive director finds are temporary with no significant adverse impact per adopted guidelines.

An exclusion or waiver under §30610(i) does not diminish or prevent the Commission from asserting CDP jurisdiction over a temporary event when the Commission determines that jurisdiction is necessary to implement Chapter 3.

## De Minimis Waiver — §30624.7

The executive director may waive CDP requirements for development that:
- Involves no potential for any adverse effect (individually or cumulatively) on coastal resources
- Will be consistent with Chapter 3 policies

Waiver reported to Commission at next meeting; 1/3 of members can block it.

## Application to Development Review

1. Is there development (construction, grading, change of use) in the Coastal Zone? → Start with the §30600 default and test §30600(e)'s emergency-project exceptions.
2. Check every applicable §30610 exemption/exclusion and implementing regulation; do not decide from a category label alone.
3. If SFR improvement: check 14 CCR §13250(b) exceptions — is it in a sensitive location or exceeding thresholds?
4. If repair/maintenance: confirm there is no addition, enlargement, or expansion, then check 14 CCR §13252 for regulated methods and replacement work.
5. Check any applicable Commission categorical-exclusion order and its geographic/project scope.
6. If exempt or excluded, document the exact basis. If not, proceed to CDP application.
7. For a project with no potential for adverse effects: test the de minimis-waiver process under §30624.7 and 14 CCR §§13238-13238.2; a waiver must become effective before relying on it.
