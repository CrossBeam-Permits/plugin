# Laguna Beach Sign Regulations (LBMC Chapter 25.54)

## Section 25.54.002: Intent and purpose

The intent and purpose of this chapter is to establish sign regulations that:
(A) Protect and promote the general public welfare and artistic village character of the community;
(B) Implement community design criteria consistent with the general plan;
(C) Preserve and enhance the community's appearance by regulating the design, character, location, type, quality, scale, color, illumination and maintenance of signs;
(D) Promote signs that clearly identify uses and premises without confusion;
(E) Encourage creative, artistic and well-designed signs that contribute to the visual environment of Laguna Beach, express local character and develop a distinctive image;
(F) Encourage signs that are responsive to the aesthetics and character of their location and building architecture;
(G) Recognize that many businesses in Laguna Beach are small, non-franchise establishments that depend on their sign's clear communication to draw customers, and that sign regulations should accommodate this need;
(H) Promote traffic and pedestrian safety.

---

## Section 25.54.004: Applicability and effect

Signs shall not be placed or maintained in Laguna Beach unless they conform to the standards and procedures of this chapter. The effect of this chapter is as follows:
(A) To establish a permit system for signs in various zoning districts;
(B) To provide for temporary signs in limited circumstances;
(C) To prohibit all signs not expressly permitted by this chapter.

## Section 25.54.006: Definitions (key definitions)

- **"Abate"** -- to put an end to and to physically remove. Discontinuance of use without removal shall not constitute abatement.
- **"Advertising sign"** -- a sign which contains the name of a person other than the occupant of the property on which the sign is located, or contains the name of a product not sold on the premises or a service not rendered on the premises.
- **"Alley sign"** -- a sign within or adjacent to an alley.
- **"Animated sign"** -- any sign that uses movement, lighting or special materials to depict action. This includes motor-activated elements, any type of electronically controllable changeable copy signs including LED and LCD signs, and open flames.
- **"Approval authority"** -- either the director of community development for signs eligible for administrative review per Section 25.54.018(E), or the planning commission.
- **"Banner"** -- any temporary sign of lightweight fabric, plastic, paper or other non-rigid material.
- **"Beacon"** -- a stationary or revolving light which flashes or projects illumination, single color or multicolored, in any direction.
- **"Blade or projecting sign"** -- a sign, other than a wall sign, which is attached to or projects from a building or structure.
- **"Building marker"** -- a sign cut into or attached flat against a masonry surface indicating the name of the building and/or the date of construction.
- **"Bulletin board"** -- a sign consisting of a rectangular surface placed flat against or perpendicular to a wall used for the posting of temporary messages.
- **"Canopy or awning sign"** -- a sign or letters affixed to or integral with the face of a canopy or awning.
- **"Changeable display sign"** -- a sign which is designed so that characters, letters, or illustrations can be changed or rearranged by hand without altering the face or surface of the sign.
- **"Commercial message"** -- a message advertising a product, commodity, business name, franchise name, or service available or sold at the location where the sign is displayed.
- **"Construction sign"** -- a temporary on-site sign identifying the project name, architect, engineer, contractor, financing entity, and/or future tenants for a building under construction or approved for construction.
- **"Copy"** -- the word(s), letter(s), number(s), figure(s), design(s), symbol(s), or graphics on a sign surface.
- **"Digital sign"** -- a sign that electronically changes the fixed display screen composed of electronically illuminated elements, including LED, LCD, plasma or other similar technology.
- **"Directory sign"** -- a sign listing the names of persons or businesses within a building or complex.
- **"Downtown area"** -- the area subject to the Downtown Specific Plan.
- **"Electric sign"** -- a sign or sign structure in which electrical wiring, connections, or fixtures are used.
- **"Electronic message center (EMC)"** -- a type of sign whose alphabetic, pictographic, or symbolic informational content can be changed by electronic process.
- **"Externally illuminated sign"** -- a sign illuminated by an external artificial light source not contained within the sign structure.
- **"Facade"** -- the exterior face of a building. The term "facade" does not include roof areas.
- **"Freestanding sign"** -- a sign supported by one or more columns, uprights, or braces from the ground, or a monument-style sign on a solid base.
- **"Identification sign"** -- a sign displaying only the name and/or address of a person occupying a residential dwelling or the name of an establishment located on the premises.
- **"Incidental sign"** -- a sign affixed to a wall or structure that conveys information about an on-site activity, but does not contain a commercial message (e.g., "entrance," "exit," "restrooms," "open," "closed").
- **"Interior building sign"** -- a sign located within the interior of a building.
- **"Internally illuminated sign"** -- a sign directly lit from an interior light source contained within the sign structure through a transparent or translucent surface.
- **"Luminance"** -- the light that is reflected from a surface toward the eyes, measured in footlamberts (fL), candelas per meter squared (cd/m2 or nit) or candelas per square foot (cd/sq. ft). One footlambert equals 3.4263 candelas per meter squared.
- **"Luminous tube sign"** -- a sign which consists of or is illuminated by exposed electrically-charged gas-filled tubing, such as neon or argon signs, or by fiber optics.
- **"Marquee"** -- a permanent structure projecting beyond a building wall at an entrance to a building or extending along and projecting beyond the wall, generally designed to provide protection from weather.
- **"Marquee sign"** -- a sign painted on, attached to, or installed in or on a marquee.
- **"Master sign program"** -- a coordinated sign program for a commercial center, multi-tenant building, shopping center, or other multi-use development.
- **"Menu A-frame"** -- a portable, double-sided sign used by a restaurant for displaying menus.
- **"Menu board"** -- a sign permanently installed on an exterior wall of a restaurant for displaying menus, including drive-through menu boards.
- **"Monument sign"** -- a type of freestanding sign that is mounted on a solid base or pedestal.
- **"Mural"** -- a hand-painted, tile, or bas-relief picture or scene applied to the exterior wall of a building, which does not contain a commercial message.
- **"Nonconforming sign"** -- a legal sign that does not conform to the provisions of this chapter.
- **"Open house sign"** -- a temporary off-site sign advertising an open house for inspection of real property.
- **"Pole sign"** -- a freestanding sign supported by one or more structural members placed in the ground. (Note: Pole signs are prohibited.)
- **"Portable sign"** -- a sign not permanently attached to the ground or to a building. (Note: Portable signs are prohibited.)
- **"Public right-of-way"** -- a street, curb, sidewalk, alley, or other dedicated public way.
- **"Real estate sign"** -- a sign advertising a property or any portion thereof for sale, lease, rent, or exchange by the owner.
- **"Roof sign"** -- a sign erected, constructed, or placed upon a roof or parapet of a building. (Note: Roof signs are prohibited.)
- **"Searchlight"** -- a device which projects a high intensity beam of light.
- **"Sign"** -- any device, structure, fixture, or placard using color, form, graphic, illumination, symbol, or writing to advertise, announce the purpose of, or identify the purpose of a person or entity.
- **"Sign area"** -- the area of the sign computed as set forth in Section 25.54.008.
- **"Sign face"** -- the surface upon which the copy is placed.
- **"Sign program"** -- see "Master sign program."
- **"Site"** -- the total area within the property lines of a parcel of land, or for multi-tenant commercial buildings, the individual storefront or tenant space.
- **"Subdivision sign"** -- a temporary sign advertising a residential subdivision for sale.
- **"Suspended sign"** -- a sign hanging from a canopy, covered walkway, or soffit.
- **"Temporary sign"** -- a sign not intended to be permanently installed, with a defined display duration.
- **"Tract/area identity sign"** -- a permanent sign identifying a neighborhood or subdivision.
- **"Wall sign"** -- a sign attached flat to, painted on, or placed against the facade of a building, not extending more than 18 inches from the face of the wall.
- **"Window sign"** -- a sign placed on or affixed to the window glass.

## Section 25.54.008: Computations

(A) **Computation of Area of Individual Signs.** The area of a single-faced sign shall be computed by means of the simplest geometric shape(s) that will encompass the extreme limits of the sign, including any material or color forming an integral part of the background of the display, but not including any supporting framework or bracing. A background panel of not more than 2-inch thickness, painted the same color as the wall or structure to which it is attached, shall not be considered part of the sign. Windows with mullions less than 3 inches in width shall be considered as one window.

(B) **Computation of Area of Multi-faced Signs.** The sign area for a sign with more than one face shall be computed by adding together the area of all sign faces visible from any one point.

(C) **Computation of Height.** Sign height shall be computed as the distance from the top of the sign (or sign structure, whichever is taller) to the grade of the nearest adjacent street or access way, unless otherwise noted.

(D) **Maximum Allowable Sign Area.** The combined allowable sign area of all permanent signs not exempt under Section 25.54.014 on any site shall not exceed **1 sq ft per foot of linear building or suite frontage**, up to a maximum of **150 sq ft** total. Single signs shall not exceed **75 sq ft** in size. Code text: "Alley, blade or projecting, building marker, incidental, marquee, miscellaneous business signs, open house, real estate, temporary and suspended signs are exempt from the maximum combined sign area limit." A blade or projecting sign never counts toward the frontage cap.

## Section 25.54.010: Signs permitted on private property

(A) Signs may be allowed on private property only in accordance with Table 1 (Permitted Signs by Type and Use) and the sign luminance table in subsection (C)(3). Key: A = Allowed without permit; P = Permit Required; N = Not Allowed.

(B) **Design, Construction and Maintenance.** All signs shall comply with Table 1 and Table 2. All signs shall be maintained in good condition. Torn canopies/awnings, broken elements, faded/flaking paint, or similar evidence of poor maintenance are the responsibility of the owner to repair in a timely manner. Failure to maintain signs may be cause for a code enforcement action.

(C) **Lighting.** Verbatim (codified 25.54.010(C); Ord. 1732 did not amend it):

(1) "Method. Where not otherwise restricted, signs may be indirectly lighted. High intensity discharge light sources including, but not necessarily limited to mercury vapor, metal halide, and high pressure sodium, shall not be permitted."

(2) "Shielding. The lighting source shall be shielded in an appropriate manner to direct light only upon the sign and minimize glare and/or light spillage onto the public way or adjacent properties."

How the lighting rules fit together (our summary, not code text):
- "Indirectly lighted" means "illumination of a sign by a light source that is externally located to the sign surface. This method of lighting may include, but is not limited to, spotlighting or backlighting" (25.54.006). External spotlighting is allowed; halo (reverse channel) letters with opaque faces read as backlighting (our reading; confirm with Planning).
- Internally illuminated signs, including channel letters lit through a translucent face, are prohibited except marquees (25.54.016(E), below).
- Luminous tube (neon/argon) signs: the sign tables' footnote 10 and 25.54.018 (interior location at least 4 ft from the nearest window, by permit).
- Backlit canopy/awning signs are prohibited (25.54.016; Table 2 canopy/awning row: copy "may not be backlit").
- Lighting that depicts action makes a sign an animated sign (25.54.006), prohibited except as an interior building sign (25.54.016(A)).

(3) **Luminance.** Verbatim from codified 25.54.010(C)(3) (corpus `ch25-54-sign-regulations.md`; Ord. 1732 did not amend it): "The maximum allowable lighting intensity shall be based on the following table:"

| Range of Sign Luminance (Candelas/square foot) | Ambient Light Environment |
|---|---|
| 1 to 70 | LOW areas with no exterior lighting, other than street lighting. Such areas include, but are not limited to, most residential areas and the beaches. |
| 71 to 100 | MEDIUM areas where signs are relatively isolated or areas adjacent to "Low" ambient light areas. |
| 101 to 140 | HIGH areas such as the downtown, streets or parking areas that are highly lighted or areas with high sign competition. |

"Illumination of signs shall be measured with a light meter using appropriate methodology that ensures accurate luminance readings. notwithstanding the above standards, the director of community development may order the dimming of any sign illumination determined to be excessively bright. Lighting fixtures used to illuminate signs from the front shall be mounted from the top of the sign structure."

The code sets no day/night nit limits and does not require automatic dimming controls.

## Section 25.54.012: Signs in the public right-of-way

Excepting those signs allowed under this section, no signs shall be allowed in the public right-of-way or beyond a street plan line.

(A) **Signs Requiring a Permit:**
1. Canopy/awning, blade or projecting, suspended or wall signs projecting over a public right-of-way (bottom edge at least 7 ft above public ROW surface)
2. Signs erected by or on behalf of a public agency to identify public property or convey public information
3. City sponsored signs or master sign programs, including temporary signs

(B) **Signs Not Requiring a Permit:**
1. Bus stop signs erected by a public transit company
2. Emergency warning or directional signs by a governmental agency or public utility
3. Signs pertaining to persons or property
4. Signs on awnings, canopies, marquees, or the side of a building abutting a public right-of-way

(C) **Prohibited in the Public Right-of-Way:** Except as specified above, all other signs in the public right-of-way are prohibited, including but not limited to:
1. Advertising of any kind on street fixtures (lamp posts, fire hydrants, traffic signs, parking meters, trees)
2. Private incidental signs (including "no parking") in the public ROW to monopolize/control public parking
3. Temporary or portable signs (unless specifically approved by city council or as part of an approved sign program)

## Section 25.54.014: Regulation exemption

The following shall be exempt from regulation under this chapter:
(A) Any public notice or warning signs required by federal, state, or local law
(B) Holiday lights and decorations with no commercial message (November 15 through January 15 only)
(C) Except for luminous tube signs, all interior building signs not positioned primarily for exterior visibility. (Note: All luminous tube signs require a sign permit regardless of location if visible from a public street.)
(D) Miscellaneous business signs (credit card stickers, open/closed signs, hours of operation) -- each group shall not exceed 2.25 sq ft, one group per entrance. Luminous tube open and closed signs require a sign permit.
(E) Signs located within a sports stadium or other enclosed athletic or performance facility not visible from outside
(F) Flags of national, state, or local government displayed in conformity with protocol, and flags of religious, fraternal, or civic organizations

## Section 25.54.016: Prohibited signs

All signs not expressly permitted under this chapter or exempt from regulation are prohibited. Such signs include, but are not limited to:

(A) **Animated signs** except those that qualify as an interior building sign
(B) A **sign no longer identifying a bona fide existing business** (abandoned signs)
(C) **Banners and beacons**, except as permitted per Sections 25.54.012 and 25.54.020
(D) **Billboards**, including mobile billboards on trailers
(E) Verbatim: "Excepting marquees, internally illuminated signs;". Marquees are the only exception; channel letters are not excepted. An "internally illuminated sign" is "a sign directly lit from an interior light source contained within the sign structure through a transparent or translucent surface" (25.54.006), which describes a front-lit channel letter
(F) **Inflated display signs and tethered balloons**, except as permitted per Section 25.54.020 or as part of an approved outdoor display or sign program
(G) **Portable signs**
(H) **Roof signs**
(I) Signs of any type associated with a bus stop (public transit agency signs excepted)
(J) **Back-lit canopy/awning signs**
(K) **Pole signs**
(L) Private incidental signs in the public ROW to monopolize/control public parking
(M) **Signs emitting sound, odor, or visible matter** (smoke, steam, sparks, particles)
(N) **Electronic message centers (EMC)** and digital signs except as interior building signs
(O) **Snipe signs** (small advertising signs tacked to poles, fences, or trees)

## Section 25.54.018: General permit procedures

Current text: codified 25.54.018 (the City source document (not included in this plugin), codified through 2026-04-28) as amended by Ord. 1732 § 48 (signed text pp. 74-75, effective 2026-07-22; not yet certified by the Coastal Commission).

(A) **Applications.** Sign permit and master sign program applications go to Community Development on the City form, with scaled drawings (dimensions, sign area, location, height, copy, letter size, materials, colors, lighting) for all existing and proposed signs on the site, and construction specifications (electrical, attachment). Master sign programs add common standards and a plot plan.

(B) **Fees.** Set by City Council resolution. Use the fee tool; do not quote an amount from memory.

(C) **Approval required.** "The modification of an existing sign or the construction, creation or installation of a new sign requiring a sign permit shall be subject to the review and approval of the approval authority." The approval authority is the Director for (E)-eligible signs, otherwise the Planning Commission (25.54.006).

(D) **Design criteria** considered in review: compatibility with the architecture, high-quality and natural materials (blade, projecting and suspended signs strongly encouraged), opaque canopies and awnings, font size proportional to sign size and location (smaller downtown), Downtown Specific Plan urban design guidelines in the DSP area, and luminous tube signs as one-of-a-kind artistic elements.

(E) **Signs eligible for administrative approval** (Director). Except for luminous tube signs. The Director may refer any of them to the Planning Commission. Eligible:
1. Signs in compliance with an approved master sign program;
2. Signs that meet all of: (a) all design standards in this chapter, including 25.54.010 and Tables 1 and 2; (b) total site signage within the maximum allowable sign area; (c) "The letter or font height must not exceed fourteen inches" (Ord. 1732 changed eight to fourteen);
3. Temporary signs complying with 25.54.020 (not temporary signs or programs for City Council special events);
4. "Replacement of signs previously authorized by a sign permit provided that the sign meets all applicable design standards" (added by Ord. 1732);
5. "Signs that are not eligible for Administrative Approval shall be considered by the Planning Commission." (added by Ord. 1732)

(F) **Appeals.** A person disagreeing with a determination of the Community Development Director or Planning Commission may appeal "in accordance with the appeal provisions in Section 25.05.070" (Ord. 1732 deleted the former ten-business-day sign appeal; 25.05.070 now sets 14 calendar days on the City form).

(G) **Creativity encouraged.** "The Planning Commission may, at its discretion, grant permits for signs not otherwise in conformance with this chapter up to a twenty-five percent increase in allowable sign area or height, if it finds the sign design to be extraordinarily creative and significantly contributing to the charm and character of Laguna Beach."

Master sign programs (25.54.010(D) as amended by Ord. 1732 § 47): required before sign permits for new office or shopping center construction, or substantial facade improvements, with four or more businesses; the **Planning Commission** may set conditions.

## Section 25.54.020: Temporary sign permits

(A) **Application.** Applications for temporary sign permits shall be submitted to the department of community development.

(B) **Fees.** Applicable fees per city council resolution.

(C) **Term.**
1. Beacons, searchlights and similar devices for commercial advertising: max **7 successive nights**
2. Other temporary signs: max **30 days**, with one additional 30-day extension permitted

(D) **Limitation.** A business license holder may post temporary signs up to a maximum of **60 days per calendar year**.

(E) **Special Temporary Signs:**
1. **Banners** -- May be approved by Planning Commission; shall not exceed 32 sq ft; shall not be internally illuminated; shall be securely attached; shall not project over public ROW without approved encroachment permit and liability insurance
2. **Construction signs** -- One per street frontage; max 32 sq ft; may be installed upon building permit issuance and shall be removed upon final inspection or certificate of occupancy
3. **Subdivision signs** -- One per street frontage; max 32 sq ft; may be installed upon final map recordation; shall be removed upon sale of last unit
4. **Special event signs** -- May be approved by city council for community events

## Section 25.54.022: Nonconforming signs

(A) **Amortization Period.** Any sign that does not conform but lawfully existed at the time of enactment shall be abated or made to conform within:
1. Temporary signs: within 1 day of written notice
2. Luminous tube or internally illuminated signs: within 60 days of written notice
3. Window signs: within 120 days of written notice
4. Interior signs positioned primarily for exterior viewing: within 30 days of written notice
5. All other signs: within 3 years of the effective date of the ordinance (May 2, 2002)

(B) **Alteration.** Nonconforming signs shall not be structurally altered or relocated except to bring them into conformance with this chapter. Ordinary maintenance and repair, including replacement of lighting or sign face copy, is permitted.

(C) **Damage or Destruction.** If a nonconforming sign is damaged or destroyed to the extent exceeding 50% of its replacement cost, it shall not be restored except in conformity with this chapter.

(D) **Abandonment.** A nonconforming sign shall be deemed abandoned when the business it identifies ceases to operate for a period of 90 continuous days. An abandoned sign shall be removed within 30 days.

(E) **Removal Responsibility.** When a nonconforming sign is required to be removed, the property owner and tenant/lessee are jointly responsible for removal.

## Section 25.54.024: Murals

(A) Murals shall conform to the design standards and permit procedures outlined below; murals proposed in compliance with Chapter 1.09 (Art in Public Places) shall be governed by that chapter. Signature(s) of the artist(s) shall be allowed and limited to a max of 2 sq ft.

(B) **Procedures:**
1. Applications submitted to the department of community development with: site plan, scale drawing/photo of building showing proposed size and placement, colored drawing of proposed mural, and proposed maintenance schedule
2. Application fee per city council resolution
3. Approval by Planning Commission at a noticed public hearing
4. Property owners within 300 ft notified per Section 25.05.065

(C) **Mural Design Standards:**
1. Shall be compatible with the village character
2. Shall be appropriate to its location and surrounding environment
3. Shall not adversely affect neighboring property values
4. Shall be maintained in good condition; deteriorated murals must be restored or removed
5. Shall not contain any commercial message (commercial messages make it a wall sign)
6. Shall not be internally illuminated

## Section 25.54.026: Arts and cultural organizations

(A) Public benefit non-profit arts **and cultural** organizations, "such as" the Festival of Arts, Sawdust Festival, Art-A-Fair, Laguna Playhouse, Laguna Art Museum and Laguna College of Art and Design, may apply for sign permits or sign programs, including facade changes, from the planning commission (Ord. 1732 § 49 retitled the section "Arts and cultural organizations" and replaced "specifically" with "such as").

(B) The planning commission has complete discretion for size, design, maximum display term, and location regardless of other provisions of this chapter.

(C) The planning commission may authorize administrative approval by the director of community development for changes within an approved sign program.

(D) Signs for private commercial events at an arts organization's site require separate sign permits under this chapter.

**OTC-eligible per the city reference OTC item 11 — commercial signs *without structural alterations*.** A sign that alters structure is not OTC-eligible; it goes to full plan review. Hours and the full OTC list live in `../../city-reference/SKILL.md` §"Permit Processing Tracks".

- **Turnaround:** Same day (30-min appointment or walk-in)

Note: This applies to straightforward commercial signs that the Director can approve under 25.54.018(E) (conforming signs with letters 14 in or less, master-program signs, replacements of permitted signs). Signs that go to the Planning Commission under 25.54.018(E)(5) are not same-day.

## Coastal Zone Interaction

Most of Laguna Beach is within the Coastal Zone. Signs that constitute "development" under the Coastal Act may require a Coastal Development Permit (CDP) per Chapter 25.07, in addition to a sign permit. New freestanding signs, signs requiring significant structural work, or signs in environmentally sensitive areas are most likely to trigger CDP requirements.
