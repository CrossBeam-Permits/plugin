# Minor Scope Families

Each family below is work a city issues over the counter. For each: what it is, what still
applies, and what turns it into a plan check.

---

## 1. Interior finish and drywall repair

Patching or replacing drywall, plaster, tile, trim, or paint in an existing room; replacing
cabinets in place.

- Gypsum board application and fastening still governs the repair (CRC R702, R702.3.5).
- Wall covering in a wet area must be the rated material for that location (CRC R702).
- **Escalates** when the wall is a shear wall or bearing wall, when a new opening is cut, or
  when the repair follows fire, water, or impact damage that reached framing.

## 2. Exterior patch — stucco or siding

A limited area of stucco, siding, or trim repaired or replaced. Not a wall, not an elevation.

- Weather-resistive barrier and flashing continuity at the patch is the whole review.
- **Route to `california-siding-replacement`** when the work covers a whole wall or elevation,
  changes the cladding material, or exposes the stud cavity — that skill owns the material,
  fastener, energy, and fire-zone checks.
- **Escalates** when sheathing or framing is replaced.

## 3. Skylight in an existing roof opening

A skylight unit set in an opening that already exists, or replaced in kind.

- Replacement fenestration meets the Energy Code U-factor and SHGC requirements for the climate
  zone, with the small-area exception for replacements at or below 75 sq ft
  (Energy Code §150.2(b)1B). `california-window-replacement` carries the tables.
- Flashing and the manufacturer's curb detail carry the water test.
- **Escalates** the moment the opening is new or enlarged: that cuts rafters and needs headers,
  which is a plan check, not a counter permit.

## 4. Shed and small detached accessory structure

A one-story storage shed or similar accessory structure.

- At or below the exemption threshold, no permit is required (see
  `permit-thresholds-exemptions.md`). Say so.
- Above the threshold, the structure needs a permit and this skill covers it only when it stays
  simple: one story, no plumbing, no sleeping or habitable use, prefabricated or light-frame,
  set on a slab or piers.
- Setbacks, lot coverage, and fire-zone material limits come from the city.
- **Escalates** with utilities, habitable use, a second story, or an engineered foundation.

## 5. Hardscape at grade

A patio slab, walkway, driveway apron, or planter curb that retains no soil and sits at or near
grade.

- The building review is drainage: water must not be directed onto a neighbouring property or
  against the building.
- **Escalates** into grading when the work cuts or fills earth above the city's adopted
  Appendix J thresholds, and into a retaining-wall review the moment any element holds soil.

## 6. Plumbing fixture replaced in place

A water closet, lavatory, sink, tub, or shower valve swapped in the same location with no change
to the drain, vent, or layout.

- Fixtures and fittings must be listed and installed per the California Plumbing Code, Chapter 4.
- A shower or tub-shower valve must be a listed pressure-balance or thermostatic mixing valve.
- In a single-family dwelling built before 1994, non-compliant water fixtures throughout the
  dwelling must be replaced with water-conserving fixtures (Civil Code §1101.4).
- **Route to `california-kitchen-bath-remodel`** when the layout changes, a fixture moves, or
  finishes and cabinets are part of the work.
- **Escalates** when drain, waste, or vent piping is re-routed, or a wall opens for it.

## 7. Residential fire-sprinkler retrofit

An NFPA 13D system added to an existing one- or two-family dwelling.

- The system is designed and installed to CRC §R309 or NFPA 13D.
- **There is no state-level retrofit trigger for an existing un-sprinklered single-family
  dwelling.** CRC §R309.2 Exception 1 exempts existing dwellings on additions and alterations.
  Every retrofit trigger is city law — commonly a cumulative valuation test, a percentage-of-
  scope test, or both, and either one firing is enough. Read the city delta, and never assert a
  state trigger.
- The plan-check depth belongs to the fire authority. The building counter check is scope,
  licence (C-16), and whether the city's trigger fired.

## 8. Repair defined by an engineer's or architect's letter

A framing, connection, or settlement repair described in a stamped letter rather than a drawing
set — vehicle impact, dry rot, a cracked member.

- The letter is the construction document. It must be stamped and signed, name the damaged
  member and the repair, and specify materials and fasteners.
- The repair may be reviewed at the counter when the letter is complete, the repair is
  in-kind or better, and no additional load path changes.
- **Escalates** when the letter changes the structure, when the damage is not fully characterised,
  or when the repair depends on calculations that are not attached (CRC R105.2.2).
