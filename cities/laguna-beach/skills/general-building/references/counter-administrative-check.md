# The Counter Check

Source: California Business and Professions Code Division 3, Chapter 9 (Contractors State
License Law); EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule, 40 CFR 745; Cal/OSHA Title 8 §§1529 and
1532.1; SCAQMD Rule 1403.

A minor permit has no plan set, so the review is the application itself. These are the items a
counter tech clears, in the order they matter.

---

## 1. Scope

The application must say **what**, **where**, **how much**, and **of what material**. "Repair as
needed", "misc. work", or a bare dollar value is not a scope, and the correct output is a FLAG
naming what is missing — not a guess.

Read the scope as written. The scope line governs the review; a photo, a product brochure, or a
contractor's proposal supports it but does not replace it.

## 2. Valuation

A stated dollar value that is plausible for the described quantity. Valuation matters beyond the
fee:

- Many cities run a **cumulative valuation trigger** — additions, alterations and repairs within
  a rolling period, measured against the pre-work value of the dwelling. Small permits add up,
  and the trigger commonly forces a sprinkler retrofit or a broader upgrade. The number and the
  window are city law; see the city delta.
- A valuation far below the described scope is a FLAG, not a FAIL. Say why it looks low.

## 3. Contractor licence

- An active CSLB licence is required to contract for the work (B&P §7028). Check the
  classification against the scope: general building work versus a specialty trade.
- **Owner-builder** is available under B&P §7044 for an owner-occupied residential property
  where the owner personally performs or directly employs the work. Verify the signed
  owner-builder declaration on the application.
- Workers' compensation coverage, or the signed exemption declaration, must be on the
  application. This is a CSLB licensing requirement, cleared at intake.

## 4. Code cycle

Review against the code edition in force for the date the **application was received**, not the
date of review and not a planning filing date. California Building Standards Commission cycles
run on published dates; the city delta carries the city's own cycle table when it has one.

## 5. Hazardous materials

| Condition | What to check | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Dwelling built before 1978 and the work disturbs paint | EPA RRP certification and lead-safe work practices | 40 CFR 745; Cal/OSHA Title 8 §1532.1 |
| Building built before 1981 and the work disturbs suspect material (drywall joint compound, stucco, mastic, floor tile, transite) | Survey, notification, and certified abatement | Cal/OSHA Title 8 §1529; SCAQMD Rule 1403 in Southern California |

These are EPA and Cal-OSHA overlays, not building-department pass/fail items. The reviewer
**FLAGs** them for documentation.

## 6. City overlay

Even the smallest permit can sit in a fire hazard severity zone, the coastal zone, a historic
district, or a hillside overlay. The state floor never answers those. Load the city delta skill
and say which local rules were checked.
