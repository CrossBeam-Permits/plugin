# Laguna Beach Building: Over-the-counter permits (windows, siding, patio covers, minor trade work, signs)

_Generated by the plugin publisher from the maintained Laguna Beach requirements source. Do not edit by hand; changes are overwritten._

Applies to: Window or door replacement; Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair).

Each item names when it is due and what triggers it. A conditional item applies only when its question is answered yes from the project evidence. Decide that by reading the plans and application, never from a keyword.

## Due with the application

### Completed Building Permit Application with the correct project address

- id: `laguna_otc_permit_application`
- Scopes: Window or door replacement; Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair)
- What the City asks for: The deliverable is the City's Building Permit Application page. The City asks for two things about it: that it is complete and that the project address on it is correct. Complete means the form's own blocks are filled — the description of work, the valuation, and the applicant, owner and contractor blocks the form prints. An accurate address matters more here than on a plan-checked project, because an OTC permit can issue the same day against whatever address the application carries. A blank block that applies to the project makes the application incomplete: the description of work, the valuation, and the contractor block on a contractor-pulled permit. A block that does not apply to the project may be blank — the architect and engineer blocks on a job with no design professional, the contractor block on an owner-builder permit — and a blank there is not a defect.
- Source: Laguna Beach Building Plan Review Services (edition 06/08/2026), "Submittal requirements for OTC review": "Completed and accurately filled out permit application with proper address" (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/departments/community-development/building-division)

### Plans include an accurate site plan

- id: `laguna_otc_site_plan`
- Scopes: Window or door replacement; Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair)
- What the City asks for: Judge the file, not the EnerGov (or other portal) slot it was uploaded into. A site plan shows the parcel: the property lines, the building footprint on the lot, and where the work sits on the site. A drawing that does that is a site plan even if the applicant parked it in For Reference Only or another label; a file sitting in Plans that is a product specification, waterproofing spec, or catalog cut is not a site plan. Do not rebuild the City's file-category taxonomy. The City also uses a reference slot for original stamped sets — decide from the document whether this file is this filing's working drawings or an archived original set. The City states this item without a condition, so it is asked on every OTC scope, including an in-kind replacement. Do not soften it and do not read the floor-plan item below as an alternative to it — the handout lists both.
- Source: Laguna Beach Building Plan Review Services (edition 06/08/2026), "Submittal requirements for OTC review": "Two (2) sets of 24\" x 36\" plans identifying: An accurate site plan" (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/departments/community-development/building-division)

### Plans show the complete scope of work

- id: `laguna_otc_scope_of_work_on_plans`
- Scopes: Window or door replacement; Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair)
- What the City asks for: Judge the file, not the portal slot. The working drawings identify the whole scope of work — every item the permit covers, at the level of detail the trade needs — and the cover (or the sheet that functions as the cover) carries the project address and that complete scope, including a small repair. On a window job that is the count, the locations and the type of every opening being changed. On a deck or waterproofing repair it is which members or surfaces are being replaced, and it states whether a guardrail is being removed and replaced when that is in play. A specification sitting in Plans does not show scope. Judge it against the application's own description of work.
- Source: Laguna Beach Building Plan Review Services (edition 06/08/2026), "Submittal requirements for OTC review": "Two (2) sets of 24\" x 36\" plans identifying: The complete scope of work" (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/departments/community-development/building-division)

### Floor plan locating existing and all new proposed work

- id: `laguna_otc_floor_plan_existing_proposed`
- Scopes: Window or door replacement; Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair)
- What the City asks for: Judge the file, not the portal slot. The working drawings contain a floor plan (or, on a scope with no interior work such as a fence or a sign, the drawing that locates the work on the structure or the site), and that drawing locates two things: what exists and what is proposed. Locating is the test — the reviewer must be able to point at the sheet and say where each item of work happens. A specification, product cut, unstamped catalog sheet, captioned photograph, or annotated aerial is not a floor plan — even on an exterior-only repair. The cover of the working drawings must carry the project address and the complete scope of work, the same identity the site-plan and scope rows require.
- Source: Laguna Beach Building Plan Review Services (edition 06/08/2026), "Submittal requirements for OTC review": "Two (2) sets of 24\" x 36\" plans identifying: Floor plan that indicates locations of existing and all new proposed work" (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/departments/community-development/building-division)

### Stamped and signed engineered plans and calculations

- id: `laguna_otc_engineered_plans_calcs`
- Scopes: Window or door replacement; Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair)
- Applies when: Does the work include structural modification of a building, deck or sign support — a new or enlarged opening, a header, a beam, or a change to a load path? Ordinary fence posts and a wall that follows the City's unmodified standard detail do not count. A sign counts as OTC item 11 only when it is "without structural alterations"; when its support or mounting alters a structure, answer yes.
  - If yes: Submit the engineered drawings, stamped and signed by the California-registered design professional who prepared them. The supporting calculation package is due before plan check, not at this intake counter.
  - If no: A stamp, calculation, or engineering document that happens to be in the packet does not make the structural trigger apply.
- What the City asks for: Where the work includes structural modification of a building or deck, the City's published item is stamped and signed engineered plans and calculations. Split that item at intake. The intake deliverable is the engineered DRAWINGS: plan sheets stamped and signed by the California-registered design professional who prepared them. Judge the file, not the portal slot. The City writes "(if applicable)", and the qualified-projects list says what makes it applicable: items 2 and 3 both read "with minor structural modifications". Item 10 is a wood or metal fence: its ordinary posts and footings are the trade, not this trigger. Item 11 admits only "Commercial signs without structural alterations"; the handout does not say a sign's own posts, footing or mounting are non-structural, so a sign whose support or attachment alters a structure is outside item 11 and is a structural question, not an exclusion. A wall filed against the City's unmodified Standard Block Wall Detail is answered by that bulletin's own row, not this one. A stamp and a signature are part of the drawings deliverable: an unstamped sheet is not engineered drawings.
- Source: Laguna Beach Building Plan Review Services (edition 06/08/2026), "Submittal requirements for OTC review": "Two (2) sets of stamped and signed engineered plans and calculations (if applicable)" (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/departments/community-development/building-division)

### Energy Certificate of Compliance

- id: `state_energy_compliance_certificate`
- Scopes: Deck, balcony or patio cover repair; Siding or exterior finish replacement; Fence, gate or freestanding block wall; Sign permit; Multi-unit exterior repair (deck, balcony, window, door, siding or minor trade repair)
- Level: California state requirement the City applies
- Applies when: Is the proposed work regulated by Title 24, Part 6?
  - If yes: Include the applicable Certificate of Compliance with the permit documents.
- What the City asks for: Submit the applicable current-cycle Certificate of Compliance produced by approved compliance software; a form-family naming mismatch is not a completeness failure.
- Source: 2025 Title 24, Part 1, §10-103(a)2 (https://www.energy.ca.gov/programs-and-topics/programs/building-energy-efficiency-standards/2025-building-energy-efficiency)

### Energy Certificate of Compliance for replacement fenestration

- id: `state_energy_alteration_fenestration`
- Scopes: Window or door replacement
- Level: California state requirement the City applies
- Applies when: Does the work replace fenestration — windows, glazed doors or skylights — in an existing dwelling?
  - If yes: Include the CF1R-ALT-05-E Certificate of Compliance, or CF1R-ALT-01-E with section H completed, with the permit application, unless Exception 1 to §150.2(b)1B covers the whole job.
- What the City asks for: Replacement fenestration is an alteration regulated by 2025 Energy Code §150.2(b)1B: new manufactured fenestration products installed to replace existing fenestration products of the same total area meet the U-factor requirement of §150.1(c)3A and the SHGC requirement of §150.1(c)4. The certificate is CF1R-ALT-05-E, or section H of CF1R-ALT-01-E, and Title 24, Part 1, §10-103(a)2A places it with the building permit application. A form-family naming mismatch is not a completeness failure.
- Source: 2025 Title 24, Part 1, §10-103(a)2A; 2025 Energy Code §150.2(b)1B (https://www.energy.ca.gov/programs-and-topics/programs/building-energy-efficiency-standards/2025-building-energy-efficiency)

### Energy Certificate of Compliance for a space-conditioning change-out

- id: `state_energy_alteration_hvac`
- Scopes: Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit
- Level: California state requirement the City applies
- Applies when: Does the work replace or alter a space-conditioning system, including its ducts?
  - If yes: Include the CF1R-ALT-02-E Certificate of Compliance with the permit application.
- What the City asks for: A space-conditioning change-out is an alteration regulated by 2025 Energy Code §150.2(b)1C, which carries the requirements for a complete replacement system. Three neighbouring subsections travel with it: §150.2(b)1E duct sealing, §150.2(b)1F refrigerant charge and airflow verification, and §150.2(b)1G, which bars electric resistance as the primary heating system. The certificate is CF1R-ALT-02-E, and Title 24, Part 1, §10-103(a)2A places it with the building permit application. A form-family naming mismatch is not a completeness failure.
- Source: 2025 Title 24, Part 1, §10-103(a)2A; 2025 Energy Code §150.2(b)1C (https://www.energy.ca.gov/programs-and-topics/programs/building-energy-efficiency-standards/2025-building-energy-efficiency)

- id: `state_energy_alteration_water_heater`
- Scopes: Minor mechanical, plumbing or temporary-power permit
- Level: California state requirement the City applies
- Applies when: Does the work replace a water heater or alter the hot-water distribution system?

A form-family naming mismatch is not a completeness failure.
