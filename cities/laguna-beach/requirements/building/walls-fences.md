# Laguna Beach Building: Fences, block walls and retaining walls

_Generated by the plugin publisher from the maintained Laguna Beach requirements source. Do not edit by hand; changes are overwritten._

Applies to: Fence, block wall or retaining wall.

Each item names when it is due and what triggers it. A conditional item applies only when its question is answered yes from the project evidence. Decide that by reading the plans and application, never from a keyword.

## Due with the application

### Wall height above adjacent grade and distance to the property line shown

- id: `laguna_wall_height_and_property_line_dimensions`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- What the City asks for: The plan dimensions the wall height measured from adjacent grade and the distance from the wall to each property line. These two numbers decide whether a permit is required at all — over 30 inches from adjacent grade, or any height within three feet of a property line — so a drawing that omits either cannot be screened. The bulletin also limits the grade difference across the wall to a maximum 6-inch offset with no surcharge loads, which the same dimensions establish.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Zoning/Planning Division approval of the wall plans

- id: `laguna_wall_planning_approval_before_permit`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Checked against: the City permit record, not the plan set
- What the City asks for: The Zoning/Planning Division regulates the wall's height and location based on the zone, its position on the property, and community design standards, and approves the plans before any permit issues. Laguna sequences the two lanes here exactly as it does for buildings: the Planning approval exists first, and the Building packet is filed against the plans Planning approved.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Standard detail followed exactly, or plans by a registered design professional

- id: `laguna_wall_standard_detail_unmodified_or_engineered`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Applies when: Does this wall follow the City's standard detail without modification, on a site under 10 percent slope with no adverse soil conditions?
  - If yes: File against the standard detail; no engineered design is submitted.
  - If no: A masonry or concrete wall that leaves the standard detail — slope over 10 percent, adverse soils, or any design departure — needs plans by a California registered design professional. A wood, vinyl or wrought-iron fence is outside this bulletin and is not held to that engineered-plan demand.
- What the City asks for: The City's standard detail is all-or-nothing: an applicant relying on it may not modify any portion of it. The June 2026 revision uses the 2025 CBC and ASCE 7-22 with a 95 mph basic wind speed, exposure C. It is designed for the average condition, at an allowable soil bearing pressure of 1,500 psf with passive bearing at 100 psf per foot and footings a minimum 12 inches into undisturbed natural soil. Where the site slope exceeds 10 percent, where soil conditions are adverse — expansive soil, liquefaction, flood hazard — or where the design departs from the detail in any way, a design professional registered in California prepares the plans instead.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Fence construction details show materials, design, specifications, post layout, fasteners and finishes

- id: `laguna_fence_construction_details_staff_check`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Applies when: Is this a wood, vinyl, wrought-iron or metal fence rather than a masonry or concrete wall?
  - If yes: Staff look for construction details identifying the fence material and design, the applicable specifications, post layout, fasteners and finishes.

## Raised during plan check

### Clearances to fire hydrants and water meters

- id: `laguna_wall_hydrant_and_water_meter_clearances`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- What the City asks for: No wall or fence within 36 inches of a fire hydrant. No wall, fence or foundation within 12 inches of a water meter. The meter clearance reaches the footing, not only the wall above it, which is the part a plan most often misses.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### No projection into the public right-of-way

- id: `laguna_wall_no_right_of_way_projection`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- What the City asks for: Nothing projects into the public right-of-way, and the City names the decorative elements specifically: a stone cap or trim counts as a projection even where the wall face itself is clear.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Site wall stands alone and retains nothing

- id: `laguna_wall_freestanding_no_retaining`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- What the City asks for: The standard detail covers a freestanding site wall: it retains no soil and forms no part of a building or other structure. A wall shown retaining grade, or tied into a structure, has left the bulletin and needs an engineered design.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Combined wall and attachment height at or under six feet

- id: `laguna_wall_combined_height_with_attachments`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Applies when: Does the design mount a railing, guard or screen on top of the wall?
  - If yes: The plan dimensions the combined wall-plus-attachment height from the lowest adjacent grade and keeps it at or under 6 feet 0 inches.
- What the City asks for: A railing, guard or screen may not sit on the wall where the wall plus the attachment exceeds 6 feet 0 inches above the lowest adjacent grade. The measurement is the combined height from the lowest adjacent grade, not the wall height alone.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Solid grouting, bond pattern, cleanouts and expansion joints called out

- id: `laguna_wall_construction_detailing_notes`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Applies when: Is this a concrete block or masonry wall?
  - If yes: Call out solid grouting of the CMU, running-bond pattern, cleanouts for lifts greater than 4 feet, and expansion joints at no more than 30 feet.
- What the City asks for: The wall is solid grouted. Both footing alternates on page 2 of the June 2026 revision label the wall 6" MIN. CMU (SOLID GROUTED); the April 2020 revision labelled it only 6" MIN. CMU. A partially grouted wall that would have passed against the older sheet no longer matches the standard detail. Blocks are laid in running bond so head joints in successive courses offset horizontally by at least a quarter unit length. Cleanouts are provided for all lifts greater than 4 feet. Expansion joint spacing does not exceed 30 feet. The cleanout note is the one that bites in the field, because it is checked at the reinforcing steel inspection before grouting — the same inspection that makes solid grouting verifiable.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

## Due before the permit issues

### Property corners monumented by a licensed surveyor before construction

- id: `laguna_wall_property_corner_monumentation`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- What the City asks for: A licensed surveyor locates and monuments the property corners before construction starts. This is a pre-construction gate rather than a counter item, so its absence is a condition to satisfy, never a reason to refuse the packet.
- Source: the City's published copy, document 5099, still served the April 2020 revision when checked 2026-08-27) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### Surveyor certification for a wall or fence within three feet of a property line

- id: `laguna_wall_surveyor_certification_within_three_feet`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Applies when: Is any part of the new wall or fence within three feet of a property line?
  - If yes: For a masonry or concrete wall, submit the county-recorded corner monumentation by a licensed land surveyor or civil engineer. For a wood, vinyl or wrought-iron fence, submit the surveyor's setback certification, or the notarized waiver signed by each adjoining owner.
  - If no: No surveyor certification is submitted for a wall set back more than three feet from every property line.
- What the City asks for: Within three feet of a property line the City draws a line by material. For a masonry or concrete wall, the affected property corners are located and monumented by a California licensed land surveyor or qualified licensed civil engineer and recorded with the county before building or rebuilding — and the survey waiver is not available for it. For a wood, vinyl or wrought-iron fence, a licensed surveyor instead certifies the setback, documenting the placement of the fence and posts relative to the property line, and is retained before construction to identify the property line. That certification lands before the footing inspection and the concrete pour. The waiver route exists only for the light-fence materials: a notarized Request to Waive Survey Requirement signed by each adjoining property owner, filed with the Building Division before the permit issues.
- Source: Laguna Beach Standard Block Wall Detail (Information Bulletin, revised June 2026), GENERAL NOTE 10; waiver form verified live 2026-08-20) (https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/building-permits/applications-forms)

### “Survey Required” stamp on approved plans accepted for permit issuance

- id: `laguna_fence_survey_required_stamp_issuance`
- Scopes: Fence, block wall or retaining wall
- Applies when: Do the plan-check-approved plans carry a “Survey Required” stamp?
  - If yes: Accept the stamp for permit issuance; the survey condition is handled in the field.
  - If no: This staff disposition does not apply. Follow the published survey-certification or waiver path that applies to the wall or fence material and property-line distance.
- What the City asks for: The counter does not require the applicant to clear that stamped survey condition again before issuance.
- Source: City of Laguna Beach Building Division staff guidance
