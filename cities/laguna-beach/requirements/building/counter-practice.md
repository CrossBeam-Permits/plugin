# Laguna Beach Building: City counter practice at intake

_Generated by the plugin publisher from the maintained Laguna Beach requirements source. Do not edit by hand; changes are overwritten._

City counter practice: how City staff apply the published lists at intake, as staff have stated it or as City review decisions show it. It is not code and not a published handout, and it can change. Confirm with the City before relying on it.

The Building checklists in this folder cover both what the counter needs to accept an application and what plan check reviews later. This page says which items the counter requires before it accepts the application, which it leaves to plan check, and how staff have answered common filing questions.

## What the counter requires

### A condominium of 3 or more units files a Multifamily permit

- City practice: Residential condominium filings must be refiled as multi-family; the residential record will close.
- Basis: Building Division staff direction, September 2026, with the City Multifamily Permit Type Guidelines (April 2026)

### The work site is inside Laguna Beach city limits

- City practice: The City of Laguna Beach reviews and issues permits only for property inside its city limits. When the plans or application place the property in another city, the City closes the Laguna Beach record and the applicant applies to that city. A mailing address or ZIP code alone does not decide it: parts of unincorporated Orange County carry a Laguna Beach mailing address.

### Simple permits are issued at the counter, not online

- Status: The City has not yet said which items this covers.
- Open City question: which over-the-counter items are simple permits the City issues only in person. Items: (1) kitchen and bath remodels; (2) residential decks, balconies and patio cover repairs with minor structural modifications; (3) window and door replacement with minor structural modifications; (4) construction revisions to approved plans; (5) exterior siding replacement; (6) meter upgrade to 200A; (7) reroof with same material or lighter; (8) residential HVAC change outs; (9) minor plumbing, electrical or mechanical repairs or replacements; (10) wood or metal fences; (11) commercial signs without structural alterations. Items 6 and 7 are express permits today and stay on the portal unless the City says otherwise.
- City practice: For simple permits, applications are not processed through the portal, as permits are not issued online.
- Basis: Building Division staff direction, September 2026

### The plans must be a drawing of this work

- Applies when: A contractor estimate or proposal, a quote, a specification or product data sheet, product literature, or a floor-plan graphic that does not locate the proposed work (a listing or brochure plan with a scope list pasted beside it) is not a drawing of this work, wherever it was uploaded. An estimate may stay in the packet as supporting documentation.
- City practice: Please provide a complete plan set that shows enough to identify and review the proposed scope of work. An estimate or contractor proposal may be included as supporting documentation, but it does not replace the construction plans. At a minimum: a title or cover sheet with the property address, the complete scope of work, the applicable codes and the designer or preparer; existing and proposed floor plans, drawn to scale and dimensioned, that identify the areas and walls affected; existing construction, new construction and any demolition shown distinctly; details for any new wall (height, framing, finishes); the use of each affected room, with door openings, windows, stairs and other relevant existing features; any electrical, plumbing or mechanical work, or a note on the plans that there is none; and enough notes and details to show how the work will be built. Plan sizes: 8.5 x 11, 11 x 17 or 24 x 36. Combine all plan sheets into one PDF plan set.
- Exceptions: The express e-permits (panel upgrade, water heater, reroof) keep their own guide lists.
- Basis: Building counter decisions, September 2026

## Items due in plan check, not at intake

### Soils (geotechnical) report

- Covers: The geotechnical (soils) report for this site and this project.
- When: Not needed to file the application. Due during Building plan check.
- City practice: A geotechnical report is required before plan check begins, but it is not required to submit the packet for intake.
- Exception: The filed work constructs a new building and no soils report for this property and project is in the packet. A new building is a new single-family dwelling, a new multifamily or commercial building, or a new detached structure with habitable space other than an ADU. It is not an addition, a JADU, a conversion, a remodel, or a new detached structure with no habitable space, such as a detached garage. A City review letter or checklist that approves a report is not the report.
  - When: Due at intake. The City returns an application that lacks it.
  - Basis: Building Official guidance, September 2026
- Exception: The filed work creates an ADU (detached, attached, or a conversion) and no soils report for this property and project is in the packet, AND at least one of these applies: the lot slope is over 20%; the plans propose a deep foundation, such as grade beams or piles; or the plans propose a retaining wall over 5 ft. An ADU that meets none of the three is not this exception: it needs no soils report.
  - When: Due at intake. The City returns an application that lacks it.
  - Basis: Building Official and permit technician guidance, September 2026
- Basis: Building Division intake decisions, March to August 2026

## Questions City staff have answered

### For a Building submittal whose scope falls on the §25.05.040(B)(2) list (exempt from Design Review, Zone Clearance under §25.05.045), must the applicant hold Planning approval before Building accepts the set, or does the counter clear it at intake?

Exterior work on the (B)(2) list must hold Planning approval before Building intake. Interior-only work ((B)(2)(d)) is accepted at Building intake without a prior Planning record.

Basis: Building Division staff answer, September 2026

### When the parcel has several Planning records, which one clears Building intake, and is a filed Zone Clearance application an approval?

The Planning record whose description covers this work. An approval for other work on the same parcel does not cover it. Staff compare the descriptions; they must match closely, not drawing for drawing. What counts is the record's approved status: a Zone Clearance application that is filed but not yet approved is not an approval.

Basis: Building Division staff answer, September 2026

### When a parcel in the Coastal Zone carries ZPC, CDP or CDP-exemption records whose descriptions do not clearly cover the filed exterior scope, does the City block Building intake, or does it ask the planner?

It blocks intake on Planning approval and does not name the coastal instrument.

Basis: Building Division staff answer, September 2026

### Roof-mounted PV on an existing single-family dwelling changes the exterior. Must the applicant hold Planning approval (Zone Clearance, or a CDP exemption in the Coastal Zone appealable area) before Building intake?

No. LBMC Chapter 14.84 and Government Code §65850.5 make the solar permit an administrative, nondiscretionary building permit. §14.84.070(b) limits review to the building official's health and safety check. LBMC §25.07.008(A)(1) exempts improvements to single-family dwellings from a CDP, and rooftop panels add no floor area and no height.

Basis: Building Division staff answer, September 2026

### A reroof is (B)(2)(e) exterior work. Must the applicant hold Planning approval before Building intake, as for other exterior (B)(2) scopes?

Yes. "Planning is always going to look at a reroof... 100%." The City record shows the reroof Zoning Plan Check filed on or before the Building application day, then the Building permit.

Basis: Building Division staff answer, September 2026
