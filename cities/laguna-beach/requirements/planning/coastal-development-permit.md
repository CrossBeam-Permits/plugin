**When to file.** The City states the trigger on page 1: "The California Coastal Act of 1976 (Coastal Act) and the City's certified Local Coastal Program (LCP) requires a Coastal Development Permit (CDP) for all development within the coastal zone, unless specifically exempted or excluded (see Laguna Beach Municipal Code Section 25.07.008 and 25.07.010, respectively)." The City also states that its CDP decisions are appealable to the California Coastal Commission in certain areas [LBMC 25.07.006], and that in Blue Lagoon, Lower Irvine Cove, and Three Arch Bay the Coastal Commission retains jurisdiction over development, including the initial consideration of Coastal Development Permits. The City further states: "Additional applications are required when an applicant is requesting additional entitlements, such as a permit for Design Review."

"Required" items are on every submittal of this application. "Conditional" items apply only when their trigger is true for the project. Decide triggers from the plans, application and site facts.

## APPLICATION AND FEES

- **Coastal Development Permit Application** (Required) — the completed City application form: project location (address and APN), applicant, applicant representative, and property owner contact information, and a project description that briefly describes the proposed improvements. The City files it through the Public Permit Portal and states "Applications without the required application fees will not be accepted."
- **Advance Deposit and Reimbursement Agreement and Title Report** (Conditional) — At intake, require the agreement and a current title report only when the City will peer-review a geotechnical or coastal-hazards report.

## OTHER SUBMITTAL REQUIREMENTS

- **Digital Site Photos** (Required) — label file names with the direction of the photo and a description.

- **Title Report** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: "Required for projects that require a topographic survey." Dated within one year of the application date. Title insurance or a title policy is a substitute, not the deliverable. Apply filing-context before requesting a replacement.
- **Affidavit and Use and Disclosure of Architectural Drawings** (Required) — the City states: "Affidavit and Use Architectural Drawings – sign and submit Exhibits I and J."

## SUBMITTAL ITEMS DUE BEFORE THE CITY WILL NOTICE A PUBLIC HEARING

- **Noticing** (Conditional) — at least 30 calendar days before the public hearing, provide proof that the property has been posted with notice, and a receipt of payment to an approved Listing Service that prepares the mailing list of property owners within 300 feet and tenants within 100 feet. The Listing Service prints and mails the notices and provides staff an affidavit of mailing.

## OTHER SUBMITTAL REQUIREMENTS, AS NEEDED

- **Biological Assessment** (Conditional)
- **Historic Assessment** (Conditional) — Clearly identify the character-defining features and the development that will be removed and retained.
- **Water Quality Management Plan (WQMP) and WQMP Submittal Form (Exhibit M)** (Conditional) — An intake requirement only for a new home or major remodel on a slope greater than 25 percent, established from the survey or site facts. A slope alone does not establish new-home or major-remodel scope. At 25 percent or below, use the LID checklist. When the intake trigger applies, provide the WQMP and applicable submittal form; the same requirement governs its full-sized plan sheet.
- **Low Impact Development Checklist** (Conditional) — trigger: the developed slope is 25 percent or below. Do not demand a LID checklist as a substitute when WQMP is only a later staff determination for another scope over 25 percent.
- **Coastal Hazards Study and Bluff Edge Determination** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: oceanfront or bluff property, when the project proposes new development (including accessory structures), major remodels, or additions. The wave uprush and impact report considers a seasonally eroded beach combined with long-term (75 year) erosion, high tide conditions combined with long-term (75 year) projections for sea level rise, and storm waves from a 100-year event or a storm comparable to the 1982/83 El Niño event, prepared by a licensed civil engineer with expertise in coastal processes. Coastal bluff lots identify the bluff edge location per Land Use Element Definitions 101 and 102; Exhibit O carries the City's guidance.
- **Geological, soils, or geotechnical study** (Conditional) — trigger: vacant and oceanfront sites. A licensed professional Engineering Geologist or Geotechnical Engineer prepares and signs it.
- **Hydraulic Report** (Conditional) — A parcel-level floodplain overlap alone does not trigger it. The later report is a professional engineer’s No-Rise / No-Impact certification supported by hydrologic and hydraulic analyses under LBMC Chapter 25.38.
- **Traffic Study** (Conditional) — trigger: subdivisions, new commercial developments, and major commercial renovations. The City states a traffic study may also be required to comply with the California Environmental Quality Act.
- **Parking Study** (Conditional) — trigger: the proposed project does not meet the minimum parking requirements, or LBMC Chapter 25.52 specifies one.
