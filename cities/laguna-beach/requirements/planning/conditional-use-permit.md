**When to file.** The City states the trigger on page 1: "Some uses are allowed in a zoning district but may not fit every location because of their size, intensity, or other characteristics. These uses require a CUP because they could have moderate to significant impacts on the site or nearby properties." The form covers "an application for a Conditional Use Permit ('CUP'), including a CUP amendment." The City states the Planning Commission reviews all CUP applications, and that where a project requires multiple entitlements the Planning Commission is the approval authority except as the Municipal Code otherwise specifies. The City also states: "Additional applications are required when an applicant is requesting additional entitlements, such as a permit for Design Review and/or a Coastal Development Permit."

"Required" items are on every submittal of this application. "Conditional" items apply only when their trigger is true for the project. Decide triggers from the plans, application and site facts.

## APPLICATION AND FEES

- **Conditional Use Permit Application** (Required) — the completed City application form: project location (address and APN), applicant, applicant representative, and property owner contact information, and a project description. For business uses the City asks the description to include a brief description of the business operations. The City files it through the Public Permit Portal and states "Applications without the required application fees will not be accepted."
- **Advance Deposit and Reimbursement Agreement and Title Report** (Conditional) — At intake, require the agreement and a current title report only when the City will peer-review a geotechnical or coastal-hazards report.

## LETTER OF INTENT AND JUSTIFICATION

- **Letter of Intent and Justification** (Required) — the City states: "A written letter with the information required below is required to be submitted by the owner or agent/applicant with the application." This is one deliverable. The content bullets below are what a reviewer inspects inside it; they are not separate completeness rows.
  - Operational information: a complete description of the intended uses, including hours of operation; the number of employees anticipated during peak hours; a sample beverage menu and the associated California Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control license type if alcoholic beverages are proposed to be served or sold; and a list of products for retail sale, a food and beverage menu, or a list of services.
  - For food services: a delivery plan (loading and unloading area, truck size and route, delivery hours, and methods to minimize traffic impacts); a trash collection plan (size, quantity, location and type of bins, truck routes, collection frequency, spill control, means of trash transport to the bins, and methods to minimize traffic impacts); a kitchen equipment schedule including any existing or proposed grease interceptor devices; a mechanical equipment and vent schedule for new or replacement exterior equipment with keynotes tying the schedule to symbols on a roof or site plan; and the occupant load based on Building Code requirements.
  - A description of any live entertainment offered on the premises and whether prerecorded music would be provided, including proposed speaker placement, the type of live entertainment, the performance area, and performance hours.
  - Occupancy information, such as class sizes and maximum restaurant seating count.
  - A list of other similar businesses operated by the applicant or franchise.
  - Written justification addressing the findings. The City prints one set of findings and questions for sites outside the Downtown Specific Plan [LBMC 25.05.030] and a different set for sites inside it. Inside the Downtown Specific Plan the City also requires a written justification for each applicable special finding: full-service restaurants; non-full-service restaurants and food services; art galleries; formula-based businesses and souvenir stores; exceptions to the single-story height limit; and seasonal uses coinciding with the art festivals (see pages 160–161 of the Downtown Specific Plan).

## OTHER SUBMITTAL REQUIREMENTS

- **Tenant Mix Table** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: commercial projects on multi-tenant sites. Provide a table listing, for each tenant, the business name, type of business, size of tenant space in square feet, and for restaurants the seat count. For vacant tenant spaces, provide this information for the most recent occupant.
- **Digital Photographs** (Required) — interior and exterior project site photos. Label file names with the direction of the photo and a description. The City states internet photos such as Google Maps are not acceptable because they may not show current conditions.
  - The direction of the photo
  - A description
- **Title Report** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: "Required for projects that require a topographic survey." Dated within one year of the application date. Title insurance or a title policy is a substitute, not the deliverable. Apply filing-context before requesting a replacement.
- **Owner Affidavit and Use and Disclosure of Architectural Drawings** (Required) — the City states: "sign and submit Exhibits I and J." Exhibit I is titled "Affidavit"; Exhibit J is titled "Use and Disclosure of Architectural Drawings."
- **Hazardous Waste & Substances Form (Exhibit K)** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: commercial, mixed-use, and multi-family projects. Complete and return the form at Exhibit K.
- **Parking and Traffic Studies** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: CUP applications "for shared parking, four or more in-lieu parking certificates, or innovative parking solutions as prescribed by Section 25.52.006 of the Laguna Beach Municipal Code." A qualified professional prepares the study. The City retains a third party at the applicant's cost to review it, and states an initial deposit of $5,000 is required.

## SUBMITTAL ITEMS DUE BEFORE THE CITY WILL NOTICE A PUBLIC HEARING

- **Noticing** (Conditional) — at least 30 calendar days before the public hearing, provide proof that the property has been posted with notice, and a receipt of payment to an approved Listing Service that prepares the mailing list of property owners within 300 feet and tenants within 100 feet. The Listing Service prints and mails the notices and provides staff an affidavit of mailing. This is a pre-hearing item, not a completeness item.
