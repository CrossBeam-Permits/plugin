**When to file.** The City states the trigger on page 1 of the application: this is the application "for projects that DO NOT require a discretionary planning entitlement, such as Design Review or a Coastal Development Permit." A planner screens the application against the checklist below. When the application is complete and no discretionary entitlement is required, the City issues a zoning clearance and the applicant may then submit for a Building Permit.

"Required" items are on every submittal of this application. "Conditional" items apply only when their trigger is true for the project. Decide triggers from the plans, application and site facts.

## APPLICATION AND FEES

- **Zone Clearance Application** (Required) — the completed City application form: project location (address and APN), applicant, applicant representative, and property owner contact information, and a project description that briefly describes the proposed use and improvements. The City files it through the Public Permit Portal.
- **Advance Deposit and Reimbursement Agreement and Title Report** (Conditional) — At intake, require the agreement and a current title report only when the City will peer-review a geotechnical or coastal-hazards report.
- **Completed Objective Design Standards checklists** (Conditional) — trigger: the applicant elects Chapter 25.13 for a new single-family or two-family primary dwelling, including an SB 9 project. Include the completed Chapter 2 General Design Standards checklist and exactly one applicant-selected Chapter 3 Form Group checklist (A, B, C, or D), with the corresponding plan-sheet location for every applicable standard. Source: City document 26790 §§1.2–1.4 and Chapters 2–3.

## TITLE SHEET

- **Title Sheet** (Required) — provide address of site and assessor's parcel number, legal description of the site, and contact information for the property owner, project architect or designer, civil engineer, and other design professionals (names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses).
  - Include a detailed scope of work and a plan sheet index.
  - Include the Project Summary Table in the format and content of the City's template (Exhibit D).
  - State the zoning designation.
  - State site constraints, environmentally sensitive areas, and fire safety designation, if applicable. The City points to its GIS map for this information.

## SURVEY

- **Topographic Survey** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires a survey for the project's type. Required scale 1/8" = 1'-0". Depict existing conditions and all public and private easements. Prepared, signed, and stamped by a licensed surveyor or civil engineer. The Site Survey Requirements handout (Exhibit E) carries additional requirements.
- **Record of Survey** (Conditional) — Applies only when the project involves boundary or monument work, including resolving unclear lot lines or locating, resetting, or setting monuments. Show all property boundary lines, public and private easements, and whether new monuments were set to show the boundary lines. Prepared, signed, and stamped by a licensed surveyor or civil engineer, and filed for processing with the County Surveyor's Office for review and recordation.

## SITE PLAN

- **Site Plan** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires a site plan for the project's type. Required scale 1/8" = 1'-0".
  - Include a north arrow (magnetic and project north).
  - Include a legend (line types, hatched and shaded areas, abbreviations).
  - Show and label all buildings on the lot. Delineate and dimension all lot boundaries, required setbacks, and existing and proposed structures at the narrowest point. Dimension the interior of garages and carports, exterior parking spaces, vehicular maneuvering, and back-up areas.
  - Show topographic contours at two-foot intervals covering the full site to the center of the right-of-way.
  - Label, locate, and dimension all proposed site hardscape improvements. Identify the height and material of all existing and proposed fences or walls, and show the location of any required pool security fencing.
  - Show average lot width and depth [LBMC 25.08.022].
  - Provide flagged height elevations at: the fixed datum point from a survey showing a permanent, identifiable benchmark in the field; the top of curb or centerline of the street right-of-way, midway between the projected side lot lines [LBMC 25.08.016]; the centerline of the street opposite the driveway and at the intersection of the driveway and front property line [LBMC 25.52.008(I)(3)(b)]; and both front and rear property corners [LBMC 25.08.034].
  - Label, dimension, and show the centerline of the right-of-way of record and the paved (improved) centerline width of all abutting streets and alleyways. Show all existing and proposed improvements within the right-of-way, including driveways, sidewalks, meters, and utility boxes and poles.
  - Depict the location and dimensions of all existing and proposed public or private easements and all property to be dedicated to the City for street or other purposes.
  - Note the average driveway gradient, calculated from the property line to the face of the garage or parking spaces [LBMC 25.52.008(I)(3)].
  - On vacant and oceanfront sites, show the immediate outline of existing structures on adjacent contiguous properties.
  - Locate and dimension the trash storage area [LBMC 25.53.012].
  - For new development, major remodels (defined in Exhibit F), and additions exceeding 50 percent of the existing structure size, note that all utility lines to the nearest existing pole will be undergrounded [LBMC 25.53.010]. Specify the type and location of the utility connection (pole or box) and indicate the size of all utility boxes.
  - Show the proposed connection to the public sewer system, if applicable.

## ROOF PLAN

- **Roof Plan** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires a roof plan for the project's type. Required scale 1/8" = 1'-0". Dimension and show the existing and proposed roof plans.
  - On the existing roof plan, delineate and dimension the existing roof area and the proposed demolition areas (eaves are included in roof area). Provide a table that tallies the existing and demolition roof areas.
  - Show the roof form (ridges, hips, valleys, crickets, parapets) and note elevation heights of ridges, eaves, skylights, and chimneys. Show roof slopes and pitch, show the solar-ready roof area, and dimension eave overhangs.

## FLOOR PLANS

- **Floor Plans (as-built and proposed)** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires floor plans for the project's type. Required scale 1/4" = 1'-0".
  - Orient the plans to match the site plan, including a north arrow (magnetic and project north) and property lines.
  - Indicate the use and dimensions of all existing and proposed rooms and interior garages.
  - On the as-built floor plan, dimension and show the existing walls and the walls proposed to be demolished, rebuilt, and new. Provide a table that tallies the existing and demolished wall lengths in linear feet. Provide a legend identifying existing walls to remain (plain), existing walls to be rebuilt (shaded), walls to be demolished (dashed), and proposed new walls (dark shade).
  - Note the finish floor elevation of each level, including finish surface elevations for all decks, patios, terraces, and landings.

## ELEVATION VIEWS

- **Elevation Views (as-built and proposed)** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires elevation views for the project's type. Required scale 1/4" = 1'-0".
  - Label height elevations of roofs, floors, plate lines, decks, and railings. Dimension ceiling heights.
  - Differentiate new from existing construction using shading or crosshatching, and provide a legend stating what the shading or crosshatch represents.
  - Show existing grades as a dashed line and proposed grades as a solid line.
  - Illustrate the maximum allowed building envelope with dashed lines [LBMC 25.50.004(d)].
  - Include landscape site walls exceeding five feet in height.
  - On the as-built elevations, show and dimension the demolition areas using shading or crosshatch, and provide a legend stating what the shading or crosshatch represents.

## CROSS SECTIONS

- **Cross Sections** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires cross sections for the project's type. Required scale 1/4" = 1'-0". Provide a minimum of two perpendicular views through the complete site showing the structure and the lines of existing and proposed grade. Include the maximum allowed building envelope and elevation heights, and site improvements such as walls, terraces, and pools or spas. The City states that existing and proposed building heights are measured from the outside structural walls, including retaining walls when below grade.

## FLOOR AREA CALCULATIONS

- **Floor Area Calculations** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires floor area calculations for the project's type. Required scale 1/4" = 1'-0". Provide a separate, dimensioned plan using geometric shapes to show the method used to determine the existing and proposed floor areas. Identify living, garage, deck, and storage or mechanical areas, referenced to a table that itemizes and tallies each area. Floor area is calculated from the outside structural wall, including retaining walls when below grade.

## GRADING AND DRAINAGE PLAN

- **Grading and Drainage Plan** (Conditional) — trigger confirmed by staff: the project proposes grading and its cut and/or fill outside the existing building footprint exceeds 20 cubic yards; grading inside that footprint does not count. The matrix heads its column "Grading & Drainage (When > 20 Cu. Yd.)", marks it for the Grading and Hardscape project types, and marks it "if req." for an accessory dwelling unit, an addition under 10 percent, and retaining walls.
  - Provide a preliminary grading and drainage plan with the proposed cut and fill area delineated. Provide slope profiles to illustrate the extent of the proposed grading. Show contours for existing conditions and proposed work.
  - For project sites containing a mapped significant watercourse or sensitive habitat, show temporary protective barriers beyond the required setback for the protection of the watercourse and sensitive habitat during construction.

## LANDSCAPE PLAN

- **Landscape Plan** (Conditional) — the matrix marks a landscape plan for the Landscape Improvements project type only. Required scale 1/8" = 1'-0". When landscape scope is in play, provide landscape plans including new and existing-to-remain plant material. The Landscape Submittal Checklist (Exhibit G) carries the comprehensive list of requirements. A pool, ground-level lounge deck, patio cover, or lawn shown incidentally does not by itself create landscape scope.
  - For R-1 zoned property, provide a landscape open space dimensioned plan [LBMC 25.10.008(O)].

## LIGHTING PLAN

- **Lighting Plan** (Conditional) — trigger: Exhibit C requires a lighting plan for the project's type. The matrix marks no lighting plan for any of the ministerial project types, so on a Zone Clearance this sheet is rarely in play. Required scale 1/8" = 1'-0". Show exterior site lighting, including all lighting fixtures proposed for the exterior of the building (soffit and surface mounted). Include a summary table, keyed to the plan, indicating proposed fixture type, quantity, lumens, and wattage. Include lighting cut sheets for verification against the City's Good Neighbor Outdoor Lighting requirements [LBMC 7.70].

## FIRE DEPARTMENT - SITE ACCESS PLAN

- **Fire Department Site Access Plan** (Conditional) — Required only when proposed new building-type construction (a new home, detached ADU, or other detached structure) could block existing fire access. Attached ADUs, additions, grading alone, interior work, roof or deck work, and surface replacement never trigger it. Neither VHFHSZ nor another hazard overlay triggers it. A legible, project-identifiable site-access depiction satisfies intake. Completed Fire review and technical adequacy belong to Building/Fire.
- **Fire Protection Plan** (Conditional) — the current City application package or an identity-qualified official City designation combined with the form's exact VHFHSZ trigger may establish that this sheet applies. Planning checks presence, legibility, and identity only and routes it to Fire/Building.
- **Fuel modification program** (Conditional) — an identity-qualified official FM designation combined with the current form's exact trigger may establish applicability. Planning checks presence, legibility, and identity only. Fire/Building decides technical content and any Alternative Materials and Methods request; raw overlay truthiness is not the Planning decision rule.

## SITE AND RIGHT-OF-WAY IMPROVEMENT PLAN

- **Site and Right-of-Way Improvement Plan** (Conditional) — Applies for a new home or a major remodel under the Local Coastal Program's 50-percent test; for other projects, evaluate it only when site or right-of-way improvements are actually in scope. Required scale 1/8" = 1'-0". When applicable, provide a topographic survey (including public and private easements) and the improvement overlay.

## OTHER SUBMITTAL REQUIREMENTS, AS NEEDED

- **Biological Assessment** (Conditional)
- **Historic Assessment** (Conditional) — Clearly identify the character-defining features and the development that will be removed and retained.
- **Water Quality Management Plan (WQMP) and WQMP Submittal Form (Exhibit I)** (Conditional) — An intake requirement only for a new home or major remodel on a slope greater than 25 percent, established from the survey or site facts. A slope alone does not establish new-home or major-remodel scope. At 25 percent or below, use the LID checklist. When the intake trigger applies, provide the WQMP and applicable submittal form; the same requirement governs its full-sized plan sheet.
- **Low Impact Development Checklist** (Conditional) — trigger: the developed slope is 25 percent or below. Do not demand a LID checklist as a substitute when WQMP is only a later staff determination for another scope over 25 percent.
- **Coastal Hazards Study and Bluff Edge Determination** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: oceanfront or bluff property, when the project proposes new development (including accessory structures), major remodels, or additions. The wave uprush and impact report considers a seasonally eroded beach combined with long-term (75 year) erosion, high tide conditions combined with long-term (75 year) projections for sea level rise, and storm waves from a 100-year event or a storm comparable to the 1982/83 El Niño event. A licensed civil engineer with expertise in coastal processes prepares it. Coastal bluff lots identify the bluff edge location per Land Use Element Definitions 101 and 102; Exhibit J carries the City's guidance.
- **Geological, soils, or geotechnical study** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: vacant and oceanfront sites. A licensed professional Engineering Geologist or Geotechnical Engineer prepares and signs it.
- **Hydraulic Report** (Conditional) — A parcel-level floodplain overlap alone does not trigger it. The later report is a professional engineer’s No-Rise / No-Impact certification supported by hydrologic and hydraulic analyses under LBMC Chapter 25.38.
- **Traffic Study** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: subdivisions, new commercial developments, and major commercial renovations. The study identifies measures to mitigate identified impacts. The City states a traffic study may also be required to comply with the California Environmental Quality Act.
- **Parking Study** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: the proposed project does not meet the minimum parking requirements, or LBMC Chapter 25.52 specifies one.
