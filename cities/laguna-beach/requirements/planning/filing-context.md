# Existing applications and public-owner authorization

_Generated by the plugin publisher from the maintained Laguna Beach requirements source. Do not edit by hand; changes are overwritten._

This is evidence-handling guidance, not a City waiver. It does not change the entitlement the project requires. Read the operative submittal and City receipt, prior acceptance, reviewer direction and any prior application of record. A form's publication date is not its submission date. An old edition alone neither proves acceptance nor proves the applicant owes a replacement. For a clearly new application use the current published requirements. Continue evaluating the known entitlement's supporting checklist while that question is unresolved. A new entitlement not covered by that prior filing is a separate application, provided its need is established. For a City or Caltrans right-of-way site filed by a private applicant, identify the public owner and inspect the authorization or agreement actually supplied. Do not presume a private-landowner signature is the only acceptable instrument; equally, public ownership does not establish a waiver. Do not request a private title report merely because a survey of a public street was submitted. A public agency's or public utility's own project follows "Public agency projects" below instead. Keep each source's owner affidavit, drawing disclosure and right-of-way access requirement distinct. One artifact can satisfy several requirements only when its contents do so. When the semantic filing classification establishes an extension-of-time request, these five rows are not applicable: geotechnical study, coastal hazards study, advance deposit agreement, title report, and digital site photos. The filing extends an existing approval; it does not require replacement studies or owner documents merely because the ordinary application checklist lists them. Judge every other row on any scope change the request carries, as for any filing. A physical addition or deck extension alone is not an extension of time. Fire access and hydraulic applicability follow their own scope rules, not this five-row exclusion. Record the prior approval and the request evidence; never infer this class from the permit prefix or a filename. Planners return an unsigned application by page number. Only when none of those applications came in does the first case apply. A signature that counts under these rules (on the other line, or on the owner's letter) must meet the same test as the affidavit: a typed name alone is not a signature, and when the packet cannot settle authenticity, staff decide. - **The owner is also the applicant: one signature counts for both lines.** When the application names the same person or company as applicant and as property owner, that person's signature on either the applicant line or the owner line satisfies both, on the affidavit and on every other page of that package that has both lines. Decide who is who from the application's contact section, the affidavit and the plans' title block, not from a file name. When the applicant is someone else (an architect, a designer, a contractor, a permit runner), the owner's line still needs the owner. - **A public agency's or utility's own project: no owner signature.** Follow "Public agency projects" below. A wireless carrier's facility is not this case; follow the Wireless Facility application. - **An owner's letter of authorization counts for the owner's signature.** When the owner line on the affidavit is blank but the packet holds a letter signed by the property owner (or an officer or manager of an owner company) that names the property and authorizes the applicant to apply for this work, the owner's signature is supplied. A letter the applicant, contractor or agent signs for itself is not the owner's authorization (a contractor's letter authorizing its own employee to pull permits), and an owner's signature on a Building Division form does not sign the Planning affidavit. - **No architect, no architect's signature.** The Use and Disclosure of Architectural Drawings (Exhibit J on Design Review, ADU & JADU, Coastal Development Permit and Conditional Use Permit; Exhibit I on the Variance; Exhibit B on the Sign Permit; Exhibit C on the Wireless Facility Permit; the Zone Clearance has none) is the architect's or building designer's permission for the City to post their architectural drawings online. Its landscape architect and engineer lines say "if applicable" and follow the same rule. Decide who the applicant is from the application and the project description, never from a permit prefix or a file name. A private owner or business working in the right-of-way is not a public agency project. The Digital Site Photos row comes only from the applications that print it (Design Review, ADU & JADU, Coastal Development Permit, Variance and Conditional Use Permit). The Zone Clearance Application has no photo row; never add one. The direction and description labels are never asked at intake.
