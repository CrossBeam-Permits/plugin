**When to file.** The City states the trigger on page 1: a Revocable Encroachment Permit ("REP") "may allow minor construction in an otherwise unimproved, unused public right-of-way." The City limits the class of project to "minor structures such as low fences and retaining walls, walkways, stairs and construction incidentally related to landscape activities such as steppingstones, walking surfaces, planter areas and irrigation systems." The City also states a REP "shall not be used for any structure or improvement that, if demand is made for its removal, will create any nonconforming conditions or unlawful conditions under the provisions of the municipal code or any other codes, regulations or standards incorporated therein." The REP "consists of discretionary approval by the Community Development Director or their designee, following a noticed public hearing." The City states that use and maintenance of an encroachment in the public right-of-way "is not a right but a privilege extended by the City acting as the custodian of the overall public interest." The City directs the applicant to the procedures for a revocable encroachment permit at LBMC 11.50 and to the Zoning Code more generally at Title 25. The City also states: "Additional applications are required when an applicant is requesting additional entitlements, such as Design Review."

"Required" items are on every submittal of this application. "Conditional" items apply only when their trigger is true for the project. Decide triggers from the plans, application and site facts.

## APPLICATION AND FEES

- **Revocable Encroachment Permit Application** (Required) — the completed City application form. The form carries the project location (address and APN), the applicant, applicant representative, and property owner contact information, and a description field. The City prints the description instruction as "Briefly describe the project in its entirety and more specifically the improvements proposed to be located in the right of way" — read the description for both halves. The City states all communications go to the emails in file. The City files the application through the Public Permit Portal and states "Applications without the required application fees will not be accepted."

## ENCROACHMENT PLAN

- **Encroachment Plan** (Required) — item 2. The City states: "A detailed plan shall be on an 8 ½" x 11" sheet of paper entitled 'Encroachment Plan,' drawn clearly and legibly using conventional drafting practices." The City lists the contents: - The plan is based on accurate field measurements, at a scale of no less than 1" = 20', and shows existing gradients and improvements within twenty-five feet of the project area (item 2a). - Property lines, easements, drainage patterns and cultural features are shown true to scale and dimension within twenty-five feet of the project area. The plan identifies the complete right-of-way, and identifies the adjacent and opposite edge-of-pavement. If the property lies within 25 feet of a cul-de-sac, the plan identifies the complete cul-de-sac right-of-way and edge of pavement (item 2b). - Proposed improvements are shown legibly and accurately as to line and grade, including surface elevations, wall heights, fence heights, cut/fill slopes and materials (items 2c and 2d). - Grading information is shown if applicable (item 2e).
- **Geological, soils, or geotechnical study** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City in item 2e: "If the proposal involves the grading of more than twenty cubic yards, a geotechnical report is also required."

## AFFIDAVIT AND EXHIBITS

- **Affidavit** (Required) — Exhibit A. The property owner signs it and certifies the seven statements the City prints, including that the information in the application is true, that the owner has read and understands LBMC Chapter 25.05, and that the owner is the legal owner and consents to the filing. The City states acceptable signatures are a wet signature (a scan is acceptable) or an authenticated electronic signature such as DocuSign, and that a typed name alone is not sufficient.
- Exhibit C. The applicant signs three separate statements: that the application's list of information and documents is the criteria the City applies to determine completeness under Government Code Sections 65940 and 65941; that the applicant understands the completeness review procedure under Sections 65940 and 65943; and a certification of compliance with Government Code Section 65962.5 on the lists of hazardous waste and substance facilities and sites. Apply the same signature rule as the affidavit.

## SUBMITTAL ITEMS DUE BEFORE THE CITY WILL NOTICE A PUBLIC HEARING

- **Noticing** (Conditional) — item 4a. At least 20 calendar days before the public hearing, provide proof that the property has been posted with notice, and a receipt of payment to an approved Listing Service that prepares the mailing list of property owners within 300 feet and tenants within 100 feet of the subject property. The Listing Service prints and mails the public notices and provides staff an affidavit of mailing by the date staff specifies. This application states 20 calendar days. The Variance application states 30. Use 20 here; it is this application's number.

## LATER-STAGE ITEMS

- Site Posting — item 5a, due before the public hearing. Staff sends the applicant the public hearing notice. The applicant posts it on the property 10 days before the hearing. Staff also provides a Posting Affidavit form, which the applicant completes and returns before the hearing.
- Notarized Revocable License and Encroachment Permit Agreement — item 6a and Exhibit B, due only if the REP is approved. The City states that once the 14-day appeal period has lapsed, the City returns the executed agreement to the applicant for recording with the Orange County Clerk. Exhibit B is the agreement form itself, signed by the Community Development Director and notarized, and accepted and notarized by the property owner.

## APPROVAL CRITERIA (CONTEXT ONLY)

- a. It should be located in a manner that is not hazardous to the traveling public, including motorists, bicyclists and pedestrians.
- b. It should be sufficiently set back from the edge of pavement or street centerline to provide adequate travel, parking and walking lanes.
- c. It should not conflict with preexisting public utility structures, especially hydrants, vault and service meters, in any manner that necessitates relocation thereof at public expense or causes any other unacceptable interference, including impediments to the maintenance, relocation or repair of pipelines, conduits or substructures of any public utility.
- d. It will not preclude public access, use or enjoyment of any area that has historically established such access, use or enjoyment.
- e. It is not precedent setting in nature to the extent that it creates a noticeable projection into the streetscape as established by existing construction and improvements on neighboring properties.
- f. It does not create structures of unusual or unacceptable appearance, form, shape or height that detract from the general quality of the streetscape.
