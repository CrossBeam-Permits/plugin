**When to file.** The City states the trigger on page 1: "This application includes a comprehensive list of information and documents that are required to submit an application for a variance." On when a variance applies, the City states that the variance procedure "is designed to permit adjustments to the zoning regulations when there are special or extraordinary circumstances applying to a parcel of land or building which prevent the property from being used to the extent intended by the zoning regulations," and that "A variance is intended to be the rare exception, not the rule." The City also states: "Additional applications are required when an applicant is requesting additional entitlements, such as a permit for Design Review." The procedures are at LBMC 25.05.025.

"Required" items are on every submittal of this application. "Conditional" items apply only when their trigger is true for the project. Decide triggers from the plans, application and site facts.

## APPLICATION AND FEES

- **Variance Application** (Required) — the completed City application form: project location (address and APN), applicant, applicant representative, and property owner contact information, a project description, and the variance request itself — the City prints "I hereby request a Variance from the following provisions of the Zoning Code" as a write-in field. The City files it through the Public Permit Portal and states "Applications without the required application fees will not be accepted."
- **Written justification for the four findings** (Required) — the application form carries four write-in narrative answers, one per finding, under the heading "STATE JUSTIFICATION FOR REQUEST (ATTACH ADDITIONAL SHEETS IF NEEDED)." The City states the decision-making authority must make all four findings, each supported by substantial evidence: special circumstances applicable to the property; necessity for the preservation and enjoyment of a substantial property right; no detriment to public health, safety, convenience and welfare or injury to property improvements in the vicinity; and no conflict with the objectives of the zoning ordinance or the General Plan. Presence means the justification narratives are in the packet. Whether they are persuasive is the Planning Commission's call, never a completeness call.
- **Advance Deposit and Reimbursement Agreement and Title Report** (Conditional) — At intake, require the agreement and a current title report only when the City will peer-review a geotechnical or coastal-hazards report.

## SUBMITTAL ITEMS DUE BEFORE THE CITY WILL NOTICE A PUBLIC HEARING

- **Noticing** (Conditional) — at least 30 calendar days before the public hearing, provide proof that the property has been posted with notice, and a receipt of payment to an approved Listing Service that prepares the mailing list of property owners within 300 feet and tenants within 100 feet. The Listing Service prints and mails the notices and provides staff an affidavit of mailing.

## OTHER SUBMITTAL REQUIREMENTS, AS NEEDED

- **Biological Assessment** (Conditional)
- **Historic Assessment** (Conditional) — Clearly identify the character-defining features and the development that will be removed and retained.
- **Water Quality Management Plan (WQMP) and WQMP Submittal Form (Exhibit J)** (Conditional) — An intake requirement only for a new home or major remodel on a slope greater than 25 percent, established from the survey or site facts. A slope alone does not establish new-home or major-remodel scope. At 25 percent or below, use the LID checklist. When the intake trigger applies, provide the WQMP and applicable submittal form; the same requirement governs its full-sized plan sheet.
- **Low Impact Development Checklist** (Conditional) — trigger: the developed slope is 25 percent or below. Do not demand a LID checklist as a substitute when WQMP is only a later staff determination for another scope over 25 percent.
- **Coastal Hazards Study and Bluff Edge Determination** (Conditional) — trigger stated by the City: oceanfront or bluff property, when the project proposes new development (including accessory structures), major remodels, or additions. The wave uprush and impact report considers a seasonally eroded beach combined with long-term (75 year) erosion, high tide conditions combined with long-term (75 year) projections for sea level rise, and storm waves from a 100-year event or a storm comparable to the 1982/83 El Niño event, prepared by a licensed civil engineer with expertise in coastal processes. Coastal bluff lots identify the bluff edge location per Land Use Element Definitions 101 and 102; Exhibit L carries the City's guidance.
- **Geological, soils, or geotechnical study** (Conditional) — trigger: vacant and oceanfront sites. A licensed professional Engineering Geologist or Geotechnical Engineer prepares and signs it.
- **Hydraulic Report** (Conditional) — A parcel-level floodplain overlap alone does not trigger it. The later report is a professional engineer’s No-Rise / No-Impact certification supported by hydrologic and hydraulic analyses under LBMC Chapter 25.38.
- **Traffic Study** (Conditional) — trigger: subdivisions, new commercial developments, and major commercial renovations. The City states a traffic study may also be required to comply with the California Environmental Quality Act.
- **Parking Study** (Conditional) — trigger: the proposed project does not meet the minimum parking requirements, or LBMC Chapter 25.52 specifies one. This application states a parking study "will be required"; the Design Review application states "may be required" for the same condition.
