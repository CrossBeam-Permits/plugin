> **Feasible by right: a 900 sf, 2-bed / 2-bath detached ADU fits under Los Alamitos's 1,000 sf cap for 2+ bedroom units and gets ministerial, no-hearing review.**

The unit must stay at or under **16 ft** tall, sit at least **4 ft** from the side and rear lot lines, and is approved ministerially by the Development Services Director with no public hearing (17.28.020(E)(4)(c), (E)(6)(b), (H)(3)).

**Recommended path:** a custom 900 sf single-story plan, filed as a standard ADU building permit through the City's online Permit Center. The City's pre-approved 876 sf 2-bed/2-bath plan is close in size, but it cannot be changed, and the owner's wish list (island or peninsula, bigger closet, side-by-side laundry) points to a custom layout.

**Biggest constraints:** the site sits in a CGS **liquefaction zone**, so plan on a soils report and a foundation designed to it; the **6 ft spacing** to the house noted in the field likely triggers fire-rated walls and limited openings on the facing walls; and the existing **100 A panel** will almost certainly need a service upgrade for an all-electric ADU.

## Risks, highest first

1. **Liquefaction zone:** the City will likely require a soils report, and the foundation may need to be deeper or engineered; adds a report and possibly foundation cost. Settled by ordering the soils report first.
2. **6 ft spacing to the house:** facing walls would likely need 1-hour rating and limited openings; adds wall and window cost or forces a layout change. Settled by holding 10 ft in the design, or by the City's answer on separation.
3. **100 A panel:** an all-electric ADU likely needs a service upgrade with SCE coordination; adds cost and utility lead time. Settled by an early load calc.
4. **Possible rear easement:** could push the ADU forward and shrink the buildable area. Settled by a title report and survey.
5. **Lot coverage:** if the house and covered patio use most of the 50%, the part of the ADU above 800 sf could be at issue. Settled by the house footprint and Planning's answer.
6. **Fee timing:** the impact-fee share may drop for filings after Jan 1, 2027. Settled by Building's answer on when fees lock.

## The project questions

`CODE` law text we read · `CROSSBEAM` CrossBeam data · `FIELD` site visit or owner · `INFERRED` our reading; confirm with the City

### 1. Can it be 900 sf with 2 bedrooms and 2 baths?

**Yes. The cap is 1,000 sf for a detached unit with two or more bedrooms.**

- `CODE` LAMC 17.28.020(E)(4)(a)(ii): a detached ADU may not exceed 850 sf for a studio or 1-bed, or 1,000 sf for 2+ bedrooms. Minimum is 150 sf (E)(4)(b).
- `CODE` State law requires cities to allow at least 1,000 sf for a 2+ bedroom ADU (Gov. Code 66321(b)(2)(B)). The City code matches that floor.
- `INFERRED` Covered porches and patio covers are generally not counted in ADU floor area. Ask Planning how they measure the 1,000 sf if the design adds a covered entry.

### 2. Height, setbacks and lot coverage

**Single story at or under 16 ft, 4 ft from the side and rear lines. Coverage only becomes a question for the area above 800 sf.**

- `CODE` Height: 16 ft for a detached ADU (17.28.020(E)(4)(c)(i)). The 18 ft limit applies only within ½ mile walking distance of a major transit stop or high-quality transit corridor (c)(ii).
- `CROSSBEAM` No major transit stop within ½ mile, so the 16 ft limit governs.
- `CODE` Setbacks: at least 4 ft from the side and rear lot lines (17.28.020(E)(6)(b)).
- `CODE` Lot coverage, FAR, open space and front setbacks are waived only as far as needed to allow an 800 sf ADU with 4 ft setbacks (17.28.020(E)(5)).
- `INFERRED` R-1 maximum lot coverage is 50% (from a code-host summary of Zoning Code Table 2-03, not the table itself). At 50% of about 7,400 sf, roughly 3,700 sf can be covered; the house, the covered patio and the ADU footprint must fit under that, with the first 800 sf of ADU protected. Get the house footprint from the assessor or a survey.

### 3. Separation from the main house (field note: 6+ ft)

**The zoning code sets no ADU-to-house distance, but at 6 ft the Building Code will likely require rated walls. Aim for 10 ft if the layout allows.**

- `CODE` LAMC 17.28.020 has no building-separation standard for ADUs. The 5 ft separation in 17.28.030(C)(6) applies to ordinary accessory structures, which by definition are not ADUs.
- `INFERRED` Under the Residential Code, buildings on one lot measure fire separation to an imaginary line between them. At 6 ft apart each wall sits about 3 ft from that line, so the facing walls would need 1-hour rating with limited openings and eave projections (CRC R302.1). At 10 ft, each wall is 5 ft from the line and the rating generally drops off.
- `INFERRED` A CrossBeam answer said the City "notes 10 ft" separation for ADUs but could not confirm it is a rule. Ask Planning.

### 4. Parking (field note: no additional parking needed)

**Likely none required. A bus stop is about 330 ft away in a straight line.**

- `CODE` One space is required unless the ADU is within ½ mile walking distance of public transit, including bus stops (17.28.020(E)(7)(a), (c)(i); Gov. Code 66322(a)(1)).
- `INFERRED` The stop is 330 ft straight-line, far under the ½ mile limit, so the walking route should qualify easily. Put the walking distance on the site plan.

### 5. Fire sprinklers (field note: the house has none)

**Not required.**

- `CODE` Fire sprinklers are not required in an ADU if they are not required for the primary dwelling (17.28.020(H)(1); Gov. Code 66314(d)(12)).
- `FIELD` The house is unsprinklered.
- `INFERRED` One CrossBeam answer said the ADU needs sprinklers as a new Group R building. That conflicts with the City code and state law above, so this report does not rely on it. Confirm with Building at intake.

### 6. Fees (field estimate: $10k+)

**A reasonable placeholder. Filing after Jan 1, 2027 may cut the impact-fee share.**

- `CODE` Today, ADUs of 750 sf or more pay impact fees in proportion to the size of the primary dwelling; plan-check and permit fees are separate (17.28.020(J)).
- `INFERRED` From Jan 1, 2027: SB 1117 (2026) is reported to limit that proportional fee to the square footage above 750 sf, so 150 sf instead of 900 sf here. From bill analyses, not the chaptered text: read it before you rely on it.
- `INFERRED` School developer fees apply to residential additions over 500 sf, plus state SMIP and green-building fees. CrossBeam has no Los Alamitos fee schedule, so get a fee estimate from Building, and ask whether impact fees lock at application or at permit issuance.

### 7. Extra reports: boundary survey, soils report, solar

**Budget for all three as owner-paid items outside the base construction scope.**

- `CROSSBEAM` The parcel is in a CGS liquefaction Zone of Required Investigation.
- `INFERRED` For a new habitable building in this zone, expect the City to require a soils or geotechnical report, and expect the foundation design to follow it.
- `INFERRED` A boundary survey is the cleanest way to prove the 4 ft setbacks and the lot-coverage math, especially with a possible easement at the rear.
- `INFERRED` The City's pre-approved plans page notes that solar PV takes a separate permit. The Energy Code generally requires PV on newly built detached ADUs; the builder excludes solar, so the owner should plan a separate solar contract and permit.

### 8. Easements "maybe toward the back"

**Unconfirmed. Pull a title report before fixing the ADU's location.**

- `FIELD` The owner mentioned a possible rear easement.
- `INFERRED` A building cannot sit on a utility easement without the holder's consent, even where the 4 ft setback would otherwise allow it. A preliminary title report plus the survey settles this.

### 9. Owner occupancy, rentals and number of units

**No owner-occupancy requirement. No short-term rentals. One detached ADU now, possibly two after Jan 1, 2027.**

- `CODE` Owner occupancy is not required for an ADU (17.28.020(G)(3)(a)). Rentals must be longer than 30 days (G)(2). The ADU cannot be sold separately (G)(1).
- `CODE` The City allows one detached new-construction ADU plus one attached or converted ADU and one JADU on a single-family lot (17.28.020(E)(3)(a)).
- `INFERRED` From Jan 1, 2027: AB 956 (2026) is reported to require ministerial approval of up to two detached ADUs on a single-family lot, each possibly limited to 800 sf. From bill analyses, not the chaptered text: read it before you rely on it.

## Recommended path

1. **Design a custom 900 sf, single-story, 2-bed / 2-bath plan** at or under 16 ft, with 4 ft side and rear setbacks. Stay under 1,000 sf, including how the City counts any covered entry.
2. **Keep at least 10 ft from the house if the yard allows.** If it must be about 6 ft, design the facing walls as 1-hour rated with limited openings.
3. **Order the survey, title report and soils report first.** They fix the footprint, rule the rear easement in or out, and set the foundation for the liquefaction zone.
4. **Run an electrical load calc** and plan the service upgrade with SCE alongside design.
5. **Show the transit stop walking distance** on the site plan so no parking space is required.
6. **Time the filing around Jan 1, 2027** if the fee change holds. Ask Building whether fees lock at application or at issuance.
7. **File a standard ADU building permit** through the online Permit Center.

Review: Development Services (Planning, then Building); ministerial, no hearing (17.28.020(H)(3)); state law sets a 60-day decision clock on a complete application (Gov. Code 66317).
